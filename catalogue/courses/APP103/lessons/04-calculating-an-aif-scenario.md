## What AIF does in the braid

Apprenticeship Innovation Funding (AIF) is California money administered by the Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS). It goes to the **program sponsor**, not to the apprentice and not to your board, and it is generally tied to the number of eligible apprentices in an eligible program. For you, that matters in two ways:

- AIF can lower the sponsor's real cost per apprentice, which strengthens your pitch to an employer.
- Whatever AIF covers is off the table for WIOA. You build the WIOA share around it.

Eligibility (which programs and occupations qualify), the per-apprentice amount, what the sponsor may spend it on, and how often it is paid are set by DAS and change over time. **Before any real calculation, get the current AIF guidance from DAS and confirm the sponsor's eligibility with the sponsor.** This lesson teaches the method; you plug in today's numbers.

## The calculation method

Work one apprentice, one year at a time.

1. **List total costs by component** (OJT wages, related instruction, supportive services, program costs).
2. **Subtract what is already funded** at the source: RSI hours the college claims, any grant-funded hours, the employer's committed share.
3. **Apply AIF** to the costs the sponsor says it will cover with AIF for this apprentice. Use the current DAS rate times the number of eligible apprentices, and ask the sponsor how it allocates that money.
4. **Apply WIOA** last, only to remaining gaps the participant is eligible for: OJT reimbursement under 20 CFR 680.730 and your board's policy, an ITA for uncovered instruction costs, supportive services under your board's limits.
5. **What is left is the employer's (or sponsor's) net cost.** Show it. Employers decide on net cost.

Order matters. Putting WIOA last protects it as the gap-filler and makes double-dipping easy to spot.

## Worked example (fictional figures)

The numbers below are **workshop figures only**. They are not current AIF, RSI, or WIOA rates. Replace every bracketed item with real values from current guidance and your board's policy.

Apprentice: Andre, 24, a WIOA Adult participant, entering year one of a pharmacy technician apprenticeship sponsored by Coastline Health Partners. Coastline confirms with DAS that the program is AIF-eligible.

**Step 1. Total year-one costs**

| Component | Item | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OJT | Wages, 2,000 hours at $20/hour | $40,000 |
| Related instruction | 160 hours at partner college; textbooks and lab fees | Instruction: college-funded; books and fees $900 |
| Supportive services | Scrubs, background check, transit pass | $600 |
| Program costs | Mentor time and coordination for Andre | $3,000 |
| **Total** | | **$44,500** (excluding college-funded instruction) |

**Step 2. Already funded at the source**

The college claims RSI for all 160 instruction hours. No grant covers these hours, so RSI is clean. WIOA will not pay tuition.

**Step 3. Apply AIF**

Workshop assumption: Coastline expects [AIF per-apprentice amount], shown here as **$3,000**, and tells you it will use it for Andre's mentor and coordination costs.

| Cost | Before AIF | AIF | Remaining |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Program costs | $3,000 | $3,000 | $0 |

**Step 4. Apply WIOA**

Workshop assumption: Valley Regional's OJT policy reimburses [board OJT rate], shown here as **50%** of wages, for [board-set training period], shown here as the **first 1,000 hours**.

| Cost | Remaining | WIOA | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OJT wages, hours 1-1,000 | $20,000 | $10,000 | OJT contract, 50% of $20/hr x 1,000 hrs |
| Books and lab fees | $900 | $900 | Training cost, not covered by RSI |
| Supportive services | $600 | $600 | Within board limits |

**Step 5. Net cost to Coastline**

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Total year-one costs | $44,500 |
| Less AIF | -$3,000 |
| Less WIOA | -$11,500 |
| **Coastline's net cost** | **$30,000** |

In plain terms for the employer: "Of $44,500 in first-year costs outside college instruction, about $14,500 comes from AIF and WIOA, and the college covers instruction. Your net is about $30,000, most of it wages for productive work."

## Checks before you share the numbers

- Every AIF figure traces to current DAS guidance and the sponsor's own confirmation.
- No cost appears in more than one column.
- The WIOA share matches your board's OJT and supportive services policies and the participant's eligibility.
- You labelled the scenario as an estimate. Final amounts depend on the AIF award, the OJT contract, and enrollment.

## Discussion prompt

Employers often hear "free money" when you say AIF. How would you explain to an employer that AIF goes to the sponsor, and what that means if the employer is not the sponsor?

## Practice

Redo Andre's scenario with these changes, using the same workshop figures:

1. Coastline registers Andre plus two other eligible apprentices, and applies $3,000 of AIF per apprentice to each apprentice's program costs.
2. Andre's wage rises to $21/hour.
3. Coastline decides to use $400 of Andre's AIF for his scrubs and transit instead of program costs.

Rebuild all five steps for Andre. Show which WIOA amount changes in item 3 and why, and write the one-sentence employer summary.
