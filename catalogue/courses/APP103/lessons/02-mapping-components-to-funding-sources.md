## Start with the components, not the money

When an employer asks "how much can you give me for an apprentice?", the honest answer is "it depends on which part you mean." Every Registered Apprenticeship has the same building blocks you met in APP 101. For funding purposes, sort them into four components:

1. **On-the-job training (OJT).** The paid, supervised work the apprentice does at the job site. The employer pays the wages.
2. **Related instruction.** The classroom or online learning that goes with the job. In California this is called **related and supplemental instruction (RSI)**, and it is usually delivered by a community college, adult school, or the program sponsor's own training center.
3. **Supportive services.** What the apprentice needs to show up and stay: tools, work boots, uniforms, transportation, child care, testing or licensing fees.
4. **Program development.** The work of building or growing the program itself: writing standards, designing curriculum, recruiting the first group, setting up the sponsor's administration.

Once you name the component, you can ask the right question: which streams are allowed to pay for this, and who does the money go to?

## The main streams in this course

| Stream | Who runs it | Who usually receives the money | What it is built to pay for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WIOA Title I (Adult, Dislocated Worker, Youth) | Your local workforce board / AJCC | Employer (OJT), training provider (ITA), or vendor for the participant (supportive services) | Training and support for an eligible, enrolled participant |
| RSI funding | California Community Colleges system, through the partnering college or adult school | The local education agency delivering the instruction | Hours of related instruction for registered apprentices |
| AIF (Apprenticeship Innovation Funding) | Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS) | The program sponsor | Supporting apprentices in eligible (often newer, non-traditional) programs |
| CAI (California Apprenticeship Initiative) grants | California Community Colleges Chancellor's Office | The grantee (often a college or partner) | Creating and expanding new and innovative apprenticeship and pre-apprenticeship programs |
| GI Bill | U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs | The veteran apprentice | A monthly benefit while the veteran trains in an approved program |
| Employer | The employer | n/a | Wages, always; often a share of instruction and tools |

Eligibility details, current rates, and allowable-cost lists for each stream change. Treat this table as the map, then check the current DAS guidance for AIF and RSI, the current CAI request for applications, and your board's WIOA policies before you promise anything.

## Matching components to streams

Here is the sorting most frontline staff need. "Can" means the stream is designed for it, not that every participant or program qualifies.

| Component | Streams that can pay | Notes for Monday |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OJT wages | Employer (always pays the wage); WIOA OJT contract reimburses the employer a share of wages for an enrolled participant | WIOA reimbursement rules are in 20 CFR 680.730; your board sets the local rate and contract terms. |
| Related instruction | RSI funding to the college or adult school; WIOA ITA if the program is on the ETPL; CAI grant if it covers instruction for a new program; employer | Ask the sponsor first how instruction is already funded. |
| Supportive services | WIOA supportive services for an enrolled participant; possibly sponsor funds (including AIF, if the sponsor's AIF terms allow); employer; partner agencies | WIOA supportive services follow your board's policy on types and limits. |
| Program development | CAI grants; employer or sponsor funds; WIOA business services staff time (not direct payments, unless your board has a specific mechanism) | Program development money goes to an organization, never to an individual apprentice. |
| Veteran's living costs during training | GI Bill housing allowance paid to the veteran | Does not replace wages or reimburse the employer. Covered in lesson 5. |

Two habits make this table work:

- **Follow the recipient.** WIOA money attaches to an enrolled *participant*. RSI attaches to *instruction hours* delivered by an education agency. AIF attaches to the *sponsor* for its apprentices. CAI attaches to a *grant project*. The GI Bill attaches to the *veteran*. Different recipients is how streams can sit side by side.
- **Ask what is already paid.** Before you offer WIOA dollars, find out what the sponsor, college, or grant already covers. You are filling gaps, not stacking on top.

## Worked example

Valley Regional Workforce Board's business services rep, Dana, meets with Sunrise Community Health, which sponsors a new medical assistant apprenticeship. Dana sorts the costs:

| Component | Cost item | Likely payer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OJT | Apprentice wages, first year | Sunrise pays; WIOA OJT can reimburse a share for enrolled participants |
| Related instruction | 200 hours at the partner community college | College claims RSI for those hours, unless a grant already pays for them |
| Supportive services | Scrubs, background check, bus pass | WIOA supportive services for enrolled participants, per board policy |
| Program development | Curriculum alignment and standards writing | Sunrise and its college partner, possibly a CAI grant |

Notice what Dana did not do: she did not offer an ITA for instruction the college is already funding through RSI. That question belongs to the next lesson.

## Discussion prompt

In the session chat: which component do employers in your area ask about most, and which one do you most often leave unfunded because nobody owns it?

## Practice

Pick an apprenticeship program you know (or use the Sunrise example). On a blank page:

1. List at least two cost items under each of the four components.
2. For each item, write every stream from the table above that *could* pay for it.
3. Circle the stream you would try first, and write one sentence on why (who receives the money, what is already funded).
4. Mark any item where the answer depends on your board's local policy, and note where you would look it up.
