## One cost, one payer

Braiding means several streams fund one apprentice. Double-dipping means two streams pay for the **same cost**. The first is good practice; the second is a disallowed cost that can be clawed back in a monitoring review, and it puts your board, the sponsor, and the college at risk.

The core rule is simple: **every dollar of cost is charged to exactly one source.** Federal funds, including WIOA, follow the federal cost principles in the Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200): a cost has to be allocable to the award that pays for it. State streams carry their own terms. You do not need to quote the regulations to apply the rule, but you do need to ask the question every time.

## The rules you will apply most

| Situation | Rule | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A CAI grant pays for related instruction hours | Those RSI hours are **not claimable** for RSI funding | Confirm with the college which hours the grant covers before anyone files for RSI |
| The college claims RSI for instruction hours | WIOA should not also pay tuition for those same hours | Use an ITA only for instruction costs RSI and other sources do not cover |
| Employer receives WIOA OJT reimbursement for wages | The same wage hours cannot be reimbursed again by another wage subsidy | Check whether the employer has any other wage-reimbursement agreement for this apprentice |
| Sponsor uses AIF for an apprentice's tools | WIOA supportive services should not also buy those tools | Ask the sponsor what AIF is covering for this apprentice |
| Veteran receives GI Bill housing allowance | This is a benefit to the veteran, not a payment for a cost | It does not create a double-dip with OJT wages, but confirm any WIOA supportive services are not paying for the same need under your board's policy |

The CAI-and-RSI rule is the one practitioners miss most often, because the two streams both come through the community college system and the people claiming them may sit in different offices.

## A three-question test

Before you approve or recommend any funding, ask:

1. **What exactly is the cost?** Name it at the item level: "Semester 1 instruction, 120 hours," not "training."
2. **Who else could be paying for this?** Ask the sponsor, the college, and the employer directly. Check grant budgets you know about.
3. **Is it documented?** Your case file should show which source paid for which item, and how you confirmed no other source did.

If you cannot answer question 2, you are not ready to commit funds.

## Worked example

Luis, a case manager at the Valley Regional AJCC, is enrolling Maria, a laid-off medical billing clerk, into an IT support apprenticeship sponsored by Delta Tech Services. The partner college received a CAI grant to launch this program.

- The college tells Luis the CAI grant pays for the first 80 instruction hours of the program's opening group. Those 80 hours cannot also be claimed as RSI.
- The remaining 64 hours of instruction are claimed as RSI by the college.
- Maria needs a certification exam voucher not covered by either. Luis approves it as a WIOA training cost under board policy.
- Delta Tech pays Maria's wages, and Valley Regional writes a WIOA OJT contract reimbursing a share of those wages.

Luis's case note:

```text
Funding confirmation, M. [last initial], Delta Tech IT Support Apprenticeship.
Instruction hrs 1-80: CAI grant (per college apprenticeship coordinator, email on file).
Instruction hrs 81-144: RSI claimed by college. No WIOA tuition for these hours.
Exam voucher: WIOA training funds; not covered by CAI, RSI, or employer (confirmed with sponsor).
OJT wages: employer pays; WIOA OJT contract reimbursement per board policy. Employer reports no other wage subsidy for this apprentice.
```

The note names each item, one payer per item, and how Luis confirmed it.

## Red flags

- A sponsor says "the college handles instruction" but nobody can tell you how it is funded.
- Two agencies both list the same apprentice's tools or fees in their budgets.
- A grant budget line covers "apprentice instruction" without specifying which hours or which cohort.
- An employer is in more than one wage-subsidy program at once.

## Practice

Review this scenario and mark each item **OK** or **Double-dip**, with one sentence of reasoning:

1. A CAI grant funds 100 instruction hours; the college also files RSI for those 100 hours.
2. The college claims RSI for semester 2; WIOA pays for the apprentice's textbooks for semester 2, which RSI does not cover.
3. The sponsor buys the apprentice's tool kit with AIF; the AJCC also issues a supportive services payment for the same tool kit.
4. The employer receives WIOA OJT reimbursement; the veteran apprentice also receives a GI Bill housing allowance.

Then rewrite the case note for any item you marked "Double-dip" so the cost has one payer.
