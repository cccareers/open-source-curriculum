## Why this matters to you

Many veterans do not know their GI Bill can be used for an apprenticeship or on-the-job training, not just college. For a veteran apprentice, the benefit can make a starting apprentice wage workable. For you, it is a stream that sits beside WIOA, RSI, and AIF without replacing any of them, because it pays the **veteran**, not the employer or the school.

## When the benefit applies

Check three conditions. All three must be true.

1. **The veteran has remaining GI Bill entitlement.** The veteran confirms this with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA), for example through their Certificate of Eligibility. You do not determine VA eligibility; you prompt the veteran to check it.
2. **The program is approved for VA benefits.** A Registered Apprenticeship is not automatically approved for GI Bill use. The program or employer must be approved by the State Approving Agency; in California that is CSAAVE (the California State Approving Agency for Veterans Education). Ask the sponsor whether the program is approved. If it is not, the sponsor can seek approval, which takes time, so raise it early.
3. **The training is full-time, paid, and leads to the job.** GI Bill OJT and apprenticeship benefits are for veterans employed in the training position. Confirm current requirements on the VA's OJT and apprenticeship page.

If any condition is not met, the GI Bill is not part of this braid, and you plan with the other streams only.

## OJT versus apprenticeship under the GI Bill

The VA uses both terms. An **apprenticeship** follows registered standards and combines work with related instruction. An **OJT program** is employer-based training for a specific job without the full apprenticeship structure. Both can be approved, and the housing allowance works the same way for both under the Post-9/11 GI Bill. Use whichever term matches how the program was approved.

## How the housing allowance steps down

Under the Post-9/11 GI Bill, a veteran in an approved OJT or apprenticeship program receives a monthly housing allowance that **decreases every six months** as their wages are expected to rise:

| Months in training | Share of the full housing allowance |
| --- | --- |
| 1-6 | 100% |
| 7-12 | 80% |
| 13-18 | 60% |
| 19-24 | 40% |
| 25 and after | 20% |

The full housing allowance amount is based on a VA-set rate for the employer's location, and the allowance is reduced if the veteran works fewer hours than required in a month. Other GI Bill chapters (for example, the Montgomery GI Bill) pay a different benefit amount that also steps down over time. Always confirm the current schedule, rates, and rules with the VA before you put numbers in front of a veteran.

## Worked example

James, an Army veteran, is starting a 24-month electrical apprenticeship with Bright Line Electric. The program is CSAAVE-approved, and James's Certificate of Eligibility shows remaining Post-9/11 entitlement.

Assume, for the exercise only, that the full housing allowance for Bright Line's location is [current VA rate], shown here as **$2,500** per month.

| Months | Share | Monthly allowance (workshop figure) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-6 | 100% | $2,500 |
| 7-12 | 80% | $2,000 |
| 13-18 | 60% | $1,500 |
| 19-24 | 40% | $1,000 |

Meanwhile, Bright Line raises James's wage on its apprenticeship wage schedule. Talking through the two together helps James see that his total income does not fall off a cliff: the allowance steps down as the wage steps up.

## How it fits with the rest of the braid

- **GI Bill and WIOA OJT can coexist.** The housing allowance goes to James; a WIOA OJT contract would reimburse Bright Line for a share of wages. They pay for different things. Confirm your board's policy on co-enrolling veterans in WIOA and on supportive services for someone receiving the allowance.
- **Use your veterans partners.** Refer James to your AJCC's veterans staff (such as Jobs for Veterans State Grant staff) and keep the referral in the case file.
- **Plan for the step-down.** If James needs supportive services, time them for when the allowance drops, within board policy.
