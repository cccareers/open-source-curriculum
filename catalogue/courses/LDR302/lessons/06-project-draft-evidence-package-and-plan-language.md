## Goal

Produce a connected set of three working drafts for one AJCC: an indicator #6 evidence package outline, the frontline staff training section of a local plan modification, and a Continuous Improvement Plan with training actions. The three pieces should tell one consistent story about how the center trains its staff.

You may use your own center. If you prefer, use the case below.

## The case: Foothill Junction AJCC

Foothill Junction is the comprehensive AJCC for the fictional Sierra Gateway Workforce Board. The center has eight frontline staff, including two career coaches who joined in the last six months, plus co-located Adult Education and Vocational Rehabilitation partner staff who are on site part of the week.

What the operator manager, Marcus, has on hand:

- A training calendar for the past year listing webinar titles, with no attendance records attached.
- A new-hire checklist, completed for one of the two new coaches.
- One partner-led session from Adult Education, with a dated sign-in sheet showing six of eight frontline staff.
- Staff meeting notes that mention "policy update" twice, with no detail.
- A referral log showing very few completed referrals to Vocational Rehabilitation.

The board's recent certification review produced these findings:

1. Evidence that staff are cross-trained on partner programs was limited.
2. One new career coach had no record of completing onboarding.
3. Staff could not consistently describe how they learn about new state guidance.

The board's local plan modification is due April 30, 2027, and the board wants language it can back up at the next certification.

## Requirements

1. **Evidence package outline** for indicator #6, "high-quality, well-informed, and cross-trained staff," with a one-page cover summary, the section structure from this course, at least one labeled evidence item per section using what Marcus has, and a gaps section.
2. **Local plan staff training section** of 200 to 300 words using the four-part structure: who is trained, on what, how and how often, and how you will know it works.
3. **Continuous Improvement Plan** with one row per finding. Each row includes the training action, trained roles, owner, target date placeholder, success measure, and the record that will prove it.
4. **A short crosswalk** (a table or bullet list) showing how each CIP action connects to a plan commitment and to a future evidence item.

## Constraints

- Quote indicator #6 exactly as given. For any other indicator or directive detail, write "check WSD25-05" rather than inventing language.
- Do not put specific hours, percentages, or frequencies in the plan language unless you mark them "[confirm with board policy]."
- Use target date placeholders such as "[date set with board]."
- No customer personal information in any draft; refer to staff by role.
- Every plan commitment must be something the CIP or the evidence package can show.

## Definition of Done

- All three drafts and the crosswalk exist and use the case facts consistently.
- Every evidence item is labeled, dated or marked "[date needed]," and tied to a role.
- Every CIP row answers all six questions from the CIP lesson.
- A colleague reading only your plan language could find the matching CIP action and evidence item for each commitment.
- Every policy-specific detail is either quoted from the course materials or flagged for checking against WSD25-05, the state's local plan guidance, or board policy.

## Hints

- Start with the CIP. Once you know what you will fix, the plan language and the evidence gaps follow.
- Finding 3 is partly a process problem. Build the update routine first, then train staff on it.
- The Vocational Rehabilitation referral log is a natural proof-of-effect measure for cross-training.
- Your gaps section can honestly name the missing attendance records. Pair each gap with a CIP action.
