## What indicator #6 asks you to show

Indicator #6 under WSD25-05 is "high-quality, well-informed, and cross-trained staff." Read it as three linked claims:

- **High-quality:** staff have the skills their role requires and you check that they do.
- **Well-informed:** staff know current policy, programs, and local resources, and stay current when guidance changes.
- **Cross-trained:** staff understand partner programs well enough to serve a customer at first contact and make a correct, warm referral.

Check WSD25-05 for the exact language reviewers score against and any required format or tool for the evidence. Your board may also set local expectations, such as a minimum annual training plan. Confirm those with your board before you build.

## The package structure

An evidence package is not a pile of files. It is an organized argument with proof behind each point. Use a simple structure:

1. **Cover summary (one page).** The claim, the period covered, who prepared it, and a short index.
2. **Training design.** Your staff training plan, new-hire onboarding checklist, and role-based competency expectations.
3. **Training delivery records.** Dated attendance or LMS completion records, by role, for the review period.
4. **Cross-training records.** Evidence that staff learned partner programs: sessions led by partners, job shadowing logs, partner program quick-reference guides staff actually use.
5. **Staying current.** How policy updates reach frontline staff: dated staff meeting notes, update briefs, read-and-acknowledge logs.
6. **Proof of effect.** Something showing training changed practice: referral data, case file review results, customer feedback, supervisor observation notes.
7. **Gaps and next steps.** An honest note on what is not yet in place, linked to your improvement plan.

Section 7 often surprises people. A reviewer who sees you know your gaps trusts the rest of the package more.

## Turning everyday records into evidence

Most centers already have the raw material. The work is selecting, labeling, and connecting it.

| Raw record | Make it evidence by | Label example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Zoom attendance export | Add session title, date, presenter, and staff roles | "6-3a Partner session: Adult Ed services, March, 14 of 16 frontline staff" |
| New-hire checklist | Show completed copies for recent hires, with names redacted to role | "6-2b Onboarding completion, 3 recent hires" |
| Partner overview sheet | Show date issued and where staff keep it | "6-4a Quick reference: Vocational Rehabilitation referral path" |
| Referral tracking log | Summarize referrals by partner before and after cross-training | "6-6a Referral volume to Adult Ed, two quarters" |

Number every item to its section. A reviewer should be able to go from your cover summary to any piece of proof in seconds.

Protect privacy. Evidence about staff training rarely needs customer personal information. Where you use case file reviews or referral data, summarize or redact before it goes in the package, following your board's confidentiality policy.

## Worked example: cross-training at a comprehensive center

Priya manages frontline staff at the Riverbend AJCC, operated for the fictional Valley Regional Workforce Board. Her first draft for indicator #6 is a training calendar and a list of webinar titles. It shows intent but not that staff are cross-trained.

She upgrades it in three moves:

- **Adds partner-led sessions.** Over the review period, the Adult Education, Vocational Rehabilitation, and Employment Development Department partners each ran a short session for her team. She collects the dated sign-in sheets and the slides.
- **Adds a skills check.** After each session, staff completed a five-question scenario quiz, for example "Maria, a laid-off medical billing clerk, mentions she has a learning disability. What do you offer and who do you refer to?" Priya includes the quiz and the summarized results, not individual scores.
- **Adds proof of effect.** She pulls referral counts to each partner for the quarter before and after the sessions and shows the change, noting the data source and date range.

The final package tells a story: we planned cross-training, we delivered it, staff learned it, and referrals reflect it.

## Quality check before you submit

- Every item is dated and tied to a role, not just a name.
- Every section has at least one record, not only a document.
- Partner programs are covered, not just WIOA Title I.
- The "staying current" section shows how a recent guidance change reached frontline staff.
- Numbers have a source and a date range.
- Nothing contains customer personal information that is not needed.
- Someone other than the author can find any item from the index.

## Activity

Pair up. Swap your current indicator #6 evidence lists. Each partner plays the reviewer and asks, for every item, "How do I know this actually happened?" Note every item where the answer is "trust me."

## Practice

1. Draft the one-page cover summary for your center's indicator #6 package, including the review period and an index of sections 2 through 7.
2. Identify at least two raw records you already have for the cross-training section and write an evidence label for each in the format shown above.
3. Choose one proof-of-effect measure you could produce within two weeks and write down its data source and date range.
4. Write three to five bullets for section 7 naming your honest gaps.
