## What this section is for

Local plan modifications in the current cycle are due April 30, 2027, and they include a narrative on how your local area trains its frontline staff. This is where the board tells the state, in writing, how it keeps the people serving customers skilled, informed, and cross-trained over the life of the plan.

Before you write a word, get the state's current local plan modification guidance and your board's internal timeline. Copy the exact prompt or question for the staff training section into the top of your draft. The guidance sets what you must address; this lesson shows you how to answer it well. If the guidance lists required elements, make each one a subheading so a reviewer can check them off.

## Plan language versus certification evidence

The evidence package you built for indicator #6 and the plan narrative are related but different.

| | Indicator #6 evidence package | Local plan staff training section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience | Certification reviewers | State plan reviewers and the public |
| Time frame | What has already happened | What the local area will do over the plan period |
| Form | Organized records and proof | Narrative commitments |
| Tone | "Here is what we did" | "Here is what we will do, and how we will know" |

Use the evidence package as your foundation. Strong plan language commits to things you can already partly show, so the next certification cycle has evidence ready.

## A four-part structure that works

Most strong staff training narratives answer four questions, in this order:

1. **Who is trained?** Name the roles: frontline career services staff, partner staff co-located in the center, operator staff, front desk and resource room staff. Avoid "all staff" without saying who that includes.
2. **On what?** The content areas: WIOA program rules and eligibility, partner programs and referral paths, customer service and accessibility, and how staff get updates when state or federal guidance changes.
3. **How and how often?** Delivery methods (onboarding, partner-led cross-training, periodic refreshers, policy update briefs) and a rhythm. Say "your board's local policy sets the minimum" if that is how your area works, and state it.
4. **How will you know it works?** How the board or operator checks quality: completion tracking, skills checks, case file reviews, customer feedback, and how results feed the improvement plan.

Keep each part short. Reviewers read many plans; specific and brief beats long and general.

## Words to avoid and words to use

| Weak | Stronger |
| --- | --- |
| "Staff receive ongoing training." | "Frontline staff complete onboarding within their first weeks and attend partner-led cross-training sessions on a schedule set in the operator agreement." |
| "We ensure staff are well-informed." | "The operator issues a short policy update brief to frontline staff after each new state directive, with a read-and-acknowledge record." |
| "Training is high quality." | "The board reviews training completion and a sample of case files each year and uses the results to update the training plan." |

Notice that the stronger versions name a role, an action, and a record. Do not put specific numbers, such as hours per year or a percentage completion target, into the plan unless your board has actually adopted them. Every commitment in the plan is something you may be monitored against.

## Worked example: Valley Regional Workforce Board draft

Dana, the board's one-stop coordinator, drafts the first paragraph:

> Valley Regional Workforce Board requires all frontline AJCC staff, including co-located partner staff, to complete a role-based onboarding program and participate in partner-led cross-training covering each required partner program in the center. The AJCC operator maintains a training calendar and dated completion records for each staff member by role. When the state issues new or revised guidance affecting service delivery, the operator distributes a plain-language update brief to frontline staff and records acknowledgement. The board reviews these records, together with a sample of case files and customer feedback, as part of AJCC certification and its annual operator review, and incorporates training actions into the center's Continuous Improvement Plan.

Before sending it for review, Dana checks each sentence against two questions: "Does the guidance prompt ask for this?" and "Could we show evidence of this next year?" She removes a sentence promising quarterly staff surveys because the operator agreement does not fund them.

## Review and approval

Plan language goes through your board's process, which may include partner review, a public comment period, and board approval before submission. Check your board's timeline and work backward from April 30, 2027. Give operator and partner leads time to confirm that they can deliver what the language commits them to.

## Discussion prompt

Read Dana's paragraph aloud in your group. Which single sentence would be hardest for your local area to back up with evidence at the next certification review? Rewrite it so you could.

## Practice

1. Paste the current staff training prompt from the state's local plan modification guidance at the top of a blank document. If you do not have it yet, write down who you will ask for it.
2. Draft a staff training section of 200 to 300 words for your local area using the four-part structure: who, on what, how and how often, how you will know.
3. Underline every commitment in your draft and, beside each, name the record that would prove it next year.
4. Mark any number or frequency you used and confirm it against your board's adopted policy or operator agreement, or remove it.
