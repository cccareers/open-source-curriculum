## Description

Everything you have learned so far in this pathway has been about making a system safe. You can segment a network, provision an account with the right entitlements, harden a laptop, and work an incident from alert to closure. This course is about a different question, and it is the one that keeps organizations in business: can you prove any of it, to someone who does not trust you, using records instead of assurances?

That proof has a shape. A regulator or a customer states an obligation in their own language. Somebody translates it into a control your organization will actually operate. Somebody decides how much risk is left over and whether the organization will live with it. Somebody writes the rule down so the next person does the same thing. Somebody collects the evidence that the rule was followed for the whole year and not just the week before the audit. And somebody explains the result to an executive who has ten minutes and no security background. You will practise all six of those moves, in that order, on one running scenario, and you will finish with three linked artifacts of your own: a risk register, a policy set, and a compliance gap report.

Be clear about your seat in the room. You support compliance; you do not own it. You will draft the policy, but a named accountable owner approves it. You will rate the risk, but an executive accepts it. You will assemble the evidence, but the auditor forms the opinion. This course gives you no legal training and no legal authority, and a real part of the skill is recognising the moment when a question stops being technical and starts depending on jurisdiction, contract, or law — at which point the correct professional move is to escalate it, in writing, to the person who can answer it. The technical controls themselves are assumed from your earlier courses and are not retaught here. This course is about proving they exist and work.

## Objectives

- Map a stated security requirement to the right control in NIST, ISO 27001, HIPAA, or SOC 2
- Rate a risk by likelihood and impact and defend its priority against the rest of the register
- Draft a security policy that is specific, enforceable, and traceable to a control requirement
- Collect the evidence an auditor needs to confirm that a control operates as written
- Write a security posture report a non-technical stakeholder can act on
