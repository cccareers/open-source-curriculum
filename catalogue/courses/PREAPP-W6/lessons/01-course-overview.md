## Description

For five weeks you have been marketing yourself — connection requests, follow-ups, posts about what you were building, a portfolio you kept updating. Most of that was instinct. This week you turn it into strategy. Digital marketing is not a different skill from what you have already been doing; it is the same activity done deliberately, with a stated audience, a stated message, and a number you check afterward to find out whether it worked.

You will start by writing a positioning statement — one plain sentence that says who you serve, what you offer, and what makes you different — and then take apart posts that performed well to see how a hook, a structure, and a call to action do their separate jobs. From there you will open your own LinkedIn analytics and read the real numbers your weeks 1 through 5 posts produced. They will be small numbers, and that is fine: the skill is telling reach apart from engagement apart from the posts that actually put a conversation in your inbox.

The week ends where every marketing week should end — with a plan. You will design a simple campaign aimed at getting people to show up for Demo Day and lay it out as a two-week content calendar you will actually execute. By Friday you owe two things: that calendar, and a written analysis of your best-performing post that explains why it performed, not just that it did.

## Objectives

- Craft a positioning statement and deconstruct high-performing content into hook, structure, and CTA
- Interpret impressions and engagement on real posts and identify what generated conversations
- Design a simple campaign with audience, message, channel, cadence, and metric
