## The requirement in one sentence

Under WIOA, local areas must spend at least **20 percent** of their Youth formula funds on paid and unpaid work experience. Many boards find that target hard to hit with summer jobs and internships alone. Pre-apprenticeship is one of the recognized types of youth work experience, so a well-documented youth pre-apprenticeship can help your board meet the requirement while moving a young person toward a career.

The key words are **well-documented**. Spending only counts if your fiscal and program records show it was youth work experience.

## Where pre-apprenticeship fits

WIOA youth work experience is a planned, structured learning experience in a workplace for a limited time. The recognized types include:

- summer and year-round employment opportunities,
- **pre-apprenticeship programs**,
- internships and job shadowing, and
- on-the-job training.

Because pre-apprenticeship is on that list, you do not have to argue that it "looks like" work experience. You do have to show that the youth was actually enrolled in it as a work experience activity and that the costs you count are allowable work experience costs.

## Who decides what counts

Three layers are involved, and you need to know all three:

| Layer | What it controls | Where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Federal rule | That the 20 percent requirement exists, and which types of activity count | WIOA youth regulations and federal guidance |
| State guidance | How California boards calculate, track, and report the percentage | Current EDD Workforce Services directives on youth work experience |
| Local policy | Which costs your board tracks as work experience, how providers code them, and who reconciles | Your board's youth policy and provider contract |

Your job as a youth provider or case manager is usually the bottom of that chain: enter the activity correctly and keep records that let fiscal staff count it.

## Which costs can count

Work experience spending is not just wages. Depending on federal and state guidance and your board's policy, countable costs can include things like participant wages or stipends tied to the work experience, and staff time spent developing and managing the work experience. The exact list of allowable cost categories, and how staff time is allocated, should be confirmed in the current state youth work experience directive and with your fiscal team before you report.

**What usually does not count** as work experience spending: costs for a different program element that happens to run at the same time (for example, a separate tutoring service), and anything the provider cannot tie to a specific youth enrolled in the activity.

## Worked example: Jordan

Jordan (19, out-of-school youth) enters Valley Build Pathways, paid by youth funds through the youth provider's agreement with the program. Here is how the case manager makes it count:

1. **Enrollment and plan.** Jordan's Individual Service Strategy (ISS) lists pre-apprenticeship as a work experience activity, with start date, expected end date, and the goal: entry into the electrical apprenticeship.
2. **Activity coding.** The case manager records the activity in CalJOBS using the work experience/pre-apprenticeship activity code your board requires. Confirm the exact code in the current CalJOBS activity code guidance.
3. **Cost tracking.** The provider's invoice separates Jordan's pre-apprenticeship costs from other services (supportive services such as bus passes are tracked under supportive services, not work experience).
4. **Attendance.** Weekly attendance sheets from the program go into the file. These support both the stipend (if one is paid under WSD23-08 and local policy) and the work experience record.
5. **Close out.** On completion, the case manager records the end date, outcome, and any credential earned.

Sample case note:

```text
04/15 - Jordan enrolled in Valley Build Pathways pre-apprenticeship as a youth
work experience activity per ISS dated 04/10. Activity entered in CalJOBS with
pre-apprenticeship WEX code per board guidance. Provider invoice will itemize
WEX costs separately from supportive services (bus pass). Weekly attendance
to be uploaded each Monday. Fiscal notified for WEX expenditure tracking.
```

## Common mistakes

- Coding the pre-apprenticeship as occupational skills training in one place and work experience in another. Pick one per your board's guidance and be consistent.
- Mixing supportive services costs into the work experience line.
- Counting a pre-apprenticeship that was funded entirely through an Adult ITA as youth work experience spending. Only youth funds spent on work experience count toward the youth requirement; confirm co-enrollment cases with fiscal staff.
- Missing attendance records, which leaves fiscal staff unable to defend the expenditure.
