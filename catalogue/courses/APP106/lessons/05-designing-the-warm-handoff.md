## Where on-ramps fail

Most pre-apprenticeship graduates who never reach an apprenticeship do not fail the training. They fall into the gap after it. The sponsor's next application window is months away. The interview is across town and the bus does not run that early. The participant's phone number changed. Nobody called.

A **warm handoff** is your plan to close that gap: a deliberate, person-to-person transfer from the pre-apprenticeship to the registered program, with you still in the picture until the participant is registered and working.

## Cold versus warm

| Cold referral | Warm handoff |
| --- | --- |
| "Here's the JATC website; apply when they open." | You know the next intake date and have the application steps on paper |
| Participant contacts the sponsor alone | You, the program, or both introduce the participant to a named sponsor contact |
| Case closes at pre-apprenticeship completion | You stay in contact through application, selection, and the first weeks of apprenticeship |
| Barriers discovered after they cause a no-show | Barriers to the first day (tools, transportation, schedule) are solved before it |

## The five parts of a warm handoff

**1. Know the target program's entry requirements early.** Before the pre-apprenticeship ends, and ideally before it starts, write down the sponsor's minimum qualifications, application window, testing, interview process, and required documents. Ask the sponsor or the pre-apprenticeship coordinator; requirements change, so confirm them each cycle.

**2. Name the people.** Identify three contacts: the pre-apprenticeship coordinator, the apprenticeship sponsor contact (training director or coordinator), and you. Get the participant's consent to share information among them, using your organization's release form.

**3. Line up timing.** Map pre-apprenticeship completion against the sponsor's application and selection dates. If there is a gap, plan what the participant does in it: a bridge job, additional test prep, a short credential, or continued youth services.

**4. Remove the first-day barriers.** Apprentices often need tools, work clothes, reliable transportation to job sites that change, and early start times. Plan supportive services under your board's policy before the start date, not after the first missed shift. If your board co-enrolls apprentices and funds related instruction or supportive services, coordinate that through your board's co-enrollment process.

**5. Follow through after entry.** Check in at defined points: application submitted, interview, selection, registration with the apprenticeship program, first week, first month. For youth, this follow-up can be part of the youth program's follow-up services.

## Worked example: Jordan's handoff plan

```text
Participant: Jordan (youth, 19)   Target: local electrical apprenticeship
Pre-apprenticeship: Valley Build Pathways, ends 06/20

Entry requirements (confirmed with sponsor coordinator 05/01):
  - application packet, proof of HS diploma, aptitude test, interview
  - VBP agreement: completers meeting attendance + math criteria
    get guaranteed interview + ranking points
Contacts (release signed 05/02):
  - VBP coordinator: Ms. Alvarez
  - Sponsor training coordinator: Mr. Chen
  - Case manager: [your name]
Timing: application window opens 07/01; test 07/22; interviews August
  Gap plan (06/20-08/31): part-time job via business services team;
  weekly math practice with youth tutor
Barriers: no car -> bus pass continues; tool list from sponsor;
  supportive services request for tools/boots submitted 06/10
Follow-up checkpoints: application 07/05, test 07/22, interview,
  selection, registration, week 1, day 30
```

Every name and date here is fictional. The structure is what you copy.

## When the participant is not selected

Plan for this from the start, because it happens. A quality pre-apprenticeship (Lesson 2) has a strategy for graduates who are not accepted immediately. Your plan should say what you will do: re-apply next cycle with targeted prep, apply to a second registered program in a related trade, or enter related employment that builds hours and experience. Record the decision in the case file so the next staff member knows the plan.
