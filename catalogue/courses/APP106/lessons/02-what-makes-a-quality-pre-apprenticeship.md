## Why "quality" matters

Plenty of programs call themselves pre-apprenticeship. Some are excellent. Others are a job-readiness workshop with a hard hat on the flyer. The difference matters to you because you are about to spend WIOA dollars and, more importantly, a participant's time. A program that does not lead anywhere costs a young person months they could have spent earning.

The test is simple to say: **a quality pre-apprenticeship prepares people for, and connects them to, at least one specific Registered Apprenticeship program.** Everything else on this page is a way of checking that claim.

## The running case

Throughout this course you will follow one participant.

> **Jordan**, 19, is an out-of-school youth enrolled with Valley Regional Workforce Board's youth provider. Jordan finished high school, has worked short stints in fast food, and wants to become an electrician. Jordan applied to the local electrical apprenticeship last year and did not pass the aptitude test. Jordan has no car and relies on the bus.

Jordan's case manager has found the "Valley Build Pathways" pre-apprenticeship, a ten-week program run by a community college in partnership with local building trades. Is it a quality program?

## The elements to look for

Federal and state apprenticeship agencies describe quality pre-apprenticeship in similar terms. Use the elements below as your screening checklist. Your state apprenticeship agency, your board, or a funder may use slightly different wording, so confirm the current framework before you formally certify a program as "quality" in a case file.

| Element | What it looks like in practice | Question to ask the provider |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Curriculum aligned to industry and apprenticeship standards | Training is built from what the target apprenticeship actually tests and requires; sponsors helped design or reviewed it | "Which apprenticeship sponsors reviewed your curriculum?" |
| Direct link to Registered Apprenticeship | A written agreement with at least one registered program: direct entry, interview guarantee, or credit toward the program | "Can I see your agreement with the sponsor?" |
| Hands-on, meaningful training | Shop or lab time, real tools, simulated or real job tasks, not only classroom work; trainees do not replace paid workers | "How many hours are hands-on?" |
| Access to supportive services | Help with transportation, tools, work gear, childcare, and barriers that commonly stop completion | "What do you provide, and what do you expect us to provide?" |
| Strategies for long-term success | Outreach to people underrepresented in the trade, mentoring, test prep, and follow-up after placement | "What happens to graduates who are not accepted right away?" |
| Industry-recognized credentials | Credentials the target sponsor values (for example, safety or first aid certifications common in construction) | "Which credentials do graduates leave with, and does the sponsor care about them?" |

Notice which row carries the most weight. A program can have great classes and strong support, but **without a direct, documented link to a registered program it is a job-readiness program, not an on-ramp.**

## What a "direct link" really means

Ask for paper. Links vary in strength:

1. **Direct entry or articulation agreement.** The sponsor agrees that graduates who meet stated criteria enter the apprenticeship (or skip a step, such as the waiting list or part of the application). Strongest.
2. **Guaranteed interview or ranking credit.** Graduates are interviewed or receive points in the selection process. Solid.
3. **Informal relationship.** "The JATC knows us and likes our grads." Weakest; treat as a warning sign until you see something written.

For Jordan, the case manager calls Valley Build Pathways and learns that the program has a signed agreement with the local electrical apprenticeship committee: graduates who complete with 90 percent attendance and pass the program's math module are guaranteed an interview and receive extra ranking points. (These criteria are fictional; real agreements set their own terms.) That is a real link.

## Red flags

Use this quick list when a provider pitches you:

- The provider cannot name the registered program(s) graduates enter.
- Placement numbers are about "employment" generally, not apprenticeship entry.
- The curriculum was not reviewed by any apprenticeship sponsor or employer.
- Trainees do unpaid production work that a paid employee would otherwise do.
- No plan for graduates who are not accepted in the next intake cycle.

## Sample case note

Good documentation shows your reasoning, not just the program name.

```text
03/04 - Reviewed Valley Build Pathways (VBP) with youth participant Jordan.
Quality screen: curriculum co-developed with local electrical and carpentry
sponsors; ~60% hands-on shop hours; supportive services include bus passes
and work boots; graduates earn construction safety and first aid credentials.
Direct link: signed agreement with electrical apprenticeship committee
(guaranteed interview + ranking points for completers meeting attendance
and math criteria). Copy of agreement requested for file.
Conclusion: meets quality screen. Next: confirm funding path.
```

## Discussion prompt

In the live session, take two minutes: think of a pre-apprenticeship or "trades readiness" program your office has referred to. Which elements in the table could you verify today, and which are you assuming?

## Practice

Use the quality checklist on a real or fictional program.

1. Pick one pre-apprenticeship your office refers to (or invent one modeled on what you see locally).
2. For each element in the table, write one sentence of evidence or mark it "unknown."
3. Rate the direct link to Registered Apprenticeship as direct entry, interview/ranking credit, informal, or none. Name the registered program.
4. List the two questions you would ask the provider to close your biggest gaps.
5. Write a three-to-five-line case note for Jordan's file, in the style of the sample above, recording whether the program meets your quality screen.

Keep your notes. In the next lessons you will add the funding source, the work experience counting, and the handoff plan to the same case.
