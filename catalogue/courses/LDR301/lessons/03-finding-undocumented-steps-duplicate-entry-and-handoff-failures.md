## From picture to diagnosis

Your first-draft map shows how work flows. Now you read it for trouble. This lesson teaches you to find three kinds of problems that cause most of the pain in an AJCC process:

1. **Undocumented steps** — work that happens only because someone remembers to do it.
2. **Duplicate data entry** — the same information typed into more than one place.
3. **Handoff failures** — points where work, a customer, or a file crosses lanes and something gets lost, delayed, or misunderstood.

The course catalog notes that no California board publishes a process map and that 20 to 30 percent of the process is tribal knowledge. Whatever the exact share at your center, the goal of this lesson is to make that hidden share visible, one mark at a time.

## Undocumented steps

A step is undocumented when there is no written procedure, form, checklist, or system prompt that tells a new person to do it. The work is real; the instructions live in someone's head.

**How to find them:**

- **Look for blank artifact or rule fields** from Lesson 2. A step with no artifact often has no procedure either.
- **Ask the "who would know" question** at each box: "If you were out for two weeks, how would your backup know to do this?" If the answer is "they'd ask me" or "they wouldn't," the step is undocumented.
- **Listen for workaround language:** "We always…," "Denise has a spreadsheet for that," "You just have to know to…"
- **Compare two people in the same lane.** If two career coaches describe a step differently, there is no shared procedure.
- **Check the "no" branches.** What happens when a customer is not eligible, misses an appointment, or drops out of training? These paths are rarely written.

**Mark it:** Put a red dot on the box and write "UNDOC" with a one-line note of what the hidden knowledge is.

| Clue you hear | Likely hidden knowledge |
| --- | --- |
| "I keep a list of who needs follow-up calls." | Follow-up tracking lives in a personal spreadsheet |
| "You have to call the school before you submit the ITA." | An unwritten pre-approval check with training providers |
| "Only Luis knows how to fix that in CalJOBS." | A system correction procedure held by one person |

## Duplicate data entry

Duplicate entry happens when staff key the same information into two or more places: CalJOBS, a local tracking spreadsheet, a paper intake form, a partner's system, a fiscal system. Each copy is a chance for the numbers to disagree, and when they disagree, someone has to decide which one is right.

**How to find them:**

1. For each artifact on the map, ask: "Where else does this same information get written down?"
2. Draw a small symbol (for example, a "D" in a circle) on every box where data is entered.
3. Trace one data element, such as the customer's training start date, across the map. Count how many times it is entered.

**Worked example.** At the fictional Valley Regional AJCC, the team traced Maria's training start date:

| Where it is entered | Who enters it | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ITA request form (paper) | Case manager | Supervisor approval |
| Local ITA tracking spreadsheet | Training services | Budget tracking |
| CalJOBS activity record | Case manager | State reporting |
| Fiscal system encumbrance | Fiscal staff | Payment |

Four entries, three people, one date. When the training provider moved the start date by a week, only CalJOBS was updated. The spreadsheet and the fiscal record were wrong for the rest of the term.

Not every duplicate can be removed. Some systems do not talk to each other, and some copies are required for fiscal controls. The point is to *know* where the copies are, so the team can decide which one is the system of record and who updates the others when it changes.

## Handoff failures

Every arrow that crosses a lane is a handoff. A handoff fails when the receiving person does not get what they need, when they need it, in a form they can use.

**Check every crossing arrow with four questions:**

| Question | Failure if the answer is no |
| --- | --- |
| Does the receiver know the handoff happened? | Customer waits; file sits in an inbox |
| Is there a clear trigger and timeline? | "Someone will call you" with no date |
| Does the receiver get everything they need? | Repeated document requests; customer retells their story |
| Does the sender learn whether it worked? | Referrals vanish; no one notices drop-off |

**Typical AJCC handoff failure points** to check first:

- Front desk to orientation, especially for walk-ins on busy days
- Eligibility to case management, when the file moves by email or shared folder
- Case manager to training services, for ITA or OJT requests
- WIOA staff to partner programs, and back again
- Case manager to follow-up staff, after exit
- Any staff to MIS, when data errors are found and need a correction

**Worked example.** In Lesson 2 the Valley Regional team marked step 7, "File handed to a case manager," with a question mark. Using the four questions, they found: the eligibility technician emails a supervisor, who assigns a case manager "when caseloads allow." There is no timeline, and the technician never learns whether the assignment happened. Two customers in the past quarter waited more than a month. That is a handoff failure, and it is also undocumented: the assignment rule exists only in the supervisor's head.

## Marking and logging what you find

Use one consistent set of marks on the map so anyone can read it later:

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Red dot + "UNDOC" | Undocumented step |
| "D" in a circle | Data entry point (two or more on one data element = duplicate) |
| Lightning bolt on an arrow | Handoff failure or risk |
| Question mark | Unknown rule, artifact, or owner |

Then copy every mark into a simple **gap log**. You will use it in Lesson 4 to set priorities.

```text
Gap ID: G-07
Map step: 7 - Eligibility to case manager
Type: Handoff failure; undocumented
What happens now: Technician emails supervisor; assignment "when caseloads allow"
Who holds the knowledge: Program supervisor
Effect seen: Two customers waited over 30 days in the last quarter
Governing rule: Unknown - check your board's service-flow policy
```

Keep the log factual. "Supervisor is slow" is a judgment; "no written assignment timeline" is a gap you can fix.

**Discussion prompt:** Which mark appeared most often on your map? Is it concentrated in one lane or spread across the process? Concentration in one lane usually points to one person carrying a lot of hidden knowledge.

## Practice

Using your draft map from Lesson 2:

1. Walk every box and mark any undocumented step. Write one line about what the hidden knowledge is.
2. Pick two data elements (for example, the customer's eligibility date and training start date) and trace every place each one is entered. Circle every duplicate.
3. Check every arrow that crosses a lane against the four handoff questions. Put a lightning bolt on each failure or risk.
4. Copy every mark into a gap log, one entry per gap, using the template above.
5. Count your gaps by type and by lane, and write two sentences about what the pattern tells you.
