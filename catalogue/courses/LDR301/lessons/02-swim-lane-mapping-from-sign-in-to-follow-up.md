## Why swim lanes

A list of steps tells you *what* happens. A swim-lane map also tells you *who* does it and *where the work changes hands*. That second part matters most in an AJCC, because most problems do not happen inside a step. They happen between steps, when a customer, a file, or a data entry moves from one person to another.

A swim-lane map is a grid. Each horizontal lane belongs to one role. Time runs left to right. Each step sits in the lane of the role that does it. When an arrow crosses from one lane into another, that is a handoff.

In this lesson you build the first version of your center's map. You will mark problems on it in the next lesson. For now, the goal is an honest picture of how the process runs today, not how the policy manual says it should run.

## What each step on the map carries

Every box on the map should answer four questions. Write them on the sticky note, card, or shape for that step.

| Field | Question it answers | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Step | What happens? | Collect eligibility documents |
| Role | Which lane, which job title? | Eligibility technician |
| Artifact | What record proves it happened? | Uploaded documents and eligibility determination in CalJOBS |
| Governing rule | What requires or shapes it? | State documentation guidance; your board's eligibility policy |

If your team cannot fill in the artifact or the rule for a step, do not guess. Leave the field blank and put a mark on the box. Those blanks are some of the most useful things you will produce today.

Use roles, not names. Write "Career coach," not "Denise." A map built on names breaks the day someone changes jobs.

## Choosing your lanes

Start with the roles that touch a typical WIOA Title I Adult or Dislocated Worker customer. A common starting set:

1. Customer
2. Front desk or greeter
3. Resource room or navigator
4. Eligibility technician
5. Case manager or career coach
6. Training and business services (ITA, OJT, ETPL provider contact)
7. Supervisor or program manager
8. MIS or data staff
9. Partner programs (for example, Adult Education, Vocational Rehabilitation)

Keep the customer lane at the top. It reminds the team that every arrow you draw is something the customer experiences as waiting, a phone call, or a trip back to the center.

Merge lanes if one person does several roles in your center. Split a lane if two different people do the "same" job in very different ways. Your lanes should match your center, not a textbook.

## Start from the base map, not a blank wall

Mapping from zero takes days. This course gives you CCC's statewide base map, which lays out the steps most California AJCCs share, from sign-in to follow-up. CCC estimates that it gets a team about 70 percent of the way to its own map. Your work today is the other 30 percent: the local order, the local roles, and the local workarounds.

The base map follows the same arc you learned in FND 101: sign-in, orientation and triage, registration, eligibility, participation, assessment, plan, services and training, progress, exit, and follow-up, with reporting running underneath. Check the version your facilitator hands out; if your board has added steps (for example, a local intake interview), add them.

How to use it:

1. **Lay out the base map** across the wall or virtual whiteboard.
2. **Move each step into your lanes.** Ask, "In our center, who actually does this?"
3. **Re-order** any steps that happen in a different order for you.
4. **Add** steps the base map does not show. These are often local: a waiting list, a supervisor sign-off, a second data system.
5. **Strike** steps you genuinely do not do, and write why.

## Walk one real customer

Abstract mapping produces a policy chart. To get the real process, walk one fictional but typical customer through the map, out loud, one step at a time.

**Worked example: Valley Regional AJCC**

The team at the fictional Valley Regional AJCC walks Maria, a laid-off medical billing clerk who wants to retrain as a medical coder.

| # | Step | Lane | Artifact | Governing rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Maria signs in at the kiosk | Customer / Front desk | Kiosk sign-in record | Local traffic-tracking practice |
| 2 | Greeter asks what she needs, points her to orientation | Front desk | None written | Local service-flow policy |
| 3 | Group orientation | Navigator | Attendance sheet | Local orientation design |
| 4 | Maria registers in CalJOBS with help | Customer / Navigator | CalJOBS registration | State CalJOBS guidance |
| 5 | Intake appointment booked | Front desk | Calendar entry | Local scheduling practice |
| 6 | Documents collected, DW eligibility determined | Eligibility technician | Eligibility determination, source documents | State documentation guidance; local eligibility policy |
| 7 | File handed to a case manager | Eligibility technician to Case manager | Email | ? |
| 8 | Comprehensive assessment | Case manager | Assessment record | WIOA career services; local tools |
| 9 | IEP written with Maria | Case manager / Customer | IEP | WIOA plan requirements; local plan format |
| 10 | ITA request for a coding program on the ETPL | Case manager to Training services | ITA request form | ETPL rules; local ITA policy |
| 11 | Supervisor approves the ITA | Supervisor | Signed approval | Local ITA policy |
| 12 | Monthly check-ins during training | Case manager | Case notes | Local case note standard |
| 13 | Credential and employment recorded | Case manager / MIS | CalJOBS activity and outcome entries | State performance guidance |
| 14 | Exit | System, confirmed by case manager | Exit date | Federal exit definition |
| 15 | Follow-up contacts | Follow-up specialist | Follow-up notes | Follow-up requirements; local schedule |

Notice step 7. The team knew the file moved by email, but nobody could name the rule or the expected timeline. That question mark is exactly what you want to surface. Mark it and move on; Lesson 3 is where you analyze it.

## Mapping conventions that keep the map readable

- **One box, one action.** "Collect documents and determine eligibility and book assessment" is three boxes.
- **Verbs first.** "Approve ITA," not "ITA approval stage."
- **Arrows show flow, not importance.** Every arrow should be a real handoff or the next step in the same lane.
- **Decisions get a diamond.** "Eligible for DW?" splits into yes and no paths. Draw the "no" path too: where does a customer go when they are not eligible?
- **Show the waits.** If customers wait two weeks between orientation and intake, write the wait on the arrow. Waits are where customers drop out.
- **Keep the artifacts visible.** Use a different color for the artifact field so the team can scan for blanks.
- **Date the map.** Write "As of [date], version [number]" in the corner. You will need this in Lesson 5.

## Running the session

A full-day mapping session works best with a small, mixed team: at least one person from each lane, a supervisor who can answer "is that allowed?", and someone who does data entry. Without the data person you will miss half the duplicate entry.

Suggested flow for this block:

1. **Set the rule (5 minutes):** "We map what we do, not what the manual says." Say it out loud. People will otherwise describe the ideal.
2. **Lay out lanes and base map (20 minutes).**
3. **Walk one customer (45 minutes):** One person narrates as the customer; the role holders say what they do at each step.
4. **Walk a second, different customer (20 minutes):** Pick a contrast, such as a Youth participant or a customer referred by a partner. New lanes and branches will appear.
5. **Quick check (10 minutes):** Every box has a role. Every box has an artifact or a mark. Every handoff has an arrow.

**Discussion prompt:** Where did two people in the room describe the same step differently? Do not settle it yet. Write both versions on the map and mark the box.

## Common mistakes

- **Mapping the policy instead of the practice.** If the manual says supervisors review every IEP but they actually review a sample, map the sample.
- **Stopping at exit.** The objective runs to follow-up. Follow-up is often the least documented part of the process, so do not skip it.
- **Too much detail too early.** A first map with 30 to 50 boxes is normal. A first map with 200 boxes will never be finished.
- **One person draws, everyone watches.** Hand out the markers. The person who does the step should place its box.

## Practice

Build a first-draft swim-lane map for one customer type at your center.

1. Pick one customer type (for example, a Dislocated Worker who wants training).
2. Draw lanes for every role that touches that customer, with the customer lane on top.
3. Using the base map as a starting point, place each step in the correct lane, from sign-in through follow-up.
4. For each step, fill in the four fields: step, role, artifact, governing rule. Mark any field you cannot fill.
5. Draw every handoff as an arrow that crosses lanes, and write any known wait time on the arrow.
6. Date the map and give it a version number.

Check yourself: Can someone who has never worked at your center follow the customer from the first box to the last box without asking you a question? Every place they would need to ask is a mark on your map.
