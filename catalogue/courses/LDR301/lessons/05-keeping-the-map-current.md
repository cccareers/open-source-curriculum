## A map ages the day you finish it

Directives are revised. Your board changes a local policy. A provider contract turns over. A case manager who held three undocumented steps retires. Each change makes part of your map wrong, and a wrong map is worse than no map, because people trust it. This lesson gives you a light routine to keep the map honest without turning it into a second job.

## Name an owner and a home

A map with no owner will not be updated. Assign:

- **A map owner** — usually a program manager or operations lead. They do not make every edit; they make sure edits happen.
- **Lane stewards** — one person per lane who tells the owner when their part of the process changes.
- **One home** — a single shared location for the current map, the gap log, and the procedures. Retire old copies so nobody works from last year's version.

Every version carries the date and version number you added in Lesson 2.

## Know what triggers an update

Some changes should prompt a map review right away. Build this list into the owner's routine:

| Trigger | What to check on the map |
| --- | --- |
| New or revised state directive | Every step whose governing rule cites that directive |
| Local policy change from your board | Steps that cite the local policy; any value in bracketed placeholders |
| System change (screens, fields, workflows) | Every data entry point and any procedure with screenshots |
| Staffing or contract change | Lanes, owners, backups, and handoffs |
| Monitoring or validation finding | The step where the finding originated and its procedure |

Policy changes arrive in batches. For example, CCC's course catalog describes its 2026 edition as covering several changes, including a new ETPL directive (WSD25-02) and AJCC certification under WSD25-05. When a change like that lands, search the map's governing-rule fields for the old reference and review every step that cites it.

## Planning for the CalJOBS modernization

The CalJOBS modernization is a system change that touches almost every data entry point on your map. This course does not describe what the modernized system will look like; screens, fields, and timelines will come from EDD's official announcements and training. Check EDD's current guidance and your board's communications for dates and details.

What you can do now, whatever the final design:

1. **Tag every CalJOBS touchpoint.** On your map, mark every box where staff enter or read CalJOBS data. This is your impact list.
2. **Separate the rule from the screen.** In each procedure, write the *requirement* (for example, "record the exit date") separately from the *keystrokes* (which menu, which field). When the system changes, you rewrite the keystrokes, not the whole procedure.
3. **Note your workarounds.** Duplicate entry and side spreadsheets often exist because the current system cannot do something. Flag each one so you can check whether the new system removes the need, or creates new ones.
4. **Plan a post-change walkthrough.** After the change goes live, walk one customer through the map again, as you did in Lesson 2, and update every box that no longer matches.

## A simple maintenance rhythm

| When | What | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| On any trigger | Review affected steps; update map and procedures | Lane steward, map owner |
| Quarterly | 30-minute review: one customer walk-through, check open gaps | Map owner with lane stewards |
| Yearly | Full re-walk of the map with a mixed team | Map owner, managers |

Log each update in a short change record:

```text
Version 1.3 - [date]
Trigger: Board revised local supportive services policy
Steps changed: 12 (artifact), 13 (approval role)
Procedures updated: SP-04
Reviewed by: [map owner]
```

**Discussion prompt:** What change in the last year would have made your map wrong, and how long would it have taken anyone to notice?

## Practice

1. Name the map owner, a steward for each lane, and the single shared home for your map.
2. Write your center's trigger list, adding any local triggers not in the table above.
3. Mark every CalJOBS touchpoint on your map and count them.
4. Pick one procedure and rewrite it so the requirement is separate from the system keystrokes.
5. Put the first quarterly review on the calendar and draft the change record for your current version.
