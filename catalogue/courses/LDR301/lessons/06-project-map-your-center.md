## Goal

Produce a working swim-lane map of your own center's process, from sign-in to follow-up, with every gap flagged and a prioritized list of the procedures your team will write first. This is the work product you take back to your center on Monday.

If you are not able to map your own center (for example, you work at a board office and do not run a center), use the practice case below.

**Practice case: Foothill Gateway AJCC.** Foothill Gateway is a fictional comprehensive AJCC run by a contracted operator for the fictional Sierra Foothills Workforce Board. It serves Adult, Dislocated Worker, and Youth customers, with Adult Education and Vocational Rehabilitation staff on site two days a week. Staff describe it this way:

- Walk-ins sign in on paper; the front desk later types names into a spreadsheet "for the monthly count."
- Eligibility is done by two technicians. One scans documents into CalJOBS; the other keeps paper files and scans "when there's time."
- New participants are assigned to case managers by the operations lead, who "just knows who has room."
- ITA requests go to a training coordinator by email, then to the operations lead for approval, then to fiscal. The coordinator keeps her own tracker.
- Follow-up after exit is done by whichever case manager remembers. One case manager, Tomas, keeps a calendar of follow-up dates; others do not.
- Partner referrals to Vocational Rehabilitation are made by phone, with no record of whether the customer was seen.

## Requirements

Your submission has three parts.

**1. Swim-lane map**

- Lanes for every role that touches the customer, with the customer lane on top.
- Steps from sign-in through follow-up, placed in the correct lanes, built from CCC's statewide base map and adjusted to your center.
- Each step shows the role, the artifact, and the governing rule, or a mark where any of these is unknown.
- Every handoff drawn as an arrow crossing lanes, with known wait times written on it.
- At least one decision point with both its "yes" and "no" paths.
- A date and version number.

**2. Gap log**

- Every undocumented step, duplicate data entry point, and handoff failure on the map, marked with the symbols from Lesson 3 and logged one entry per gap.
- At least one data element traced through every place it is entered.
- Each entry states what happens now, who holds the knowledge, and the effect seen, in factual language.

**3. Prioritized procedure list**

- Every gap scored on compliance risk, customer impact, knowledge concentration, and frequency, with an effort rating.
- Your top three procedures to write, each with a one-sentence rationale.
- Any gaps that are really policy questions, listed separately with the person or body who should decide.
- A first draft of your number one procedure, using the one-page template from Lesson 4.

## Constraints

- **Map the practice, not the policy.** Show how work actually happens today.
- **Roles, not names.** No staff names on the map or in the log. Use role titles.
- **No real customer data.** Walk a fictional customer. Do not copy any real participant's information into the map, log, or procedure.
- **Do not invent rules.** Where you do not know the governing rule, write "unknown" and the place you will check (for example, your board's local policy or the current state directive). Do not fill in a directive number, timeline, or threshold from memory.
- **Keep it workable.** Aim for a map your team can read on one wall or one screen. Detail belongs in procedures, not boxes.
- **Time box:** about 90 minutes in the workshop. Finish the procedure draft back at your center if needed.

## Definition of Done

- [ ] A colleague who does not work at your center can follow a customer from the first box to the last without asking you a question, or every place they would need to ask is marked.
- [ ] Every step has a role, and every step has an artifact and rule or a visible mark.
- [ ] Every lane-crossing arrow has been checked against the four handoff questions.
- [ ] The gap log has one entry for every mark on the map, and no entry without a mark.
- [ ] Every gap is scored, and the top three choices follow from the scores (or the reason for an exception is written down).
- [ ] Policy questions are separated from procedures to write.
- [ ] The number one procedure names its map step, owner, backup, trigger, and governing rule, with bracketed placeholders for any local value you have not confirmed.

## Hints

- **Start with the base map** and move boxes into lanes before you add anything new. Most of your time should go to the local 30 percent, not redrawing the shared 70 percent.
- **For the Foothill Gateway case,** look closely at the paper sign-in spreadsheet, the two technicians who work differently, the operations lead's case assignments, the training coordinator's private tracker, Tomas's follow-up calendar, and the Vocational Rehabilitation phone referral. Each is at least one gap. Several are more than one.
- **Use the single-person test.** Ask which steps stop if one role holder is gone tomorrow. At Foothill Gateway, that question alone points to at least three gaps.
- **Trace the ITA.** A training request that passes through three lanes and two trackers is a good place to find both duplicate entry and handoff failures.
- **Watch the "no" paths.** What happens to a customer who is found ineligible, or who misses the Vocational Rehabilitation appointment? If nobody knows, that is a gap.
- **Optional extension:** add a note on who will own the map and when it will next be reviewed, using what you learned in Lesson 5.
