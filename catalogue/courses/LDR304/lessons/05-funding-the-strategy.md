## Fund the strategy, not just the apprentice

APP 103 teaches frontline staff how to braid funds for one apprentice. Leadership has a different question: **what pays for the strategy itself**, the navigator time, the training, the partnership work, and the services your goal commits you to?

Split the costs into two buckets:

1. **Board operating costs.** Staff time for navigators or champions, staff training, data work, and business services time spent with sponsors.
2. **Participant and program costs.** OJT reimbursements, ITAs, supportive services, and, at intermediary level, program-development work.

Most of the first bucket comes from your own WIOA funds. Most of the second can draw on several streams, many of which your board does not control.

## The streams to know

| Stream | Source | Who controls it | What it can support in your strategy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| WIOA Title I (Adult, Dislocated Worker, Youth) | Federal | Your board | Navigator and staff time, OJT, ITAs, supportive services for enrolled participants |
| TEGL 05-25 waivers | Federal | Requested through the state, per the TEGL | Flexibility in how WIOA funds can be used; check what the TEGL makes available |
| 90 percent OJT waiver | Waiver (confirm current status and terms) | Applied through your board's OJT policy | Higher OJT reimbursement for eligible small employers, making apprenticeship OJT more attractive |
| AIF (Apprenticeship Innovation Funding) | State | DAS, paid to sponsors | Support for apprentices in eligible programs; reduces what your WIOA funds must cover |
| RSI funding | State | Community college system, through the partnering college or adult school | Related instruction hours; means you often do not need an ITA for instruction |
| CAI (California Apprenticeship Initiative) grants | State | Community Colleges Chancellor's Office, to grantees | New and expanded programs; a natural fit for an intermediary role, usually as a partner |
| GI Bill | Federal | U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, paid to the veteran | Living support for veteran apprentices in approved programs |
| Employer | Private | The employer | Wages always; often instruction and tools |

Rates, eligibility, and application cycles for every stream change. Treat the table as the map and check the current DAS guidance for AIF and RSI, the current CAI request for applications, TEGL 05-25 itself, and your board's OJT policy before you put a figure in your plan.

## Match funding to role

The role you chose in lesson 3 tells you which streams matter most.

| Role | Main streams |
| --- | --- |
| Referrer | WIOA staff time only; partners fund the apprentice |
| Navigator | WIOA staff time, OJT, ITAs, supportive services; leverage AIF, RSI, and GI Bill so WIOA fills gaps rather than paying for everything |
| Intermediary | All of the above, plus CAI or other program-development grants, usually with a college partner |
| Sponsor | All of the above, plus a stable long-term base for program administration; confirm sponsor eligibility for each stream |

The leadership habit is the same as the frontline one: **ask what is already paid before you spend WIOA dollars.** Every apprentice whose instruction is covered by RSI, and whose sponsor draws AIF, is an apprentice your formula funds can support at lower cost. That is how a fixed budget reaches a larger co-enrollment goal.

## Plan for reauthorization risk

WIOA reauthorization is pending. Build your plan so it does not collapse if federal rules or funding change:

- Fund the core of the strategy, such as navigator staffing, from sources you control now.
- Treat any waiver-dependent piece as a separate line you can drop or expand.
- Note which state streams would continue regardless of federal changes, and confirm that with the administering agency.

## Worked example

Valley Regional's year-one plan funds two center champions' apprenticeship time and their training from WIOA Adult and Dislocated Worker funds. It budgets OJT reimbursements at the board's current rate, using the 90 percent waiver for eligible small employers where board policy allows. Andre's team assumes partner colleges claim RSI for related instruction and that the hospital sponsor will check AIF eligibility. No CAI grant is planned until the intermediary step in year two.

## Discussion prompt

In your group or in the chat: which of these streams does your board currently leave on the table because no one has asked the partner about it?

## Practice

1. List your strategy's costs in the two buckets: board operating costs and participant/program costs.
2. For each cost, name every stream from the table that could pay for it, and circle the one you control.
3. Mark each item that depends on a waiver, a state agency's rules, or your board's local policy, and note where you will confirm it.
4. Write one sentence on how your plan holds up if reauthorization changes federal funding.
