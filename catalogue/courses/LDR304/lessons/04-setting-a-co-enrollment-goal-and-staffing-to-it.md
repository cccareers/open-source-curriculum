## Why a co-enrollment goal

A role is a statement of intent. A co-enrollment goal turns it into a number your staff can hit and your board can track. When an apprentice is co-enrolled in WIOA Title I and recorded correctly, the services you provide, and the credentials and skill gains the apprentice earns, can count. When they are not, the work disappears from your results.

CCC's catalog cites TEGL 05-25 as setting a **10 percent co-enrollment goal**. Before you adopt it, read the TEGL to confirm exactly what the 10 percent is measured against and whether it is a target, an expectation, or an example. Your goal has to use the same definition, or you will be measuring something different from what the state and DOL are watching.

## Build the goal from your baseline

Do not pick a number from the air. Build it in three steps.

1. **Start with the denominator.** Use the measure the TEGL defines. Pull last year's figure from your data.
2. **Apply the goal.** Calculate what 10 percent of that figure would be.
3. **Compare it with your baseline.** Put the result next to your current co-enrolled count and your staff estimate from lesson 2. The distance between them tells you how much staffing and training you need.

### Worked example

Valley Regional's MIS lead confirms the denominator definition from the TEGL. For illustration, assume that figure for last program year was 400. Ten percent is 40.

| Item | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Goal at 10 percent | 40 |
| Currently co-enrolled and recorded | 6 |
| Staff estimate of apprentices actually helped | about 25 |

Andre sees two gaps. About 19 apprentices were served but not recorded (25 minus 6): that is a **coding and process gap**, closed mostly through training. Another 15 or so would need to be newly reached (40 minus 25): that is an **outreach and capacity gap**, closed through staffing and partnerships.

He sets a stepped goal: 25 in year one (capturing those already served), 40 in year two. A stepped goal is fine as long as you show the path to the full number.

## Choose a navigator staffing model

The goal tells you how much work there is. The staffing model decides who does it. Three common models:

| Model | How it works | Fits when | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dedicated navigator** | One or more full-time staff own apprenticeship co-enrollment for the board | The goal is large enough to fill a position; you want one accountable owner | A single point of failure; plan cross-training |
| **Center champions** | One designated staff member per AJCC spends part of their time on apprenticeship | Several centers, moderate goal, strong center managers | Apprenticeship work gets pushed aside by other caseload |
| **Shared regional navigator** | Boards in a region jointly fund a navigator | Small boards; programs that cross board lines | Clear agreements on supervision, data, and who gets credit |

Size it with a rough workload estimate. Ask experienced staff how many hours a typical apprentice co-enrollment takes, from first contact through enrollment, services, and recording outcomes, and multiply by your goal. Then add time for sponsor relationships. Your board's own experience is the best source for these numbers; there is no standard figure to borrow.

Valley Regional chose center champions in year one, with a plan to convert to a dedicated navigator once the goal reached 40.

## Train to the goal

A staffing model fails if the people in it cannot do the work. Map training to the gaps you found:

| Gap | Who needs it | Course in this pathway |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic understanding of Registered Apprenticeship | All frontline staff, center managers | APP 101 |
| Correct enrollment, coding, and outcome recording | Navigators or champions, MIS and data staff | APP 104 |
| OJT contracts that fit a multi-year apprenticeship | Business services, OJT coordinators | APP 102 |
| Who pays for which component | Navigators, program managers | APP 103 |

Put dates on it. A training plan without completion dates is a wish.

## Discussion prompt

In your group or the chat: in your system, is the bigger gap apprentices served but never recorded, or apprentices never reached? What would you change first?

## Practice

1. Confirm the 10 percent definition in TEGL 05-25 and write down the denominator you will use. Then calculate your 10 percent figure.
2. Compare it with your recorded count and staff estimate. Split the gap into a coding gap and an outreach gap.
3. Set a goal for year one and year two.
4. Choose a navigator staffing model and write two sentences on why it fits.
5. List the training each role needs and a target completion month.
