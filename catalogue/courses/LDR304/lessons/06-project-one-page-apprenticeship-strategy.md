## Goal

Produce a one-page apprenticeship strategy you could hand to your board chair or leadership team next week. It pulls together the work from the four lessons: where you stand, the role you will take, the goal you will hit, who will do the work, and what pays for it.

Use your own board if you can. If you cannot share your own numbers, use the case below.

## The case (if you need one)

**Coastal Hills Workforce Development Board** serves two counties with three AJCCs. Its MIS lead reports 4 co-enrolled apprentices last program year; center managers estimate about 18 were helped. Business services has a good relationship with one community college and a regional IT apprenticeship intermediary, but no sponsor relationships in health care, which is a local-plan priority sector. No staff member has apprenticeship in their job description. The board has no apprenticeship-specific OJT template. One board member, a hospital HR director, has offered to "help get something started." Assume the denominator for the co-enrollment goal, once confirmed against TEGL 05-25, is 300.

## Requirements

Your one page must include five labeled sections:

1. **Capacity assessment.** Outcome counts (recorded and estimated) and a none/some/strong rating for people, partnerships, programs in reach, systems, and money, with one line of evidence each.
2. **Chosen role.** Referrer, navigator, intermediary, or sponsor, the weakest essential area that limits it, and one move-up trigger.
3. **Co-enrollment goal.** The denominator you used, the 10 percent figure, and your year-one and year-two goals, split into coding gap and outreach gap.
4. **Staffing and training plan.** Your navigator staffing model, who fills it, and the training each role needs with target completion months.
5. **Funding sources.** The main stream for each major cost, marked as controlled by the board or by a partner.

## Constraints

- One page. If it does not fit, cut detail, not sections.
- Plain language a board member without workforce background can follow.
- Cite TEGL 05-25 and other guidance only for what you have confirmed in the current version. Mark anything unconfirmed as "to verify."
- Label every local-policy assumption, such as your OJT reimbursement rate or supportive services limits, as "per board policy."
- No real participant names or case data.

## Definition of Done

- All five sections are present and labeled.
- The chosen role is justified by the weakest essential area, not the strongest.
- The co-enrollment goal shows its arithmetic and its denominator.
- Every staff role named has a training line and a month.
- Every major cost has at least one named funding stream, and WIOA is not shown paying for something a partner stream already covers.
- The page names at least one assumption to revisit if federal reauthorization changes the rules.
- A colleague can read it in three minutes and tell you what the board is committing to.

## Hints

- Write the role section last, even though it appears second. The goal and funding sections often show that your first-choice role is out of reach.
- For the Coastal Hills case, notice the gap between 4 recorded and about 18 helped. Much of the year-one goal is coding, not outreach.
- An enthusiastic board member is a partnership asset, not a sponsorship plan. Rate it honestly.
- Reuse your Practice pages from lessons 2 through 5; most of the content is already written.
