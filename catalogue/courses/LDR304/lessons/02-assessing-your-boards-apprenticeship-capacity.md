## Start with what is true today

Most apprenticeship strategies fail in the same way: a board picks an ambitious role before anyone has written down what the system is actually doing. The first job is not vision. It is an honest baseline.

A capacity assessment answers two questions:

1. **Outcomes.** What has apprenticeship produced for your participants so far, and can you prove it from your data?
2. **Capacity.** What people, partnerships, systems, and money do you have that could produce more?

Keep them separate. A board can have strong relationships with sponsors (capacity) and almost nothing in its records to show for it (outcomes), usually because apprentices were served but never co-enrolled or coded. That gap is a finding, not a failure, and it shapes every decision that follows.

## Outcomes: what your data can and cannot tell you

Ask your MIS or data lead to pull the last two or three program years. You are looking for a short set of counts, not a research report.

| Question | Where it usually comes from | Common problem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How many WIOA Title I participants were also registered apprentices? | CalJOBS / PIRL apprenticeship fields | Apprentices served but never flagged |
| How many entered apprenticeship after a WIOA service? | Case notes, training records, OJT contracts | Recorded in notes only, not in data fields |
| How many apprenticeship-related OJT contracts or ITAs did you fund? | Contract and ITA logs | Not tagged as apprenticeship-related |
| How many apprentices earned a credential or Measurable Skill Gain you recorded? | Performance reports | Outcomes happen years later and are missed |
| Which programs and sponsors did those apprentices come from? | Business services records | Lives in one staff member's memory |

The exact data elements and codes are taught in APP 104 (Co-Enrollment and Coding). For this briefing, the leadership question is simpler: **if a monitor or a board member asked "how many apprentices did we serve last year," could we answer from the system within a day?** If not, write that down.

Be careful with the "zero" answer. A count of zero in the data often means "not recorded," not "not served." Ask frontline staff for a rough count of apprentices they know they helped, and record both numbers. The difference is the size of your documentation problem.

## Capacity: the five things to inventory

Use these five areas. Rate each one plainly: **none, some, or strong**, with one line of evidence.

1. **People.** Who on staff knows how Registered Apprenticeship works? Is anyone's job description tied to it, or is it everyone's side task?
2. **Partnerships.** Which sponsors, intermediaries, colleges, adult schools, and labor partners do you actively work with? "We met them once" counts as none.
3. **Programs in reach.** How many registered programs in your region are accepting apprentices in occupations that matter to your local plan? Include new-collar occupations, not only the building trades.
4. **Systems.** Can your staff enroll, code, and track an apprentice correctly today? Do you have an OJT contract approach that fits a multi-year apprenticeship?
5. **Money.** What WIOA formula funds are realistically available after existing commitments? Do you hold or partner on any apprenticeship-specific grants?

## Worked example: Valley Regional Workforce Board

Valley Regional's program director, Andre, ran the assessment in two weeks with his MIS lead and two business services reps.

| Area | Rating | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcomes | Some | Data shows 6 co-enrolled apprentices last year; staff estimate about 25 were helped |
| People | Some | One business services rep has apprenticeship experience; no one has it in their job description |
| Partnerships | Strong | Active relationships with an electrical JATC, a hospital sponsor, and the community college |
| Programs in reach | Some | Four programs accepting apprentices in local-plan sectors |
| Systems | None | No apprenticeship OJT template; coding practice inconsistent across centers |
| Money | Some | Limited Adult and Dislocated Worker training funds after current commitments; no apprenticeship grant |

Andre's one-sentence summary for his board: "We have the relationships, but we serve apprentices quietly and our data shows less than a quarter of it." That sentence becomes the starting line for the role decision in the next lesson.

## Leadership traps to avoid

- **Counting enthusiasm as capacity.** A partner's interest is not an open apprenticeship slot.
- **Letting one person be the capacity.** If one staff member leaving would drop a rating from strong to none, rate it "some."
- **Skipping the data pull.** Without counts, you cannot set the co-enrollment goal in lesson 4.
- **Rating the region instead of the board.** A busy regional apprenticeship scene is not your capacity unless you are connected to it.

## Discussion prompt

With your table group or in the briefing chat: which of the five capacity areas would your board rate highest, and what evidence would you show a skeptical board member to back that rating?

## Practice

Complete a baseline for your own board.

1. Write down the three outcome counts you can get from data, and next to each the staff estimate. If you do not have the numbers today, write who you will ask and by when.
2. Rate each of the five capacity areas as none, some, or strong, with one line of evidence each.
3. Write a one-sentence summary in the style of Andre's: what you have, and what the gap is.
4. Mark any rating that depends on a single person.
