## Four roles, four levels of commitment

A board can take one of four roles in apprenticeship. Each step up adds responsibility, staff, and risk, and each one includes the work of the step below it.

| Role | What the board does | What it takes | What it risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Referrer** | Sends interested job seekers to apprenticeship openings and sponsors | Basic staff awareness, an up-to-date list of open programs | Low. But apprentices are rarely co-enrolled, so outcomes go uncounted |
| **Navigator** | Assigns staff to guide participants into apprenticeship, co-enroll them, fund supports and training, and work with sponsors on openings | Dedicated or designated staff, correct enrollment and coding, OJT and ITA approaches that fit apprenticeship | Moderate. Staff time must be protected from other priorities |
| **Intermediary** | Convenes employers, colleges, and sponsors to build or expand programs; may help employers join existing programs or support a group program | Business services depth, partner trust, program-development capacity, often grant funds | Higher. Relationship and grant management; results take longer |
| **Sponsor** | Holds a registered program's standards and is responsible for its apprentices | Deep apprenticeship expertise, long-term administrative capacity, a stable funding base | Highest. A multi-year legal and operational commitment |

Two reminders from APP 101: in California, program registration runs through the Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS), and the sponsor carries responsibility for the program and its apprentices. A board that sponsors is taking on a different kind of obligation from a board that funds training. Confirm the current sponsor requirements with DAS before treating sponsorship as an option.

## Match the role to the baseline

Take the capacity ratings from lesson 2 and test each role against them. The rule of thumb: **choose the highest role your weakest essential area can support**, not the one your strongest area suggests.

| If your baseline shows... | The realistic role is usually... |
| --- | --- |
| No designated staff, weak systems, few partner relationships | Referrer, with a plan to move up |
| Some staff knowledge, workable systems, active sponsor relationships | Navigator |
| Strong business services, trusted partners, program-development funds | Intermediary |
| All of the above, plus long-term administrative capacity and a stable funding base | Sponsor, if your board's governance supports it |

Most boards land at navigator. That is not settling. Navigator is the role where WIOA co-enrollment happens, and co-enrollment is what makes apprenticeship count in your performance.

## Worked example: Valley Regional decides

Andre's baseline showed strong partnerships but weak systems and only part-time staff knowledge. One board member argued for becoming an intermediary because of the strong relationships with the hospital and the JATC.

Andre tested it against the weakest essential area. An intermediary still needs staff who can enroll and code apprentices correctly, and Valley Regional's systems rating was "none." Building a new program on top of a system that cannot count its own apprentices would repeat the original problem at a larger scale.

His recommendation to the board: **navigator now, intermediary as a two-year option** once coding and an apprenticeship OJT approach are in place. He wrote down what would trigger the move up: a stable co-enrollment count for a full program year, and an identified program-development funding source.

## Factor in the policy landscape

Two things can change the math. Treat both as inputs you check, not facts you assume.

- **TEGL 05-25 waiver opportunities.** CCC's catalog identifies TEGL 05-25 as describing waiver opportunities relevant to apprenticeship strategy. Before you choose a role, read the current TEGL to see which waivers exist, what they would let your board do differently, and how a request is made. A waiver can make a higher role more feasible, but plan around it only once you know it applies to you.
- **The pending federal reauthorization.** WIOA reauthorization is pending, and its final terms are not known. Pick a role you can sustain under current rules, and note in your strategy which assumptions you would revisit if reauthorization changes the requirements or funding for apprenticeship.

## Questions to ask before you commit

- Who, by name, will do the work of this role next quarter?
- What stops if they do it?
- What evidence in a year would tell us the role is working?
- What would make us step up, or step back?

## Discussion prompt

Board members often push for the most visible role. With your table or in the chat: what would you say to a board member who wants to "become a sponsor" when your baseline supports navigator?

## Practice

Using your lesson 2 baseline:

1. Identify your weakest *essential* capacity area for each of the four roles.
2. Choose the highest role that area can support, and write two sentences explaining why.
3. Write one "move-up trigger": the specific evidence that would justify the next role.
4. List one question you need to answer from TEGL 05-25 and one assumption that reauthorization could change.
