## Goal

Work as a small team to run a tabletop exercise on a fictional provider transition and produce a short transition package: a transition plan, an at-risk indicator analysis, and an onboarding sequence. If time allows, add draft contract transition language.

## The scenario

The Harbor Plains Workforce Board has chosen a new provider for its Northgate AJCC career services contract. The outgoing provider, Crestline Employment Partners, has served the center for several years. The incoming provider, Summit Pathways, has run youth programs nearby but has never run Adult and Dislocated Worker services. Service changes over in six weeks.

What you know:

- About 280 active participants across Adult and Dislocated Worker, and about 120 in follow-up.
- 35 participants are enrolled in training; 8 completed training recently and credentials are not yet recorded.
- 5 OJT agreements are open with local employers.
- Crestline's two most experienced case managers have already given notice.
- Summit Pathways has hired 6 new case managers; 2 have prior WIOA experience.

Midway through the exercise, the facilitator will add a complication (for example, a delay in system access for new staff, or a box of files found with missing eligibility documents). Your plan must absorb it.

## Requirements

Your package must include:

1. **Transition plan** covering caseloads (with tiers), files (with chain of custody), open contracts (with a decision for each type), and data entry (with owners and deadlines). Include key dates and named leads by role.
2. **At-risk indicator analysis** naming the indicators most exposed in this scenario, the participant groups behind each, an owner, and the weekly report you will watch.
3. **Rapid onboarding sequence** for Summit Pathways' new staff, from before day one through a 30-day readiness check, including how you will capture knowledge from Crestline staff before they leave.
4. **Your response to the complication**, as a short note showing what changed in your plan.

Optional: two or three exit or start-up duties written as contract deliverables.

## Constraints

- Use only the fictional facts above plus reasonable assumptions you state out loud. Do not use real participant data.
- Where a step depends on a rule you are not sure of (record retention, data-entry timeliness, how exit is determined, how agreements are reassigned), write "confirm with [source]" rather than guessing.
- Assign each team member a role: board lead, outgoing provider lead, incoming provider lead.
- Keep the package to what fits on about three pages.

## Definition of Done

- Every task in the plan has a named owner role and a deadline.
- Every at-risk group has an action, an owner, and a report to watch.
- The onboarding sequence puts system access before training and ends with a practical readiness check.
- The complication is addressed with a specific change, not a general promise.
- Another team can read your package and tell who does what in week 1.

## Hints

- Start with the 35 participants in training and the 8 missing credentials. That is where performance is lost fastest.
- The departing case managers are your biggest knowledge risk. Schedule their time first.
- Summit's youth experience is useful, but do not assume it transfers to Adult and Dislocated Worker eligibility or training rules.
- Play the outgoing provider honestly: they are short-staffed too. A plan that asks too much of them will fail.
