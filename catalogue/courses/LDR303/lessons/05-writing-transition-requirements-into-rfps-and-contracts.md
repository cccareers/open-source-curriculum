## Plan the next transition on day one

The cheapest time to plan a transition is before the contract is signed. Once a provider knows it is leaving, the board has little leverage to ask for extra work it never contracted for. If transition duties are written into the RFP and the contract, they are simply part of the job, priced in, and enforceable.

This lesson covers two directions at once: what you require of a provider **when it leaves** (exit duties) and what you require **when it arrives** (start-up duties). Both belong in every service provider and operator procurement.

## Where the language goes

| Document | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| RFP scope of work | A description of transition expectations so bidders can plan and price them |
| RFP proposal questions | Ask bidders how they would start up and how they would hand off |
| RFP evaluation criteria | Points for a credible start-up and knowledge-transfer plan |
| Contract scope of work | The specific exit and start-up duties, with deliverables and timelines |
| Contract payment terms | Whether transition deliverables are tied to payment |
| Contract close-out provisions | Records, property, data, and final reporting obligations |

Your board's procurement policy, legal counsel, and the federal and state procurement rules that apply to WIOA funds govern the final wording. Treat the samples below as starting points to bring to them, not finished clauses.

## Exit duties: what the outgoing provider owes

Write these so they survive a contract that ends early, not just one that runs to term.

- **Notice cooperation.** Within a set period after notice, name a transition lead and attend joint transition meetings.
- **Caseload roster.** Deliver a reconciled roster of active and follow-up participants, with the fields the board specifies.
- **Records transfer.** Transfer participant files and records as directed, with an inventory and transfer log, protecting confidential information.
- **Data completion.** Enter all services, case notes, and outcomes delivered through the last service day before system access ends.
- **Open agreements.** Provide a list of all open participant-related agreements with balances and end dates, and cooperate in their assignment or close-out.
- **Knowledge transfer.** Make named staff available for a set number of hours of handoff meetings and produce written process and partner summaries.
- **Final reporting.** Submit final fiscal and program reports on the schedule in the contract.

Sample clause, plain-language draft:

> Upon notice of termination or expiration, Contractor shall designate a Transition Lead within [number] business days and shall carry out the Transition Plan approved by the Board, including delivery of a reconciled participant roster, transfer of participant records with a signed inventory, completion of all data entry for services delivered, and participation in knowledge-transfer sessions as described in Exhibit [letter]. A portion of the final payment, as set in Exhibit [letter], is contingent on Board acceptance of these deliverables.

Fill in the bracketed values with your board and counsel. Do not borrow numbers from another area without checking that they fit your contract length and caseload.

## Start-up duties: what the incoming provider owes

- **Start-up plan.** Submit a plan with staffing, system access, onboarding, and a caseload intake schedule before the service start date.
- **Staffing readiness.** Have a minimum set of trained staff in place on the first service day.
- **Onboarding.** Deliver a rapid onboarding sequence and document completion of required training.
- **Caseload acceptance.** Contact every transferred participant within a set window, with priority for those in training or work-based placements.
- **Performance continuity.** Accept responsibility for performance outcomes of transferred participants from the start date.

## What to ask bidders

Proposal questions turn requirements into evidence you can score:

1. Describe how you would take over an existing caseload in the first 30 days, including how you would prioritize participants.
2. Describe your onboarding plan for staff who are new to WIOA Title I.
3. Describe how, if your contract ended, you would transfer records, data, and local knowledge to the next provider.

A bidder that answers question 3 vaguely is telling you something.

## Discussion prompt

Look at your current provider contract. If it ended next month, which of the exit duties above could you actually enforce?

## Practice

1. Choose three exit duties and three start-up duties from this lesson. Rewrite each as a contract deliverable with an owner, a due point (in days relative to notice or start date, using bracketed placeholders), and an acceptance test.
2. Write one RFP proposal question and a three-level scoring guide (weak, adequate, strong) for it.
3. Mark which of your deliverables you would tie to payment, and note what your board's counsel would need to confirm.
