## Performance does not pause for a transition

The board's negotiated performance levels keep counting through every week of a provider change. Participants who exit during the gap still land in the cohorts the local area is measured on. The question is not whether performance is at risk, but **which indicators, for which participants, and when.**

WIOA Title I measures the Adult and Dislocated Worker programs on employment in the second and fourth quarters after exit, median earnings in the second quarter after exit, credential attainment, and measurable skill gains. Youth is measured on education, training, or employment placement instead of employment alone. Effectiveness in serving employers is measured too. Each one breaks in a different way during a transition.

## How each indicator gets hurt

| Indicator | How a transition damages it | When you see the damage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measurable skill gains | Gains happen but never get documented or entered before staff leave | Within the current program year |
| Credential attainment | Credentials earned in training are not collected or recorded | Within about a year after exit |
| Employment 2nd and 4th quarter | Participants exit while disengaged, before placement | Two to four quarters later, when wage data arrives |
| Median earnings 2nd quarter | Rushed placements into lower-wage jobs to close cases | Two quarters later |
| Effectiveness in serving employers | Employer contacts and services stop or go unrecorded | Annual reporting |

Notice the timing column. Two of these indicators show damage fast, and they are the ones you can still save during the transition itself.

### The fast-moving pair: skill gains and credentials

**Measurable skill gains** are counted within a program year for participants in education or training. They depend almost entirely on a case manager collecting proof (a transcript, a progress report, a passed exam) and recording it. When the outgoing case manager is job hunting and the incoming one has never met the participant, that proof simply does not get collected.

**Credential attainment** depends on the same thing: someone has to ask for the certificate and record it. Participants who finished training just before the transition are the most likely to slip through.

### The slow-moving trio: employment and earnings

Employment and earnings outcomes are mostly measured from wage records after exit, so the damage hides for months. The risk is **unplanned exits**. Under WIOA's common exit approach, a participant exits after a period with no qualifying services and none planned, and the exit date reaches back to the last service. A participant who goes quiet during the handoff can exit without a job, and you will not see it in the numbers until long after the transition is over. Check your state's current guidance on what counts as a qualifying service and how exit is determined.

## Find your at-risk participants

Turn the indicator view into a list of names. From the reconciled roster built in your transition plan, flag:

1. **In training now** (skill gain and credential risk). Who has a gain or credential due this program year?
2. **Recently completed training** (credential risk). Has the credential been collected and entered?
3. **No recent service** (exit risk). Who is closest to exiting without a planned service?
4. **Recently placed but not yet exited** (earnings and retention risk). Who still needs follow-up support?
5. **Active employer relationships** (employer indicator risk). Which employers are mid-recruitment or mid-OJT?

A worked example from the fictional Eastside AJCC:

| Group | Count | Owner during transition | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In training, gain due this PY | 38 | Outgoing CM until handoff | Collect progress evidence before last day |
| Completed training, credential not entered | 11 | Board QA | Call each participant; enter credential |
| No service in a long stretch | 27 | Incoming CM | Re-engagement outreach in week 1 |
| Placed, not exited | 19 | Incoming CM | Follow-up call and retention support |
| Employers mid-OJT | 6 | Incoming business services | Introduction visit in week 1 |

The counts are illustrative. The point is that every row has an owner and an action.

## Watch it while it happens

Pick two or three reports you will pull weekly during the transition, such as a list of participants with no service in the last few weeks, open training enrollments, and credentials pending. Agree in advance what number on each report triggers escalation to the board lead.

## Discussion prompt

Which indicator in your local area would hide damage the longest? What would you need to see in the first month to catch it?

## Practice

1. Using your own area or Eastside AJCC, list the five at-risk groups above and estimate a count for each.
2. Rank them by urgency for the first 30 days and explain your top choice in two sentences.
3. Name the weekly report you would use to watch each of your top three groups, and the trigger that sends it to the board lead.
