## The first weeks decide the year

An incoming provider often starts with a mix of new hires, staff transferred from other programs, and sometimes staff who worked for the outgoing provider. On their first day they inherit participants who were promised services by someone else. A standard new-hire orientation spread over several months is too slow. You need a **rapid onboarding sequence**: the smallest set of skills and access each person needs, in the order they need it.

## Principles of a rapid sequence

- **Access before training.** A trained case manager without a system login cannot help anyone. Request CalJOBS accounts, email, phones, and building access before day one.
- **Do first, deepen later.** Teach the tasks staff will perform this week. Save the full policy library for later.
- **Learn on real work, with a safety net.** Pair new staff with experienced peers and have someone review early case notes and entries.
- **Use the people who are leaving.** Outgoing staff who will share what they know are the fastest source of local knowledge. Schedule them while they are still there.

## A sample sequence

This is a template for the fictional Northstar Careers taking over the Eastside AJCC. Adjust timing to your contract dates.

| When | Focus | What happens | Who leads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before day 1 | Access | System accounts requested, equipment issued, required confidentiality training completed | Incoming operations lead |
| Days 1–3 | Core tasks | Program basics, case-note standards, how to record a service, how to read a participant record | Incoming trainer |
| Days 1–5 | Local knowledge | Shadowing or meetings with outgoing staff; walk-through of partner referrals and key employers | Outgoing transition lead |
| Week 1 | First caseload | Warm handoffs for Tier 1 participants, with a peer present | Supervisor |
| Week 2 | Eligibility and enrollment | Eligibility determination and documentation, practiced on sample files | Incoming trainer, board QA |
| Weeks 2–3 | Quality check | Supervisor reviews a sample of each new staff member's entries and notes | Supervisor |
| Weeks 3–4 | Training and work-based services | ITAs, OJT, and supportive services under local policy | Board staff, incoming trainer |
| Day 30 | Readiness check | Each staff member shows they can complete core tasks without help | Supervisor |

## Capture local knowledge before it leaves

Much of what makes an AJCC work is never written down: which partner answers the phone, which employer prefers email, how a particular training provider sends progress reports. Ask outgoing staff for a one-page **knowledge sheet** for each key relationship and process. A simple template:

> **Knowledge sheet: Mesa Valley College, Medical Assistant program**
> Contact: program coordinator (name and phone in the shared contact list).
> How we get progress reports: emailed mid-term; ask if not received by week 8.
> Watch out for: start dates move; confirm a week ahead.
> Current participants enrolled: 7 (see roster).

Run short recorded sessions where outgoing staff talk through their most complex cases (with participant consent and PII protected) or their employer accounts. Ten minutes on a call can save weeks of rediscovery.

## Make the readiness check real

A readiness check is a short, practical demonstration, not a quiz. A new case manager might be asked to:

- Find a participant's record and summarize their next step.
- Enter a service and a case note that meets your standards.
- Explain what documentation is still missing from a sample file.
- Describe how to request a supportive service under your local policy.

Anyone who cannot yet do a task gets targeted coaching and a second check, not a lighter caseload forever.

## Discussion prompt

If your team lost its three most experienced staff on Friday, what knowledge would be gone on Monday? Where is it written down today?

## Practice

1. Draft a rapid onboarding table for your own incoming team with at least six rows: timing, focus, activity, and lead.
2. Write the list of system and building access every role needs before day one, and who requests each.
3. Create one knowledge sheet for a partner, employer, or process in your area using the template above.
4. Write four readiness-check tasks for a case manager role.
