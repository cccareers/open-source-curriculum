## One employer, three possible paths

Once an employer is interested, the next question is how to start. You have three main options, and recommending the wrong one is a fast way to lose an employer who was ready to say yes.

| | Start a new program | Join an intermediary program | Use OJT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | The employer becomes the sponsor and registers its own apprenticeship standards | The employer signs on to an existing registered program run by a sponsor such as an industry association, college, or labor-management group | A WIOA Title I contract that reimburses part of an eligible new hire's wages while they learn the job |
| Who holds the paperwork | The employer | The intermediary sponsor, largely | The employer and your board, under a contract |
| Time to start | Longest; standards must be developed and approved | Faster; standards already exist | Fastest; once the trainee is eligible and the contract is signed |
| Length | Typically a year or more | Set by the existing program | Short-term, set by the training plan and your board's policy |
| Credential | Nationally recognized completion credential | Same | None by itself; the employer's own training |
| Best for | Larger employers with HR capacity and an occupation no existing program covers | Small and mid-sized employers, or common occupations already served by a program | Employers who need one or a few hires trained now, or who want to try the approach first |

## Decision questions

Ask these in order. The first clear answer usually points to the model.

1. **Does an existing registered program already cover this occupation in your region?** If yes, joining it is almost always the easiest start. Check with your regional apprenticeship partners and the California Division of Apprenticeship Standards' program listings.
2. **Does the employer have the capacity to write and maintain its own standards?** A dedicated HR or training lead and a long-term commitment to the occupation suggest a new program. If not, steer toward an intermediary.
3. **Does the employer need the hire faster than a program can be set up, or is it hiring only one or two people?** OJT can start sooner and fits single hires, provided the trainee is WIOA-eligible and the employer meets your board's OJT requirements.
4. **Is the employer hesitant and wants to test the idea?** OJT can serve as a first step that builds trust before a longer commitment.

These models are not mutually exclusive. An OJT contract can often support an apprentice's first months in a registered program, and incumbent worker training can upskill current staff who will become mentors. SkillBridge can bring a transitioning service member into either path. Check your board's policy on combining funding sources before you put them together.

## Worked examples

**Delta Fabrication (60 employees, welders).** Teresa has no dedicated HR staff and needs welders, a common occupation. Jamal checks and finds a regional manufacturing association that sponsors a welding apprenticeship. **Recommendation: join the intermediary program**, and explore whether OJT can offset early wages for eligible hires.

**Pacific Grid Services (900 employees, line technicians).** The employer has a training department, its own safety curriculum, and plans to hire dozens of technicians a year for the foreseeable future. **Recommendation: a new employer-sponsored program**, with your board connecting them to the state apprenticeship consultant for help developing standards.

**Ortiz Family Bakery (14 employees, one maintenance mechanic).** The owner needs one person trained on specific equipment within weeks and has no interest in a multi-year program. **Recommendation: OJT**, if a WIOA-eligible candidate is identified. Revisit apprenticeship in a later conversation once trust is built.

## Explaining your recommendation

Keep the explanation short and tied to what the employer told you:

> "You said you don't have anyone to manage paperwork, and you need welders, which is a trade with an existing program in our region. So I'd recommend joining the association's program rather than starting your own. They hold the standards and handle most of the reporting. If your new hires qualify for WIOA, we can look at an OJT contract to cover part of their early wages."

Then name the next step: an introduction to the intermediary, a candidate referral, or an OJT eligibility check.

## Practice

For each employer below, choose a model, write one sentence explaining why, and name the next step you would propose. Then compare answers with a partner and discuss any disagreements.

1. A regional hospital system with an education department wants to grow its own surgical technologists. No program for that occupation exists in your region.
2. A 25-person HVAC contractor wants three new technicians a year. A local labor-management program covers HVAC.
3. A small print shop needs one press operator trained in the next month and has found a candidate who was recently laid off.
4. A logistics company is interested but nervous, and the hiring manager says, "Let's start small and see how it goes."
