## Goal

Run a complete, 15-minute employer meeting from first question to agreed next step. You will play a business services representative with Valley Regional Workforce Board meeting a fictional employer, and you will be observed and scored by a peer.

## The case

**Coastal Auto Collision** is a family-owned body shop with two locations and 35 employees. The owner, Dana Reyes, agreed to a meeting after a chamber of commerce event. The role-player playing Dana receives this private brief:

- You cannot find auto body technicians. Two bays sit empty most weeks.
- You have hired three "experienced" techs in the past year; two left within months for a dealership paying more.
- Your lead tech, Victor, trains new people informally but is frustrated and talks about retiring.
- You estimate empty bays cost you "several thousand a week" in turned-away work. Share this only if asked about impact.
- You worry about cost, and you once had a bad experience with "a government program and its paperwork." Raise at least two objections.
- You have no HR staff. Your office manager handles payroll.

## Requirements

Your meeting must include all four parts, in a natural order:

1. **Discovery.** Surface the priority role, the pain, at least one impact number, and who decides.
2. **Business case.** Present, verbally or on a one-page sheet, a comparison using Dana's numbers, an illustrative apprentice wage progression, and possible funding offsets, each labeled "to confirm."
3. **Objection handling.** Respond to each objection Dana raises using acknowledge, clarify, respond, confirm.
4. **Recommended model.** Recommend a new program, an intermediary program, or OJT (or a combination) and explain why in terms of what Dana told you.

Close by agreeing on a specific next step with a date.

## Constraints

- 15 minutes maximum, then 5 minutes of observer feedback.
- No program pitch in the first 5 minutes.
- Do not state a reimbursement rate, cap, or funding amount as certain. Refer to your board's local policy.
- Assume an existing regional program covers auto body repair only if your facilitator says so; otherwise, ask how you would find out.
- Use fictional details only.

## Definition of Done

- [ ] Discovery note written within 5 minutes after the meeting (role, pain, impact, decision-maker, next step).
- [ ] At least one impact number came from the employer, not from you.
- [ ] Business case used the employer's numbers and labeled all estimates.
- [ ] Every objection was handled with all four steps.
- [ ] Recommendation named one model and gave a reason tied to discovery.
- [ ] A dated next step was agreed.
- [ ] Observer scorecard completed and discussed.

## Hints

- If you get stuck, go back to a question: "Tell me more about what happens when a tech leaves."
- Victor is both a risk and an asset. He could be the mentor, and incumbent worker training might help prepare him; check your board's rules.
- With no HR staff, think about who would carry the paperwork.
- The best next step is small and concrete: "I'll bring you the intermediary's contact and our OJT eligibility checklist next Tuesday."
