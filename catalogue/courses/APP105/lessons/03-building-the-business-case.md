## From pain to proposal

Discovery gave you the employer's problem in their own words and, ideally, a few rough numbers. The business case turns those numbers into a simple comparison: what the problem costs today versus what apprenticeship would cost and return. Decision-makers do not buy programs; they buy a better result than the one they have.

A strong business case for a small or mid-sized employer fits on one page and uses three kinds of numbers:

1. **Wage numbers**: what the employer pays now, and what an apprentice would earn as they progress.
2. **Retention numbers**: what turnover and vacancies cost, and how a structured earn-and-learn path changes that.
3. **Subsidy numbers**: what public funding can offset during the training period.

## Wage numbers

A Registered Apprenticeship pays the apprentice from day one on a progressive wage schedule that rises as skills are gained, ending at the journeyworker rate. Employers often fear they will be paying full wages for an unproductive worker. Show them the opposite: early-stage apprentice wages start below the journeyworker rate, and productivity rises alongside pay.

Build a short wage table using the employer's own journeyworker rate. The schedule in the standards the employer registers (or joins) sets the real steps; for a first meeting, an illustrative schedule is fine as long as you label it.

| Period | Illustrative % of journeyworker rate | Hourly (if journeyworker = $30) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Months 1-6 | 60% | $18.00 |
| Months 7-12 | 70% | $21.00 |
| Months 13-18 | 80% | $24.00 |
| Months 19-24 | 90% | $27.00 |
| Completion | 100% | $30.00 |

Say plainly that these steps are an example and that the final schedule is set when the program is registered.

## Retention numbers

Here you reuse what the employer told you in discovery. You are not producing an audited cost study; you are helping the employer see the cost of the status quo using their estimates.

Common cost drivers to total up:

- **Vacancy cost**: overtime, agency or temp fees, or lost orders while the job is open.
- **Replacement cost**: recruiting, interviewing, onboarding, and supervisor time for each new hire.
- **Early-quit cost**: training invested in people who leave within the first months.

Then explain why apprenticeship changes the picture: apprentices are hired into a visible career path with scheduled raises and a credential at the end, which gives them a reason to stay. For published retention and return-on-investment figures, use the current materials on apprenticeship.gov or from your state apprenticeship partners, and cite the source on your one-pager. Do not quote a retention percentage from memory.

## Subsidy numbers

Public funding can reduce the employer's cost during training. What is available depends on the participant, the program, and your board, so present these as "likely available, to be confirmed":

- **WIOA on-the-job training (OJT).** An OJT contract can reimburse part of an eligible new hire's wages during training. WIOA generally allows reimbursement of up to 50 percent of the wage rate, with higher rates possible under conditions set out in 20 CFR 680.730. Your board's local policy sets the actual rate, duration, and any cap.
- **Related technical instruction (RTI) support.** WIOA Individual Training Accounts or other funds may help pay for classroom instruction for eligible apprentices. Check your board's policy on using ITAs for RTI and whether the provider must be on the Eligible Training Provider List.
- **Incumbent worker training.** For upskilling current employees (for example, training a future mentor), your board may offer incumbent worker training funds with an employer match. Confirm the local match requirement.
- **SkillBridge.** For transitioning service members, the Department of Defense SkillBridge program lets them train with an employer during their final months of service while still receiving military pay. Coordinate with your LVER or veterans staff.
- **State apprenticeship funds.** California offers state apprenticeship funding streams that change over time. Check current availability with your board and the regional apprenticeship partners before promising anything.

## Worked example: the Delta Fabrication one-pager

Jamal returns to Teresa with this summary. Every number came from Teresa or is clearly labeled as an estimate to confirm.

```text
DELTA FABRICATION - WELDER PIPELINE: CURRENT COST vs. APPRENTICESHIP
(prepared by Valley Regional Workforce Board; estimates for discussion)

TODAY (your estimates)
  Welder vacancies per year ................. 3
  Months open per vacancy ................... 3-4
  Overtime while short-staffed .............. ~$3,000/month
  Est. annual overtime from vacancies ....... ~$30,000
  Rush jobs declined ........................ not quantified

APPRENTICESHIP (2 apprentices, illustrative)
  Starting apprentice wage .................. 60% of $30 = $18/hr
  Scheduled raises .......................... every 6 months to $30
  OJT reimbursement (if eligible) ........... per Valley RWB policy - TO CONFIRM
  RTI cost .................................. college welding course - TO PRICE
  Possible ITA support for RTI .............. TO CONFIRM eligibility

WHAT YOU GET
  Welders trained to your weld test
  Successors in place before your 2 retirements
  A pipeline you control instead of competing for the same 5 candidates
```

Notice what Jamal did not do: he did not promise a reimbursement amount before checking the trainees' eligibility and the board's policy.

## Presenting the case

- Lead with their problem: "You told me vacancies cost you about $30,000 a year in overtime."
- Show the cost comparison, then the wage path, then the funding.
- Label every estimate and every "to confirm."
- Stop and ask: "Does this match how you see it?" Let them correct the numbers.

## Practice

Use the Sunrise Senior Living discovery note from the previous lesson (or your own). Build a one-page business case with:

1. A "today" section using at least two cost numbers from the employer.
2. An illustrative wage progression for a CNA apprenticeship, clearly labeled.
3. A subsidy section listing each possible funding source, with "to confirm" and where you would confirm it (your board's OJT policy, your ITA policy, veterans staff).

Present it in three minutes to a partner playing Linh. Your partner should challenge one number. Practice saying, "That's your estimate, so let's adjust it," and recalculating on the spot.
