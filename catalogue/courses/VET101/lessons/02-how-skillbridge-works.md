## What SkillBridge is, in one sentence

DoD SkillBridge lets an active-duty service member spend up to the final 180 days of their service training, interning, or working in an apprenticeship-style role with a civilian employer, while the military keeps paying them.

That last part is what makes SkillBridge different from anything else you see at an AJCC. The participant is **still a service member**. They draw military pay and keep their military benefits. The employer (called the **provider**) supplies the training and the work experience, but does not pay their wages. When the SkillBridge period ends, the member separates or retires and becomes a civilian job seeker, ideally walking straight into a job with that same provider.

Keep this picture in mind for the running case in this course:

> **Staff Sergeant Andre Whitfield** has served 11 years as an Army logistics NCO. He plans to separate next summer. He has applied for a 120-day SkillBridge placement with **Coastline Freight Systems**, a fictional regional logistics company, to train as a warehouse operations supervisor.

## Three things every participant must have

You do not decide anyone's SkillBridge eligibility; the military does. But you need to explain the rules accurately enough that a participant, a family member, or an employer trusts what you say. Three requirements carry almost every conversation.

| Requirement | What it means | Who controls it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Eligibility** | The person is an active-duty service member who is separating or retiring, generally within the 180-day window. | Their Service and unit |
| **Duration** | Participation can last up to 180 days before separation. Many placements are shorter. | The provider's program design plus command approval |
| **Command approval** | The member's unit commander must approve participation in writing before they start. | The member's chain of command |

### Eligibility

SkillBridge is for service members who are on their way out of the military. Each military Service sets its own implementing rules on top of the DoD policy, including minimum time in service, how early a member can apply, and whether certain career fields or units can be released. Military spouses are not SkillBridge participants; if a spouse comes in asking about it, that is a different conversation (routing spouses is covered in VET 102).

### Duration

The ceiling is the final 180 days of service. Andre's 120-day placement fits inside it. What you should not say is "you get six months." Some members get much less because of their mission, their unit's staffing, or the provider program's length.

### Command approval

This is the gate most people underestimate. A member can be accepted by a provider and still not start, because their commander decides the mission cannot spare them. Approval is discretionary, it can come late, and it can be conditional. Encourage anyone you talk to about SkillBridge to have a plan B in place in case approval is delayed or denied.

## How a placement gets from idea to day one

1. The member explores opportunities, often with their installation's transition office.
2. The member applies to a provider's program and is accepted.
3. The member requests command approval through their unit's process.
4. Approval is granted; the member begins the placement on the agreed date.
5. The placement ends at or before separation; the member transitions to civilian status.

Your center can show up at almost any of these steps, which is why the next lessons matter.

## Saying it accurately

Use language that is true and hedged in the right places:

- **Say:** "SkillBridge can let you train with an employer during up to your last 180 days of service, if your command approves it."
- **Avoid:** "You qualify for SkillBridge." You cannot determine that.
- **Say:** "Your Service has its own rules on how early you can apply. Your transition office will know the current ones."
- **Avoid:** quoting a specific number of days in advance to apply, unless you have just checked the current Service guidance.

Where the exact rule matters, check the current DoD SkillBridge policy and the member's Service guidance (the official DoD SkillBridge website links both) rather than relying on memory or on this lesson.
