## The person in front of you does not say "SkillBridge"

SkillBridge participants rarely introduce themselves that way. They say "I'm getting out soon," or "I'm doing an internship with a company before I separate," or they mention an employer name you recognize from your regional sheet. If no one asks the right question, they get treated as a general walk-in, handed a flyer, and told to come back after they separate. That is the missed moment this lesson is about.

## Cues to listen and look for

None of these alone proves anything. Two or more together are worth a question.

| Cue | What you might hear or see |
| --- | --- |
| Still in service | "I'm active duty," "I'm on my way out," a military ID at sign-in |
| A separation date | "I get out in March," "my ETS date," "I start terminal leave soon" |
| A current civilian placement | "I'm training at [company] right now," "my internship ends in eight weeks" |
| No DD-214 yet | They have not received their separation document because they have not separated |
| A known provider name | An employer on your regional SkillBridge sheet |
| Transition office mention | "My TAP counselor told me to come here" |

## The first question

Once you notice cues, ask one open, respectful question. You are not screening them out; you are finding out where they are.

> "It sounds like you're still in the service. Are you doing any training or internship with an employer before you get out, something like SkillBridge?"

Then listen for three things:

1. **Status:** Are they in a SkillBridge placement now, approved and waiting to start, or just exploring?
2. **Timing:** When does the placement end, and when do they separate?
3. **Job offer:** Has the provider offered, or signalled, a job at the end?

## Worked example: Andre at intake

Andre comes into the north AJCC on a Tuesday afternoon in civilian clothes. He is six weeks from the end of his SkillBridge placement.

> **Front desk:** Welcome in. What brings you here today?
>
> **Andre:** I'm separating from the Army in about six weeks and want to know what's out there.
>
> **Front desk:** Thanks for your service. Are you doing any training with an employer before you get out, like SkillBridge?
>
> **Andre:** Yeah, I'm at Coastline Freight. They haven't said yet if there's a job at the end.
>
> **Front desk:** That's useful to know. Coastline is an employer we work with. Can I get you connected today with someone who works with transitioning service members, so you have options either way?

What went right: the staff member asked about SkillBridge directly, learned the timing and the job-offer status, and recognized the employer. They did not promise eligibility for any program and did not tell Andre to come back after he separates.

## Starting the right conversation, not finishing it

Your goal at the front desk is a warm handoff, not a full assessment. After you recognize a likely SkillBridge participant:

- Route them following your center's protocol for transitioning service members, which usually means JVSG staff and Title I staff working together. The detailed referral and eligibility rules are taught in VET 102.
- Do not tell them they are, or are not, eligible for Title I services. That is a determination for later, made with the right documentation.
- Write a short note so the next person does not start from zero.

**Sample intake note**

```text
Walk-in, [date]. Customer reports active duty, separating approx. [date].
Currently in SkillBridge placement with [provider] ([occupation]); placement ends approx. [date].
Job offer from provider: not yet known.
Interested in: employment options, possible training.
Action: warm handoff to [staff role] per center routing protocol.
```

Store and format notes according to your board's local policy and your case management system's rules.

## Practice

**Exercise: Three walk-ins.** For each person below, list the cues you notice, write the first question you would ask, and name who you would hand them to under your center's protocol.

1. A Navy petty officer says her TAP counselor sent her, and she starts training with a solar installer next month.
2. A man in his late thirties says he "just got out" last week, finished an internship at Sierra Line Electric, and did not get hired.
3. A woman asks whether her husband, who is in the Marines, can "do that program where they train at a company," and whether she can too.

**Role-play (live-virtual):** In pairs, run the Andre conversation, then swap roles using walk-in 2. The observer checks: did the staff member ask about timing and the job offer, and avoid promising eligibility?
