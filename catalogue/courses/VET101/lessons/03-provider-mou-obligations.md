## Why the provider agreement matters to you

An employer cannot simply decide to host service members. To become an authorized SkillBridge provider, an organization goes through DoD's approval process and signs a **memorandum of understanding (MOU)** that spells out what it promises. You are not a party to that agreement, and you do not enforce it. But once you know what providers have committed to, you can:

- explain to a participant what they should expect from their placement,
- notice when something a participant describes does not sound like SkillBridge, and
- talk credibly with employers in your region who already host participants.

This lesson covers what the obligations are. Helping an employer actually apply to become a provider is a separate skill, taught in VET 103.

## The obligations that matter at the front desk

The exact MOU wording is set by DoD and can change. Check the current provider requirements on the official DoD SkillBridge website before you quote anything specific. At the level frontline staff need, provider commitments fall into these groups:

| Obligation | Plain-language meaning | What it means for the participant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A real training program** | The provider delivers structured training, an internship, or an apprenticeship-style experience tied to an actual occupation. | They should be learning a defined role, not doing unrelated labor. |
| **No cost to the member** | The member does not pay for the training. | If someone is asking a participant for tuition, ask more questions. |
| **No provider wages during SkillBridge** | The member is paid by the military, not the provider, during the placement. | Their paycheck still comes from their Service. |
| **Respect the military's authority** | The member is still on active duty; their command can recall them and their Service's rules still apply. | A placement can end early for mission reasons. |
| **The post-service job-offer requirement** | Providers commit to offering employment, or a high likelihood of employment, at the end of the program. | This is the reason SkillBridge exists. Ask about it. |
| **Information and reporting** | Providers supply program and participant information DoD asks for. | The catalog cites a GAO finding that DoD lacks post-service employment data, so your center's records of what happens next can matter. |

## The post-service job-offer requirement, up close

This is the obligation you will reference most. SkillBridge is meant to end in a civilian job, and the provider agreement reflects that: the program is supposed to lead to employment, typically with the provider itself. In practice, not every participant receives or accepts an offer, and the strength of the commitment depends on the provider's program and the current MOU terms.

Consider Andre from Lesson 2. Coastline Freight Systems accepts him for a 120-day placement as a warehouse operations supervisor trainee. Three possible endings:

1. **Offer made and accepted.** Andre separates on Friday and starts as a Coastline employee on Monday. Your center may never see him, unless he wants help with something else.
2. **Offer made, Andre declines.** He decides he wants to relocate closer to family. He walks into your AJCC as a newly separated job seeker with fresh civilian experience.
3. **No offer.** Coastline's site closes a shift and cannot hire. Andre needs a job, possibly training, and quickly.

Endings 2 and 3 are why the catalog calls SkillBridge participants some of the best-prepared Dislocated Workers and apprentices a center will see. They arrive with recent, documented civilian training. Your job is to make sure that preparation is not wasted.

## When something does not sound right

You are not an investigator. But if a participant describes an arrangement that conflicts with the basics (they are being charged, they are being paid wages by the "provider," they have no defined training), do two things:

- Encourage them to raise it with their installation transition office or chain of command.
- Note the conversation in your system following your center's policy, without making accusations about the employer.

Your board's local policy decides whether and how staff escalate concerns about an employer partner. Check it.

## Practice

**Exercise: Read the placement.** For each statement a participant might say, mark it **Consistent**, **Ask more**, or **Refer**, and write one sentence explaining why.

1. "My company is teaching me their dispatch software and I shadow a supervisor every day."
2. "They're paying me an hourly wage on top of my military pay."
3. "They told me there's a job at the end, but nothing in writing yet."
4. "I paid a fee to get into the program."
5. "My unit pulled me back for two weeks and the company held my spot."

**Discussion prompt:** Of the three endings for Andre, which one is your center most likely to see? What would you want to know about him in the first five minutes?
