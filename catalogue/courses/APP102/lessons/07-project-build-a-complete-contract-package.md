## Goal

You will build a complete OJT contract package and cohort training plan for a sample apprenticeship. The package must be good enough that your supervisor could approve it and a monitor could follow every number in it back to a document.

You will not learn anything new in this project. You will use every skill from the workshop on one realistic case: justifying a reimbursement rate, building a cohort plan, setting hours from the work process schedule, splitting OJT and ITA funds without overlap, and setting up the records, invoices, and monitoring that let the contract close cleanly.

Work alone or in pairs. Plan on about two and a half hours, including a short peer review at the end.

## The case

**The board.** Valley Regional Workforce Board (fictional). Use the fictional local policy below unless your facilitator tells you to use your own board's policy. If you use your own, note every place it differs.

**The employer.** Harbor Point Heating and Air (fictional), a residential and light commercial HVAC contractor with 18 employees on its most recent payroll report. Harbor Point sponsors a registered HVAC technician apprenticeship. It has two journeyworker technicians who serve as mentors.

**The apprentices.** Harbor Point wants to start three first-year apprentices this spring. All three have been found WIOA-eligible as dislocated workers or adults; enrollment dates are in the case files.

| Apprentice | Background | Planned start | Notes from skill gap assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tomas | Laid off from a retail warehouse after eight years | April 7 | No HVAC or electrical experience |
| Keisha | Two years as a residential electrician's helper | April 7 | Meets the "electrical fundamentals" work process; mentor confirms |
| Wen | Recently released from incarceration; completed an HVAC pre-apprenticeship course while inside | April 28 | Meets about half of "safety and tool use"; no field experience |

**The registered standards (fictional excerpt).**

- Starting wage: $20.00 an hour.
- Wage step: $21.50 an hour after 1,000 hours of employment.
- Work week: 40 hours.
- RTI: Bayshore Community College (fictional), one evening a week, three hours, paid by Harbor Point at the apprentice's hourly wage.

**First-year work process schedule (fictional).**

| Work process | Schedule hours |
| --- | --- |
| Safety and tool use | 160 |
| Electrical fundamentals | 320 |
| Refrigeration cycle and gauges | 360 |
| Residential system installation | 480 |
| Service calls with a mentor | 400 |
| **First-year total** | **1,720** |

**RTI cost (fictional).** First-year tuition and fees at Bayshore are $2,800 per apprentice. Books and a required tool kit are $500. Harbor Point's apprenticeship committee pays $700 of tuition per apprentice. Bayshore's apprenticeship program is listed on the ETPL (confirm in CalJOBS in a real case). Keisha has a Pell Grant award letter for the fall term only; the others have not applied.

**Fictional Valley Regional OJT and ITA policy (for this exercise only).**

- OJT default rate 50 percent; up to 75 percent with manager approval based on documented 680.730 factors.
- Small-employer rate up to 90 percent only where current state guidance permits and the employer meets its size condition. Staff must cite the WSD25-03 section and the date checked.
- OJT cap: $18,000 per trainee.
- OJT duration: maximum of 1,200 hours for occupations in this complexity band (local SVP-based table).
- One master contract with individual addenda is allowed.
- Invoices monthly, per trainee. Monitoring: at least a start-up visit, a midpoint visit, and a closeout visit.
- ITA cap: $4,000 per participant; books and required tools are allowable ITA costs. Other grant assistance must be applied before the ITA amount is set.

## Requirements

Your package must contain all of the following. Label each part with the letter below.

**A. Rate justification memos (one per apprentice).**
- One page each.
- Cite the local policy section, what you checked in WSD25-03 (with a placeholder for the section and the date), and at least three 680.730 factors with the document that supports each.
- State the rate, the reason, and who must approve.
- Decide whether the 90 percent rate is available. If you cannot confirm the condition from the directive text, say so and explain what you would check.

**B. Cohort training plan.**
- A shared core with at least eight fields: employer, program, occupation, OJT work processes, training methods, mentors and ratio, progress evaluation schedule, invoicing schedule, and records kept.
- Three individual addenda with start date, starting wage, credit for prior skills, OJT hours by work process, wage step periods, rate, and maximum reimbursement.
- A clause that lets one addendum be added, ended, or modified without reopening the others.

**C. Hours and duration worksheet (one per apprentice).**
- A table showing schedule hours, credit, and OJT hours by work process.
- The lower-of calculation against the 1,200-hour ceiling.
- Hours laid out by wage step period, and the reimbursement calculated per period.
- A start date, an end date, and a one-paragraph justification.

**D. OJT and ITA funding summary (one per apprentice).**
- A split table showing who pays each cost.
- The ITA amount, showing the committee's share and any Pell adjustment.
- Written answers to the three overlap tests (same hour, same cost, same activity).
- The sentence in the training plan that excludes RTI hours from OJT hours.

**E. Records, invoicing, and monitoring plan.**
- The records clause for the contract.
- A blank invoice template with every required field.
- A monitoring calendar for the cohort, with the purpose of each visit and the questions you will ask each apprentice privately.
- A closeout checklist for one addendum and for the master contract.

**F. Cohort summary and case note.**
- A one-page summary table: each apprentice's hours, rate, maximum reimbursement, ITA amount, and OJT end date, plus the total cohort obligation.
- A sample case note recording that the package was drafted and sent for approval.

## Constraints

- Use only the case facts above and your board's policy (if your facilitator allows it). Where you need a fact you do not have, write a placeholder like [section of WSD25-03] or [date confirmed in CalJOBS]. Do not invent directive numbers, section numbers, or thresholds.
- Every number in your package must be traceable to a case fact, a policy rule, or a calculation you show.
- Each apprentice's hours, rate, and reimbursement must be calculated individually. No pooled lines.
- OJT hours are worksite hours only. RTI hours never appear on an OJT invoice.
- The OJT period cannot run longer than the training need justifies, even if it would cover a wage step.
- No real names or real case data. Use the fictional people above.
- Keep each memo to one page and the cohort summary to one page.

## Definition of done

Your package is done when you can answer "yes" to every line:

- [ ] Parts A through F are all present and labeled.
- [ ] Each rate memo names the local policy section, the directive check, at least three factors with evidence, the rate, and the approver.
- [ ] Every addendum's hours equal the lower of (remaining schedule hours after credit) and the 1,200-hour ceiling, unless you explain a deliberate choice to use fewer.
- [ ] Every reimbursement calculation uses the wage in effect for each period and respects the $18,000 cap.
- [ ] Every ITA amount is net of the committee's share and accounts for Keisha's Pell award, and stays within the ITA cap.
- [ ] All three overlap tests are answered in writing for each apprentice.
- [ ] Wen's later start date is reflected in her addendum, her invoices, and the monitoring calendar.
- [ ] The invoice template lets a fiscal reviewer check every number against an attachment.
- [ ] The closeout checklist covers final invoice, de-obligation, final evaluation, work process log, retention status, and case file updates.
- [ ] A peer has reviewed the package and could not find a number they could not trace.

## Hints

- **Start with hours, not money.** Hours drive the reimbursement, the end date, the invoices, and the monitoring calendar. Get Part C right for all three apprentices first.
- **Tomas.** With no credit, his remaining first-year hours are well above the ceiling. Decide which work processes the 1,200 hours cover, and remember the wage step falls inside his OJT period.
- **Keisha.** Her electrical fundamentals credit removes 320 hours. Check whether she still hits the ceiling. Her rate memo may support a lower rate than Tomas's; let the factors decide.
- **Wen.** Her pre-apprenticeship course is evidence for both the skill gap credit and the participant-characteristics factor. Be specific about which document shows each.
- **Check the cap early.** At $20.00 an hour, work out how many hours at 75 percent reach $18,000. That tells you whether the rate or the cap actually limits each apprentice.
- **Employer size.** Harbor Point has 18 employees. That is a fact for the employer-size factor regardless of whether the 90 percent rate is available. Keep the two questions separate in your memo.
- **The ITA math.** Tuition minus the committee's share, plus books and tools, is the starting ITA amount. Then apply other grant assistance as the policy requires. Keisha's Pell award covers one term only; say how you will handle the term it does not cover.
- **Class nights.** Harbor Point pays apprentices for class time. Make sure the timesheet in your records clause has a separate line for it.
- **Peer review.** Hand your partner the cohort summary only and ask them to find the source of three random numbers in the rest of the package. If they cannot, fix the trail.
