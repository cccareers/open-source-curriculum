## The problem a cohort plan solves

Apprenticeship employers rarely hire one apprentice at a time. A contractor brings on four first-year electricians every spring. A hospital system starts six medical assistant apprentices in one class. If you write a separate OJT contract and a separate training plan for each person, you produce a stack of nearly identical paperwork, and the employer signs the same terms six times. When one apprentice leaves, nobody is sure which document to amend.

A **cohort training plan** puts multiple apprentices under one contract with one shared training design, while keeping everything that must be individual exactly that: individual. Done well, it cuts the employer's paperwork and makes monitoring easier. Done badly, it hides the fact that each apprentice has a different starting point, a different start date, and a different reimbursement amount.

First, check one thing: **does your board's local OJT policy allow more than one trainee under a single contract?** Some boards allow it, some require a master agreement plus individual addenda, and some require separate contracts. The structure below works in all three cases. Only the label on the paper changes.

## Shared core, individual addenda

Think of the cohort plan as two layers.

**Layer 1: the shared core.** This is written once and applies to everyone in the cohort.

- Employer, worksite, and the registered apprenticeship program (occupation and program identifiers from the registered standards)
- Job title and job description
- The work processes the OJT covers, taken from the work process schedule
- Training methods (for example, mentor demonstration, supervised practice, sign-off)
- Supervisors and journeyworker mentors, with the mentor-to-apprentice ratio the standards require
- Reimbursement method and invoicing schedule
- Records the employer will keep
- How the employer will evaluate progress, and how often

**Layer 2: the individual addendum.** One per apprentice, attached to the core.

- Name, WIOA eligibility date, and program enrollment (the case file reference)
- Start date and wage at start
- Skill gap result: which work processes this apprentice already has skills in, and which they need
- Training hours this apprentice will receive under the OJT, by work process
- Wage steps expected during the contract period
- Reimbursement rate (with a pointer to the rate memo) and maximum reimbursement amount
- Funding obligation for this apprentice

The rule of thumb: if it would be the same for every apprentice, it belongs in the core. If a monitor would need it to check one apprentice's invoice, it belongs in the addendum.

## Things that must stay individual

Some items look shared but are not. Treat these as individual every time.

| Item | Why it cannot be pooled |
| --- | --- |
| Eligibility | Each apprentice must be WIOA-eligible and enrolled before their OJT starts |
| Skill gap | Prior experience shortens or changes one person's training, not the cohort's |
| Training hours | Follow from the individual skill gap |
| Rate and its justification | The 680.730 factors include participant characteristics, which differ |
| Obligation and cap | Most board caps are per trainee |
| Start and end dates | Apprentices often start weeks apart |

A common error is a cohort plan that says "4 apprentices x 1,000 hours x $16.50 = $66,000." That single line hides four different gap results and four different start dates. It also invites the employer to invoice pooled hours that no single apprentice actually worked.

## Worked example: Granite Line Electric

**The case.** Granite Line Electric (fictional) is a 45-person electrical contractor with a registered inside wireman apprenticeship. Valley Regional Workforce Board (fictional) has agreed to support four first-year apprentices with OJT. Your fictional local policy allows one master contract with individual addenda.

**Shared core (excerpt).**

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Occupation | Inside wireman (per registered standards) |
| OJT work processes | Safety and tool use; conduit bending and installation; residential rough-in; commercial wiring fundamentals |
| Training method | Journeyworker demonstration, supervised practice, weekly mentor sign-off on the apprentice's work process log |
| Mentor ratio | As required by the registered standards (cite the section) |
| Progress evaluation | Written evaluation by the mentor at the midpoint and at the end of each apprentice's OJT period |
| Invoicing | Monthly, one invoice per apprentice, attached to timesheets and payroll records |

**Individual addenda (summary).**

| Apprentice | Start date | Starting wage | Prior skills | OJT hours | Rate | Max reimbursement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Marcus | March 3 | $21.00 | None | 1,000 | 75% | $15,750 |
| Priya | March 3 | $21.00 | Two years as an electrician's helper; safety and tool use already met | 800 | 50% | $8,400 |
| Daniel | March 17 | $21.00 | None | 1,000 | 75% | $15,750 |
| Rosa | April 7 | $21.00 | Construction laborer; safety and tool use already met | 880 | 75% | $13,860 |

The arithmetic checks: Marcus is 1,000 x $21.00 x 0.75 = $15,750. Priya is 800 x $21.00 x 0.50 = $8,400. Rosa is 880 x $21.00 x 0.75 = $13,860. These assume no wage step during the OJT period; if the standards put a step inside the period, calculate each period at its own wage. (The next lesson covers how to set the hours themselves.)

Look at what the addenda show that a pooled line would hide. Priya needs fewer hours because of prior experience, and her rate is lower because her rate memo found fewer factors supporting an increase. Rosa starts five weeks later, so her end date and her invoices run later than everyone else's.

**Sample case note for the cohort file.**

> 02/20 — Cohort OJT master contract drafted with Granite Line Electric for four first-year inside wireman apprentices. Shared core lists four OJT work processes from the registered work process schedule. Individual addenda prepared for Marcus, Priya, Daniel, and Rosa; each references that apprentice's eligibility determination, skill gap assessment, and rate memo. Daniel's and Rosa's addenda carry later start dates; neither may start OJT before enrollment is complete. Total cohort obligation is the sum of four individual maximums. Sent to supervisor for approval.

## When the cohort changes

Cohorts change. Plan for it in the contract language now.

- **Adding an apprentice.** Add a new individual addendum. The shared core does not change. Confirm eligibility, complete a skill gap assessment, write a rate memo, and get approval before the start date. If the new addendum raises the total obligation, your board may require a contract modification.
- **An apprentice leaves.** Close that apprentice's addendum: final timesheet, final invoice, a note on the reason for separation, and de-obligation of the unused amount. The other addenda continue unchanged.
- **The employer changes a work process or mentor.** That is a change to the shared core and affects everyone. Document it as a modification and tell every apprentice's case manager.

Write a sentence in the core that says each addendum can be added, ended, or modified without reopening the others. It saves a lot of paperwork later.

## Discussion prompt

Think about the last time an OJT employer asked to train more than one person. What did your office do? Separate contracts? One contract with a list of names? What went wrong, or what would have gone wrong if one trainee had left after a month?

## Practice

**Exercise 1: Sort the fields.** Below is a list of contract fields. Mark each one **Core** or **Addendum**, and write one line explaining your choice.

- Employer's federal employer identification number
- Apprentice's starting wage
- Mentor's name
- Work process hours already credited from prior experience
- Monthly invoicing schedule
- Reimbursement rate
- Registered program identifier
- Apprentice's planned end date

**Exercise 2: Build a cohort plan.** Bayview Medical Group (fictional) will start three medical assistant apprentices. Two start on June 2; one starts on June 23. One of the June 2 apprentices has a year of front-office experience and has already met the patient intake work process.

1. Draft the shared core in a table with at least six fields.
2. Draft three individual addenda. Use placeholders for wages and hours where you would need the employer's data, such as [starting wage from standards].
3. Write the clause that lets one addendum be ended without reopening the others.
4. Write a short case note like the one above.

**Exercise 3: Find the hidden pooling.** A colleague's draft says: "Employer will be reimbursed 75 percent of wages for up to 3,000 combined training hours for the three apprentices." Rewrite the sentence so that every number can be checked against one apprentice's records.
