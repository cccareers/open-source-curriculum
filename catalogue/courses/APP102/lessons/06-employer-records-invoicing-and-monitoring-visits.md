## Closing starts on day one

Most OJT contracts that end badly do not fail at the end. They fail in week two, when nobody told the employer which records to keep, and the first invoice arrives with a total and no timesheets. By the time you try to close the contract, the missing records cannot be rebuilt.

A contract closes cleanly when three things were set up before the first day of training:

1. **Records** the employer keeps, named in the contract.
2. **Invoices** in a set format, on a set schedule, with set attachments.
3. **Monitoring visits** on a calendar, each with a purpose.

Your job is to write these into the contract package so the employer knows exactly what you will ask for. Your board's local OJT policy and your fiscal unit set the specific forms, the invoicing frequency, and the minimum monitoring schedule. Get those first and fill them in below.

## Records the employer keeps

Name these in the contract. For a cohort contract, the employer keeps them **per apprentice**.

| Record | What it shows | Notes for apprenticeship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signed contract, training plan, and addendum | What was agreed | Keep the version in force on each date |
| Timesheets | Hours actually worked | Separate worksite hours from RTI class hours |
| Payroll records | Wages actually paid | Must show the wage step date when it happens |
| Work process log | Training actually delivered | The apprentice's log signed by the mentor; matches the OJT plan's work processes |
| Progress evaluations | Skill gains | Midpoint and final, signed by the mentor or supervisor |
| Notices of changes | Separation, schedule change, mentor change | Dated, sent to the case manager promptly |

The work process log is the apprenticeship advantage. Sponsors already track hours by work process for the registration agency. Ask for a copy for the OJT period; it is the strongest proof that the hours on your invoice were training, not just work.

Tell the employer how long to keep records. Your board's contract terms set the retention period. Do not guess it in the contract; copy the clause your fiscal unit uses.

## Invoices that can be paid without a phone call

An invoice should let a fiscal reviewer check every number against an attachment.

**Every invoice, per apprentice, should show:**

- contract number and addendum (apprentice) reference;
- invoice period dates;
- worksite OJT hours in the period, by wage rate if a wage step fell in the period;
- the wage rate and the reimbursement rate;
- the amount claimed for the period;
- the running total claimed to date and the remaining balance under the maximum;
- employer signature and date.

**Attach:** the timesheets and payroll records for the period, and the work process log pages for the period.

**Sample invoice (fictional).**

| Field | Entry |
| --- | --- |
| Contract / addendum | SF-OJT-[number] / Addendum 1, Andre T. |
| Period | [start date] to [end date], 4 weeks |
| OJT worksite hours | 160 at $22.00 |
| Reimbursement rate | 75 percent |
| Amount claimed | 160 x $16.50 = $2,640.00 |
| Claimed to date | $7,920.00 |
| Remaining under maximum | $15,000.00 - $7,920.00 = $7,080.00 |
| Attachments | Timesheets (worksite and RTI lines), payroll register, work process log pages |

**What you check before approving:**

- Hours on the invoice match worksite hours on the timesheets, not total paid hours.
- The wage on the invoice matches payroll, including any step.
- The work process log shows training in the processes listed in the OJT plan.
- The running total does not exceed the maximum, and the period is inside the contract dates.
- The apprentice was still employed for the whole period.

If anything fails, send the invoice back with a short written note. Do not fix it yourself by changing the numbers.

## Monitoring visits

Plan the visits when you write the contract and put the dates on your calendar. Your board's policy sets the minimum. A practical pattern for an apprenticeship OJT looks like this:

| Visit | When | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-award | Before the contract is signed | Confirm the worksite, mentor, and recordkeeping can support the plan |
| Start-up | In the first weeks | Check that timesheets separate RTI hours and the work process log is being kept |
| Midpoint | Around the halfway point | Review the midpoint evaluation; talk privately with the apprentice and the mentor |
| Closeout | At or just after the end date | Final evaluation, final records, retention status |

At each visit, talk with the apprentice separately from the supervisor. Ask: Are you doing the work in your training plan? Who trains you? Are your hours and pay right? These three questions find most problems early.

**Sample monitoring note.**

> 05/14 — Midpoint monitoring visit, Delta Precision Machining. Met with mentor and, separately, with Andre. Andre reports training in lathe and mill operation as planned; confirms pay at $22.00 and that Thursday class hours are recorded separately. Mentor's midpoint evaluation shows progress in blueprint reading and lathe setup; mill work behind schedule due to machine downtime. Reviewed work process log, consistent with invoices 1 and 2. No findings. Next visit: closeout, week of [date].

## Closing the contract

Closeout is a short checklist, not a project, if the records were set up on day one.

- [ ] Final invoice received, reviewed, and paid.
- [ ] Total paid is within the maximum; any unused balance is de-obligated.
- [ ] Final progress evaluation signed by the mentor.
- [ ] Work process log for the OJT period on file.
- [ ] Apprentice's employment status at the end of OJT recorded (still employed, wage, and whether they remain in the apprenticeship).
- [ ] Any early separation documented with the reason and date.
- [ ] Closeout monitoring note in the case file.
- [ ] Case notes and CalJOBS entries updated to show the OJT end date and outcome.

For a cohort contract, close each apprentice's addendum when that apprentice's OJT ends, and close the master contract when the last addendum closes.
