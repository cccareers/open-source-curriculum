## Two halves of one apprenticeship

A Registered Apprenticeship has two training components:

- **On-the-job learning** at the worksite, organized by the work process schedule and supervised by a journeyworker mentor.
- **Related technical instruction (RTI)**, the classroom or online instruction the standards require alongside the job, delivered by a college, a training center, or the sponsor itself.

WIOA Title I has a tool for each. **OJT** reimburses the employer for part of the apprentice's wage while they learn on the job. An **Individual Training Account (ITA)** pays a training provider on the Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL) for instruction. Registered Apprenticeship programs can be placed on the ETPL, which is what lets an ITA pay for RTI.

Using both for one apprentice is allowed and often smart. The risk is **overlap**: paying twice for the same hour, the same cost, or the same activity. Overlap is a disallowed cost and is easy to find in a monitoring review, because the records sit side by side in the same case file.

## The three overlap tests

Before you obligate either fund, run every planned cost through three tests.

| Test | Question | Example of overlap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Same hour | Is any hour counted on the OJT invoice also an RTI class hour? | Employer pays the apprentice for Thursday evening class and puts those hours on the OJT timesheet |
| Same cost | Is any cost paid by the ITA also paid by another source? | ITA pays tuition the sponsor's training trust already covers, or that a Pell Grant already covers |
| Same activity | Is any training activity listed in both the OJT training plan and the RTI curriculum? | The OJT plan lists "blueprint reading" as on-the-job training while the college course of the same name is paid by the ITA |

If you can answer "no" to all three with documents, you have a clean split.

## Building the split

Work from the apprenticeship's own structure. Do not invent a split that the program does not follow.

1. **Get the RTI plan.** From the sponsor or the RTI provider: course names, hours, schedule, cost, and what the cost includes (tuition, fees, books, tools).
2. **Confirm the provider is on the ETPL** for the program you intend to fund. If it is not, an ITA cannot pay for it. Check CalJOBS for the current listing.
3. **Find out who already pays what.** Ask the sponsor whether the employer, a joint training fund, a scholarship, or the apprentice already covers any RTI costs. WIOA expects you to account for other grant assistance, such as a Pell Grant, when setting the ITA amount. Your board's policy will say how.
4. **Write the OJT hours as worksite hours only.** The OJT training plan lists work processes, not classes. State in the plan that RTI hours are excluded from OJT hours and invoices, even if the employer pays the apprentice for class time.
5. **Set the ITA amount** for the RTI costs that remain after other sources, within your board's ITA cap.
6. **Line up the timelines.** Show when OJT runs and when each RTI term runs. They usually overlap in calendar time; that is fine. They must not overlap in hours or costs.
7. **Record both in the case file** with one funding summary that shows the split.

Co-enrollment and program coding for these services are covered in a separate course. Here, focus on getting the split right on paper.

## Worked example: Andre at Delta Precision Machining

**What the program looks like (fictional).** Andre works 40 hours a week at Delta. The registered standards require RTI at Foothill Technical College (fictional), one evening a week, three hours a session, for two semesters in the first year. Delta pays apprentices their hourly wage for class time.

**What the RTI costs.** The college's first-year cost is $2,400 in tuition and fees plus $350 for books. Delta's apprenticeship committee pays $600 of tuition per apprentice. Andre has applied for a Pell Grant, which has not been awarded yet.

**The split.**

| Cost | Paid by | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wages for 1,040 worksite hours (work processes in the OJT plan) | Employer, with OJT reimbursement under the contract | RTI class hours excluded |
| Wages for RTI class hours | Employer, no WIOA reimbursement | Not OJT; not on the OJT invoice |
| Tuition and fees, $2,400 | $600 apprenticeship committee; $1,800 ITA | ITA reduced by the committee's share |
| Books, $350 | ITA, if your board's ITA policy covers books | Confirm local policy |
| Pell Grant, if awarded | Applied as your board's policy requires | ITA amount revisited when the award is known |

So the ITA is $1,800 for tuition and fees, plus $350 for books if your policy allows it: $2,150 at most, before any Pell adjustment.

**The overlap tests, answered.**

- **Same hour:** No. Delta's timesheets show worksite hours and class hours on separate lines. Only worksite hours go on the OJT invoice.
- **Same cost:** No. The ITA covers only tuition the committee does not pay. The Pell award, if any, will be applied when known.
- **Same activity:** No. The OJT plan lists blueprint reading as a worksite activity (reading job prints at the machine); the college course teaches drafting standards. The plan says so in one line, so a monitor does not have to guess.

**Sample funding summary for the case file.**

> **Funding summary — Andre T. (fictional), machinist apprentice, Delta Precision Machining**
>
> OJT: master contract [number], individual addendum. 1,040 worksite hours, 75 percent, obligation $15,000 (per-trainee cap). OJT invoices include worksite hours only; RTI class hours excluded per training plan section [x].
>
> ITA: Foothill Technical College, first-year RTI, ETPL listing confirmed in CalJOBS on [date]. Tuition and fees $2,400 less $600 paid by apprenticeship committee = $1,800. Books $350 [approved/not approved under local ITA policy]. Pell application pending; ITA to be reviewed on award.
>
> Overlap review completed [date] by [name]: same hour, same cost, and same activity tests passed. Supporting documents: RTI schedule, college cost sheet, committee payment letter, Delta timesheet template.

## Red flags

Stop and fix the plan if you see any of these:

- an OJT timesheet template with no separate line for class hours;
- an ITA amount equal to the full tuition when someone else is known to pay part of it;
- an OJT training plan that lists course titles from the RTI curriculum;
- an RTI provider you have not confirmed on the ETPL;
- a funding summary that lives only in your head or your email.

## Discussion prompt

In your office, are OJT and ITA usually handled by different staff? If so, who would notice an overlap, and when? What one change in your office's process would catch it before the contract is signed?
