## The mismatch you have to resolve

A typical OJT contract runs a few months. A Registered Apprenticeship can run several years, with thousands of hours of on-the-job learning. Washington's 2026 evaluation, as cited in CCC's course catalog, names OJT rules that do not fit apprenticeship timelines as a design gap. You will meet that gap every time you write one of these contracts.

You cannot fix it by stretching the OJT to cover the whole apprenticeship. WIOA defines OJT as training that is limited in duration as appropriate to the occupation, taking into account the content of the training, the participant's prior work experience, and the participant's service strategy. That is the standard you have to meet and document.

So the real question is: **which part of the apprenticeship does the OJT cover, and how many hours is that for this apprentice?** This lesson gives you a method to answer it from two sources you can defend: how complex the occupation is, and the apprenticeship's own work process schedule.

## Source 1: occupation complexity

Most boards use an outside measure of how long it takes to learn an occupation as a starting point. A common choice is the Specific Vocational Preparation (SVP) rating listed for the occupation in O*NET, which describes how much preparation a typical worker needs. Some boards publish a table that converts SVP levels into maximum OJT hours. Others use a fixed maximum for all OJT, or a maximum set by occupation group.

Your board's local policy sets which measure you use and what maximum it produces. Before you do anything else, write down:

- the measure your board uses (SVP, a local table, or a flat maximum);
- the maximum OJT hours it produces for this occupation;
- the policy section that says so.

That maximum is a ceiling. It is not the number you put in the contract.

## Source 2: the work process schedule

The registered standards include a work process schedule. It lists the major skill areas (work processes) the apprentice must learn on the job, with approximate hours for each. This schedule is your best evidence for what "training" means at this employer, because the employer has already committed to it.

Use it in three steps.

1. **Pick the work processes the OJT will cover.** Usually these are the early, foundational ones: the period when the apprentice is least productive and the employer's training cost is highest. That is the cost OJT is meant to offset.
2. **Subtract what the apprentice already knows.** Use the skill gap assessment. If the apprentice already meets a work process, or the program grants credit for prior experience, those hours come out.
3. **Compare with the ceiling.** Take the lower of (a) the hours left after step 2, and (b) the maximum from your board's policy.

## Where wage steps come in

Apprentices move up a progressive wage schedule, usually at set hour or time intervals in the standards. Your contract hours often cross one of those steps. That matters for two reasons:

- the reimbursement per hour changes at the step, so the budget must be calculated period by period;
- an employer may want the OJT to run long enough to cover a raise. That is not a training reason. The duration has to come from the skill gap, not from the wage schedule.

Lay the hours out by wage step period in the training plan so that the budget and the invoices line up with it.

## Worked example: Delta Precision Machining

**The case.** Andre (from the earlier lesson) is a first-year machinist apprentice at Delta Precision Machining (fictional). The registered standards show a multi-year program. The first year of the work process schedule (simplified and fictional for this exercise) looks like this:

| Work process | Schedule hours |
| --- | --- |
| Shop safety and measurement | 200 |
| Blueprint reading and layout | 240 |
| Manual lathe and mill operation | 480 |
| Introductory CNC setup | 400 |
| Inspection and quality basics | 160 |
| **First-year total** | **1,480** |

**Step 1: pick the work processes.** The case manager and the employer agree that the OJT should cover the foundational first-year processes, when Andre is least productive. All five are in.

**Step 2: subtract what Andre knows.** Andre's skill gap assessment shows he already uses precision measuring tools from a previous warehouse quality-check role. The employer's mentor confirms he meets about half of "Shop safety and measurement." Remove 100 hours. Remaining: 1,380.

**Step 3: compare with the ceiling.** The fictional Sierra Foothills policy uses a local table based on SVP. For this occupation it allows a maximum of 1,040 OJT hours. The lower number wins: **1,040 hours.**

Now decide which 1,040. The case manager writes the plan to cover the first four processes in order, dropping the last part of introductory CNC setup and all of inspection, which will happen later in the apprenticeship without OJT reimbursement.

| Work process | Schedule hours | Credit | OJT hours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Shop safety and measurement | 200 | 100 | 100 |
| Blueprint reading and layout | 240 | 0 | 240 |
| Manual lathe and mill operation | 480 | 0 | 480 |
| Introductory CNC setup | 400 | 0 | 220 |
| Inspection and quality basics | 160 | 0 | 0 |
| **Total** | | | **1,040** |

**Wage steps.** Delta's standards (fictional) raise the wage from $22.00 to $23.50 after 1,000 hours of employment. Andre's OJT runs 1,040 hours, so 1,000 hours are at $22.00 and 40 at $23.50. At 75 percent, the reimbursement is (1,000 x $16.50) + (40 x $17.625) = $16,500 + $705 = $17,205, which is above the fictional $15,000 cap. The obligation is the cap. The case manager notes that the cap is reached during the first wage period, so invoices after that point will be for $0.

**Duration.** At 40 hours a week, 1,040 hours is 26 weeks. Andre also attends related technical instruction one evening a week, which is not counted in the OJT hours (that is covered in the next lesson). The contract runs 26 weeks from his start date, with a stated end date.

**Sample justification paragraph for the training plan.**

> OJT hours were set at 1,040, the lower of (a) the 1,380 hours remaining in the first-year work process schedule after credit for prior measurement skills, and (b) the 1,040-hour maximum in the Sierra Foothills OJT policy for this occupation [cite section]. Covered processes are the foundational first-year processes in the registered work process schedule, during which the apprentice's productivity is lowest. Inspection and the remainder of introductory CNC setup fall outside the OJT period. Duration: 26 weeks at 40 hours per week.

## Checklist for setting hours

- [ ] I recorded my board's measure of occupation complexity and the ceiling it produces, with the policy section.
- [ ] I attached the work process schedule from the registered standards.
- [ ] I listed the work processes the OJT covers and why those ones.
- [ ] I subtracted credit for prior experience, with evidence from the skill gap assessment.
- [ ] I used the lower of the remaining hours and the ceiling.
- [ ] I laid out hours by wage step period.
- [ ] I stated a start date, an end date, and the weekly schedule.
- [ ] I excluded related technical instruction hours from OJT hours.

## Discussion prompt

Your employer says, "Every apprentice needs the full first year. Why are you cutting hours for someone who has done this before?" How do you explain the duration rule in one or two sentences without sounding like you are reading a regulation?

## Practice

**Exercise 1: Set the hours.** Lena is a medical assistant apprentice at Riverside Family Clinic (fictional). The first-year work process schedule (fictional) is:

| Work process | Schedule hours |
| --- | --- |
| Patient intake and vital signs | 300 |
| Clinical procedures support | 500 |
| Medical records and scheduling | 250 |
| Infection control | 150 |

Lena worked two years at a front desk and already meets medical records and scheduling. Your fictional board's ceiling for this occupation is 800 hours.

1. Calculate the remaining hours after credit.
2. Apply the ceiling.
3. Choose which work processes the OJT covers and build a table like the one above.
4. Write a justification paragraph.

**Exercise 2: Add the wage step.** The clinic's standards (fictional) raise Lena's wage from $19.50 to $20.50 after 500 hours. Lay out her OJT hours by wage period and calculate the reimbursement at 75 percent.

**Exercise 3: Use your own board.** Find your board's OJT policy section on training duration. Write down the measure it uses and the maximum it would produce for one apprenticeship occupation your region supports. If your policy is silent, write the question you would bring to your supervisor.
