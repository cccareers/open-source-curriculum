## Why the words matter

A partnership that exists only in meetings does not survive staff turnover, a funding cycle, or a new dean. The written language in your local plan and in grant applications is what makes the partnership official. Reviewers read it to answer one question: do these partners actually have defined, credible commitments, or is this a list of logos?

You will most often write this language in two places:

- **A local plan modification.** WIOA local plans are revisited on a regular cycle, and that cycle is your chance to name degree-linked apprenticeship as part of the board's strategy. Check the current state local plan guidance for the required sections, page limits, and deadlines, and follow its headings exactly.
- **A grant application.** A CAI application, a Regional Coordination Project proposal under WSIN25-21, or another solicitation will usually ask for a partnership narrative and letters of commitment. Follow that solicitation's instructions over any template here.

## The five elements of strong partnership language

| Element | What to write | Weak version | Strong version |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Each partner by name and role | "Local partners will collaborate." | "Mesa Valley Health (employer), Granite Peak Community College (education partner), and Valley Regional WDB (convener) ..." |
| Commitment | What each partner will do, with numbers where they exist | "The employer supports apprenticeship." | "Mesa Valley Health will hire six apprentices in the first cohort and assign a trained journey-level mentor to each." |
| Alignment | How work and learning connect | "Training will lead to a degree." | "Granite Peak faculty will review a work-process crosswalk to identify which tasks can earn credit through existing courses or credit for prior learning." |
| Governance | How decisions are made and problems resolved | Nothing | "A partner team meets monthly; the board convenes and keeps the shared timeline." |
| Accountability | What will be measured and reported | "The program will be successful." | "Partners will report enrollments, retention at 12 months, and credits earned, using each partner's existing data systems." |

Two cautions:

- **Write commitments partners have actually made.** Get sign-off from each partner on their sentence before you submit. A college paragraph that promises credit before faculty have reviewed the crosswalk is a problem waiting to happen.
- **Do not overstate the field.** California's apprenticeship-degree landscape is early. Describe your partnership as building toward a degree pathway, and name the specific college approval step it depends on.

## Worked example: local plan modification paragraph

Valley Regional's draft, for the section on career pathways and work-based learning:

> Valley Regional Workforce Board will convene employers, Granite Peak Community College, and apprenticeship sponsors to develop degree-linked registered apprenticeships in high-demand occupations, beginning with medical assisting. Mesa Valley Health has committed to hire a first cohort of six apprentices. Granite Peak faculty will review a crosswalk of the program's work processes to the college's medical assisting course outcomes and determine, through the college's curriculum and credit for prior learning processes, which learning can earn college credit. The board will recruit and screen WIOA-eligible participants through its AJCCs, provide supportive services and training funds consistent with local policy, and coordinate development funding with regional partners. The partner team will meet monthly and report enrollment, 12-month retention, and credits earned.

Notice what it does not do: it does not promise a degree, cite a funding amount it has not secured, or claim the program is already registered.

## Grant narrative and commitment letters

A grant partnership section usually adds two things:

1. **A roles-and-resources table**, often the partner map from the first lesson, with a column for what each partner contributes (cash, in-kind staff time, facilities, data).
2. **Letters of commitment** that repeat, in the partner's own voice, the same commitments in the narrative. Reviewers compare them. A letter that says "we support this effort" when the narrative says "we will hire six apprentices" weakens the application.

A short commitment-letter checklist:

- Signed by someone with authority to commit the resource.
- States the specific commitment in the same words as the narrative.
- Names the program and the time period.
- Lists any in-kind contribution in a way the partner can document later.

**Discussion prompt:** Think of a partnership paragraph you have read in a past local plan. Which of the five elements was weakest?

## Practice

Write partnership language for the program you have been building in this course.

1. Write one paragraph, about 150 words, suitable for a local plan modification. Use all five elements.
2. Mark each sentence with the partner who must approve it.
3. Draft a three-sentence commitment letter for the employer that matches the employer's sentence in your paragraph word for word on the key commitment.
4. Underline anything you could not yet verify, and replace it with "[to confirm with partner]" rather than guessing.
