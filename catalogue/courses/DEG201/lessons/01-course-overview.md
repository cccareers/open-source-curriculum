## Description

Most boards meet apprenticeship degrees as a referral: a participant asks about one, and you point them to a program someone else built. This course is for the moment your board decides to build one instead. Building means getting an employer, a college or adult school, and an apprenticeship sponsor to agree on who does what, matching the work an apprentice does on the job to the outcomes a college course already certifies, and paying for the development work before the first apprentice is hired.

You will work through that build the way it happens in practice: first the partnership, then the alignment of work processes to course outcomes, then the money, then the written language that commits partners in a local plan modification or grant application. Each step produces a piece of a real proposal.

California's apprenticeship-degree landscape is still early, so this course teaches decision logic and partner conversations rather than a fixed model. Where a rule, deadline, or funding detail matters, you will be pointed to the current directive or your board's local policy to confirm it. The course closes with a short project in which you assemble those pieces into one draft proposal.

## Objectives

- Identify the partners and roles needed for a degree-linked apprenticeship.
- Crosswalk an apprenticeship's work processes to course learning outcomes.
- Draft a funding plan for program development.
- Write the partnership language for a local plan modification or grant application.
