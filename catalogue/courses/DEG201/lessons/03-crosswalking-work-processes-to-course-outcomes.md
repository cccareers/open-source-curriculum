## Why the crosswalk matters

A degree-linked apprenticeship only works if the college can look at what an apprentice does on the job and in related instruction and say, with confidence, "this is the same learning our course certifies." The crosswalk is the document that makes that case. It sits between two documents that were written by different people for different purposes:

- **The work processes** in the apprenticeship standards: the list of on-the-job tasks an apprentice learns, usually grouped into areas with approximate hours.
- **The course learning outcomes** in the college's official course outlines: statements of what a student can do after completing each course.

Your board does not decide whether credit is awarded. The college's faculty and curriculum process do. What your board can do is help build a clear, honest crosswalk so faculty have something concrete to review.

## The three ways credit can connect

DEG 101 introduced the models. In a crosswalk, each row will usually land in one of these:

| Connection | What it means | Who decides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Related instruction is a college course | The apprentice takes an existing credit course as part of the program; credit comes from passing the course | College, through normal enrollment |
| Credit for prior learning | Learning demonstrated on the job is assessed and awarded credit | College faculty, under the college's credit for prior learning policy |
| Articulated or embedded program | The college formally approves the apprenticeship pathway as part of a certificate or degree | College curriculum process, and any required system-level approval |

Check your college partner's current credit for prior learning policy and curriculum calendar. These vary by college and change over time.

## Building the crosswalk, step by step

1. **Get both source documents.** Ask the sponsor for the draft work processes and the college for the official course outlines for the closest courses. Do not work from a website summary.
2. **Break each work process into observable tasks.** "Clinical procedures" is too broad. "Measures and records vital signs accurately" can be matched.
3. **Match each task to a course outcome.** Look for the same verb and the same context. A task that says "assists" does not fully match an outcome that says "performs independently."
4. **Rate the match.** Use three ratings: Full, Partial, or Gap.
5. **Name the evidence.** For each match, say how the learning would be shown: a supervisor sign-off, a skills checklist, a course exam, a portfolio.
6. **Note the credit path.** Say which of the three connections applies.
7. **List the gaps.** Gaps are not failures. They tell you what related instruction must add, or which outcomes the apprenticeship will not cover.

## Worked example: medical assistant crosswalk (excerpt)

Mesa Valley Health (fictional) and Granite Peak Community College (fictional) are aligning a medical assistant apprenticeship to the college's medical assisting certificate. An excerpt:

| Work process task | Course | Course learning outcome | Match | Evidence | Credit path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Measures and records vital signs | MA clinical procedures I | Perform and document vital signs | Full | Supervisor skills checklist plus course practical | Related instruction is the course |
| Schedules patients and manages the daily appointment flow | MA administrative procedures | Apply scheduling systems in a medical office | Full | Supervisor sign-off; employer system training record | Credit for prior learning, if the college's policy allows |
| Assists provider with minor procedures | MA clinical procedures II | Prepare for and assist with minor office procedures | Partial | Checklist covers only two of five procedure types | Course, with lab hours for the missing types |
| Explains insurance eligibility to patients | Medical insurance and coding | Apply basic coding conventions | Gap | Work task is front desk; course is coding | Not credit-bearing as is |
| (no work process) | Medical terminology | Use medical terminology correctly | Gap | No on-the-job match | Taken as a standalone course |

What this tells the partners:

- Two rows are strong and can go to faculty now.
- One row needs the employer to rotate apprentices through more procedure types, or the course must supply that practice.
- The insurance row looks related but is not a match. Forcing it would weaken the whole crosswalk when faculty review it.
- Medical terminology has no on-the-job equivalent, so it stays a course.

## Common mistakes

- **Matching titles, not outcomes.** A work process called "Patient Care" and a course called "Patient Care" may teach different things.
- **Ignoring hours and depth.** If a course outcome requires independent performance and the apprentice only observes, the match is partial at best.
- **Overpromising.** Never tell an employer or participant a row "will count" until the college confirms it.
- **Leaving out the evidence column.** Faculty need to know how they would verify learning, not just that the tasks look similar.

**Discussion prompt:** Which rating, Full, Partial, or Gap, do you expect faculty to challenge most often, and how would you prepare for that conversation?

## Practice

Build a five-row crosswalk for an occupation you know.

1. Write five on-the-job tasks for that occupation, each starting with an observable verb.
2. For each task, find or draft a plausible course learning outcome from a related college program. If you use a real course outline, cite it by course name only.
3. Rate each row Full, Partial, or Gap, and write one sentence explaining the rating.
4. Fill in the evidence and credit-path columns.
5. Write two sentences summarizing what you would bring to the college's faculty lead first, and what you would ask the employer to change.
