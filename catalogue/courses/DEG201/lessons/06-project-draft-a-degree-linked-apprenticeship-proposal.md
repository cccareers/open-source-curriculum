## Goal

Assemble a short, honest draft proposal for a degree-linked apprenticeship that your board could take to its partners. The proposal brings together the four pieces you practiced in this course: a partner map, a work-process crosswalk, a development funding plan, and partnership language.

## The case

North Ridge Workforce Development Board (fictional) has been asked by the Cobalt County Information Technology Department (fictional) to help fill entry-level IT support positions. The county loses new hires to private employers and wants a path that keeps people while they earn a degree.

What you know:

- The county will hire four apprentices for a first cohort and can assign two senior technicians as mentors.
- Delta Plains College (fictional) offers an IT support certificate and an associate degree in computer information systems. Its dean is interested; the faculty lead has not yet been contacted.
- No one has agreed to be the apprenticeship sponsor. A regional technology intermediary has sponsored programs for private employers before.
- The county's draft work processes include: responding to help desk tickets; imaging and deploying workstations; managing user accounts and passwords; basic network troubleshooting; documenting fixes in a knowledge base; and following the county's security policies.
- The college's IT support certificate includes courses on hardware and operating systems, networking fundamentals, security fundamentals, and customer service in technical support.
- North Ridge has WIOA Title I funds and a business services team of two. It has not yet checked eligible uses under WSIN25-21.

## Requirements

Your proposal must include four sections:

1. **Partner map.** A table with every core role (employer, education partner, sponsor, board), the organization, the contact role, what each commits to, and a status. Identify the single biggest gap.
2. **Work-process crosswalk.** At least six rows matching county work-process tasks to plausible course learning outcomes. Rate each row Full, Partial, or Gap; name the evidence and the likely credit path.
3. **Development funding plan.** Phases, cost items, proposed stream, applicant, the document you would check, and a double-dip check for each row. Include a three-sentence sustainability statement.
4. **Partnership language.** One local plan modification paragraph (about 150 words) and one short commitment letter from the county that matches the paragraph.

## Constraints

- Use only the facts in the case. Where you need something the case does not give you, write "[to confirm]" and name who you would ask.
- Do not promise college credit. Describe what faculty will review and decide.
- Do not invent funding amounts as if they were program limits. Any dollar figures must be labeled as your estimates.
- Cite directives only by the names given in this course (for example, WSIN25-21). For eligible uses, deadlines, and applicants, write where you would check rather than stating a rule.
- Keep the whole proposal to about three pages.

## Definition of Done

- Every core role has a named organization or is clearly flagged as a gap with a next step.
- The crosswalk has at least one Partial and one Gap row with an honest explanation.
- Every development cost item has a stream, an applicant, and a "where to confirm" source.
- The local plan paragraph covers who, commitment, alignment, governance, and accountability.
- The county's commitment letter uses the same key commitment as the paragraph.
- A partner reading the proposal could tell exactly what is being asked of them.

## Hints

- Start with the sponsor gap. The intermediary is the obvious candidate, but the county might prefer to sponsor its own program. Show both options.
- "Documenting fixes in a knowledge base" may not match any course outcome directly. That is a legitimate Gap row, or a Partial match to customer service in technical support.
- Faculty time for crosswalk review is a development cost. Decide which stream fits and who applies.
- Reread the weak and strong examples in the partnership language lesson before writing your paragraph.
