## Development money is not apprentice money

APP 103 covered how to pay for an individual apprentice: wages, related instruction, supportive services, and the rules against billing the same cost twice. Building a degree-linked apprenticeship adds a second, earlier layer of cost that no apprentice generates: the work of designing the program before anyone is enrolled.

A funding plan for program development answers three questions:

1. What will it cost to get from "partners agree" to "first cohort starts"?
2. Which stream pays for each piece, and who applies for it?
3. What happens to those costs once the program is running?

## What development actually costs

Break the build into phases. Each phase has its own cost drivers.

| Phase | Typical cost drivers |
| --- | --- |
| Convening and design | Staff time from the board, employer, and college; meeting costs |
| Curriculum alignment | Faculty time to review the crosswalk and revise or create courses; curriculum approval work |
| Standards and registration | Sponsor or intermediary time to draft standards and register the program |
| Launch preparation | Recruitment, outreach materials, mentor training for journey-level workers |
| First cohort | Apprentice wages and instruction (these shift to participant-level funding) |

The first four phases are development. The fifth is where the APP 103 braiding worksheet takes over.

## The streams named for this course

The course catalog names four sources for the build. Treat this as a starting list, and confirm each stream's current rules before you write it into a plan.

| Stream | What it is, at a high level | Who usually applies | Where to confirm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CAI (California Apprenticeship Initiative) | Community College Chancellor's Office grants for new and innovative apprenticeship programs | Typically the college or a college-led partnership | The current CAI request for applications |
| AIF | State apprenticeship funding covered in APP 103, used for apprentice-level scenarios | Sponsor or program, per the funding rules | DAS guidance and APP 103 |
| Regional Coordination Project funds (WSIN25-21) | Regional funds named in the catalog for this purpose | Per the information notice | WSIN25-21 itself, for eligible uses, applicants, and timelines |
| WIOA Title I | Your board's formula funds | The board | Your board's policies on ITAs, OJT, business services, and staff allocation |

Keep two rules from APP 103 in front of you:

- **No double-dipping.** The same cost cannot be charged to two sources. APP 103's example, that RSI hours are not claimable when funded by a CAI grant, is exactly the kind of overlap a development budget can create by accident.
- **WIOA has limits.** WIOA is most useful for staff time on business services and convening, and for participant costs once the program runs: for example, individual training accounts for eligible apprentices in related instruction, OJT contracts, and supportive services. Whether WIOA can pay for a specific development cost depends on federal cost rules and your board's local policy. Confirm with your fiscal team before you put it in a plan.

Registered apprenticeship programs have a streamlined route onto the Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL). If your plan depends on individual training accounts, check the current California ETPL process with your ETPL coordinator early.

The amounts below are fictional estimates for one board's planning, not program limits.

| Phase | Cost item | Estimate | Proposed stream | Applicant | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Convening | Business services staff time, six months | $18,000 | WIOA Title I (business services) | Board | Confirm with fiscal |
| Curriculum | Faculty review and course revisions | $40,000 | CAI | Granite Peak CC | Next RFA cycle |
| Standards | Intermediary drafts standards and registers program | $15,000 | Regional Coordination Project funds | Board with region | Check WSIN25-21 eligible uses |
| Launch | Mentor training, outreach | $9,000 | Regional Coordination Project funds or employer in-kind | Board, employer | To decide |
| First cohort | Wages, related instruction, supports | See APP 103 worksheet | Employer, AIF, WIOA ITA/OJT | Multiple | After registration |

Dana adds a "double-dip check" column to the final draft and asks one question per row: is any part of this cost also in another partner's grant budget?

## A sustainability line

Funders and boards will ask what happens after the grant. Add a short statement to the plan: which costs end when development ends, which costs move to the employer or to ongoing college funding, and which participant costs WIOA will carry going forward.

**Discussion prompt:** Which development phase is your region least likely to have money for? What partner could carry it in-kind?

## Practice

Draft a one-page development funding plan for the program you mapped in the earlier lessons.

1. List the five phases and at least one cost item for each.
2. Assign a proposed stream and an applicant to every development cost item. Use "To confirm" wherever you are not sure of eligibility.
3. For each stream, write the document you would check (for example, "WSIN25-21" or "current CAI request for applications").
4. Add a double-dip check for each row.
5. Write three sentences on sustainability: what ends, what transfers, and what WIOA carries.
