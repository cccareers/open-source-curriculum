## Description

Every other course in this pathway has taught you to work on systems. This one is about the part of the attack surface that does not run an operating system: the people who receive the mail, answer the phone, and approve the payment. Attackers went after that surface because it is cheaper than exploiting software, and it has stayed the most common way into an organization for the same reason. You cannot patch it, and you cannot firewall it, but you can absolutely change how it behaves.

You will start with why social engineering works at all, because a red-flag checklist that is not grounded in the psychology behind the pressure teaches pattern matching that the next unfamiliar lure defeats. From there you will work through the attacks a support technician actually triages across mail, phone, and text, and practise the two decisions that matter: what to do with the message in front of you, and who else needs to know. Then you will move up a level and design the organizational response — a curriculum, a reporting procedure, a simulation program with the ethics constraints that keep it honest — and finally deliver a session and choose the metrics that show whether anything changed.

One position runs through the whole course, and it is worth stating plainly before you begin: blaming users is a failed control design. A person who clicks is telling you something about the message, the process, and the workload, not about their intelligence. Awareness work that makes people feel stupid produces people who hide their mistakes, which is the single worst outcome available to a security team. Your job is to make reporting the easiest thing a busy person can do, and then to prove with numbers that it worked.

## Objectives

- Explain the psychological levers social engineers use and recognize them in a described interaction
- Analyze a suspicious message, decide what to do with it, and route it to the right people
- Design an awareness curriculum and a reporting procedure that fit a described workforce
- Deliver a short awareness session and choose metrics that show whether behavior actually changed
