## Goal

Plan a complete thirty-day social media campaign for a product launch, run it on paper, and then prove you can read what came back.

You will deliver two documents. The first is a **campaign plan** a colleague could execute on Monday without asking you a single clarifying question: platform choices with the arithmetic behind them, a full content calendar, adapted creative copy, a community management operation, and a paid overlay derived from the organic plan. The second is a **post-campaign analysis** written against a supplied thirty days of performance data, a sentiment log, and a mid-campaign incident you did not plan for.

Nothing in this project runs live. Nothing is posted, nothing is boosted, no money is spent. The work is judged on whether your decisions follow from your numbers, whether your arithmetic is right, and whether you can tell an owner an uncomfortable truth in writing.

## Scenario

**Fernwood Coffee Roasters** — the roaster you have worked with all course — is launching **Fernwood Cold Brew**, a canned ready-to-drink cold brew, on **1 September**. It launches in all three cafés, in the DTC store, and into the wholesale channel simultaneously.

**The product and its economics**

```txt
DTC 4-pack                      $22.00   gross margin 52%
  Gross profit per 4-pack       $22.00 x 0.52 = $11.44
Cafe single can                  $5.50   gross margin 68%
  Gross profit per can          $5.50 x 0.68 = $3.74
Wholesale case of 24            $54.00   gross margin 34%
  Gross profit per case         $54.00 x 0.34 = $18.36
  A new wholesale account orders about 3 cases a month
  and lasts about 19 months
```

**The targets Priya has set for the 30 days**

```txt
1. Sell 400 DTC 4-packs.
2. Lift Tuesday-Thursday cafe transactions 8% against the August baseline.
3. Open 4 new wholesale accounts for the cold brew line.
```

**What you have to work with**

```txt
Paid social budget, 30 days                            $1,500
Team capacity                                12 hours per week
  (Tomas coordinates; Alex films and shoots in the cafes;
   June owns wholesale relationships; Priya approves claims)
Loaded labour cost, for costing purposes             $28/hour
Live accounts at launch    Instagram (9,850 followers),
                           Facebook (4,220), LinkedIn (1,140 company)
Existing site visitors, 30 days                         4,100
Existing DTC subscribers                                1,180
Instagram 90-day medians   saves per 1,000 reached        9.4
                           shares per 1,000 reached       7.1
Baseline net sentiment score (5-month mean)             +39.2
Baseline negative share of mentions                     7.41%
```

Everything you need is in this brief. Anything not supplied is an assumption you must label.

## Requirements

Deliver all nine items. Show your arithmetic everywhere a number appears.

### Part One — the plan (items 1-6)

**1. Objectives and platform selection.**
Restate each of Priya's three targets in the outcome/audience/horizon form. For each, describe the audience as a *situation*, not a demographic, and say where those people already are. Then score at least four platforms against at least four weighted criteria, per objective — not per brand — showing the weighted arithmetic for every row. Follow it with an explicit capacity check: state the weekly hours, subtract the response and reporting load *first*, price each candidate platform at a realistic cadence, and produce a final allocation with at least 10% slack. Name the platform you declined, why, and the specific trigger that would make you revisit it.

**2. Content pillars and the calendar.**
Derive three to five pillars from the three objectives, each with a target share of the calendar and a justification tied to an objective. Show how the shares convert into slot counts at your chosen cadence, and resolve — in writing — any conflict between pillar shares and platform allocation.

Then build the **full thirty-day calendar**. Every slot carries all eight fields: date, platform, pillar, format, working hook, owner, asset needed, status. It must include at least one row that exists purely to schedule an asset (a film day or shoot), one reserve slot per week held empty, and a pillar check at the end comparing planned share to target share with the arithmetic.

**3. Creative adaptation.**
Choose one launch idea and write its spine — claim, evidence, payoff — in three lines. Then produce **actual publishable copy**, not descriptions, for at least five containers across at least three platforms. At minimum:

- a carousel or multi-card piece with the text of every card, the full caption, and alt text for one image
- a short vertical video script with timestamps, spoken lines, and on-screen text
- a text-only post aimed at a *different* audience segment than the others — the wholesale buyer is the obvious candidate
- a link-bearing post on a platform that actually distributes links
- one ephemeral piece with a call to action appropriate to that surface

Close with a paragraph naming what you changed between containers and why: hook length, audience situation, call to action, and the level of commitment requested.

**4. Community management operation.**
Produce the operating document: coverage windows, first-response SLA with a separate window for safety and legal content, named owners including a backup for each, and the after-hours policy. Include your triage tree — you may adapt lesson 04's, but you must add at least one branch specific to a beverage product launch and justify it. Include the hide/delete/block/report rules and a full escalation matrix with owners and time windows.

Then estimate the inbound volume you expect during a launch month, state the assumption behind the estimate, convert it to hours, and confirm it fits inside the capacity you allocated in item 1. If it does not, change the plan.

**5. Paid social overlay.**
Write the promotion eligibility gate: metrics, multiples off the supplied medians, waiting period, and any qualitative requirement, with each multiple defended in a sentence. Build the audience ladder — every rung, its approximate size, the organic activity that created it, what that audience already knows, the message, and the asset — plus every exclusion and why.

Split the $1,500 across the jobs paid social does, count your cells, and divide. If a cell cannot be sized to produce roughly 50 results over 30 days, restructure and say what you dropped. Derive the allowable cost per result from the unit economics above, separating break-even on the first purchase from break-even on a wholesale account's lifetime. Using labelled planning assumptions for cost per thousand impressions and click-through rate, work forward to the conversion rate your plan implicitly requires, and say whether you believe it.

Deliver it as a one-page overlay table: asset, its organic evidence, audience, exclusions, objective, budget, run length, and the success signal declared **before** launch.

**6. Measurement plan.**
Name, before any data exists, the one number that tells you each platform is serving its objective. Specify which metrics come from platform analytics and which come from web analytics, and state the attribution window attached to each. Write the UTM convention — the closed vocabulary for `utm_medium`, the rules for the other three parameters — and produce at least six complete tagged URLs covering organic and paid across two platforms. Say how you will measure the part neither tool can see, and name the size of platform-versus-web gap you would consider normal and the size that would make you stop and investigate.

- **Triage and reply to all six items below.** For each: the branch of your tree, whether the response is public, private, both, or none, the owner, the response window, and then the actual reply text.

- One paragraph on the leading indicator you would add so that the next incident is visible on day 17 rather than in the week-3 report.

**9. Recommendation.**

- Three defended decisions for the next thirty days. Each states the observation, the ratio or figure supporting it, the action, the expected effect as a **range** rather than a point, and the condition that would make you reverse it. At least one must move at least $400 of budget or at least two hours a week of capacity.
- A verdict paragraph for Priya of **under 200 words**, in plain language, leading with money, stating target attainment honestly, and disclosing one thing you do not know and cannot know from this data.

## Constraints

A submission that violates any of these is returned unassessed.

- **Nothing goes live.** No post is published, no campaign is enabled, no money is spent, no real customer is contacted. Everything is a document, a draft, or a spreadsheet.
- **Only the supplied data.** The tables in items 7 and 8 are the entire performance record. Do not add a second month, do not invent segment breakdowns, do not report a figure you did not compute from something in this brief.
- **Label every assumption inline**, in the form `assumption: ...`, with one sentence on why it is reasonable and one on how it could be wrong.
- **Every number is traceable.** A reviewer must be able to follow any figure back to a supplied value through visible arithmetic. A spreadsheet with live formulas is preferred; a spreadsheet of pasted values is not.
- **Stay inside the course's scope.** Do not build an ad account structure, choose a bid strategy, model auction dynamics, or run a statistical significance test — those belong to the paid advertising course, and importing them here will not earn credit. Do not deliver edited video or finished graphic design; formats are specified in copy, scripts, and card text.
- **Substantiate every claim in your copy.** You may use the figures in this brief. You may not claim a caffeine level, a health benefit, a shelf life, an award, a competitor comparison, or a customer count that is not supplied here.
- **No employee is named or discussed publicly**, in any reply, ever.
- **Numerical honesty is enforced.** Do not compute a percentage change on a count below about 30. Writing "this number is too small to trend" is a correct answer and scores better than a confident one built on three data points.

## Hints

**Start with the capacity arithmetic, not the calendar.** Everything downstream is constrained by it, and a calendar built first will have to be thrown away.

**Do the sentiment table before you write a single reply.** The size of the incident determines the tone, the channel, and who owns it. Replying first and measuring second is how brands over-react to a small problem and under-react to a large one.

**Watch the day-21 Reel.** It has the campaign's largest reach and one of its worst conversion rates. Resist the two easy conclusions — "Reels don't work" and "reach is vanity." Walk the chain and find the actual link where it lost, then ask what objective that post should have been judged on instead.

**The 4 September and 14 September carousels are doing most of the work.** Compute their packs per 1,000 reached against the Reels before you decide what to make more of next month.

**The wholesale lifetime number is a trap worth walking into deliberately.** Three accounts at 3 cases a month for 19 months is a large figure, and putting it in a thirty-day contribution line makes the campaign look profitable on money that has not arrived. Report both, and say which one you would put in front of the owner and why.

**Item (c) is not a customer service problem.** An allegation of illness involving a child goes down the safety branch, gets a holding line, and reaches the owner within the hour. If your submission has a friendly, helpful, detailed public reply to (c), you have failed the most important line in the definition of done.

**Item (f) is not a throwaway.** Praise arriving in the middle of an incident is the single most useful thing in the week-4 sentiment recovery, and how you respond to it — and whether you ask permission to amplify it — is part of the assessment.

**Write the verdict paragraph last, then delete half of it.** Owners read the first sentence and the number. Lead with the contribution figure, state the three targets against their attainment, name the one thing you do not know, and stop.
