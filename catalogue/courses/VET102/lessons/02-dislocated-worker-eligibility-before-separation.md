## Why this decision comes first

A separating service member is not "laid off" in the way a warehouse worker is, and a military spouse who follows orders to a new duty station did not "quit" in the usual sense. If you apply the civilian Dislocated Worker (DW) checklist literally, you will turn away people the rules were written to serve. The federal rule at 20 CFR 680.660 and the guidance in TEGL 19-16 exist so that you can enroll these customers in Title I Dislocated Worker services, often **before** the separation date, rather than making them wait until they are unemployed and out of options.

Your job in this lesson is the eligibility decision itself: which category the customer fits, what evidence you need to see, and what to do when the evidence is not in hand yet.

## Two customer types, two paths

| Customer | What makes them a potential DW | Key question you ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Separating service member | Notice of separation from active duty, under conditions other than dishonorable | "Do you have separation orders or a DD-214, and what is the character of service?" |
| Military spouse | Loss of employment tied to the service member's relocation (permanent change of station), or other spouse situations named in the guidance | "Did you lose a job because of a PCS move, or is your situation tied to the service member's change in duty status?" |

### Separating service members

Under 20 CFR 680.660, a service member who is separating from the Armed Forces with a discharge other than dishonorable can qualify as a dislocated worker. The notice of separation stands in for the layoff notice a civilian would show. TEGL 19-16 walks through how the remaining DW criteria (such as being unlikely to return to the prior occupation) are treated for this group. Read that section before you rely on it; do not reconstruct it from memory.

The practical consequences:

- You can enroll **before** the separation date if the customer has documented notice of separation. That is the whole point: training can start while they still draw military pay.
- Character of discharge matters. A dishonorable discharge does not qualify under this path. If the discharge type is pending or unclear, note it and check with your supervisor before enrolling.
- Retirees and members of the Guard or Reserve leaving active duty raise their own questions. Check the current TEGL language and your state guidance rather than assuming either way.

### Military spouses

WIOA treats certain military spouses as dislocated workers, most commonly a spouse who lost employment as a direct result of relocating with the service member to a new permanent duty station. TEGL 19-16 also addresses other spouse situations. The decision logic is: identify the qualifying event, connect it to the service member's military status, and document both.

## Documentation: decide what proves each fact

Do not memorize a document list from training. California's acceptable documentation is set in the state's eligibility directive; **check the current Acceptable Documentation List in WSD24-04** (or whatever directive has replaced it) and your board's local policy. What you should know cold is *which facts* you are proving:

1. **Military status and separation** (for example, separation orders or a DD-214 once issued).
2. **Character of discharge** (other than dishonorable).
3. **For spouses:** the marriage or dependency relationship, and the qualifying event, such as PCS orders plus evidence of the job loss.
4. **Standard WIOA items** that apply to every participant (identity, right to work, and so on) per your state list.

### Worked example

**Maria Delgado**, a Navy logistics specialist, visits Valley Regional AJCC four months before her separation date. She has separation orders showing an honorable characterization expected. She wants a commercial driver training program.

Your case note might read:

```text
Customer presented separation orders (dated [date]) showing separation from
active duty on [date], characterization expected honorable. Meets DW category
for separating service members per 20 CFR 680.660 / TEGL 19-16. Enrolled
pre-separation. DD-214 to be added to file upon issuance. Follow-up set [date].
```

**James Okafor**, Maria's neighbor, is the spouse of an Army sergeant. He lost his dental assistant job in Texas when the family received PCS orders to a base in your area. His path is the military spouse category: you document the PCS orders, the marriage, and the job loss.

## Common mistakes

- Telling a service member to "come back after you separate."
- Asking for a DD-214 before it can possibly exist instead of accepting the evidence the guidance allows.
- Treating every military spouse as automatically eligible without a qualifying event.
- Forgetting to note the follow-up to collect the final DD-214.
