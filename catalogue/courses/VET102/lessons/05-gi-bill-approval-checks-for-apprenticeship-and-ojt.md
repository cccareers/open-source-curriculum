## Why you check before the veteran signs on

Many veterans do not know that GI Bill education benefits can be used for registered apprenticeships and on-the-job training, not just college. When a program is approved, the veteran can receive VA benefit payments **in addition to** the wages the employer pays. That can make a lower starting apprentice wage workable for a family. When a program is not approved, the veteran cannot use those benefits there, however good the job is.

You are not the VA and you do not determine anyone's GI Bill entitlement. Your job is narrower and practical: **check whether the program is approved, explain in plain terms what that could mean, and connect the veteran to the people who confirm the rest.**

## Who approves programs

Apprenticeship and OJT programs are approved for GI Bill use through a State Approving Agency acting for the VA. In California, that is the California State Approving Agency for Veterans Education (CSAAVE). Approval belongs to the specific employer or sponsor program, not to an occupation in general: "electrician apprenticeships" are not approved, a particular sponsor's program is.

## How to check: a four-step routine

1. **Get the exact program details.** Employer or sponsor legal name, program name, location, and whether it is a registered apprenticeship or an OJT program.
2. **Search VA's public tools.** VA's GI Bill Comparison Tool lets you filter for on-the-job training and apprenticeship programs and search by employer name and location. Record what you found and the date you looked.
3. **If you cannot find it, ask.** Contact the employer's training coordinator to ask whether the program is VA approved, and if they are unsure, point them to CSAAVE. Employers can seek approval for a program that is not yet approved; how long that takes varies, so do not promise a date.
4. **Send the veteran to VA for entitlement.** The veteran confirms which benefit they have and how much entitlement remains with VA (for example, by requesting their Certificate of Eligibility). Do not estimate it for them.

```text
[date] Checked GI Bill Comparison Tool for "Valley Electrical JATC"
apprenticeship, [city]. Program listed as approved for OJT/apprenticeship.
Explained to customer that GI Bill payments may be available alongside
apprentice wages; amounts depend on benefit type and VA rates. Customer to
request Certificate of Eligibility from VA. Follow-up [date].
```

## Explaining the benefit in plain language

Keep the explanation accurate and general. A good explanation covers:

- **It is paid to the veteran, not the employer.** The employer pays wages as normal.
- **It usually steps down over time.** Under the Post-9/11 GI Bill, the monthly payment for apprenticeship and OJT decreases at set intervals as the veteran's wages are expected to rise. Other GI Bill chapters have their own payment rules.
- **It uses entitlement.** Months used here are months not available later for college or other training. Some veterans will want to weigh that choice.
- **The employer reports hours.** The program's certifying official reports to VA; missed reports can delay payments.

Do not quote dollar amounts or percentages from memory. **Direct the veteran to VA's current published rates** and their own Certificate of Eligibility.

### Worked example

**Andre Williams**, a Marine veteran, has an offer from Coastal Pipe Trades for a plumbing apprenticeship at an entry wage that worries him. You search the Comparison Tool and find the sponsor's program listed. You explain that if he is eligible he may receive monthly GI Bill payments alongside his wages, that the payment typically decreases as the apprenticeship progresses, and that using it draws down his entitlement. He decides to request his Certificate of Eligibility before accepting.

If the program had not been listed, you would have told Andre that, contacted the sponsor about approval, and kept planning the apprenticeship on its own merits.

### What about WIOA OJT?

If your board is also writing a WIOA OJT contract with the same employer, the GI Bill check is a separate question. WIOA OJT reimburses the employer (20 CFR 680.730); GI Bill payments go to the veteran. **Confirm against your board's OJT policy** how the two are coordinated in your area.
