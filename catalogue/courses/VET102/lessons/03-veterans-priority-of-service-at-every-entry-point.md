## What priority of service is, and is not

Priority of service is a federal requirement that veterans and eligible spouses ("covered persons") receive priority over non-covered persons for employment, training, and placement services in qualified job training programs funded by the Department of Labor, including WIOA Title I. It comes from the Jobs for Veterans Act and its implementing rule at 20 CFR part 1010.

Two points trip up staff more than anything else:

- **Priority is not eligibility.** A veteran still has to meet the eligibility rules of the program. Priority of service decides who goes first among people who qualify; it does not make anyone qualify.
- **Priority applies everywhere a customer touches you,** not just at enrollment. If your system has a front door you do not think of as a "point of entry," that is probably where priority of service is failing.

Who counts as a veteran and an eligible spouse for this purpose is defined in federal law. The veteran definition is broad (active service and a discharge other than dishonorable). The eligible spouse definition is narrower and tied to specific circumstances of the service member. **Check the definition in the current guidance before you tell a spouse they are covered.**

## Map your points of entry

A point of entry is any place a customer first makes contact and could be served or turned away. For a typical AJCC, the list includes:

| Point of entry | What priority of service looks like there |
| --- | --- |
| Front desk / greeter | Ask every customer whether they or their spouse have served. Flag covered persons. |
| Phone and email inquiries | Same question, asked early, before the customer is put on a list. |
| Online registration (CalJOBS) | Ensure the veteran status question is answered; follow up on flagged registrations. |
| Workshops and orientations | Covered persons get first access to limited seats. |
| Training waitlists and ITA funding | Covered persons who qualify move ahead of non-covered persons in the same priority tier. |
| Partner and satellite sites | The same rules apply; partners need the same script. |

At each point, covered persons must be **identified** and **informed** of their entitlement to priority, the services available, and any eligibility requirements for those services.

## Applying the order when the Adult program has its own priority

WIOA Title I Adult funds carry a statutory priority for recipients of public assistance, other low-income individuals, and individuals who are basic skills deficient. Federal guidance explains how that layers with veterans' priority. As commonly described, the order is:

1. Covered persons who are also in the Adult statutory priority group.
2. Non-covered persons in the Adult statutory priority group.
3. Covered persons outside the statutory priority group.
4. Everyone else, subject to any local priorities your board sets.

**Confirm this order against your state's current priority of service directive and your board's local policy** before you build it into a waitlist procedure. The Dislocated Worker program has no equivalent income priority, so covered persons simply move ahead of non-covered persons who are otherwise in the same position.

### Worked example: one training slot

Valley Regional Workforce Board has one remaining Adult ITA for a medical assistant cohort. Three eligible applicants:

- **Rosa**, low-income, not a veteran.
- **Marcus**, Army veteran, not low-income or basic skills deficient.
- **Linh**, Navy veteran receiving CalFresh.

Linh goes first (covered and in the statutory priority group). If a second slot opened, Rosa would come before Marcus under the order above. Record the priority determination in the case file so a monitor can see why the order was applied.

## A front-desk script you can adapt

```text
"Before we get started, have you or your spouse ever served in the
U.S. military? ... Thank you for your service. Veterans and some military
spouses receive priority for our services. Let me tell you what that means
for you today and what programs you may qualify for."
```

Keep it short, ask everyone, and do not ask for discharge papers just to grant priority at the front desk; your board's policy sets when verification is required.
