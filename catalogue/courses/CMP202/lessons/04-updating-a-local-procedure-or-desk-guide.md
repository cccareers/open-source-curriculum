## From "found it" to "fixed it"

You have a citation log of stale or questionable references. Now you turn it into a corrected document that staff can trust. The goal is a desk guide that tells a coworker what to do, cites the rule that actually applies, and makes clear which parts are set by your local board.

Updating a document is not just a find-and-replace on directive numbers. A cancelled TEGL may have supported a whole step, so removing the citation can leave a step with no basis at all. Each fix needs a decision.

## The decision for each log row

For every row in your citation log, choose one of four actions:

| Situation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Citation is current and the step is right | Keep. Record that you checked it and when. |
| Citation is superseded, step is still valid | Replace the citation with the current directive. |
| Citation is cancelled or rescinded, and the step depended on it | Rewrite or remove the step. Confirm the replacement rule with your policy lead. |
| You cannot tell | Mark "pending policy review." Do not guess. |

The last row matters. A desk guide that says "pending review — ask your supervisor" is safer than one that confidently states a rule nobody verified.

## Worked example

Valley Regional Workforce Board's intake desk guide had this step:

> Step 3. Work authorization: follow TEGL 10-23 Change 2 for verification.

The 2026 refresh list shows TEGL 10-23 Change 2 has been rescinded. The citation is gone, but the step itself still matters, because staff still need to know when to verify. The updated step:

> Step 3. Work authorization: determine whether verification is required under WSD18-03. Use the documentation the directive specifies (check its current text rather than a copied list). Record the determination and the document reviewed in the eligibility case note. *Local policy variable: Valley Regional's eligibility policy, section [number], sets who signs off.*

What changed:

- The rescinded citation is removed.
- The step points to the current state directive by number.
- It tells staff where the document list lives, instead of copying a list that might change.
- The local-policy piece is labeled as local.

## Write for the person at the desk

Good desk guides share a few habits:

- **One action per step**, starting with a verb: "Confirm," "Request," "Record."
- **Cite the source at the step**, not only in a references section at the end.
- **Label local choices.** Write "Local policy variable:" before anything your board sets, such as OJT caps, ITA limits, or sign-off levels.
- **Avoid screenshots where you can.** With the CalJOBS modernization underway, describe the purpose of a step ("record the eligibility determination") and keep click paths in a separate, easy-to-replace appendix.
- **Point, don't paste.** Link to the directive instead of copying long passages that will go stale.

## Add a revision block

Every updated document should open with a short revision block so the next reviewer knows what you did:

```text
Revision: 2026 policy refresh
Revised by: [name, role]    Date: [date]
Reviewed with: [policy lead or supervisor]
Changes: Removed TEGL 10-23 Change 2 citation (rescinded); Step 3 now
  anchored to WSD18-03. Youth flexibility language removed (TEGL 09-22
  cancelled); Step 7 pending policy review.
Next scheduled review: [date set by your routine]
```

## Get it approved and announced

A corrected file sitting in your personal folder changes nothing. Before you finish:

1. Have your policy lead or supervisor review every "rewrite or remove" and "pending" item.
2. Replace the old version in the shared location, and archive the old one so staff cannot grab it by mistake.
3. Tell the team what changed in one or two sentences per change.

## Practice

Use the citation log you built in the first lesson.

1. Choose one section of your document that has at least two flagged citations.
2. Apply the decision table to each flagged row and write down your action.
3. Rewrite that section using the desk-guide habits above: verb-first steps, citation at the step, local variables labeled.
4. Add a revision block at the top.
5. Ask a partner to read your rewritten section cold and tell you what they would do. If they hesitate, revise the step.
