## How to read this lesson

This lesson walks through the six changes on this year's refresh list. For each one you get three things: **what changed** (stated at the level the course catalog states it), **what it touches on the front line**, and **where to confirm the details**. It does not reproduce directive text, and it does not give effective dates. Before you change practice, open the current directive or guidance and your board's local policy. Effective dates, transition rules, and exact documentation lists live there, not here.

A useful habit for every change: ask "Who does this touch, at which step, and what do they do differently?"

## 1. TEGL 09-22 cancelled (youth flexibilities)

**What changed.** The federal guidance known as TEGL 09-22, which addressed flexibilities for youth programs, has been cancelled.

**Frontline effect.** Any practice your team adopted *because of* that guidance needs a second look. Youth case managers, youth provider staff, and anyone who reviews youth files are most affected. The risk is that a flexibility is still written into a youth desk guide or provider contract and staff keep using it.

**What you do Monday.**

- Find every youth document that cites TEGL 09-22 or describes a "flexibility" without a source.
- Ask your policy lead which rule now governs each practice the flexibility covered.
- Until you have an answer, do not apply the flexibility to new enrollments on your own judgment. Escalate.

**Confirm against:** the cancellation notice from the issuing agency, any state guidance issued in response, and your board's youth policy.

## 2. TEGL 10-23 Change 2 rescinded (work authorization)

**What changed.** TEGL 10-23 Change 2, which addressed work authorization, has been rescinded. The catalog for this pathway frames the current question as identifying when work authorization must be verified under the state directive **WSD18-03** now that the federal change is gone.

**Frontline effect.** This lands at eligibility and intake. Staff who determine eligibility, and anyone who builds document-request lists, need to know which rule now drives verification.

**What you do Monday.**

- Pull your intake checklist and eligibility desk guide. Remove references to the rescinded change.
- Read WSD18-03 for when verification is required and what documentation it accepts.
- Make sure case notes cite the rule that actually applies, not the rescinded TEGL.

**Confirm against:** WSD18-03 and any newer state notice on work authorization.

## 3. WSD25-02: the new ETPL directive

**What changed.** California has issued a new Eligible Training Provider List directive, WSD25-02. The catalog notes one key point from it: every registered program is automatically eligible for the ETPL under WSD25-02 (the pathway raises this in its apprenticeship coursework).

**Frontline effect.** Career coaches and training staff who help participants choose training, and staff who work with apprenticeship sponsors, are affected. When a participant asks about a registered apprenticeship, "is it on the ETPL?" should not be a barrier you invent.

**What you do Monday.**

- Replace older ETPL citations in your ITA and training-selection guides with WSD25-02 where it now governs.
- Check whether your local ITA policy treats registered apprenticeships the way the new directive does.
- Read the directive for anything else that changed in provider eligibility or performance reporting. The catalog does not list those details, so neither does this lesson.

**Confirm against:** WSD25-02 itself and your board's ITA policy.

## 4. AJCC certification under WSD25-05

**What changed.** AJCC certification now runs under WSD25-05. The catalog describes it as built around **seven certification indicators**, each needing evidence.

**Frontline effect.** This is often seen as a manager's task, but the evidence comes from daily work: how customers are greeted and served, how staff are trained, how partners coordinate. Frontline staff produce that evidence whether they know it or not.

**What you do Monday.**

- Ask your manager which indicators your center is gathering evidence for and what your role is.
- Keep training records, customer-flow procedures, and partner referral notes in a form someone could hand to a reviewer.

**Confirm against:** WSD25-05 for the indicators and evidence expectations, and your board's certification timeline.

## 5. The 90 percent OJT waiver

**What changed.** The catalog lists a 90 percent OJT reimbursement waiver, described elsewhere in this pathway as a small-employer waiver. On-the-job training reimbursement is set under 20 CFR 680.730, and this pathway's catalog ties rate decisions (50, 75, or 90 percent) to 20 CFR 680.730 and WSD25-03.

**Frontline effect.** Business services and OJT staff now have a higher rate available in some cases. That means more questions from employers and a greater need to document *why* a rate was chosen.

**What you do Monday.**

- Do not quote 90 percent to an employer until you confirm which employers qualify and whether your board has adopted the higher rate.
- Update OJT rate worksheets so the justification for any rate is written down.

**Confirm against:** the waiver itself, WSD25-03, and your board's OJT policy, which sets local caps and rate rules.

## 6. The CalJOBS modernization

**What changed.** CalJOBS, the state's case management system, is being modernized.

**Frontline effect.** Screens, steps, and terms in your desk guides may stop matching what you see. Screenshots and click-by-click instructions age fastest.

**What you do Monday.**

- Flag every desk guide that contains CalJOBS screenshots or click paths.
- Follow state and board announcements for training and release notes, and update guides once the new screens are live.

**Confirm against:** official CalJOBS announcements and your board's system administrator.

## Discussion prompt

Which of the six changes touches your daily work most? Describe one conversation with a participant or employer that will sound different because of it.

## Practice

Pick **two** of the six changes: one that affects your role directly and one that does not.

1. For each, write a three-sentence "Monday explanation" a new coworker could understand: what changed, who it affects, and what they do differently.
2. For each, name the exact source you would open to confirm the details.
3. Trade explanations with a partner. Check each other for any detail that goes beyond what the source states, such as a date or dollar figure you are not sure of, and remove it or mark it "confirm."
