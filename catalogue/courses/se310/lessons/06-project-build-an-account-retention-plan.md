## Goal

You have inherited a real-feeling account in trouble, four months from its renewal decision. Produce a complete account retention plan: a churn-risk assessment scored from evidence, a dated relationship and touchpoint plan through the renewal, an account review you have prepared and can run, a renewal conversation plan, and a corrected set of account records that a colleague could pick up cold. Everything you need is below. Where you add an assumption, say so in writing.

## The scenario

You joined the company six weeks ago and inherited a book of eighteen accounts from a rep who left. **Ardmore Cold Chain Services** is your third largest. Today is **3 September**.

Ardmore maintains refrigeration and cold-storage plant for supermarket and food-distribution clients across four depots. They bought your field service platform to cut repeat visits and get engineers off paperwork. The account has not had a documented review since April.

## The contract

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Licensed seats | 310 |
| Price | $24.90 per seat per month |
| Annual contract value | $92,628 |
| Term | 12 months, 1 January–31 December; month 1 is January |
| Notice period | 45 days; auto-renews if no notice is given |
| Mid-term amendment | 52 seats added in month 5 (May) for the Scotland depot, acquired in April; 258 seats before the amendment |

## The people

- **Ola Danjuma**, Head of Service — your champion, chose the platform, defended it internally. Has rescheduled the last two reviews and sent a scheduling analyst to the one before that.
- **Fiona Kerr**, Finance Director — signed the contract. You have never met her. Your predecessor met her once, at signature.
- **Gary Prescott**, IT Manager — argued for keeping their previous system. Still in post.
- **Neil Ashby**, Group Operations Director — joined in June, newly sits above Ola. Came from a national logistics business. Nobody at your company has ever spoken to him.
- **Morag Sinclair**, Depot Manager, Scotland — inherited with the acquisition. Has never been contacted by anyone at your company.

## The usage picture

| Depot | Licensed seats | Active users | Adoption | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Midlands | 96 | 87 | 91% | Live since month 1 |
| South | 84 | 72 | 86% | Live since month 2 |
| North | 78 | 32 | 41% | Live since month 3; adoption fell from 71% in June |
| Scotland | 52 | 6 | 12% | Seats added month 5; no onboarding was ever run |
| **Total** | **310** | **197** | **64%** | Was 230 active in month 4, across Midlands, South and North |

The current usage snapshot is 3 September: 197 / 310 = 63.5%, rounded to 64%. The month-4 peak was 230 / 258 = 89.1%, before Scotland's seats were added; all three original depots were live by then. The $92,628 contract value is the annualized current seat run rate (310 × $24.90 × 12), not a claim that all 310 seats were billed for the full first year.

## Other things on the record

- Procurement emailed your company two weeks ago asking for "a copy of the termination provisions." Nobody replied.
- Two North depot engineers told your predecessor in May that the app "takes forever to load in the basements." Nothing was logged after that.
- Ardmore's budget for next year is set in **October**.

## The account record, as you inherited it

- **Account:** Ardmore Cold Chain Services. Last logged activity: 24 June, "Called Ola, all good." No notes since.
- **Churn risk:** never assessed.
- **Renewal opportunity:** "Ardmore Renewal FY2" — amount $92,628; close date 31 December; stage *Negotiation*; forecast category *Commit*; created by your predecessor in March; description field empty.
- **Expansion opportunity:** "Ardmore — Fleet Telematics Module" — amount $22,000; close date 30 September; stage *Proposal*; created 11 February by your predecessor; last modified 14 March; no notes. You can find no evidence that Ardmore has ever discussed telematics with anyone.

## What you must deliver

A single document with seven parts.

## Requirements

1. **Churn-risk assessment.** Score Ardmore against all twelve factors of the scorecard from lesson 4: score, weight, weighted score, and the specific evidence you scored from for every factor. Give the total and the band. Where the evidence does not exist, apply the default rule and say so explicitly.

2. **The mechanism, in numbers.** In no more than five sentences, state how this account most plausibly ends up cancelled or cut, and by whom. Then compute two figures with the arithmetic shown: the annualized revenue at risk if seats are trued down to current active usage, and the annualized revenue at risk if the Scotland and North depots are removed from the contract entirely.

3. **Transcript analysis.** Take the 29 August call apart. Identify at least six specific moments where information was available and was not pursued — quote the line, name what it signalled, and write the question you should have asked instead of the one you did. Then state, in one sentence each, the three risks in this account that the call touched and left buried.

4. **The account review you will actually run.** Prepare the next review with Ola, to happen within two weeks. Deliver: the agenda; your first four questions written verbatim, none of which may name your product or ask for a verdict on it; the two hedges or evasions from the 29 August call you intend to return to and how you will raise them; and a written playback of no more than five sentences that names risk rather than symptom and ends with an explicit invitation to correct you. Mark on your agenda where you will deliberately stay silent.

5. **The relationship and touchpoint plan.** A dated plan from today through 31 December and one month past it, with four fields per entry: when, who on their side, why, and what they get. It must include a first contact with Neil Ashby before the October budget is set, a route to Fiona Kerr, a plan for Morag Sinclair and the Scotland depot, a defined response to North's adoption collapse, the reply to procurement, and every renewal milestone placed against the **notice date** rather than the contract end date. State the notice deadline explicitly, as a date.

6. **The renewal conversation plan.** The evidence pack you will carry, listed. The opening ninety seconds, written verbatim, leading with a miss. The one question you will ask before going silent. And a prepared response to each of these four:
   - "Neil wants to look at consolidating onto one platform group-wide."
   - "We're paying for 310 seats and using under 200."
   - "Can we do a three-month extension while the review finishes?"
   - "What's your best price if we sign for two years?"

7. **The corrected records.** Write out exactly what you change, and why, for: the renewal opportunity (close date, amount, stage, forecast category, description), the fleet telematics opportunity, the churn score and driving factor, and the next dated action. Then answer two questions in writing. What did your company's forecast believe about Ardmore before you touched it, and what does it believe now? And: if you were unreachable for two weeks starting tomorrow, what does a colleague opening this account now know that they did not know this morning?

## Constraints

- Every claim in the churn assessment and the renewal evidence pack must trace to a fact in this brief. Where you assume something, mark it as an assumption in the text and say how you would confirm it.
- You may not propose an expansion, a discount, or a multi-year commitment anywhere in the plan. If you believe one is warranted, write the trigger that would have to fire first and the date you would review it.
- The touchpoint plan must fit a rep with eighteen accounts. Do not plan more than **six hours a month** of your own time on Ardmore, and show the arithmetic that proves your plan fits.
- Do not write implementation work, support processes, or training content. Where the plan depends on a specialist, name the dependency, the owner, and the date; that is your job, and doing theirs is not.
- No CRM configuration. You are consuming the system, not designing it.
- The plan must be readable in ten minutes. Tables over paragraphs.

## Definition of done

- [ ] All twelve churn factors scored, each with cited evidence or an explicit "no evidence" default; total and band stated.
- [ ] Both revenue-at-risk figures computed with the arithmetic visible.
- [ ] At least six missed moments identified in the transcript, each with a quoted line and a replacement question.
- [ ] Four verbatim review questions, none naming the product or asking for a verdict.
- [ ] A written playback of five sentences or fewer that names risk, not symptom.
- [ ] Touchpoint plan is dated, has all four fields per entry, and includes Neil Ashby before October, a route to Fiona Kerr, Scotland, North, and the procurement reply.
- [ ] The notice deadline appears as a specific date, and every renewal milestone is placed against it.
- [ ] Opening ninety seconds written verbatim and leading with a miss.
- [ ] All four hard responses prepared.
- [ ] Every record correction stated with its reasoning, including the fate of the telematics opportunity.
- [ ] Time budget shown and inside six hours a month.
- [ ] No expansion, discount, or multi-year offer anywhere in the document.

## Hints

- Score the account before you plan anything. The band tells you the cadence, and the cadence tells you what fits in six hours a month.
- The account has two separate problems that look like one: a depot that stopped using the product, and a depot that was never given it. They have different owners, different fixes, and different consequences at renewal. Do not write a single action that covers both.
- The most dangerous sentence in the transcript is delivered as reassurance and contains a company name. Read the last long answer again.
- "That's a management thing at our end really, not you" is a customer letting you off the hook. Accepting it is the single most expensive thing that happens in that call.
- A measure that stopped being produced is not a measurement problem. Ask who stopped caring, and why.
- Procurement asking for termination provisions before you have opened a renewal is not an administrative request. Treat the two-week silence as your problem to fix in the first entry of your plan.
- Work the calendar backwards from two dates, not one: their budget is set in October, and the notice deadline falls in November. The later date is not the binding one.
- The telematics opportunity has no evidence behind it and no customer who has ever discussed it. Deciding what to do with it is part of the assessment, not a detail.
- When you write the corrected renewal amount, write the reasoning beside it. An amount with no reasoning is the exact defect you are fixing.
