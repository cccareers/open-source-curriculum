## Goal

Run one complete commercial negotiation, live and unscripted, from a written prep sheet through to a signed-shape agreement and a mutual action plan — and then prove on paper that every concession you made was paid for.

You will be assessed on three things and nothing else: whether you diagnosed objections before answering them and kept the deal on merit, whether your anchors and concessions followed a plan you wrote *before* the conversation, and whether you finished with a dated mutual action plan carrying named owners on both sides. Charm is not on the list.

## The scenario

**Seller (you):** an account executive at **Meridian Support Cloud**, a customer-support platform sold per agent seat.

**Buyer:** **Calder Logistics**, a 2,000-person freight and warehousing company. Their support organization handles carrier queries, claims, and tracking escalations.

### What discovery has already established

These facts are given. You may use them; you may not invent additional facts about Calder without your buyer's agreement.

- 180 support agents across three sites; 40 of them handle claims specifically.
- Claims escalations to the operations team run about 500 a month. Operations logs roughly 45 minutes of handling on each.
- **Priya Raman, VP Customer Operations**, is your champion. She told you: *"Last year claims escalations cost us the equivalent of four full-time operations people. It's the number my COO asks me about, and I've explained it three quarters in a row."*
- Calder is opening two more distribution centres next year; support headcount is planned to grow about 25%.
- Their current tool is an on-premise system on extended support. There is no contractual exit cost.
- Budget cycle closes for approvals on **30 November**. Priya wants to be live before the peak season starting **1 March**.

### Your commercial position

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| List price | $62 per agent seat per month |
| Deal size at 180 seats | $133,920 per year |
| Gross margin | 76% |
| Standard implementation fee | $28,000 one-time |
| Approval authority — you | up to 10% off list |
| Approval authority — your VP | up to 18% off list, requires a written justification of what was received in return |
| Below 18% | not available at this scope; scope must be reduced |
| Terms available | 12, 24, or 36 months |
| Support tiers | Standard (included), Priority (list $18k/yr), Premier (list $34k/yr) |
| Training | $2,400 per day, your cost roughly $600 |
| Sandbox environments | first included; additional ones cost you nothing meaningful |
| Renewal uplift | standard 7%; caps below that need the same VP approval as discount |

### The other side of the table

Your partner plays two roles. **Priya** is broadly supportive and time-poor. **Dominic Vasquez, Senior Procurement Manager**, joins at the commercial stage and is measured on savings percentage against a vendor's opening number.

Dominic's brief (the seller does not read this before the simulation; the person playing him does):

- Open by demanding 25% off list, a 12-month term, monthly billing, and the right to reduce seats with 30 days' notice.
- Claim a competitor has quoted "around $45 a seat." Do not volunteer that the competing quote excludes implementation and claims handling, but answer honestly if asked what is in it.
- Deploy at least four of these, unannounced: the flinch, the nibble after agreement, higher authority, a manufactured deadline, item-by-item slicing.
- Insert two legal requests into the commercial list: a custom SLA with uncapped service credits, and an amendment to the limitation-of-liability clause.
- Ask for most-favored-nation pricing once, casually, as though it were routine.
- Near the end, raise a functionality concern about mobile that has never come up before. It is really about a regional manager who watched a demo recording and was unimpressed.

## Requirements

Produce five artifacts. Four are written; one is a recording.

**1. A negotiation prep sheet, written and timestamped before the simulation.** It must contain your four numbers (opening, target, walk-away, BATNA) each with a one-sentence justification; the margin cost of 5%, 10%, and 18% discounts across the full term; a concession ladder of at least three rungs with decreasing decrements, each naming what you require in return and its margin impact; the scope-reduction option that sits below your floor; and your list of cheap-to-give items with what you intend each to buy.

**2. A trade inventory for this deal.** At least ten terms sorted into accept / trade / refuse / route-to-legal, with a one-line reason each. The two legal items and the MFN request must be correctly classified.

**3. A recorded live negotiation, 30 to 40 minutes.** Unscripted, no pausing to consult a script, one take. Both roles in the room. Audio is sufficient.

**4. A confirmed agreement summary.** A same-day email containing the final package as a numbered list: price, term, billing schedule, ramp or seat commitment if any, support tier, training, renewal uplift treatment, reference commitment, and every item routed to legal. Under 250 words.

**5. A mutual action plan.** A table with at least eight steps, backwards-planned from a go-live date that clears 1 March, each with a date, an owner named on the Calder side, an owner on the Meridian side, and dependencies. The security or IT review step and the approval deadline of 30 November must both appear, and the critical path must be identified in one sentence beneath the table.

Add a short **self-review**: half a page listing every concession you made, what you received for it, and any concession for which the received column is empty.

## Constraints

- **You may not go below 18% off list.** If the buyer will not close above that, reduce scope — seats, modules, support tier, or term — and price the smaller deal. Walking away with a reduced-scope proposal is a passing outcome; an unauthorized discount is a failing one.
- **You may not negotiate contract language.** The SLA credit regime and the liability amendment must be routed to legal, unedited, with no opinion offered. Say so out loud in the recording.
- **Most-favored-nation pricing is refused.** Not deferred, not escalated — refused, with a reason you can say politely.
- **No invented facts.** You may not create discovery findings, roadmap commitments, or customer references that were not established above. "I don't know, I'll find out" is always available.
- **No fabricated urgency.** Any deadline you introduce must be real within the scenario.
- **Every concession must have its payment named in the same sentence.** The observer counts violations.
- One take. Restarting on a bad answer defeats the exercise.

## Definition of done

You are finished when all of the following are true.

- [ ] Prep sheet exists, is timestamped before the recording, and contains all four numbers with justifications.
- [ ] The concession ladder has decreasing decrements, and the concessions actually made in the recording appear on it.
- [ ] Every concession made in the recording has a named payment; the self-review's "received" column has no blanks.
- [ ] The final discount is at or above your authority limit, or scope was reduced instead.
- [ ] Both legal items were routed audibly and neither was negotiated.
- [ ] The MFN request was refused, politely and clearly.
- [ ] At least three of Dominic's tactics were recognized — you named or neutralized them rather than absorbing them.
- [ ] The mobile concern was diagnosed rather than answered: you asked what changed and reached the regional manager.
- [ ] Procurement's demands were answered as one bundled package, not item by item, at least once.
- [ ] The recording contains a clear, direct ask for the business.
- [ ] The mutual action plan has eight or more steps, all with dates and named owners on both sides, and identifies the critical path.
- [ ] The summary email was written the same day and matches the recording.
- [ ] Total contract value of your final package is stated and compared against the flat-discount alternative Dominic opened with.

## Hints

**Do the arithmetic before you dial.** At 76% margin, 25% off list removes about a third of the deal's margin. Knowing that number in advance is what makes it possible to say no calmly.

**Priya gave you the whole case in one sentence.** Four full-time operations people is her number, not yours, and at any plausible loaded cost for an operations person it is a multiple of the annual subscription — work out the multiple before you walk in, and check her four against what 500 escalations at 45 minutes each actually adds up to. Quote her to Dominic. Their own numbers are the strongest thing in the room.

**The growth plan is a lever, not a footnote.** 25% headcount growth is what makes a ramp possible: commit fewer seats in year one and more later, hold the rate, and their year-one number falls without your price moving.

**Push concessions toward the one-time items.** The $28,000 implementation fee costs you once. A point off the subscription costs you every year of the term and then becomes the renewal baseline.

**Spend the cheap things before the expensive ones.** A Premier support upgrade and four training days list at over $40,000 and cost you a fraction of that. Dominic needs a win to report; let him report that one.

**Ask for the whole list up front.** "Send me everything that's open, commercial and legal, and I'll come back with one answer" defeats slicing before it starts, and it tells you whether price is the last issue or the first of nine.

**When the competitor's price appears, ask what is in it.** $45 a seat and $62 a seat are not comparable until you know whether implementation, the claims module, and a price hold are included.

**The mobile concern is not about mobile.** It arrived late, from nowhere, in a deal that was going well. Ask what changed and go find the person who is actually worried.

**Build the action plan backwards from 1 March, not forwards from today.** The 30 November approval deadline and a multi-week IT review will do more to create urgency than anything you could say — and they will do it in Priya's voice rather than yours.

**When you have said your number, stop talking.** The most expensive sentence in this simulation is the one you add after the price.
