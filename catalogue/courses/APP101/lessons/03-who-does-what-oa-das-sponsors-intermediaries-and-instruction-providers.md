## Why this is the most-missed topic

CCC's catalog cites a 2023 practitioner survey (Safal/NAWDP) in which about half of respondents said they did not understand the difference between the federal Office of Apprenticeship and the state apprenticeship agency. That confusion has real costs. Staff send employers to the wrong office, give them the wrong timeline, or assume your board can "register" a program. It cannot.

By the end of this lesson you should be able to say, in one sentence each, what every player does and which questions belong to which player.

## The government layer

### The Office of Apprenticeship (OA)

The Office of Apprenticeship sits inside the U.S. Department of Labor's Employment and Training Administration. Think of it as the national rule-setter and promoter for Registered Apprenticeship. OA:

- Sets the national framework for what a registered program must include.
- Recognizes states that run their own apprenticeship agency.
- Registers programs directly in states that do *not* have their own agency.
- Runs national tools and outreach, including the Apprenticeship.gov website.

### DAS, the Division of Apprenticeship Standards

California is a **State Apprenticeship Agency** state. That means the main registration body here is the state, not OA. In California that agency is the Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS), part of the Department of Industrial Relations. DAS:

- Reviews and approves program standards, the written rules of a program.
- Registers individual apprentices into approved programs.
- Monitors programs and issues completion certificates.
- Works with sponsors and employers that want to start new programs.

**Plain rule:** if a California employer asks "Who do I register with?", the answer is almost always DAS.

### The councils: CAC and IACA

Two state bodies also shape apprenticeship in California, and the course catalog names both:

- The **California Apprenticeship Council (CAC)** is the state council that adopts apprenticeship regulations and advises on apprenticeship policy.
- The **Interagency Advisory Committee on Apprenticeship (IACA)** advises on apprenticeship in industries outside the traditional building trades, such as IT, healthcare and advanced manufacturing.

Confirm the current description on the DAS website before you explain them to an employer. Which body's rules apply to a given program, and how that affects registration, is covered in detail in the DAS registration course in this pathway. For now, know that both exist and that "which jurisdiction is this?" is a question for DAS, not something you decide.

## The program layer

### Sponsors

The **sponsor** is the organization that operates the registered program and is responsible for it. A sponsor can be a single employer, a group of employers, an employer association, a joint labor-management committee, or an intermediary. The sponsor holds the standards, signs the apprentice agreement, and answers to DAS.

### Intermediaries

An **intermediary** is an organization that helps employers start and run apprenticeship without building everything themselves. Intermediaries may recruit apprentices, write standards, line up instruction, handle paperwork, or act as the sponsor for a group of small employers. Industry associations, community colleges, nonprofits and some workforce boards play this role.

### Related instruction providers

The **related instruction provider** teaches the classroom or online part of the program. In California this is often a community college, adult school or other local education partner, but it can be a private provider or the employer itself, as the standards allow.

### Where your AJCC fits

Your job is **connector and funder**, not registrar. You recruit and refer candidates, help employers find a sponsor or intermediary, and may fund training or supportive services for eligible participants under WIOA.

## Worked example: one program, five players

*Valley Regional Workforce Board* (fictional) is helping launch a medical assistant apprenticeship.

| Player | Who it is in this example | What they do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OA | U.S. DOL | Sets the national framework; not directly involved because California has its own agency |
| DAS | California DAS | Approves the program standards and registers each apprentice |
| Sponsor | Central Valley Health Careers Consortium | Holds the standards and signs apprentice agreements for four clinics |
| Intermediary | The same consortium | Recruits clinics, writes the standards, coordinates instruction |
| Instruction provider | Valley Community College | Teaches the related instruction on Tuesday evenings |
| AJCC | Valley Regional AJCC | Refers candidates and funds eligible participants |

Notice that one organization can wear two hats. The consortium is both intermediary and sponsor.

## Discussion prompt

An employer says, "The workforce board approved our apprenticeship." What is wrong with that sentence, and how would you correct it politely?
