## Why the words matter

An employer tells you, "We already have an apprenticeship program." A job seeker says she "did an apprenticeship" at a clinic last summer. Either statement could mean four very different things. If you file the wrong one, you may promise funding that doesn't apply, enroll someone in the wrong service, or miss a credential the participant actually earned.

This lesson gives you a quick way to tell the four models apart. The details of contracts, funding and registration come later in this pathway. Here you only need to sort a situation into the right box.

## The four models at a glance

| | Registered Apprenticeship | Pre-apprenticeship | WIOA OJT | Internship |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main purpose | Train a worker into a skilled occupation while employed | Prepare someone to enter and succeed in an apprenticeship | Help an employer hire and train a new worker | Short-term exposure to a workplace |
| Is the person employed? | Yes, from day one, as an apprentice | Usually not yet | Yes, hired by the employer | Sometimes; may be paid or unpaid |
| Structured classroom learning? | Yes, required related instruction | Yes, readiness and basic skills | Not required | Rarely |
| Wage progression built in? | Yes, wages rise as skills rise | No | No | No |
| Credential at the end? | Yes, a nationally recognized completion certificate | A certificate of completion, not an occupational credential | No formal credential | No |
| Registered with a government agency? | Yes (in California, through DAS) | No, though quality programs link to registered ones | No; it is a WIOA contract | No |

## Registered Apprenticeship

Registered Apprenticeship is an employer-driven earn-and-learn model that has been approved and registered by a government apprenticeship agency. Every registered program combines the same core parts:

- **Employer involvement.** The employer, or a group of employers, shapes the program and hires the apprentice.
- **Structured on-the-job learning.** A mentor teaches defined work processes on the job.
- **Related instruction.** Classroom, online or lab learning tied to the occupation.
- **Rewards for skill gains.** The apprentice's wage steps up on a schedule as they progress.
- **A credential.** Completers earn a nationally recognized, portable certificate.

The word to listen for is *registered*. If the program cannot show you its registration, it is not a Registered Apprenticeship, however good it is.

## Pre-apprenticeship

Pre-apprenticeship gets people *ready* for a registered program. It may teach math, safety, digital skills, or what the work is really like. A quality pre-apprenticeship has a working relationship with one or more registered programs, so completers have a real path in. It does not pay apprentice wages and does not award the occupational credential. You'll go deeper on pre-apprenticeship in a later course in this pathway.

## On-the-Job Training (OJT)

WIOA OJT is a contract between your board and an employer. The employer hires a participant, trains them, and is partially reimbursed for the extra cost of training. The federal OJT rules are in 20 CFR 680.700–680.730; your board's OJT policy sets the local details. OJT can stand alone, or it can be used to support the on-the-job part of an apprenticeship. On its own, though, OJT has no required classroom component, no wage schedule, and no credential.

## Internships

An internship is short-term work experience. It may be paid or unpaid, and it is usually aimed at exposure and references rather than a credential. Under WIOA, internships often show up as work experience, especially for youth. Check your board's work experience policy for how they are set up and paid.

## Worked example: sorting real conversations

| What you hear | Most likely model | What to ask next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Maria did a 10-week program at a hospital, then got hired." | Internship or pre-apprenticeship | "Were you paid? Did you get a certificate, and from whom?" |
| "Pinewood Machining wants us to cover part of a new hire's training cost." | OJT | "Is the role part of a registered program? Could it become one?" |
| "Our IT help-desk apprentices take a class every Thursday and get a raise every six months." | Possibly Registered Apprenticeship | "Is the program registered with DAS? Who is the sponsor?" |
| "The community college runs a six-week course that feeds the electricians' program." | Pre-apprenticeship | "Which registered programs accept your completers?" |

Notice that the next question is almost always about **registration** and **who runs it**. That is the habit to build.

## Practice

1. Read each case and name the model. Write one sentence explaining your choice.
   - Devon, 19, attends a 12-week course on tools and job-site safety that guarantees him an interview with a registered carpentry program.
   - Lakeview Medical Group hires Ana as a medical assistant. Your board reimburses part of her training cost for three months. There is no classroom part.
   - Sierra Tech Services hires Jordan as an IT support apprentice. He attends evening classes at the community college, his pay rises at set milestones, and his program is registered.
   - Kim spends eight unpaid weeks shadowing staff at a school district office.
2. For the case you found hardest, write the one question you would ask the customer to settle it.
3. Write a two-sentence explanation of the difference between Registered Apprenticeship and OJT that you could say to an employer over the phone.
