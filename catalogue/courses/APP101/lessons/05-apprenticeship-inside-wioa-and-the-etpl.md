## The question you'll get

A sponsor calls: "We run a registered IT support apprenticeship. One of our new apprentices is a WIOA participant. Can your board help pay for his classes? Do we have to apply to some list first?"

To answer, you need two facts: what the Eligible Training Provider List (ETPL) is, and why registered apprenticeship programs get onto it differently from every other provider.

## What the ETPL does

Under WIOA Title I, when your board funds occupational training for an adult or dislocated worker through an Individual Training Account (ITA), the training generally has to come from a program on the state's ETPL. The list is a quality gate. A college, private school or other provider normally has to apply, meet the state's criteria, and keep reporting performance to stay on it.

California's current ETPL directive is **WSD25-02**. It is the document to check for how the list is run in this state.

## Why registered programs are automatically eligible

WIOA itself treats Registered Apprenticeship differently. The federal rule (20 CFR 680.470) says a registered program is automatically eligible for the ETPL. It does not have to go through the regular initial application and performance review that other providers face. WSD25-02 carries that rule into California's ETPL process.

The reasoning is simple once you see it:

| Other training providers | Registered Apprenticeship programs |
| --- | --- |
| Quality is checked *by the ETPL process* | Quality has already been checked *by the registration agency* (DAS in California) |
| Must show results to earn a place | Already built around a job, a wage schedule and a recognized credential |
| Eligibility depends on meeting state criteria | Eligibility follows from being registered |

In other words, registration *is* the quality review. WIOA does not make an approved program prove itself twice.

Two points to keep straight:

- **Automatic eligibility is not the same as being listed.** The program generally has to tell the state it wants to be on the list and provide basic program information. Check WSD25-02 for exactly how a sponsor does this in California.
- **Eligibility follows registration.** If a program stops being registered, its automatic eligibility goes with it. Confirm the program's current registration with DAS before you rely on its ETPL listing.

## What this means for your participants

Once a registered program is on the ETPL, your board can use an ITA to help an eligible participant with training costs in that program, subject to your board's ITA policy, including any local cost limits.

Apprenticeship also connects to WIOA in other ways, such as OJT for the work-based part, supportive services, and recruiting candidates through the AJCC. How to braid those funding streams and how to record apprentices correctly in CalJOBS are covered in other courses in this pathway. For this lesson, the key point is the ETPL link.

## Worked example

Back to the call. Sam, a business services representative at a fictional *Valley Regional AJCC*, answers the sponsor:

> "Because your program is registered, it's automatically eligible for California's ETPL. You don't have to go through the regular application and performance review. You do need to let the state know you want to be listed. I'll send you the steps from WSD25-02. Once you're listed, if your apprentice is eligible for WIOA, his career specialist can look at an ITA for his classes under our board's policy."

Notice what Sam did *not* say: he did not promise a dollar amount, and he did not say the listing was already done.

## Discussion prompt

A coworker says, "Apprenticeships get a free pass onto the ETPL." What is right about that statement, and what is misleading?
