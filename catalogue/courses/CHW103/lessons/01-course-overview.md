## Description

You already know what a community health worker does. This course is where you learn to do it. Everything here is drill and practice against behaviors someone can watch you perform: the words you choose, the questions you ask, the way you check that a client actually understood you, the notes you write afterward, and the judgment calls you make when a conversation goes somewhere you did not plan.

The work is arranged the way skill builds. Communication comes first, because every other skill in the CHW role rides on it. Then you practice the relationship skills that make a client willing to keep talking to you — trust, motivational interviewing, ambivalence, boundaries. From there you move into the applied service skills: navigation and coaching, advocacy and health education, needs assessment, outreach, and documentation. The course closes with the professional foundation that keeps all of it legitimate — confidentiality, supervision, mandated reporting, accurate program data, and your part in evaluating whether the program is working.

Expect to spend most of your time in role-play, not reading. Each lesson gives you worked dialogue to study, then asks you to run the same exchange yourself with a partner, get feedback, and run it again. One boundary applies to every scenario in this course: you do not diagnose and you do not give medical advice. Your job is to explain, listen, check understanding, connect, document, and refer — and to be very clear with yourself about where that line sits.

## Objectives

- Apply plain-language communication, active listening, and teach-back with clients
- Use motivational interviewing and relationship-building skills while maintaining boundaries
- Apply navigation, advocacy, education, assessment, outreach, documentation, and evaluation skills in practice scenarios
