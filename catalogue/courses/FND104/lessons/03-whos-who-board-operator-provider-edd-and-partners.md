## Same building, different employers

In the last lesson you saw the system in layers. This lesson is about the people you will actually meet. In many AJCCs, the person at the front desk, the person running the resource room, the career services specialist, and the employment services staff member down the hall each work for a different organization. Knowing who works for whom tells you who can answer a question, who can approve something, and who you should never ask to do your job for you.

## The five roles you need to know

### Local Workforce Development Board

The Local Board sets direction for the whole local area. Its members are appointed locally and include business representatives along with labor, education, and community members. Board staff support the board's work.

What the board does that affects you:

- Writes local policies (for example, on eligibility documentation, training, and supportive services).
- Selects the AJCC Operator and the providers of career services.
- Oversees performance and monitors providers, including reviewing case files.

What the board usually does not do: work directly with your participants day to day. If board staff are reviewing your files, that is oversight, not supervision.

### AJCC Operator

The Operator coordinates the center. WIOA requires the operator to be selected competitively. Depending on the local area, the Operator may be a public agency, a nonprofit, a consortium of partners, or a private company.

Typical Operator responsibilities:

- Coordinating partners in the center so services fit together.
- Managing shared processes such as front-desk flow, referrals between partners, and the center's calendar.
- Reporting to the board on how the center is working.

The Operator coordinates; it does not necessarily employ the career services staff. Ask your supervisor what the Operator does in your specific area, because the scope is set by the board's contract.

### Career Services Provider

The Career Services Provider delivers WIOA Title I services to participants: eligibility, assessment, the individual employment plan, case management, and referral to training. For many new hires, this is your employer.

Your provider's own procedures sit underneath the board's local policy. If your provider's desk guide and the board's policy seem to disagree, raise it with your supervisor; do not pick one on your own.

### EDD

The Employment Development Department plays two roles you will notice:

- **State administrator:** EDD's Workforce Services Division issues the Workforce Services Directives that tell local areas how to run WIOA programs, and it administers CalJOBS.
- **Service partner:** EDD staff in many AJCCs deliver Wagner-Peyser employment services and may connect people to Unemployment Insurance information.

So "EDD" might mean the agency writing the directive you are reading, or the colleague down the hall. Be clear which one you mean when you ask a question.

### Partners

Partners are the other programs connected to the AJCC: adult education, vocational rehabilitation, programs for veterans, older workers, and others. The required partners and the local area agree on how they work together and share costs, usually in a memorandum of understanding (MOU). The partner list in your center is a local fact you should look up, not assume.

## A one-page roles map

Here is how Jordan, our new specialist at the fictional Riverbend AJCC, filled in a roles map in week one.

| Role | Organization at Riverbend | What I go to them for | Contact |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Local Board | Valley Regional Workforce Board | Local policy questions (through my supervisor) | Board policy page |
| AJCC Operator | Riverbend Partners Consortium | Room bookings, partner referral process, center events | Operator coordinator |
| Career Services Provider | Northfield Career Services (my employer) | My supervisor, procedures, approvals | My supervisor |
| EDD | EDD staff on site; Workforce Services Division for directives | Employment services referrals; CalJOBS help desk | Front desk list |
| Partners | Adult school, Department of Rehabilitation counselor (Tuesdays), veterans representative | Warm referrals | Partner contact sheet |

Notice that Jordan does not go to the board directly with policy questions. In most local areas, policy questions go through your supervisor so answers stay consistent. Check how your area handles this.

## Common mix-ups

- **"The Operator is my boss."** Maybe, maybe not. Check who signs your paycheck.
- **"EDD will handle that."** Which part of EDD? A directive question and a walk-in referral go to very different places.
- **"That partner isn't here, so I can't refer."** Partners can be connected by referral or technology even when they are not on site. Ask how your center connects to off-site partners.

## Discussion prompt

Share with a peer or mentor: which role surprised you most when you learned who fills it in your local area?

## Practice

Build the roles map for your own center, using the table above as a template.

1. Fill in all five rows with the real organization names for your local area.
2. For each row, write one thing you would go to them for and how you would reach them.
3. Ask your supervisor to confirm your map. Correct anything they change.
4. Write a two-sentence answer, in plain language, to a participant who asks, "Who runs this place?"
5. Save the map as part of your onboarding record.
