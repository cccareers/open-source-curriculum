## Why access comes with conditions

CalJOBS is California's system of record for WIOA services. Participant applications, eligibility, services, case notes, and outcomes all live there, and the data flows up into state and federal reporting. It also holds a great deal of personal information: Social Security numbers, dates of birth, income, disability status, and more.

Because of that, you do not get a CalJOBS staff account just by being hired. You get it after you meet the access requirements, and you keep it only as long as you keep meeting them. This lesson walks you through those requirements and, just as important, why they exist.

## The security and confidentiality training requirement

California requires staff who access participant data to complete security and confidentiality training. The state directive that sets this requirement is **WSD24-12**. Read the current version of that directive yourself; it is the authority for who must be trained, what the training covers, and how often it must be repeated. Your board or provider may add local requirements on top.

Do not rely on a colleague's summary of WSD24-12, including this lesson's. Directives get revised. Check the current version on EDD's directives page and confirm with your supervisor which training your local area uses.

## The typical access path

The exact steps vary by local area. Most follow this general sequence. Use it as a checklist, and fill in your local details.

| Step | What happens | Your local detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Request | Your supervisor requests a staff account for you through your area's CalJOBS administrator | Who is our administrator? |
| 2. Train | You complete the required security and confidentiality training (see WSD24-12) | Which course? Where is it? |
| 3. Acknowledge | You sign any confidentiality or user acknowledgment your area requires | Which form? Who keeps it? |
| 4. Provision | The administrator sets up your account with the access level your job needs | What level am I getting? |
| 5. First login | You log in, set your credentials, and confirm you can see what you need | Did anything fail? |
| 6. Maintain | You repeat training on the required schedule and report changes in your role | When is my next renewal? |

Keep a copy of your training completion record. If anyone asks whether you were trained before you touched participant data, you should be able to show it.

## Why each requirement matters

It is easy to treat these steps as hurdles. They are not. Each one protects someone.

- **Training protects participants.** People share private information because they need help. A breach can expose them to identity theft or worse. Training is how you learn what counts as protected information and how to handle it.
- **Individual accounts create accountability.** CalJOBS records who did what. If you share your login, every action under your name is yours, including mistakes you did not make.
- **Role-based access limits damage.** You get the access your job requires, not more. That limits what can go wrong if an account is misused.
- **Renewal keeps knowledge current.** Rules and threats change. Refresher training keeps everyone working from the same standard.
- **Accurate data keeps the system funded.** The records you enter become the performance results your local area reports. Careless entry has consequences far beyond one file.

## Everyday habits that keep you compliant

Training will cover the formal rules. These habits are where most problems are prevented on a normal day:

- Lock your screen every time you step away, even for a minute.
- Never share your password, and never use someone else's login, even "just to check something."
- Do not leave documents with personal information on your desk, the printer, or the copier.
- Do not send personal information by unsecured email or text. Follow your area's approved method for sending documents.
- Talk about participants only with people who need to know, and not in hallways or the lobby.
- If you think personal information was exposed, tell your supervisor right away. Your area will have an incident procedure; follow it, and do not try to fix it quietly yourself.

## Worked example

Jordan's account was approved, but on day three a coworker offers to "let you use my login so you can start entering notes." Jordan declines, explains that the training is not finished yet, and tells the supervisor the account is still pending. The supervisor checks with the CalJOBS administrator and pairs Jordan with the coworker to observe instead. Jordan loses a day of data entry and avoids a security violation that would have been recorded under someone else's name.

## Discussion prompt

With a mentor or peer, discuss: what is one situation in your center where it would be tempting to cut a corner on confidentiality? What would you do instead?

## Practice

Add your CalJOBS access steps to your onboarding record.

1. Fill in the "Your local detail" column of the access table for your area.
2. Find the current version of WSD24-12 on EDD's directives page. Write down its title and the date you checked it.
3. Record the date you completed security and confidentiality training and when your next renewal is due.
4. Write three sentences, in plain language, that you could say to a participant who asks, "Who can see my information?" Check them with your supervisor.
5. Write down your area's procedure for reporting a possible data exposure, including who you would call first.
