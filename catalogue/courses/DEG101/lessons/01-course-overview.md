## Description

Some of the people you serve want a career now and a degree someday, and they assume they have to pick one. An apprenticeship degree lets a person earn a paycheck, a journey-level credential, and college credit at the same time. This course shows you how that works and how to talk about it honestly with a participant.

You will learn how the learning that happens on the job and in related instruction can turn into college credit, what California's early apprenticeship-degree models look like, how to compare the cost, time, and earnings of each path with a participant, and where WIOA training funds and the ETPL fit. One running case, a paraeducator who wants to become a teacher, ties the lessons together.

California's apprenticeship-degree landscape is still young, so a large part of this course is learning what to verify before you promise anything. You will leave knowing which questions to ask a college, a sponsor, and your own board, and where to find the current answers.

## Objectives

- Explain how apprenticeship hours and related instruction can convert to college credit.
- Describe California's current apprenticeship-degree models and their requirements.
- Advise a participant comparing a traditional degree, an apprenticeship, and an apprenticeship degree on cost, time, and earnings.
- Identify how WIOA training funds and ETPL rules apply to degree-linked apprenticeships.
