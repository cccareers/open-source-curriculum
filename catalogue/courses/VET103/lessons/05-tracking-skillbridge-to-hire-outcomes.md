## Why tracking matters

CCC's catalog notes that GAO found DoD lacks post-service employment data for SkillBridge and that the workforce system is largely absent from the program. That gap is your opportunity. If your board can show which SkillBridge participants were hired, entered apprenticeships, and stayed employed, you can make the case to employers, to your board, and to installation partners that this work pays off.

Two audiences need outcome information, and they want different things.

| Audience | What they want to know |
| --- | --- |
| The employer | Did participants finish? Did we hire them? Are they still here? Is this helping our veteran hiring and apprenticeship pipeline? |
| The board | How many employers did we coach? How many participants did we connect to Title I or apprenticeship? What were their employment and wage outcomes? |

## What to track

Keep a simple tracker for each employer you coach. A spreadsheet is fine; follow your board's rules on where data may be stored and who can see it.

**Employer-level fields**

- Employer name, contact, and staff owner
- Coaching milestones: first meeting, application submitted, provider approval, first participant placed
- Linked apprenticeship program (if any)
- Medallion interest and application status

**Participant-level fields** (only what you are allowed to collect, and only with consent where your board requires it)

- SkillBridge start and end dates
- Completed: yes / no
- Job offer made / accepted
- Hire date and starting wage
- Entered Registered Apprenticeship: yes / no, and date
- Enrolled in WIOA Title I: yes / no
- Still employed at follow-up checkpoints your board chooses

## Connecting to WIOA records

When a SkillBridge participant enrolls in a WIOA Title I program, your normal case management and performance reporting apply, and their outcomes flow into your state system and the federal participant record (the PIRL). Your tracker should not duplicate that record. Instead, use it to link employer-side milestones (provider approval, cohort, job offer) with the participant's enrollment so you can tell the full story.

Several details depend on local and state rules. Confirm with your board's MIS or data lead:

- Whether and how to flag a participant as a SkillBridge referral in your case management system
- What consent or release you need before sharing outcome data with an employer
- How veteran priority of service and JVSG staff roles are recorded

Never share a participant's personal information or WIOA records with an employer. Give employers **aggregate** results ("four of five participants were hired") unless the participant has agreed otherwise in the way your board requires.

## Worked example

Valley Regional Workforce Board's quarterly SkillBridge summary for its business services committee (fictional data):

| Measure | This quarter |
| --- | --- |
| Employers coached | 6 |
| Employers approved as providers | 2 |
| Participants placed | 5 |
| Completed SkillBridge | 5 |
| Hired by host employer | 4 |
| Entered Registered Apprenticeship | 3 |
| Enrolled in WIOA Title I | 3 |

And the one-paragraph employer update to Coastal Valley Logistics: "Your first cohort of three completed; you hired all three, and two are now registered diesel technician apprentices. We'll check in again at your six-month mark."

## Discussion prompt

What's one outcome your board leadership would most want to see from SkillBridge employer work? Where would that number come from today?

## Practice

1. Build a tracker (on paper or in a spreadsheet) with the employer-level and participant-level fields above. Add a column noting which fields come from the employer, which from the participant, and which from your case management system.
2. Using made-up data for two employers and four participants, write a quarterly summary table for your board and a two-sentence aggregate update for one employer.
3. List the questions you will take to your board's MIS lead about flagging SkillBridge referrals and sharing data.
