## Why employers need a coach

VET 101 covered SkillBridge from the service member's side: a participant spends part of their final 180 days of service training with an employer while still on military pay, with their command's approval. This lesson flips the view. When an employer says "we'd love to host one of those," your job is to explain what they are signing up for and help them get through the door.

Most employers stall for one of three reasons:

- They think they have to hire the service member as an employee during the program. They do not; the participant remains a service member.
- They don't know there is an application and a formal agreement, so they try to arrange something informally with a single service member and get stuck.
- They don't realize the program expects a genuine path to a job afterwards.

## The two pieces: the application and the MOU

Becoming an authorized provider has two connected parts.

**1. The provider application.** The employer applies through the DoD SkillBridge program office (the official SkillBridge website hosts the current application and instructions). The application describes the organization and the opportunity it will offer: what the participant will do, where, for how long, and what job it leads to. Help the employer prepare their answers before they open the form.

**2. The agreement (MOU).** An approved provider enters a memorandum of understanding that sets out its obligations. You do not need to recite the MOU language; you need to make sure the employer understands the obligations before they sign. The obligation employers most often miss, and the one VET 101 flagged, is the **post-service job-offer requirement**: the opportunity is expected to lead to employment, not just to be a training experience.

Requirements, review steps, and timelines change. Before you coach an employer, pull the current application and MOU guidance from the DoD SkillBridge website rather than relying on a colleague's notes or this lesson.

## What to help the employer prepare

Use this checklist in your first coaching meeting. Each item is something the employer will need to describe or commit to; confirm the exact wording the current application asks for.

| Topic | Question to ask the employer |
| --- | --- |
| The role | What job does this lead to, and do you expect to have openings? |
| The training | What will the participant actually learn and do, week by week? |
| Supervision | Who will supervise and mentor the participant day to day? |
| Location | Where will the training happen, and is it reachable from nearby installations? |
| Duration | How long is the experience, and does it fit inside the participant's eligible window? |
| Cost | Can you deliver it at no cost to the service member? |
| Hiring commitment | Are you prepared to make a job offer to a participant who completes successfully? |
| Point of contact | Who in your company owns the program and answers DoD and command questions? |

If the employer cannot answer "the role" and "hiring commitment" with confidence, pause. Help them pick a position with real, recurring demand before they apply. An application built around a job that may not exist sets up the participant, and your relationship, to fail.

## Where your AJCC fits

You are a coach, not a party to the agreement. The application and MOU are between the employer and DoD. What you bring:

- **Labor-market context** showing which roles the employer struggles to fill.
- **Help drafting the training description** in plain, specific terms.
- **Connections** to installation transition offices and to your LVERs, so the employer can reach candidates once approved.
- **A follow-up plan** (Lessons 3 to 5) so SkillBridge is not a dead end.

Keep a simple contact note for each employer you coach. For example:

> **Coaching note, Sierra Ridge Electric (fictional).** Met with HR lead Dana Ortiz. Target role: electrical apprentice / helper; 6 to 8 openings a year. Dana assumed participants go on payroll; clarified that they stay on military pay. Reviewed provider application topics and job-offer expectation. Next step: Dana drafts training description; I send installation transition office contact. Follow-up in two weeks.
