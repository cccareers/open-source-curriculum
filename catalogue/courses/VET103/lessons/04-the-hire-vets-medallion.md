## What the Medallion is

The HIRE Vets Medallion is a U.S. Department of Labor award, created under the HIRE Vets Act, that recognizes employers for recruiting, employing, and retaining veterans. Employers apply for a specific award year, and DOL reviews the application against published criteria. Many employers value it because it is a federal recognition they can use in recruiting and marketing.

For you, the Medallion is a conversation tool. It gives an employer a concrete goal, and a SkillBridge-to-apprenticeship pathway is one of the most direct ways to work toward it.

## How the criteria are structured

You don't need to memorize thresholds. You need to understand the shape of the criteria so you can help an employer judge whether they're close.

| Feature | What to know |
| --- | --- |
| Award levels | There is more than one award tier (Gold and Platinum). Higher tiers need stronger results. |
| Employer size | Criteria differ by employer size (small, medium, large), so a small business is not measured like a large corporation. |
| Hiring | A share of the employer's new hires during the review period must be veterans. |
| Retention | The employer must show it keeps veteran employees over time. |
| Supporting practices | Additional criteria cover workplace practices such as veteran employee groups, leadership or training programs, dedicated HR staff for veterans, and support for employees in the Guard or Reserve. |
| Application | Employers apply online during the open application period; a fee applies. |

Exact percentages, which practices count at which tier, size cut-offs, fees, and dates are set by DOL for each award year. **Always pull the current criteria from HIREVets.gov** before you advise an employer, and let the employer make their own determination about whether they qualify.

## How SkillBridge supports the criteria

Help the employer connect what they're already doing with what the Medallion measures.

- **Hiring.** A SkillBridge participant who completes the program and accepts the job offer is a veteran when they start as an employee. A steady SkillBridge pipeline can increase the employer's veteran hiring numbers. Have the employer confirm how DOL counts hires for the award year.
- **Retention.** Participants who have already trained with the employer, and who move straight into a Registered Apprenticeship with a wage progression and a credential, have strong reasons to stay. Lesson 3's design supports retention.
- **Supporting practices.** Running a SkillBridge program often leads employers to set up the practices the Medallion looks at: a named veteran program lead in HR, structured mentoring, and an apprenticeship that works as a training and development path.

Be honest about limits. SkillBridge does not earn the Medallion on its own. An employer with one participant a year will need more than that to move its hiring percentage.

## Worked example

Valley Regional Workforce Board's LVER, Marcus, meets with Sierra Ridge Electric (fictional, about 120 employees). They currently hire a handful of veterans a year through referrals. Marcus:

1. Pulls the current medium-employer criteria from HIREVets.gov.
2. Asks HR for last year's total hires and veteran hires, and how many veterans are still employed.
3. Lists the supporting practices Sierra Ridge already has (a veteran mentor informally assigned) and the ones it could add (formalizing that role, documenting apprenticeship as a training path).
4. Shows how hosting two SkillBridge cohorts a year would add to veteran hires.

He frames it as "here's where you stand and what would move you," not as a promise of the award.

## Discussion prompt

Which employers on your list would be most motivated by the Medallion? What would you need to know about their hiring before you raise it?

## Practice

For one employer you serve (or the fictional Coastal Valley Logistics, about 300 employees):

1. Note which size category they likely fall into and which current criteria you would look up for it on HIREVets.gov.
2. List the hiring and retention data you would ask the employer for, and who in the company would have it.
3. Write two or three sentences explaining to the employer how a SkillBridge-to-apprenticeship pathway could help with hiring, retention, and supporting practices, without promising they will win the award.
