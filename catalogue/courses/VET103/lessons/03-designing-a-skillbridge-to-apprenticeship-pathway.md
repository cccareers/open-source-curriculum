## The idea: no gap between service and an earn-and-learn job

A SkillBridge placement on its own ends when the service member separates. A Registered Apprenticeship gives them a paid job with structured training and a recognized credential. When you design the two together, the last weeks of military service become the on-ramp to the apprenticeship, and the participant starts their civilian career already enrolled in a program instead of starting from zero.

You already know the apprenticeship building blocks from APP 101: a sponsor, a registered program with standards, on-the-job learning, related technical instruction, and a wage progression. This lesson is about lining SkillBridge up with those blocks.

## Three design questions

Work through these with the employer in order.

**1. Is there a registered program to flow into?**

- If the employer already sponsors a Registered Apprenticeship, the SkillBridge experience should mirror its early on-the-job learning.
- If they participate through an intermediary or group sponsor, bring that sponsor into the conversation early.
- If there is no program, the apprenticeship must be built or joined first. Connect the employer with your apprenticeship staff or the state apprenticeship agency; in California, that is the Division of Apprenticeship Standards.

**2. What does the SkillBridge period cover?**

Map the SkillBridge weeks to the first part of the apprenticeship's work process schedule. Choose tasks a new apprentice would do in their first months, so the participant arrives on day one of the apprenticeship already productive.

**3. Can SkillBridge time count toward the apprenticeship?**

This depends on the program's standards and on how the sponsor and the registration agency treat pre-registration training. Some programs may give credit for prior learning or experience; others will not. Never promise credit. Have the sponsor confirm with their apprenticeship consultant, and write the answer down.

## Lining up the timeline

| Phase | What happens | Who owns it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before SkillBridge | Employer is an authorized provider; apprenticeship program is registered or identified; participant has command approval | Employer, DoD, service member |
| SkillBridge weeks | Participant trains on tasks from the early work process schedule; mentor assigned | Employer |
| Final weeks of service | Job offer made; apprenticeship agreement prepared; your AJCC checks WIOA eligibility for support | Employer, sponsor, AJCC |
| Day one after separation | Participant starts as a paid employee and registered apprentice | Employer, sponsor |
| First months | Related instruction begins; any support services are in place | Sponsor, training provider, AJCC |

The key design principle: **the job offer and apprenticeship enrollment are ready before separation**, so there is no unpaid gap.

## Worked example

Coastal Valley Logistics (fictional) wants diesel technicians. It joins a group-sponsored diesel technician apprenticeship run by a regional training trust. Business services staff at Valley Regional Workforce Board help design the pathway:

- SkillBridge period: participant shadows and then performs preventive maintenance inspections and basic brake work, both early tasks in the apprenticeship's work process schedule.
- Mentor: a senior technician who also serves as the journey-level mentor in the apprenticeship.
- Credit: the trust's coordinator confirms with the apprenticeship consultant whether documented SkillBridge hours can be credited. Answer recorded in the employer file.
- Hand-off: offer letter and apprenticeship paperwork prepared in the participant's final weeks; the AJCC schedules a WIOA eligibility appointment for related-instruction support.

## Watch-outs

- **Wage progression starts at hire, not during SkillBridge.** The participant is on military pay during SkillBridge; the apprenticeship wage scale applies once they are an employee.
- **Don't design around one person.** Build a repeatable pathway the employer can use for every cohort.
- **Check installation timing.** Agree a realistic start date with the employer that fits commands' approval timelines.
