## Description

Almost every application you will ever support runs on a computer somebody else owns, in a building you will never enter, on hardware you cannot touch. That arrangement is what people mean by "the cloud," and it changes the job in one specific way: you stop asking what the machine is doing and start asking what you are responsible for. This course is about learning to answer that question quickly and correctly.

You will start with the vocabulary that makes the rest of the field readable — the service models, the responsibility boundary, the way one physical server is subdivided into virtual machines, containers, and functions — and then work through the two service families every cloud workload touches no matter who sells it: storage and identity. Along the way you will learn to price a workload, deploy a small application to a real cloud environment, verify it is actually serving traffic, and explain any of it to a customer who does not want a lecture.

This course is deliberately not a product tour. AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud appear constantly, but always as three instances of the same idea, never as the subject itself. The names change every eighteen months; the concepts underneath them have barely moved in a decade. Learn the concepts here, and picking up whichever platform your employer actually runs becomes a lookup problem rather than a relearning problem.

## Objectives

- Distinguish IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS by what the provider operates and what the customer remains responsible for
- Explain how virtualization, containers, and serverless functions divide physical hardware into usable compute
- Compare the core service families of the major cloud providers without depending on any one platform
- Choose a cloud storage class and lifecycle policy that fits a workload's access pattern
- Apply least-privilege identity and access management to a cloud account
- Estimate what a cloud workload costs and explain the business case for running it in the cloud
- Deploy a simple application to a cloud environment and verify it is serving traffic
- Explain a cloud concept or a cloud bill to a non-technical customer in plain language
