## What a case note is for

A case note is the record that a service happened, what it was, and what came next. Someone who has never met the participant, such as a monitor, a validator, or the case manager who inherits your caseload, should be able to read your notes and reconstruct the story. CCC's catalog notes that San Joaquin WorkNet's Directive 23-04 lists opinions in notes and late codes among the repeated staff errors it found. Both are habits you can fix.

Good case notes have three properties:

- **Factual.** They record what was observed, said, or provided, not your judgment of the person.
- **Timely.** They are written close to the event, within the timeframe your board's local policy sets.
- **Tied to an activity code.** Each note supports a specific service or activity recorded in CalJOBS, so the note and the code tell the same story.

## Fact versus opinion

| Opinion (avoid) | Fact (use) |
| --- | --- |
| Participant seems unmotivated. | Participant missed scheduled appointments on [date] and [date]; did not respond to two voicemails. |
| He has a bad attitude about training. | Participant stated he does not want to attend classes before 10 a.m. |
| She is doing great in class. | Provider attendance sheet shows 18 of 20 sessions attended; instructor reports passing grade on module 2 quiz. |
| Probably will drop out. | Participant reported a new evening job that conflicts with the class schedule. Discussed options; follow-up set for [date]. |

A simple test: could the participant read the note and agree that it describes what happened? If they would dispute your characterization but not the facts, rewrite it as facts. Quote the participant when their exact words matter, and attribute information to its source ("per provider," "participant reported").

## A pattern for every note

Use the same four parts each time so nothing is left out:

1. **Contact:** date, type (in person, phone, email, virtual), and who was involved.
2. **Service or activity:** what was provided, matched to the activity code you are recording.
3. **Outcome:** what happened, what was learned, what documents were received.
4. **Next step:** what the participant and you will do, with a date.

Example note:

> [date] In-person appointment with participant. Provided career guidance on medical assistant pathways (supports career counseling activity entered today). Reviewed local MA job postings; participant identified three clinics hiring. Participant provided high school transcript (scanned to file). Next step: participant to complete provider orientation on [date]; case manager to follow up by phone on [date].

## Tying notes to activity codes

Every activity you enter should have at least one note that explains it, and every note that describes a service should match an activity in the system. Before you save, check:

- Is the activity code for this service entered, with the right date?
- Does the note's date match the activity date?
- Would a reader know from the note why this activity was appropriate (link it to the IEP or ISS goal)?

The specific activity codes and their definitions come from CalJOBS and state guidance. Check your local code list or the current CalJOBS activity code guidance rather than relying on memory.

## Timeliness

A note written two weeks later from memory is less accurate and, to a reviewer, less credible. Your board's local policy sets the deadline for entering notes and activities. Build a habit: notes go in the same day, before you close the participant's record.

## Discussion prompt

What makes it hard to write notes on time in your office? Name one change to your daily routine that would help.

## Practice

Rewrite each of these notes so it is factual, uses the four-part pattern, and points to an activity. Invent reasonable details and use [date] placeholders.

1. "Called Jamal. He still doesn't have his act together about the forklift class."
2. "Ana came in, nice lady, very eager. Gave her some job leads."
3. "Training going ok I think. Will check in later."

Then answer: for each rewritten note, which activity would you record in CalJOBS, and what date would you give it?
