## Why the IEP has to show its work

For Adult and Dislocated Worker participants, the Individual Employment Plan (IEP) is the document that explains why a service makes sense for this person. For Youth, the same job is done by the Individual Service Strategy (ISS). When a participant moves into training or an on-the-job training (OJT) placement, a reviewer will open the plan and ask one question: does this file show that the person has the interest, the aptitude, and the skills (or the ability to gain them) to succeed in this specific occupation?

A plan that says only "Participant wants to be a medical assistant" does not answer that question. A plan that connects the goal to assessment results does. The objective assessment is the evidence; the IEP is where you show the reasoning that links that evidence to the goal.

## Three things the plan must connect

| Element | What it answers | Typical evidence from the objective assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interest | Does the participant actually want this work, and do they know what it involves? | Interest inventory results, labor market research the participant completed, informational interview notes |
| Aptitude | Is the participant likely to do well in the training or job? | Basic skills test scores, prior coursework, work history in related tasks |
| Skills | What can they already do, and what is the gap the service will close? | Work history, transferable skills review, credentials held, employer requirements for the occupation |

The plan also needs the barriers you identified and how the plan addresses them (supportive services, scheduling, referrals). Your board's local policy sets which assessment tools you use and which IEP fields are required in CalJOBS, so confirm the required elements against your local IEP or ISS procedure before you finalize a plan.

## From assessment to plan, step by step

1. **Pull the assessment results together.** Before you write, list every result you have: test scores, inventories, work history, education, barriers.
2. **State the employment goal in occupational terms.** "Entry-level medical assistant in a clinic setting," not "a job in healthcare."
3. **Write one sentence each for interest, aptitude, and skills**, and name the evidence in each sentence.
4. **Name the gap and the service that closes it.** Training, OJT, work experience, or another service. Explain why this service, not another.
5. **Set measurable steps with target dates.** Each step should be something you could later point to in a case note.
6. **Have the participant review and agree to the plan**, following your board's signature or acknowledgment procedure.

## Worked example

Maria, a laid-off medical billing clerk with the Valley Regional Workforce Board, wants to train as a medical assistant.

Weak plan language:

> Maria is interested in medical assisting and would be a good fit. Will enroll in MA training.

Stronger plan language:

> **Goal:** Entry-level clinical medical assistant in an outpatient clinic.
> **Interest:** Maria's interest inventory ranked healthcare support among her top areas. She completed labor market research on local MA openings and interviewed a clinic office manager (see case note dated [date]).
> **Aptitude:** Basic skills assessment results meet the training provider's entry requirement (scores in file). She completed medical terminology coursework in her prior job.
> **Skills:** Six years in medical billing gives her coding, insurance, and patient-communication skills. Gap: clinical skills (vitals, phlebotomy) and the credential employers request.
> **Service:** Occupational skills training in medical assisting, because the clinical gap requires structured instruction and a credential that OJT at a billing office would not provide.
> **Barrier:** Childcare during evening labs; referral to supportive services.

Notice that every claim points at something a reviewer can find in the file.

## Training versus OJT

The same logic applies to an OJT placement, but the skills section looks different. For OJT, document what the participant cannot yet do that the employer will teach, which supports the need for training on the job. If the participant already has every skill the position requires, the plan does not justify OJT. Confirm the specific OJT documentation your board expects against your local OJT policy.

## Discussion prompt

Think of a recent plan you wrote or reviewed. If you removed the participant's name, could a stranger tell from the plan alone why that service was chosen? What was missing?

## Practice

Use this fictional profile: **Devon, 34, a former warehouse lead**, wants to become an HVAC technician. His objective assessment shows math scores that meet the training provider's entry level, a mechanical aptitude inventory in the high range, eight years supervising shipping crews, no HVAC experience, and unreliable transportation.

1. Write a one-sentence occupational goal for Devon.
2. Write one sentence each for interest, aptitude, and skills, naming the evidence. Mark anything you would still need to collect as [missing: describe it].
3. State the gap and the service, and explain in one sentence why you chose training rather than OJT (or the reverse).
4. Trade with a partner. Each of you circles any claim that does not point at evidence in the file.
