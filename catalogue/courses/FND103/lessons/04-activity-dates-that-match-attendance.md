## Dates are data

An activity's begin and end dates are not paperwork details. They feed performance reporting, they determine when a participant counts as receiving services, and they are among the first things a reviewer compares against source documents. If the dates in CalJOBS do not match what the training provider's records show, the activity fails even if the participant really attended.

## The rule: attendance, not vouchers

The most common date error is using the date a document was *issued* instead of the date the service *happened*.

| Date you might be tempted to use | Why it is wrong | Date to use instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Date the voucher, ITA, or purchase order was issued | Paying for training is not the same as attending it | First day the participant actually attended, per provider attendance records |
| Scheduled class start date on the enrollment form | Participants start late, or not at all | First day of recorded attendance |
| Scheduled program end date | Participants finish early, late, or drop | Last day of attendance or completion date, per provider records |
| Date you entered the activity | Data entry is not service delivery | The service date the note and source document support |

The source document is what the provider sends you: sign-in sheets, attendance rosters, enrollment verification, a completion certificate, or a transcript. Your board's local policy and the provider agreement set what attendance documentation you collect and how often. Confirm those requirements before you rely on any one document.

## Worked example

Maria's medical assistant program is scheduled to start March 3. The voucher was issued February 20. She enrolled February 25. Because of a childcare gap, her first recorded attendance is March 10.

- **Begin date:** March 10 (first attendance on the provider roster), not February 20 or March 3.
- **Projected end date:** the provider's scheduled completion for her cohort, entered as projected, then updated.
- **Actual end date:** the date on the provider's completion record, or her last day of attendance if she exits early.

Your case note for March 10 should say where the begin date came from: "Provider attendance roster received [date]; first attendance March 10."

## Why activities close on their own

Systems like CalJOBS track whether a participant is still receiving services. If an open activity's projected end date passes without an update, or if no service is recorded for a long stretch, the system or your reporting process may close the activity or move the participant toward exit before the work is actually done. An exit triggered by inactivity can start the performance clock at the wrong time and cut off services you still intended to provide.

The exact auto-closure behavior and timeframes come from CalJOBS and current state guidance, and your board may set its own monitoring rules. Check the current CalJOBS guidance and your local policy for the specifics. The habits that prevent the problem are the same regardless:

- **Keep projected end dates realistic**, and update them as soon as you learn a schedule changed.
- **Record services as they happen**, with a note, so the record shows ongoing engagement.
- **Close activities deliberately**, with the actual end date and an outcome, rather than letting them lapse.
- **Run a weekly check** of your caseload for activities with projected end dates in the next few weeks.

## A weekly date check

| Check | Action if it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Every open training activity has attendance documentation from the provider | Request the roster; note the request |
| Begin dates match the first attendance in that documentation | Correct the date; add a case note explaining the correction |
| Projected end dates are still accurate | Update the projected date; note why |
| Completed activities are closed with actual end dates | Close with the provider's completion date |

## Discussion prompt

Where does attendance information come from in your office, and how quickly does it reach the case manager? What would it take to get it sooner?

## Practice

Use this fictional file. Devon's HVAC voucher was issued April 1. The provider's enrollment form shows a scheduled start of April 14 and a scheduled end of August 29. The attendance roster shows his first day as April 16. In June, the provider tells you the cohort will finish September 12. Devon completes on September 12.

1. What begin date do you enter, and what source document supports it?
2. What projected end date do you enter at the start, and what do you do in June?
3. What actual end date do you enter, and what document do you need in the file?
4. Write the June case note documenting the change.
