## Why the CRM comes first

Every activity in this program lands in your CRM — your **customer relationship management** system, the database sales and recruiting teams use to track every person they are talking to. Your outreach, your follow-ups, your informational interviews, the employer who said "check back in March" — all of it. If it is not in the CRM, it did not happen, because nobody, including you, can remember the state of a hundred conversations.

Your CRM is a system of record and a decision tool at the same time. As a record it answers "what did I say to this person and when." As a decision tool it answers the only question that matters at 9:00 a.m.: *who do I touch today, and with what?* A pipeline you have to think hard about is a pipeline you will stop using by Thursday. The setup work in this lab exists to make that morning question take thirty seconds.

This lesson is vendor-neutral. Whatever CRM your cohort is using, the objects are the same: a **contact** (a person), an **account or company** (where they work), a **stage** (where the conversation is), and an **activity or task** (what you did and what is next). Find those four things in your tool before you go further; the labels differ, the model does not.

## The stages of a prospecting pipeline

Your pipeline models one thing: the journey from a name on a list to a conversation with a human being. Use these stages unless your instructor gives you a variant:

1. **Researched** — the contact exists in your CRM with a company, a role, and one specific reason you are reaching out. No message sent yet.
2. **Initiated** — you sent the first touch (connection request, email, or InMail — LinkedIn's paid message to someone you are not connected to). The clock starts here.
3. **Connected** — they accepted, replied, or otherwise acknowledged you. This is a real human relationship now, however thin.
4. **In conversation** — an actual back-and-forth is happening, or a meeting is scheduled.
5. **Interviewed** — you completed an informational interview or a hiring conversation.
6. **Nurture** — a real contact who is not active right now ("not hiring until Q3"). Not dead, just parked with a date.
7. **Closed – no fit** — wrong company, wrong role, hard no, or bounced twice with no signal.

Two rules keep stages honest. First, **stages move forward only when the contact does something.** You sending a third follow-up does not advance anyone; their reply does. Second, **nothing sits in "Researched" overnight.** Research is not progress. If a contact is worth adding, they are worth initiating within a day.

## Staging your pre-work target list

Your pre-work list is raw material, not a pipeline. Staging it means getting every row into the CRM with enough structure to act on. At minimum each contact needs: full name, company, role title, the channel you will use to reach them, a **priority tier**, and a one-line **personalization hook** — the specific, checkable detail your first message will reference.

Import in bulk if your CRM offers it, and clean the spreadsheet before you import rather than fixing records one at a time afterward. Watch for the three defects that break every import: names split across columns inconsistently, duplicate companies spelled three ways ("Acme Inc", "Acme, Inc.", "ACME"), and empty role titles. Standardize company names in the sheet first — your funnel metrics later depend on being able to count contacts per account.

Two fields do the heavy lifting for the rest of the week:

- **Priority tier.** Tier 1 is employer partners of the program. Tier 2 is companies that match your target role and are within your region or fully remote. Tier 3 is everything else you would say yes to. Set this on every record; a blank tier is a record you will never work.
- **Employer-partner flag.** A separate yes/no field or tag, not just a tier. These contacts already know the program, so your reply rate is higher and the stakes are higher too. You want to be able to filter to them in one click, and you want your instructor to be able to see that list.

Order of operations matters: **employer-partner contacts get initiated first.** Not because the other contacts do not count toward your fifty, but because a warm channel teaches you what a good message feels like before you spend cold contacts learning it.

## Choosing the next action

A working pipeline hands you the day's work. Build a saved view — most tools call it a view, filter, or smart list — that shows, sorted in this order:

1. Contacts who replied and are waiting on **you**. Always first. A reply going cold is the most expensive mistake in this pipeline.
2. Follow-ups that are **due today** (see the cadence in the next lesson).
3. Tier 1 contacts still in **Researched**.
4. Tier 2 and 3 contacts still in **Researched**, enough of them to hit quota.

That ordering encodes a judgment: the highest-value next action is almost never a brand-new cold send. Value follows the amount of relationship already built. When two actions look equal, pick the one closer to a scheduled conversation.

Every contact you touch must leave the touch with a logged activity and a **next action with a date**. A contact with no next step is invisible — it will not appear in any view, and it silently dies. Many CRMs will show you a "no open task" count; if yours does, watch that number, because it is a direct measure of leakage.

## Reading your funnel

At the end of each lab day, read five numbers off your pipeline:

- **Contacts initiated** (cumulative and today)
- **Connect rate**: Connected ÷ Initiated
- **Conversation rate**: In conversation ÷ Connected
- **Contacts with no open next action**
- **Follow-ups overdue**

Count each stage as "reached at least this stage," not "sitting in this stage right now." A contact who replied and then booked a call has left Connected, but they still count as connected; if you use your CRM's current-stage totals, your connect rate falls every time you succeed. Most CRMs show current-stage counts by default, so add them up yourself — Connected = Connected + In conversation + Interviewed, plus any Nurture or Closed contacts who accepted, replied, or otherwise acknowledged you first. Track human replies separately: a silent connection acceptance counts as Connected, but not as a reply in Week 2 tests or the Week 3 reply funnel.

A quick worked read: 40 initiated, 9 have replied at some point (6 still in Connected, 2 in conversation, 1 interviewed), 3 of those 9 reached a real back-and-forth. Connect rate = 9 ÷ 40 = 22.5%. Conversation rate = 3 ÷ 9 = 33%.

These are diagnostics, not a scoreboard. Each one points at a different fix:

| What you see | What it usually means | What to change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Initiated is low | Routine problem, not a skill problem | Protect the send block; batch research separately |
| Connect rate is low | Your first touch reads generic | Rework the hook (Lesson 3), test on Tier 2 |
| Connect rate fine, conversation rate low | Your ask is too big or too vague | Shrink the ask to one specific question |
| Many contacts with no next action | You are logging but not scheduling | Add the task before you close the record |
| Follow-ups overdue climbing | Quota is set above your real capacity | Fix the cadence, do not abandon it |

Small numbers are noisy. With twenty sends, one reply swings your rate by five points, so read direction across the week, not a single day, and change one variable at a time. If you rewrite your message and change channels on the same day, you learn nothing from the result.

Bring these five numbers to every lab. They are the fastest way for your instructor to tell you whether the fix you need is a message fix, a routine fix, or a targeting fix.

## Practice

Work these in your live CRM, on your real target list. Everything here counts toward the Week 1 milestone.

1. **Build the pipeline.** Create the seven stages above in your CRM. Add one test contact, walk it from Researched to Closed – no fit, then delete it. If any stage change is awkward, fix the setup now, not in week 3.
2. **Clean and import.** Standardize company names and role titles in your pre-work sheet, then import the full target list. Confirm the record count in the CRM matches the row count in the sheet, and fix every duplicate account before you continue.
3. **Tier and flag.** Set a priority tier on every contact — zero blanks — and set the employer-partner flag on every Tier 1 contact. Write a one-line personalization hook on at least twenty-five records.
4. **Build the daily view.** Create the saved view described above, sorted so replies surface first. Screenshot it for your instructor.
5. **Run the loop once.** Take the top ten contacts from that view, initiate each one, log the activity, and set a dated next action on all ten. Then re-open the view and confirm those ten have moved out of the top.
6. **Read the funnel.** Record today's five numbers in your lab notes. Write one sentence naming the number you most want to move tomorrow, and the single change you will make to move it.
