## Description

Five courses have handed you pieces. You can model a schema, write the query that makes it fast, move a few gigabytes with a distributed engine, and say what a governed dataset looks like. What you have not done yet is own all of it at once — one system, running in a cloud account with your name on the bill, that takes data nobody curated for you and produces an answer somebody actually asked for.

That is the whole job of this course. There is very little new material here, and that is deliberate. You will spend two short lessons deciding what to build and standing up an account that will not surprise you, then work through three graded milestones on one continuous pipeline — landing the data, modelling it, and making it survive being run unattended — followed by a lesson on the reviews and documentation that turn a working pipeline into something a colleague can inherit. The course ends where real work ends: in front of people who do not write code, explaining what you built and what it says.

You choose your own dataset from the program's approved list, and it must be public or synthetic. You work in a training cloud account with a spending limit. Expect to be reviewed at each milestone rather than only at the end, and expect the review to ask for evidence — a run log, a query, a cost figure — not a description. Scope small and finish. A modest pipeline that runs on a schedule, recovers from a bad day, and answers one question well is worth far more than an ambitious one that only ever ran on your laptop.

## Objectives

- Scope an end-to-end pipeline against a stated business question and document the architecture
- Stand up a cloud data environment with sensible cost and access controls
- Ingest source data into a landing zone reliably and repeatably
- Model ingested data into a warehouse layer that answers the business question
- Orchestrate, monitor, and harden the pipeline so it survives a bad run
- Collaborate through reviews, working agreements, and handoff documentation
- Present the finished pipeline and its findings to a non-engineering audience

## Prerequisite Knowledge

- All prior Data Engineer pathway coursework: de101, de102, de201, de210, and ds320
- A cloud account with a spending limit, provided by the program
