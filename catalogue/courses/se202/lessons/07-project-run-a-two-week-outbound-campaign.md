## Goal

Run a real two-week outbound campaign against your own target list, using the ICP, research, call plan, email sequence, and qualification scorecard you built in lessons 2 through 6. Produce a campaign packet and a results debrief that a sales manager could read and act on.

This is a campaign, not a simulation. Every artifact you assemble already exists in draft from the earlier lessons; the work here is executing the cadence for ten business days, recording what happened honestly, and drawing conclusions you can defend.

## Requirements

**1. Campaign plan (submit before day 1)**

- Your one-page ICP, revised from anything you learned in lessons 3 through 6.
- A target list of at least **25 accounts** with the ten columns from lesson 2, of which at least **10 are tier A** with a named trigger and trigger date.
- At least **40 verified contacts** across those accounts, averaging **two or more contacts per tier-A account**. State your verification method and your measured decay rate.
- Full buying-committee maps for your **three highest-priority tier-A accounts**, with a source next to every fact and at least one named committee gap each.
- Your written cadence table: day, channel, and touch, for the full ten business days, interleaving calls and email.

**2. Execution (days 1–10)**

- **150 or more dials**, made in blocks of at least 45 uninterrupted minutes.
- A voicemail on every no-answer for tier-A accounts, using the lesson 4 structure.
- Your **four-touch email sequence** run to completion on every contact you start, including the close-out. Starting a sequence and abandoning it mid-way does not count.
- A **daily activity log**: dials, connects, conversations, emails sent, replies, bounces, meetings set. One row per day.
- **Call notes for every conversation**, capturing the prospect's own words about their current state and the objection you handled worst.
- At least **two recorded calls** (with consent where required by your jurisdiction and your employer's policy) that you have listened back to.

**3. Qualification (end of week 2)**

- A completed MEDDIC scorecard for **every account that produced any reply**, minimum eight accounts, with a source and date beside every score of 2 or 3.
- A dated, owned next action for every account scoring 6 or above.
- At least **three explicit disqualifications**, each with the evidence, a nurture date if applicable, and the sentence you would say to the prospect.

**4. Debrief (1,000–1,500 words)**

- Your funnel with real numbers: dials → connects → conversations → meetings; emails sent → replies → positive replies → meetings; bounce rate.
- Conversion rate at each step, and which step is your weakest.
- The objection you heard most, quoted verbatim, and your revised response to it.
- Which subject line and which opener performed best, with the volume behind the claim.
- What you would change about your **ICP** based on evidence from the campaign — specific attributes to add, tighten, or drop, and the observation that justifies each.
- One paragraph on the gap between your activity numbers and your qualified pipeline.

## Constraints

- **Real accounts, real outreach.** Use your employer's market, or a market you have chosen and can research. Fabricated logs defeat the entire exercise and are trivially obvious to a reviewer.
- **Verified contacts only.** Do not send to low-confidence addresses. If your bounce rate exceeds a couple of percent, stop sending, fix the data, and note the interruption in your log.
- **Stay inside the rules.** Follow all applicable outreach law and your employer's policy for every market you contact: honest sender identification, a genuine opt-out path, immediate and permanent honoring of do-not-contact requests, do-not-call list compliance, and consent for call recording where it is required. Log every opt-out and every do-not-contact request.
- **No deception.** No fake "Re:" subject lines, no false claims of a prior conversation, no misrepresenting yourself to a gatekeeper.
- **Scope.** This campaign is prospecting only. Do not run discovery calls, demos, proposals, or negotiations as part of the deliverable — meetings you set are the output, not deals you close. CRM configuration and sequence automation are out of scope; a spreadsheet is a perfectly acceptable system of record for this project.
- **Cadence discipline.** No more than 20% of your planned touches may be skipped or late. Record every miss.
- **Personalization tiers.** Every tier-A touch 1 must contain at least one tier-1 (account-specific) line. Segment-level personalization is expected everywhere else.

## Definition of done

Your campaign is complete when all of the following are true:

- The campaign plan, activity log, call notes, email sequence as sent, scorecard, and debrief are assembled into a single packet.
- Every one of the 25+ accounts has a recorded outcome: meeting set, in sequence, no reply, disqualified, or opted out. No account is blank.
- The activity log covers all ten business days with no missing rows, and totals reconcile with the funnel numbers in your debrief.
- Every scored account has a source and date beside each score of 2 or 3, and every score of 6+ has a dated next action with an owner.
- At least three accounts are disqualified with written evidence.
- The debrief names your weakest funnel step and proposes one specific, testable change for the next campaign.
- You can present any three accounts in under two minutes each — score, weakest field, next action, date — and defend every score you claimed.

Meetings set is a reported number, not a pass/fail bar. A campaign that produced one meeting and an honest, well-reasoned debrief demonstrates the competency; a campaign that produced five meetings from an unverified list and an unsourced scorecard does not.

## Hints

- **Front-load the list work.** Learners who spend day 1 building and verifying the list out-convert learners who start dialing on hour one, every time. A bad list cannot be rescued by good calls.
- **Block your calls.** Two 45-minute blocks a day beats scattered dials, and your fifth call in a block is measurably better than your first.
- **Call the same day the email lands.** The email primes name recognition and the call gives the email a face. Neither works as well alone.
- **Multi-thread early, not after you get stuck.** Waiting for one contact to reply before trying a second one wastes a week per account.
- **Write down their exact words.** Verbatim quotes are the raw material for your follow-up emails, your scorecard sources, and your debrief. Paraphrases are worth almost nothing two weeks later.
- **Expect the close-out email to be your best performer.** It usually is. Do not skip it because the sequence "didn't work" — that is precisely when it works.
- **Score conservatively.** If you are unsure between a 2 and a 3, it is a 2. An honest 10 is more useful to you and your manager than an optimistic 15.
- **Log the misses too.** A campaign where you skipped day 6 entirely is a finding about your calendar discipline, and hiding it removes the most useful thing in your debrief.
