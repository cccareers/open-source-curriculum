## Description

In CHW103 you built the core skills of the job: listening, plain language, documentation, boundaries, referral. This course puts those skills to work with the people who most often fall through the gaps — clients coming home from incarceration, older adults managing several conditions at once, families living with diabetes or high blood pressure or asthma, and whole communities carrying trauma that was done to them by institutions that were supposed to help.

You will start with the populations themselves, because trust is what makes everything else possible. From there you move into health content at your scope: what chronic diseases actually are in plain language, how to support someone who is struggling to take medication as prescribed, and how to teach self-monitoring so a client can manage without you in the room. You will then work through nutrition education that talks about food and behavior rather than body size, and close with trauma-informed practice — the frame that should be wrapped around every service you deliver.

One boundary runs through all of it, and it does not move: community health workers educate, support, and connect. You never diagnose, you never advise on medications, and you always route clinical questions to a licensed provider. Holding that line is not a limitation on your value. It is what makes you trustworthy to both the client and the care team.

## Objectives

- Apply trust-building and culturally responsive approaches with high-risk and underserved populations
- Support clients with chronic disease basics, medication adherence, nutrition, and weight-inclusive care within CHW scope
- Apply trauma-informed principles across CHW services
