## What "closing a finding" means

A finding closes when the monitor accepts that the problem has been fixed and will not come back. That takes two kinds of action:

- **Correction** — fixing the specific files or records the monitor cited.
- **Systemic fix** — changing the process that let the problem happen, so the next files are right.

Many corrective action plans (CAPs) fail because they only do the first. Fixing Maria's file but not the intake process means the same finding appears at the next visit, often as a repeat finding, which monitors treat more seriously.

Your board or the state sets the CAP format and due dates in the monitoring report or local policy. Use the required format and deadlines; the structure below fits inside most formats.

## The five parts of a CAP that closes

| Part | What it answers | Weak version | Strong version |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Finding | What did the monitor cite? | "File issues" | Restate the finding as written, with the files cited |
| Root cause | Why did it happen? | "Staff error" | "Intake checklist did not include EO acknowledgment; new staff were not trained on it" |
| Correction | How are the cited items fixed? | "Will fix" | "EO acknowledgments obtained for the 3 cited participants by [date]; copies attached" |
| Systemic fix | What changes so it does not recur? | "Staff reminded" | "EO acknowledgment added to intake checklist; all staff trained [date]; added to onboarding" |
| Verification | How will you prove it worked? | (missing) | "QA will check this item in every internal sample for the next three cycles and report results to the board" |

Each action needs an owner and a date. "Staff will be trained" with no name and no date is not a plan.

## Finding the root cause

"Staff error" is almost never the root cause; it is a symptom. Ask "why" until you reach something you can change:

- Why was the EO acknowledgment missing? *The case manager did not collect it.*
- Why? *It was not on the intake checklist.*
- Why? *The checklist was written before the acknowledgment form was added to the intake packet.*

Now the fix is obvious: update the checklist, and add a rule to the policy register so checklists change whenever forms do.

## Evidence to attach

A monitor closes a finding based on evidence, not promises. Plan to attach:

- Copies or screenshots of the corrected records.
- The revised procedure or checklist, with its new version date.
- Training sign-in sheets and acknowledgments (Lesson 4).
- Internal sampling results showing the item now passes (Lesson 3).

If some evidence will not exist until later (for example, three cycles of sampling), say when you will submit it.

## Worked example

**Finding (from Valley Regional's monitoring report):** In 4 of 15 sampled training files, there was no documentation of the participant's need for training.

**Root cause:** The provider's training referral procedure did not require a case note on need; staff relied on the IEP alone, which often did not address it.

**Correction:** Case managers will add a dated case note documenting the assessment basis for training in each of the 4 cited files, labeled as a late entry, by [date]. Owner: Office Supervisor.

**Systemic fix:** Training referral procedure revised to require a training-need case note before ITA approval; supervisor approval form updated to include the check. All case managers trained on [date]. Owner: Program Manager.

**Verification:** QA will include all training files in the next three monthly samples and report the pass rate on this item to the board with the CAP update. Owner: QA Analyst.

Note what the plan does not do: it does not backdate notes or alter original records. Corrections are dated when made and labeled as late entries, consistent with your board's case note policy.

## Discussion prompt

Pick a finding your office has received more than once. Was the original CAP missing a root cause, a systemic fix, or verification?

## Practice

1. Take one real or sample finding from your office. Ask "why" at least three times and write down the root cause you reach.
2. Write a complete CAP for that finding using the five parts. Give every action an owner and a date.
3. Trade CAPs with a partner. Review theirs as the monitor: would you close the finding? If not, write one sentence explaining what evidence is missing.
