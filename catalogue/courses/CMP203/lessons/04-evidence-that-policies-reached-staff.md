## Written procedures are evidence

When a monitor asks, "How do your staff know the supportive services limit?", the answer "we told them" is not evidence. A monitor needs to see three links in a chain:

1. **The policy exists** — a current, dated local policy or written procedure.
2. **Staff received it** — a record that each person who needs it was told, trained, or given a copy.
3. **Staff follow it** — files that show the policy being applied.

Break any link and you have a gap. A policy that was issued but never distributed fails link 2. A policy everyone signed but no file reflects fails link 3.

## Link 1: keep a policy register

A policy register is a simple list of every local policy and internal procedure your staff must follow. It lets you answer "which version was in effect on this date?" — a question monitors ask when a file is older than the current policy.

| Policy / procedure | Version date | Issued by | Replaces | Staff who must know | Location |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Supportive Services | [date] | Valley Regional WB | [prior version] | All case managers | Shared drive / Policies |
| File Maintenance Procedure | [date] | Provider (internal) | — | Case managers, QA | Shared drive / Procedures |
| EO Complaint Procedure | [date] | Valley Regional WB | [prior version] | All staff | Shared drive / EO |

Keep superseded versions archived, not deleted. Files are judged against the policy in effect when the service happened.

## Link 2: show communication

Choose communication methods that leave a record. Common, monitor-friendly evidence includes:

- **Training sign-in sheets** with date, topic, policy version, trainer, and each attendee's name.
- **Acknowledgment forms** where each staff member confirms they received and read a specific policy version.
- **Meeting agendas and minutes** that name the policy discussed.
- **Email distributions** saved with the recipient list and attachment.
- **Learning platform completion reports**, if your organization uses one.

A record should answer who, what, which version, and when. "Team meeting — policies reviewed" answers none of those. "3/14 team meeting: Supportive Services policy, [version date], reviewed by J. Ortiz with 8 of 9 case managers; absent staff member trained 3/18" answers all of them.

Plan for new hires and absences. Add every policy in your register to new-staff onboarding, and track anyone who missed a training until they are caught up.

## Link 3: show implementation

Communication shows staff knew. Implementation shows they acted. The best implementation evidence comes from the internal sampling routine you built in Lesson 3:

- Checklist results showing the policy item passing across files after the training date.
- A before-and-after comparison: error rate on that item in the cycles before training versus after.
- Supervisor approvals that cite the policy (for example, a supportive services approval that names the limit and policy version).

## Worked example: a policy change at Valley Regional

Valley Regional Workforce Board issues a revised supportive services policy. Here is how one provider builds its evidence trail:

| Step | Date | Evidence kept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Policy received from board | Week 1 | Board email saved; register updated |
| Internal procedure updated | Week 1 | Procedure with new version date |
| Staff training | Week 2 | Sign-in sheet naming policy version |
| Acknowledgments | Week 2 | Signed forms for all case managers |
| Absent staff caught up | Week 3 | Individual training note |
| Implementation check | Next sampling cycle | Checklist results on supportive services items |

If a monitor later finds a file that does not follow the new policy, the provider can show that the policy reached staff and that most files comply. That shifts the conversation from "your system failed" to "one file needs correction."

## Discussion prompt

Think of the most recent policy change your board issued. Could you produce, today, the date each staff member learned about it? What would be missing?

## Practice

1. Build a policy register for your unit with at least five current local policies or procedures, including version dates and who must know each one.
2. Pick one policy from the register and assemble its evidence trail: the policy, proof of communication, and proof of implementation from at least three files. Note any link that is missing.
3. Rewrite one vague meeting note from your own records so it answers who, what, which version, and when.
