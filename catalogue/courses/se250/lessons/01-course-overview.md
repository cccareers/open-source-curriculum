## Description

Most sales presentations fail before a single slide is designed. A rep opens the presentation software, reaches for last quarter's deck, swaps the logo, adds two slides about a feature the prospect asked about, and arrives at the meeting with forty-one slides and no argument. The room is polite. Nothing moves. Nobody can say afterward what the meeting was for. This course exists because that outcome is not a talent problem — it is a process problem, and the process is learnable.

You will learn to build a presentation backwards from the decision it is supposed to produce. That means deciding, in writing, what one thing has to be true in the buyer's head when you leave the room, before you touch a design tool. It means structuring what you say as a narrative with a real protagonist and a real conflict, because a buyer remembers a story about themselves and forgets a list of capabilities. It means turning their operating data into charts that make a claim readable in seconds rather than tables that make them do arithmetic in a meeting. And it means building the artifact itself so that it still argues correctly when your champion forwards it to a CFO who was never in the room.

The last third of the course is about what happens live. You will work on presence, pacing, and the specific mechanics of absorbing an interruption without losing your thread — the difference between a rep who gets derailed by a question on slide four and one who answers it, banks it, and returns to the line of argument. You will learn to open with a commercial insight that reframes how the buyer understands their own situation, which is the hardest and most valuable move in this course. Then you will record a full customer presentation end to end, which is the only real evidence that any of it landed. No specific presentation product is taught here; everything you learn is meant to survive a change of tool.

## Objectives

- Decide what a specific sales presentation must accomplish before designing a single slide
- Structure a sales narrative with a protagonist, a conflict, and a resolution the product enables
- Turn a data set into a visual argument a buyer can read in seconds
- Build a deck in presentation software that survives being read without you in the room
- Deliver a presentation with presence and absorb interruptions without losing the thread
- Lead a presentation with an insight that reframes the buyer's view of their situation
