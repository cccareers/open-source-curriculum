## Goal

Secure a confirmed internship placement with a named field mentor who has agreed to supervise you, and submit a complete, accurate internship planning packet by your program's deadline.

This is the project the rest of the course was preparing you for. It is graded on two things: whether the placement is real and appropriate, and whether your documentation is complete, accurate, and on time. The second half is not paperwork discipline for its own sake — completing required documentation accurately and within designated timeframes is a core CHW competency, and this packet is the first place your program observes it in you.

## What makes a good field mentor

Before you approach anyone, know what you are looking for. A good field mentor:

- Currently does, or directly supervises, community health work — not an executive two levels away from the front line.
- Has time. A willing mentor with no calendar space is worse than a slightly less experienced one who is available weekly.
- Has supervised a student, a new hire, or a volunteer before, and can describe what that looked like.
- Can name specific tasks you would perform, not just "shadow the team."
- Has authority to approve your schedule and sign your documentation.
- Works in a setting where you can practice — assessment, education, coordination, documentation — rather than only observe.

A mentor does not have to share your background, and does not have to be the most senior person available. Availability and willingness to teach beat title every time.

## How to approach one

Approach in writing first, briefly, then in a scheduled conversation. In the written request: say who you are and what program you are in, say specifically why this organization and this person, state the hours and date range you need, state what you hope to practice, and ask for a short meeting rather than an immediate yes. Attach or link nothing longer than a one-page summary of yourself.

Bring the following to the meeting: your resume, your portfolio, your program's mentor expectations document, the required forms, and your real availability. Ask what a typical week would look like, what tasks you would own, how often you would meet, and what would make you a useful intern rather than a burden. Take notes and confirm in a follow-up email the same day.

## Requirements

Your submitted packet must contain all of the following.

1. **Mentor identification and agreement.** Mentor name, job title, organization, direct email, direct phone, and their written confirmation that they agree to supervise you for the stated dates and hours.
2. **Placement site confirmation.** Organization name, physical site address where you will report, setting type, population served, and the name of any second contact — a program manager or HR contact — in case your mentor is unavailable.
3. **A learning agreement.** Three to five learning goals stated as observable behaviors, each tied to a competency from this pathway and to specific activities at the site. "Complete client intake assessments with supervision, at least eight over the placement" is a goal. "Learn about community health" is not.
4. **Schedule.** Proposed days and hours per week, total hours, start date, and end date. It must total the hours your program requires and it must be a schedule the site has actually agreed to.
5. **Compliance documentation.** Whatever your program and site require before day one — background check status, TB clearance, immunization records, HIPAA and confidentiality acknowledgment, liability or insurance forms, and any site-specific onboarding. Include current status and expected completion date for anything still pending.
6. **A one-page placement rationale.** Why this site and this mentor, drawing on your workforce research from earlier in this course: the setting type, the population, the skills you expect to build, and the risks you identified and how you resolved them.
7. **Your professional materials.** Current resume, LinkedIn profile URL, and your portfolio.

## Constraints

- **Deadline is absolute.** The packet is due on your program's stated date. Late submission is scored as incomplete regardless of quality, for the same reason a late Medi-Cal encounter note is a problem regardless of how well written it is.
- **No client-identifying information** appears anywhere in the packet, including in your portfolio artifacts and your placement rationale.
- **Every field is filled or explicitly marked pending with a date.** Blank fields and "TBD" are the most common defect in this submission. If something is not yet resolved, say what it is, who owns it, and when it will be done.
- **Nothing is stated on someone's behalf without their confirmation.** Do not list a mentor who has not agreed in writing, and do not list a schedule the site has not seen.
- **Contact information must be current and correct.** Verify spelling of names, email addresses, and phone numbers against a source, not memory.
- **Your program's forms are the required format.** Do not substitute your own template.

## Definition of done

You are done when all of the following are true:

- A named field mentor has confirmed supervision in writing, with dates and hours stated.
- The placement site is confirmed, with a reporting address and a backup contact.
- The learning agreement lists three to five observable goals, each mapped to a pathway competency and to real activities at the site.
- The schedule totals the required hours and matches what the site agreed to.
- Every compliance item is complete or listed as pending with an owner and a completion date.
- The placement rationale is one page and references your workforce research.
- Resume, LinkedIn URL, and portfolio are attached and current.
- Every required form is filled with no blanks, signed where signatures are required, and free of client-identifying information.
- The complete packet was submitted on or before the deadline, through the channel your program specified.
- You retained your own dated copy of everything you submitted.

## Hints

- **Start with the mentor conversation, not the forms.** Everything else depends on a person saying yes. Reach out to your top three sites in the same week rather than sequentially — waiting two weeks for one polite decline costs you the deadline.
- **Work backward from the deadline.** Background checks and TB clearances routinely take one to three weeks and are the single most common reason a packet is late. Start them the day your site is confirmed, not the week the packet is due.
- **Confirm every verbal agreement in an email the same day.** A short recap message — dates, hours, tasks, next step — protects both of you and gives you the written confirmation the packet requires.
- **Have a second option alive.** Sites withdraw for reasons that have nothing to do with you: a supervisor leaves, a grant ends, an onboarding freeze lands. Keep your number two warm until number one is signed.
- **Write the learning goals in the language of behavior.** Start each with a verb someone could observe you doing and add a quantity or frequency.
- **Get an outside read before you submit.** Ask a classmate to check for blanks, wrong dates, mismatched hour totals, and any client detail that slipped through the portfolio.
- **Submit early enough to fix a rejection.** A packet submitted the morning of the deadline cannot survive a missing signature. Two days of margin is not caution; it is planning.
