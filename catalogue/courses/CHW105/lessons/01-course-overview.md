## Description

Everything you have learned so far has been preparation for working with real people in a real organization. This course is the bridge. By the end of it you will know where community health workers actually work in California, you will be able to describe your own skills in the language employers use, and you will have a confirmed internship site, a field mentor who has agreed to supervise you, and a complete planning packet submitted on time.

You will start by mapping the workforce: community clinics and federally qualified health centers, Medi-Cal health plans, community-based organizations, county public health departments, and hospitals. The daily work looks different in each one, and knowing the difference is what lets you choose a placement instead of accepting whatever comes first. From there you will build the two things every employer and every mentor will look at — a LinkedIn profile and a professional portfolio — and you will learn the single hardest rule of portfolio building for this field: your work samples must prove what you can do without ever revealing who you served.

The course ends with a project rather than an exam. You will identify and approach a field mentor, confirm a placement site, and submit your internship planning documentation complete and by the deadline. That last part is not administrative busywork. Accurate, on-time documentation is a core professional competency for community health workers, and your planning packet is the first place your program will see whether you have it.

## Objectives

- Research California CHW workforce settings and identify a fit for internship placement
- Build a LinkedIn profile and professional portfolio that evidence CHW skills
- Select a field mentor and submit complete internship planning documentation
