## Why a map

Most AJCC staff know their own step very well and the steps on either side of it only vaguely. The greeter knows sign-in. The eligibility technician knows documentation. The case manager knows the plan and the services. Nobody owns the whole line, so problems that start at the front door show up months later as a validation error that nobody can explain.

This lesson gives you one shared map. It has 12 steps. For each step you will learn three things:

- **Who** usually does it
- **What artifact** it produces (the record, document, or data entry that proves it happened)
- **Which rule** governs it

The map is a teaching model. Your center may combine steps, split them, or hand them to different roles. That is fine. What should not change is that every step produces an artifact and every artifact traces to a rule.

## The 12 steps at a glance

| # | Step | Who usually does it | Artifact | Governing rule |

| 1 | Sign-in | Greeter, resource room staff | Sign-in or traffic record | Reportable individual definition (20 CFR 677.150); confidentiality rules (WSD24-12 training) |

## Walking the steps

### Steps 1 to 3: the front door

At **sign-in**, a person becomes a reportable individual once they give identifying information and show they intend to use services. The artifact is whatever your center uses to record that visit. The confidentiality rules apply from the first minute: a sign-in sheet that lets the next customer read the previous customer's name and reason for visit is a privacy problem, not a paperwork problem.

**Orientation and triage** is where staff decide what the customer needs next. Some customers need only self-service. Others are referred to a partner, such as Adult Education or Vocational Rehabilitation. Others are routed toward WIOA Title I. Priority of service starts to matter here, because it shapes who gets individualized career and training services first.

**Registration** creates the customer's CalJOBS record. Staff often help. Accurate identifying information at this step prevents duplicate records later.

### Steps 4 and 5: eligibility and participation

**Eligibility** is where most monitoring findings begin. The technician determines eligibility for a specific program (Adult, Dislocated Worker, or Youth), collects source documents from the Acceptable Documentation List, and records priority-of-service status. The artifact is the determination plus the documents themselves.

**Participation** starts with the first qualifying service after eligibility, as you learned in Lesson 2. The artifact is the service record with its date. For Adult and DW customers, the qualifying service is often the comprehensive assessment from step 6, so steps 5 and 6 can happen on the same day. For Youth, the assessment and ISS come first, and participation starts with the first program element.

### Steps 6 to 9: the work in the middle

**Assessment** tells you what the customer already has and what they still need. Adult and DW customers get a comprehensive assessment when they move into individualized services. Youth get an objective assessment.

The **plan** turns the assessment into goals and steps. Adult and DW customers have an Individual Employment Plan (IEP). Youth have an Individual Service Strategy (ISS). The plan is an artifact a monitor will read closely: does each service in the file connect to a goal in the plan?

**Services and training** is the longest step. It can include career services, an Individual Training Account (ITA) with a provider on the Eligible Training Provider List, an on-the-job training contract with an employer, work experience, and supportive services. Each one produces its own record.

**Progress and outcomes** is where the case manager records what happened: a credential earned, a measurable skill gain, a job placement. Case notes are the artifact that ties the story together.

### Steps 10 to 12: the back door

**Exit** happens when services stop. You will spend all of Lesson 4 on it.

**Follow-up** keeps contact with the customer after exit to support job retention and to learn about outcomes. Follow-up does not reopen services.

**Reporting and validation** is where every earlier artifact gets tested. Customer records feed the Participant Individual Record Layout (PIRL), the federal reporting format. EDD's annual Data Element Validation then samples files and checks that each data element matches a source document. CCC's catalog describes a 15-business-day document window and a 10 percent error threshold for that process; confirm both against the current directive before you rely on them.

## Worked example: Maria's file, step by step

Maria, a laid-off medical billing clerk, moves through the map like this:

1. Signs in at the Valley Regional AJCC on a Monday. (Sign-in record.)
2. Attends orientation and is referred to Dislocated Worker services. (Orientation record.)
3. Completes CalJOBS registration with staff help. (CalJOBS record.)
4. Brings her layoff notice and ID; the technician determines DW eligibility. (Determination and documents.)
5. Receives career counseling the same afternoon; participation starts. (Service record.)
6. Completes a comprehensive assessment the next week. (Assessment.)
7. Signs an IEP with a goal of medical coding certification. (IEP.)
8. Starts an ITA-funded coding program on the ETPL and receives a transportation supportive service. (ITA, supportive service record.)
9. Earns her certification; the case manager records the credential. (Case note, credential record.)
10. Is hired; services end, and 90 days later the system sets her exit date. (Exit date.)
11. Gets follow-up calls about job retention. (Follow-up notes.)
12. Her file is pulled in a validation sample. (PIRL data matched to documents.)

## Practice

1. Without looking at the table, write the 12 steps in order on a sheet of paper. Then check yourself.
2. For your own role, circle the steps you personally touch. For each one, write the artifact you produce and the rule you believe governs it. Mark any rule you are not sure about with a question mark.
3. Pick one question mark and find the answer: open the current directive or your board's local policy and write down where the rule lives.
4. Discussion prompt for live sessions: "Which step in our center has no written procedure, so it only lives in someone's head?"
