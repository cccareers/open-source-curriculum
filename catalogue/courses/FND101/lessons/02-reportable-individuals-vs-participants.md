## Why this distinction matters on Monday

Two customers can walk into your center on the same morning, sit at the same computer, and talk to the same greeter. One of them will count in your board's performance results a year from now. The other will not. The difference is whether the person stays a **reportable individual** or becomes a **participant**.

Federal performance rules (20 CFR 677.150) define both terms. Getting them right matters because:

- Participants are the people whose employment, earnings, credentials, and skill gains are measured.
- Participation triggers an exit date later, and every participant you enroll will eventually be in the denominator of your outcomes.
- A file that shows services but no eligibility, or participation that started before eligibility was documented, is a common monitoring and validation problem.

## The two definitions in plain language

| | Reportable individual | Participant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who | Someone who shows they intend to use the system and gives identifying information | Someone who has been determined eligible and has received the service that starts participation for their program |
| Typical services | Self-service and information-only services (using the resource room on their own, picking up a labor market flyer, attending a general information session) | Staff-assisted career services, training services, or (for Youth) program elements |
| Counted in outcomes? | Reported as a count, but not measured for employment and earnings outcomes | Yes, after exit |
| Needs a WIOA eligibility determination? | No | Yes |

A reportable individual is not a "failed" participant. Many customers only need self-service help, and that is a legitimate result. The goal is to classify each customer accurately, not to push everyone into enrollment.

## What starts participation, program by program

The service that starts participation is different for Adult and Dislocated Worker than it is for Youth. This is the part most new staff get wrong.

**Adult and Dislocated Worker.** Participation starts when an eligible person receives a service *other than* self-service or information-only services. In practice, that is usually the first staff-assisted career service, such as a comprehensive assessment, career counseling, or staff-assisted job search help. It can also be a training service. The eligibility determination has to come first.

**Youth.** Participation does not start with a single service. A Youth customer becomes a participant only after all of these are in place:

1. Eligibility has been determined.
2. An objective assessment has been completed.
3. An Individual Service Strategy (ISS) has been developed.
4. The youth has received at least one of the youth program elements.

If any one of those is missing, the youth is not yet a participant, no matter how much time staff have spent with them.

## Worked examples

**Maria, laid off from a medical billing job.** Maria signs in, uses the resource room computers to update her resume, and leaves. She is a *reportable individual*. Two weeks later she returns, completes Dislocated Worker eligibility with a case manager, and that same day receives one-on-one career counseling. Her participation starts on that counseling date, because it is the first service beyond self-service and information-only, received after eligibility.

**Devon, age 19, out of school.** Devon's eligibility is determined on March 3. His objective assessment is finished on March 10. His ISS is signed on March 17. He starts a paid work experience on March 24. Devon becomes a participant when the last piece is in place: the first program element after the assessment and ISS exist. A case note that says "Devon enrolled March 3" would not match the record.

**Linh, attends an employer hiring event.** Linh attends a hiring event the center hosts and talks briefly with a greeter. She gives her name and contact information. She is a reportable individual. Attending a general event does not make her a participant.

## A quick decision check

Ask these questions in order:

1. Did the person give identifying information and show intent to use services? If no, they are not yet a reportable individual.
2. Has WIOA eligibility for a specific program been determined and documented? If no, they cannot be a participant.
3. **Adult or DW:** Have they received a service other than self-service or information-only? **Youth:** Are the assessment, the ISS, and a program element all complete?
4. If yes, record the participation date as the date of that qualifying service, not the date of the conversation or the eligibility interview.

For how your state wants these dates recorded in CalJOBS, check the current state performance and data entry guidance and your board's procedures. Do not rely on memory for activity codes.

## Discussion prompt

In a live session, ask: "Which service at our center most often starts participation for Adult customers? Does everyone on this team agree on what counts as 'staff-assisted'?" Disagreement here is a sign that your center needs a written procedure.

## Practice

Classify each customer as a reportable individual or a participant, and if a participant, name the date participation started.

1. Rosa, age 45, uses the resource room three times in one week and asks the greeter where the restroom is. No eligibility on file.
2. James, a Dislocated Worker, is determined eligible on May 1, attends a general orientation on May 2, and receives a comprehensive assessment with a case manager on May 6.
3. Aaliyah, age 17, in school, has eligibility determined on June 4 and an objective assessment on June 6. Her ISS is not yet written. She attended a tutoring session on June 7.
4. Tom, an Adult customer, receives one-on-one job search help from a career coach on July 9. His eligibility determination is completed on July 12.

For item 4, write a two-sentence note to your supervisor explaining what is wrong with the order of events and what you would check before correcting the record.
