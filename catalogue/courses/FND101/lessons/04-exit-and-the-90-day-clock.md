## Exit is something that happens, not something you do

New staff often think of exit as a button a case manager presses when a customer is "done." Under WIOA it works the other way. A participant exits when they stop receiving qualifying services. The exit is recognized after the fact, once 90 days have passed with no qualifying services and no future services planned.

The federal definition sits in 20 CFR 677.150, and the detailed reporting rules are in federal performance guidance. Your job is to understand how the clock runs so that the date in the record reflects what really happened.

## How the 90-day clock works

Think of it as a timer that resets every time the participant receives a qualifying service.

1. The participant receives a qualifying service. The clock starts at zero.
2. Each day without a qualifying service, the clock runs.
3. If another qualifying service happens, the clock resets to zero.
4. If 90 consecutive days pass with no qualifying service, and no future services are scheduled, the participant has exited.
5. The **exit date** is the date of the **last qualifying service**, not day 90.

That last point surprises people. If Maria's last counseling session was on April 2, and nothing else happened, her exit date is April 2, even though nobody could know it was her exit until early July.

## What resets the clock and what does not

| Resets the clock | Does not reset the clock |
| --- | --- |
| Staff-assisted career services (counseling, job search assistance, assessment) | Self-service activities |
| Training services (classroom, ITA-funded training, OJT) | Information-only services |
| Youth program elements other than follow-up | Follow-up services after exit |
| | Contacts that deliver no service, such as a call to update a phone number |

Two categories need extra care:

- **A contact is not a service.** A phone call that only says "checking in, call me if you need anything" usually does not reset the clock. A call in which you deliver job search help, and document it, may. The case note must show what service was delivered.
- **Supportive services, incentives, and planned gaps.** Federal guidance has specific rules for whether certain activities count, and for "gaps in service" (for example, a planned delay before a training start date or a health issue). These rules are detailed and have changed over time. Check the current federal performance guidance and your state's direction before you decide how to code them, and do not guess.

## Why getting it wrong hurts

**Holding a file open on paper only.** Some staff enter monthly "check-in" activities to keep a file from exiting because they hope the customer will come back. If those entries are not real services, the record no longer matches the case file. That is a validation risk, and it can also push the customer's outcome measurement into the wrong period.

**Letting an active customer exit by accident.** If a customer is between services and nobody schedules the next step, the clock runs out. The customer exits and their outcomes are measured from that date, even though staff thought the work was still going on.

**Re-enrolling after exit.** A customer who comes back after exiting starts a new period of participation. Their earlier period is already counted.

## Co-enrollment

When a customer is enrolled in more than one program, the exit rules for each program interact. Your state's policy on how exits are coordinated across co-enrolled programs controls here. Find out what California and your board require before you plan services for a co-enrolled customer.

## Worked example: Andre's timeline

Andre is a Valley Regional Adult participant.

- **January 8:** Career counseling session. Clock resets.
- **February 14:** Starts a 10-week forklift and warehouse training. Clock resets and keeps resetting while training is active.
- **April 25:** Training ends. He earns his certification.
- **May 2:** Case manager calls to deliver job leads and reviews his resume. Documented as staff-assisted job search. Clock resets.
- **June 10:** Case manager calls to ask if he has found work. No service is delivered. Clock keeps running.
- **July 15:** Andre reports he was hired. Case manager records the employment information as follow-up. Clock keeps running.
- **July 31:** 90 days have passed since May 2. No services are planned.

Andre's exit date is **May 2**. The June and July contacts did not extend it.

## Discussion prompt

"Think about a customer who exited from our center without anyone noticing. What signal could we have watched for?" Many centers run a monthly report of participants with no activity in the last 60 days so staff can decide, on purpose, whether services are still needed.
