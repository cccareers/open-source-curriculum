## Goal

Produce the Outreach Portfolio: a single package that turns eight weeks of scattered work into evidence a stranger can evaluate in ten minutes. It has three parts — an honest metrics story built from your pipeline data, a Personal Prospecting Playbook documenting the process you actually use, and a curated set of your best artifacts from every rotation. Your apprenticeship application, your resume, and your LinkedIn profile will all cite this portfolio, so every claim you make later has to point at something inside it.

This is the week's project. It is not a scrapbook and it is not a highlight reel. A hiring manager reading it should finish with an accurate picture of what you can do, what you tried, and what you learned when things did not work.

## Requirements

### Part 1 — The metrics story

Pull your full pipeline history from your tracker — your CRM plus any spreadsheet or lab log where you recorded weekly numbers — and turn it into a narrative with numbers attached. The story needs three things:

- **The totals.** Outreach attempted, responses received, conversations held, informational interviews completed, meetings booked, artifacts delivered. Whatever your tracker recorded, reported at the level of the whole program.
- **The arc.** How the numbers moved through the completed weeks. Week 8 is still ahead: mark it pending and finalize the full-program totals after Demo Day. Almost everyone's response rate improves; find yours and say by how much. If a number got worse, that goes in too, with your read on why.
- **The turn.** The specific change you made that moved a metric — a rewritten opening line, a switch in channel, a change in who you targeted, a different follow-up cadence. Name the change, name the week, and show the before and after.

Write it as prose with the numbers embedded, roughly 400 to 600 words, not as a bare table. The numbers are the evidence; the sentences are the story.

```text
Weeks 1-3   68 outreach   7 replies   10.3% reply rate
Weeks 4-6   54 outreach  14 replies   25.9% reply rate
Week 4      switched from generic intro to a one-line reference
            to something specific in the person's recent work
```

That shape — segment, numbers, named change — is what makes a metrics story checkable rather than decorative. Check the arithmetic before you publish it: 7 ÷ 68 = 0.103, or 10.3%; 14 ÷ 54 = 0.259, or 25.9%. Count outreach the same way in every segment (for example, first touches only, not follow-ups), and say which way you counted.

### Part 2 — The Personal Prospecting Playbook

Document the outreach process you actually run, in enough detail that another participant could execute it without asking you a question. Minimum contents:

- **Targeting.** How you decide who is worth contacting: role, company size, signal you look for, and what you skip.
- **Research.** What you look up before writing, where you look it up, and how long you spend. Include your stopping rule.
- **The message.** Your current template, with the variable parts marked, plus one or two lines on why the template is shaped that way.
- **Follow-up cadence.** How many touches, how far apart, through which channels, and when you stop.
- **Tracking.** What you log for each contact and when you log it.
- **What failed.** At least three approaches you tried that did not work, with your read on why. This section is not optional.

The playbook is a repeatable process, not a description of your best day. If step four is something you did once in Week 5 and never again, it does not belong.

### Part 3 — Best artifacts from each rotation

Include at least one artifact from every rotation — Software Development, Technical Sales, Digital Marketing, and AI Prompt Engineering — plus your strongest two or three from anywhere in the program. Each artifact needs a short caption card:

```text
Artifact:  Campaign brief, Week 6 Digital Marketing rotation
Skill:     Turning a vague goal into a measurable campaign plan
Context:   Two-week timeline, no budget, one channel
Result:    4 posts shipped; 3.1% engagement vs. 1.8% baseline
My part:   Wrote the brief and the copy; used cohort feedback on the CTA
```

The caption card matters more than the artifact. It states which claimed skill the artifact evidences, what the constraints were, and what you personally did. An artifact with no caption is something the reader has to interpret, and readers do not interpret — they move on.

## Constraints

- **Real data only.** Every number comes from your tracker or a system that can be checked. No estimates dressed as counts, no rounding up, no numbers you cannot reproduce on request.
- **Honest, not flattering.** A failed campaign with a clear read on why it failed is stronger evidence of judgment than a success you cannot explain. At least one artifact or metric in the portfolio must be something that did not go well.
- **Your work, attributed.** Where a deliverable was collaborative or built on a template, the caption says so. Overstating your part is the fastest way to lose an apprenticeship offer during the interview that follows.
- **Ten-minute rule.** The whole portfolio must be skimmable in ten minutes. If it takes longer, you have included too much — cut artifacts, not captions.
- **Portable format.** A shared document or folder, link-shareable, that opens without special software and without you present to explain it.
- **One pathway forward.** The portfolio includes work from all four rotations, but its ordering and framing should make your declared pathway from Lesson 02 obvious.

## Definition of done

The capstone draft is complete when all of the following are true:

1. The metrics story is written, 400 to 600 words, with program totals, the eight-week arc, and at least one named change tied to a before-and-after number.
2. Every number in the metrics story traces to a row in your tracker. You can produce the source for any of them within a minute.
3. The Personal Prospecting Playbook covers all six required sections, including at least three approaches that failed and why.
4. Another participant can read the playbook and describe your process back to you accurately without asking a clarifying question.
5. Every rotation is represented by at least one artifact, and every artifact has a complete caption card naming skill, context, result, and your part.
6. At least one included item honestly represents something that did not work.
7. The whole package is one link, opens cleanly, and can be skimmed in ten minutes by someone who has never met you.
8. A cohort-mate has reviewed it against this list and you have addressed their findings.

## Hints

**Start from the tracker, not from memory.** Open your pipeline data first and let it tell you what the story is. Participants who write the story first and then look for supporting numbers almost always find the numbers say something different — and the numbers are what a recruiter can check.

**Segment before you look for the turn.** Totals hide movement. Split your data into thirds or by week and the inflection point usually becomes obvious. Then go back to the week around it and find what you changed.

**Small numbers are fine.** Six meetings from forty-one attempts is a real result at eight weeks. A portfolio that is candid about scale reads as credible; one that inflates reads as inexperienced, and the inflation is usually detectable.

**Write the caption card first, pick the artifact second.** Decide which skill you need to evidence, then choose the artifact that evidences it best. Selecting artifacts by which one you are proudest of tends to produce four items evidencing the same skill.

**The failure section is a strength signal.** "Three approaches that failed and why" tells a reader you can run a real experiment loop. Write it plainly, without apology and without over-explaining.

**Cut to the ten-minute rule last, and cut hard.** The temptation is to include everything as insurance. A reader's attention is the scarce resource; eight strong captioned artifacts beat twenty uncaptioned ones every time.

**Build it where you can share it Thursday.** The application in Lesson 04 cites this portfolio by link. If the link is not ready and stable, the application stalls.
