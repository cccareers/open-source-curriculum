## Description

Security work looks, from the outside, like a contest between clever attackers and clever defenders. From the inside it is something quieter and more useful: a discipline of naming things precisely. What is this system supposed to protect? What could go wrong with it? How likely is that, and how bad would it be? What would we put in place, and would that thing actually help? Almost every decision you will make in this field is one of those four questions asked about a specific piece of a specific organization, and almost every bad security decision is one of them answered vaguely.

This course is where you learn the vocabulary to answer them. You will start with the three properties every security control exists to protect, then learn to describe what goes wrong in terms of those properties rather than in terms of brand names and headlines. From there you will learn how an organization decides what to do about a problem it cannot completely eliminate, and finally you will get a map of the tool categories and frameworks that the rest of this pathway fills in one at a time. The course closes with a written baseline review of a small office, which is the first piece of real security work most support technicians are trusted to produce.

You need no prior security coursework, and you will not be asked to attack anything. Everything here is defensive and conceptual by design: the networking, endpoint, and hands-on testing courses come later in the pathway and depend on this vocabulary being solid. Expect to spend as much time writing sentences as reading them. A technician who can say "this control is a detective control protecting integrity, and it fails when the logs are not reviewed" is worth more on day one than one who can recite a longer list of tool names.

## Objectives

- Explain confidentiality, integrity, and availability, and use them to describe what a security control is protecting
- Identify common threats and vulnerabilities in a described network or information system
- Rate a risk by likelihood and impact and choose a mitigation strategy for it
- Match a monitoring or response task to the right category of security tooling and to the framework function it serves

## Prerequisite Knowledge

None — this course assumes no prior coursework in this pathway.
