## Why jurisdiction matters to you

DAS registers programs, but it does not act alone. California has two advisory bodies that shape apprenticeship policy and program review:

- **The California Apprenticeship Council (CAC)**, the long-standing council associated with the traditional building and construction trades.
- **The Interagency Advisory Committee on Apprenticeship (IACA)**, which advises on apprenticeship in industries outside those traditional trades, such as healthcare, information technology, advanced manufacturing, and other "non-traditional" sectors.

Knowing which body a program falls under tells you which rules, which review path, and which existing programs are likely to apply. It also stops you from giving an employer advice that is true for a construction program but wrong for a healthcare one, or the reverse.

## The decision logic

You do not need to memorize statute. You need to ask the right questions in order:

1. **What is the occupation?** Write down the job title and what the worker actually does.
2. **Is it a building and construction trade?** Electricians, carpenters, plumbers, and similar crafts working in construction point toward the CAC side. Check DAS's current guidance on where firefighter and other public-safety programs fall rather than guessing.
3. **If not, is it in a non-traditional industry?** Healthcare, IT, advanced manufacturing, early care and education, hospitality, and similar sectors point toward IACA.
4. **Is the occupation ambiguous?** A maintenance technician at a hospital, or a solar installer, can sit near the line. Ask the DAS consultant to confirm before you tell the employer anything.

| Occupation | Industry | Likely advisory body | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inside wireman (electrician) | Construction | CAC | High |
| Medical assistant | Healthcare | IACA | High |
| Cybersecurity support technician | IT | IACA | High |
| Facilities maintenance technician at a school district | Public sector / mixed | Confirm with DAS | Low |

Treat the last column seriously. When confidence is low, the right answer is "Let me confirm with DAS," not a guess.

## What changes with jurisdiction

The practical differences you should listen for when a DAS consultant explains a program's path:

- **Existing-program considerations.** In the building and construction trades, California law has long asked whether existing programs already meet the need for that craft in the area before a new program is approved. That is a major reason construction employers are usually steered toward joining an existing program. Confirm the current rule with DAS.
- **Typical sponsors.** Construction programs are often sponsored by joint labor-management committees. Non-traditional programs are more often sponsored by a single employer, a group of employers, or an intermediary such as a college or industry association.
- **Review path.** The body that oversees the program influences who looks at the standards and how. Ask your DAS consultant how review will proceed for this specific program.

## Worked example

Marco, a program manager at a Valley Regional AJCC, gets two calls in one week:

- **Delta Electric** wants to train inside wiremen. Marco's note: "Construction trade, CAC. Very likely existing JATC programs in our area. Recommend connecting employer to existing program before discussing new standards."
- **Harbor Clinics** wants medical assistant apprentices. Marco's note: "Healthcare, IACA. Check for existing healthcare intermediary programs; if none fit, DAS consultant for new standards."

Same AJCC, same week, two very different paths, and the jurisdiction question sorted them in under a minute.
