## Two registrations, not one

Registration in California happens at two levels, and employers often blur them together:

1. **The program** is registered. A *sponsor* (an employer, a group of employers, a labor-management committee, or an intermediary) submits apprenticeship *standards* to the Division of Apprenticeship Standards (DAS). Once DAS approves the standards, the program exists.
2. **Each apprentice** is registered. Every person hired into an approved program signs an apprentice agreement, the **DAS 1**, and the sponsor submits it so that the apprentice is officially indentured in the program.

An apprentice cannot be registered until a program exists to register them into. That one fact explains most of the delay an employer will experience, and it is why the "join an existing program" option (Lesson 5) matters so much.

Your role as AJCC staff is not to write the standards or file with DAS. Your role is to know the sequence well enough to coach the employer, connect them to the right DAS consultant or intermediary, and line up WIOA supports (such as OJT or supportive services) at the right point.

## Step by step: registering a new program

| Step | What happens | Who usually does it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Define the occupation | Pick the apprenticeable occupation, the job title, and the skills a fully trained worker needs. | Employer, with a DAS consultant or intermediary |
| 2. Draft the standards | Write the program standards (details below). | Sponsor, often with help |
| 3. Arrange related instruction | Identify who delivers the classroom or online instruction that goes with on-the-job learning. | Sponsor and an education partner |
| 4. Submit to DAS | Send the draft standards to DAS for review. | Sponsor |
| 5. Review and revise | DAS reviews the standards, may request changes, and routes the program through the appropriate advisory body. | DAS, sponsor |
| 6. Approval | DAS approves and registers the program. | DAS |

The timelines that govern review are set in regulation; the catalog for this course points to **8 CCR 206**. Read the current text there, or ask your DAS consultant, for the exact review periods rather than quoting a number from memory.

### What goes into the standards

Standards are the program's rulebook. Expect them to cover, at minimum:

- **The occupation and term** of the apprenticeship.
- **A work process schedule**: the skills learned on the job and the approximate time spent on each.
- **Related and supplemental instruction (RSI)**: the subjects, approximate hours, and the provider.
- **A progressive wage schedule**: how the apprentice's pay rises as skills are gained.
- **Ratio** of apprentices to journey-level workers, and how supervision works.
- **Selection procedures and equal opportunity commitments**.
- **How the program is administered**, including who signs agreements and keeps records.

DAS provides templates and consultants who help sponsors draft standards. Point employers to those resources first; do not hand them a template you built yourself.

## Step by step: registering an apprentice

Once a program is approved, each new apprentice goes through a much shorter path:

1. The employer hires (or selects an existing worker) according to the program's selection procedures.
2. The apprentice and sponsor complete the **DAS 1 apprentice agreement**.
3. The sponsor submits the agreement to DAS for registration.
4. The apprentice begins on-the-job learning and RSI, and the sponsor tracks progress against the work process schedule.

Check with your DAS consultant for the current submission method and any supporting documents the agreement requires. Those details change, and they are not something you should quote from memory.

### Where WIOA fits

If the apprentice is also a WIOA participant, your case file needs its own documentation. A DAS 1 is evidence of registration, but it does not replace WIOA eligibility documentation, an Individual Employment Plan, or an OJT contract. Confirm your board's local policy on what proof of apprenticeship registration goes into the file and when.

## Worked example

Valley Regional Workforce Board's business services rep, Dana, meets Sunrise Home Health, a 40-employee agency that wants to train new hires as home health aides through apprenticeship. Dana's case note:

> Met Sunrise HR (J. Ortiz). Wants apprenticeship for 6 aides. No existing program. Explained two-stage process: program standards to DAS first, then DAS 1 per apprentice. Employer has draft job descriptions but no wage progression or RSI partner. Next steps: (1) intro to DAS consultant, (2) check whether a healthcare intermediary already sponsors a similar occupation, (3) hold OJT discussion until program path is decided.

Notice what Dana did *not* do: promise a start date, or start an OJT contract for an apprenticeship that does not exist yet.

## Practice

Build a one-page "registration checklist" you could hand an employer. It should:

1. Split the process into **Program registration** and **Apprentice registration**.
2. List each standards component as a checkbox, with a column for "Who on your team owns this?"
3. Note where the DAS 1 is completed and who signs it.
4. Include a line reading "Review timelines: confirm current 8 CCR 206 periods with DAS" instead of a specific number.
5. Add one row for WIOA documentation, marked "per [your board's] local policy."

Test it by walking through Sunrise Home Health's situation and marking which boxes they could check today.
