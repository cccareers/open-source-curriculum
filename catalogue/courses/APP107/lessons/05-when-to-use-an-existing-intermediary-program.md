## Register or join

An employer has two basic routes to registered apprentices:

- **Register a new program.** The employer (or a group) becomes the sponsor, writes standards, and goes through DAS approval.
- **Join an existing program.** The employer signs on as a participating employer under a program someone else already sponsors, often an *intermediary* such as an industry association, a community college, a labor-management committee, or a nonprofit.

Joining skips the slowest part of the process: getting standards approved. Once the employer participates, apprentices can be registered with a DAS 1 under the existing program. This is how intermediaries shorten the path.

## What intermediaries do

Intermediaries vary, but many of them:

- Hold already-approved standards for one or more occupations.
- Handle paperwork such as DAS 1 submission and record keeping.
- Coordinate RSI with an education partner.
- Support several small employers that could not run a program alone.

In exchange, the employer generally accepts the program's existing standards, including its work process schedule, wage progression, and ratios. Ask the intermediary what is fixed and what can flex.

## The decision questions

Work through these with the employer:

| Question | Points toward joining | Points toward registering new |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Does an existing program cover this occupation? | Yes, a close match | No match, or the occupation is unusual |
| Can the employer accept that program's standards? | Yes, or with minor local adjustments | Needs a different wage plan, skills, or ratio |
| How soon are apprentices needed? | Soon | Can wait several months |
| How many apprentices, and for how long? | A few, or uncertain | Many, ongoing, central to workforce strategy |
| Does the employer want to control the program? | Not important | Very important |
| Is it a construction trade with established programs? | Strongly yes; see Lesson 3 | Rarely |

No single row decides it. A large employer with unique skill needs may still choose to register its own program even if a partial match exists. A small employer needing two apprentices next quarter is almost always better served by joining.

## Finding an existing program

Before recommending either route:

1. Search DAS's public listing of registered programs for the occupation and region.
2. Ask your board's business services team and regional partners which intermediaries are active locally.
3. Contact the intermediary to confirm they are accepting new employers and what participation involves.

Do not assume a program is open to new employers just because it is listed.

## Worked example

Two employers call Valley Regional Workforce Board:

- **Bright Smile Dental Group** (3 offices) wants 2 dental assistant apprentices this year. A regional healthcare intermediary sponsors an approved dental assistant program. The wage progression is close to Bright Smile's pay scale. **Recommendation: join.** Business services note: "Intermediary program fits. Employer can register apprentices once participation is set up, versus months for new standards."
- **Pacific Robotics** wants 25 apprentices a year in a robotics maintenance role no program covers, and wants its own skill set and wage structure. **Recommendation: register new**, with a DAS consultant, and set a several-month timeline expectation.

## Discussion prompt

What intermediaries operate in your region? If you do not know, who on your team would?

## Practice

For each employer, decide "join" or "register new," and write two sentences explaining why using the decision table.

1. A rural hospital needs 3 certified nursing assistant apprentices and a regional healthcare apprenticeship program exists.
2. A logistics company wants 40 apprentices a year in a warehouse automation role with no matching program.
3. A small plumbing contractor wants 1 apprentice in an area with an established plumbing joint program.
4. A software company wants 5 apprentices, an IT intermediary program exists, but its wage progression starts well below the company's entry wage.

For number 4, list two questions you would ask the intermediary before deciding.
