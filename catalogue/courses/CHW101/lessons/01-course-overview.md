## Description

Community health workers have been doing this work for far longer than the job title has existed. Neighbors who knew which clinic would see someone without insurance, church members who drove elders to dialysis, promotoras who explained a diagnosis in the language a family actually spoke at home — that is the tradition you are stepping into. This course is where you learn what that tradition is, why the health system now hires for it, and what it means to hold the role professionally.

You will start with the role itself: what a CHW does, where the work came from, and how it fits alongside nurses, social workers, and care managers on a team. From there you will pick up the equity lens that makes the work make sense — why two people with the same diagnosis can have completely different outcomes, and how housing, food, transportation, income, and safety sit underneath most of what shows up in a clinic. Then you will walk through the system your clients have to navigate, including the coverage basics you will be asked about constantly, and the policies that shaped that system.

The course closes with evidence: what research actually shows about CHW impact, and how to tell the difference between counting what a program did and measuring what changed. Throughout, one boundary holds steady — CHWs educate, support, navigate, and advocate. You will never diagnose, prescribe, or give medical advice, and knowing exactly where that line sits is part of doing the job well.

## Objectives

- Describe the CHW role, its history, and its public health impact
- Explain health equity and identify social determinants of health affecting clients and communities
- Describe how the healthcare system and policies like the ACA and Triple Aim shape CHW work
