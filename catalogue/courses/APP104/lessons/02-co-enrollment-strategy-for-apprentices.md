## Why co-enrollment is a decision, not a default

A Registered Apprenticeship program runs on its own: the sponsor hires the apprentice, provides on-the-job learning, and arranges related technical instruction. Nothing about that requires WIOA. So the question you answer for every apprentice who walks into your AJCC is not "can we enroll them?" but "does enrolling them in Title I give this person a service they need, and can we record it honestly?"

When the answer is yes, co-enrollment does two things at once. It lets you pay for or provide services the apprentice actually needs (related instruction costs, tools, supportive services, career services), and it puts the apprentice's credential and skill gains on your board's record. CCC's catalog notes that California's 2024 WIOA Annual Report publishes no apprenticeship co-enrollment figures, and that TEGL 05-25 sets a 10 percent sample goal. Whatever the exact target, the takeaway is the same: apprentices your staff serve but never enroll are invisible.

When the answer is no, do not enroll just to capture an outcome. Enrollment without a real, documented service is a monitoring finding waiting to happen.

## The three Title I programs and how apprentices fit

WIOA Title I has three programs. Each has its own eligibility rules, and the apprentice must meet them on the date you enroll.

| Program | Who it is for | Typical apprentice fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adult | Adults 18 and older; priority of service for public assistance recipients, other low-income individuals, and those who are basic skills deficient | A low-wage worker entering a pre-apprenticeship or first-year apprenticeship who needs help with tools, fees, or supportive services |
| Dislocated Worker | Workers laid off or notified of layoff, and other dislocated categories defined in WIOA | A laid-off worker starting a new career through apprenticeship |
| Youth | Out-of-school and in-school youth within the WIOA age ranges who meet the other Youth criteria | A young adult out of school entering a pre-apprenticeship or apprenticeship, often with work experience or support needs |

Two practical points. First, a person can be eligible for more than one program; your board's local policy decides how you choose (for example, whether Dislocated Worker is preferred when someone qualifies for both Adult and Dislocated Worker). Second, an apprentice who is already employed by a sponsor is still employed. That does not make them ineligible, but it changes the questions you ask: is this person under-employed or low-wage under your board's self-sufficiency definition, and what service do they need that the sponsor is not providing?

## A decision sequence you can use on Monday

1. **Confirm the apprenticeship.** Is the program registered (with DAS in California or with DOL), and is the person a registered apprentice or about to be? Pre-apprenticeship is a different situation; note which one it is.
2. **Name the need.** Write down the specific service: related technical instruction tuition, required tools or gear, transportation, career services, or OJT support while the apprentice ramps up. If you cannot name one, stop.
3. **Check who else is paying.** Sponsors, apprenticeship funds, Pell, and other grants may already cover the cost. WIOA should fill a gap, not duplicate.
4. **Screen each Title I program.** Run eligibility for Adult, Dislocated Worker, and Youth as applicable. Record which ones the person meets.
5. **Apply local policy.** Use your board's co-enrollment and program-priority policy to pick the program, or programs, that fit.
6. **Consider partner co-enrollment.** Many boards also co-enroll in Wagner-Peyser or other partner programs. Follow your board's policy on which partners are expected.
7. **Document the reasoning.** A short case note explaining why this program, and what service, protects the file at monitoring.

## Worked example

Darnell, 27, was a warehouse lead until his distribution center closed. He has been accepted as a first-year inside wireman apprentice with the fictional Central Coast Electrical JATC. The sponsor pays his wages, but he must buy a tool kit and pay a related-instruction fee before classes start, and his car is unreliable.

- Apprenticeship confirmed: registered program, start date next month.
- Need: tools, instruction fee, transportation help.
- Other funds: the JATC does not cover tools; no grant is available.
- Eligibility: Darnell meets Dislocated Worker criteria because of the closure. His household income would also make him Adult-eligible.
- Local policy at the fictional Valley Regional Workforce Board: when a person qualifies for both, enroll in Dislocated Worker.
- Decision: co-enroll in Title I Dislocated Worker.

Sample case note:

```text
Darnell R. accepted into Central Coast Electrical JATC inside wireman RA (registered),
start [date]. Needs tool kit and RTI fee not covered by sponsor; transportation barrier.
Eligible DW (plant closure, [layoff documentation]) and Adult. Per VRWB co-enrollment
policy, enrolled DW. Services planned: training support for RTI, supportive services
(tools, transportation). Partner co-enrollment per local policy.
```

## Discussion prompt

In a live session, ask the group: which apprentices have you served in the past year without enrolling them? What service did they get, and would it have justified enrollment?

## Practice

For each case below, decide whether to co-enroll in Title I, which program fits, and the one local-policy question you would need answered. Write a two-sentence case note for each.

1. Lupe, 19, left high school last year and is starting a building-trades pre-apprenticeship. She needs work clothes and a bus pass.
2. Kevin, 45, a third-year plumbing apprentice earning journey-track wages, asks for a career services appointment to update his resume. He needs nothing else.
3. Amara, 33, receives CalFresh and has just been indentured as a first-year medical assistant apprentice. Her sponsor covers instruction but not childcare during evening classes.

Compare your answers with a colleague. Where you disagree, identify whether the disagreement is about federal eligibility or about your board's local policy.
