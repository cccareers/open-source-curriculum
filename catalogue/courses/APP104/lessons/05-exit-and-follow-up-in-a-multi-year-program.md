## The problem with a four-year program

Most WIOA services last weeks or months. A Registered Apprenticeship can last several years. If you treat an apprentice like any other participant, two things go wrong:

- **The participant exits too early.** Services stop after the first semester's tools and fees, and the participant exits. The apprenticeship completion arrives years later, long after the window in which it would have counted for credential attainment.
- **Nobody tracks the middle years.** Skill gains are only counted while the participant is in training and enrolled, so an early exit also ends your chance to record yearly progress.

Planning exit is not about gaming the clock. It is about matching the services the apprentice genuinely needs to the length of the program, and documenting that honestly.

## How exit works

Under WIOA's common exit approach, a participant exits when they go 90 consecutive days without receiving a qualifying service and no further service is planned. The exit date is the last date a qualifying service was provided, set after the fact. Self-service, information-only services, and follow-up services do not keep a participant from exiting.

Exit starts several clocks:

| After exit | What is measured |
| --- | --- |
| 2nd quarter after exit | Employment rate; median earnings |
| 4th quarter after exit | Employment rate |
| During participation or within one year after exit | Credential attainment (for those in education or training) |

Confirm the current definitions in the federal performance guidance and your state's exit and performance directives. Also check whether your state uses common exit across additional partner programs, since a service in a co-enrolled partner program may affect the exit date.

## Planning options for an apprentice

There is no single right answer. Choose the plan that fits the apprentice's real needs and your board's local policy.

**Option A: Stay enrolled with planned services.** If the apprentice needs support across multiple years (for example, related-instruction costs each year, required tools at each level, or supportive services during evening classes), schedule those services in the individual employment plan. Each real service keeps the participant enrolled, lets you keep recording skill gains, and brings the completion credential within reach.

**Option B: Plan a deliberate exit.** If the apprentice only needed help getting started, let them exit. Accept that a credential arriving more than a year after exit will not count for your board, but employment outcomes will still be measured. This is the honest choice when no further service is needed.

**Option C: Re-engage later.** If the apprentice's needs change, for example a layoff mid-program, a new enrollment may be appropriate. Follow your state and board rules on new periods of participation.

Do not create services to hold a file open. A service with no real need behind it is a monitoring risk and is not what the performance system is designed to measure.

## Follow-up after exit

Follow-up services begin after exit. For the Adult and Dislocated Worker programs, follow-up is provided as appropriate for up to 12 months after exit; for Youth, follow-up is required for at least 12 months. Follow-up does not reset the exit date.

For apprentices, follow-up is the time to:

- confirm the apprentice is still employed with the sponsor,
- collect progress letters or wage information for the participant file, and
- catch problems early, such as a lost job site or a failed exam.

Your board's local policy sets the follow-up schedule, contact method, and documentation standard.

## Worked example: Darnell's timeline

Darnell is in a four-year electrical apprenticeship, enrolled in Dislocated Worker. His case manager plans with him:

| Year | Planned services | Outcomes to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | RTI fee, tool kit, transportation support | Year-1 milestone (MSG) |
| 2 | RTI fee; second-level tools required by sponsor | Year-2 milestone and exam (MSG) |
| 3 | RTI fee if not covered by sponsor; check-ins | Year-3 milestone (MSG) |
| 4 | Final RTI support; completion | Credential at completion; then exit after last service |
| After exit | Follow-up per local policy | 2nd and 4th quarter employment, median earnings |

In year 3 the sponsor starts covering RTI. Darnell needs nothing else. The case manager documents that no further services are planned, and Darnell exits 90 days after his last service. His completion will now arrive more than a year after exit. The case manager notes this in the file rather than inventing a service.

Sample case note:

```text
[date] Darnell R.: sponsor now covers RTI; participant reports no further service needs.
No services planned. Participant expected to exit per common exit rules; last service
[date]. Follow-up scheduled per VRWB policy at [intervals]. Credential expected
[date], outside credential window.
```

## Discussion prompt
