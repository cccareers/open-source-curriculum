## Two outcomes, two clocks

Apprentices produce two kinds of performance outcomes, and they run on different clocks.

- **Credential attainment** is an exit-based indicator. It looks at whether a participant who was in education or training earned a recognized credential while enrolled or within the window after exit set by the performance guidance. As the catalog for this course states, an apprenticeship completion is a recognized credential.
- **Measurable Skill Gains (MSG)** is a real-time indicator. It looks at whether a participant in education or training during a program year made documented progress toward a credential or employment. It does not wait for exit, and it can be earned in every program year the participant is in training.

For a four-year apprenticeship, that difference is everything. The credential may arrive years after enrollment. Skill gains can be recorded along the way, every year, if you document them.

## What counts as a skill gain for an apprentice

Federal performance guidance defines a limited set of MSG types. Two fit apprentices most naturally:

| MSG type | What it looks like in an apprenticeship | Evidence to collect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Training milestone | Satisfactory progress at a sponsor-defined step, such as completing a year of on-the-job learning or earning a scheduled wage increase | A progress report or letter from the sponsor or training provider showing the milestone and date |
| Skills progression | Passing a required exam or competency check in the program | Documentation of the passed exam or check, with date |

Other MSG types, such as educational functioning level gains or transcripts, can apply if the apprentice is also in adult education or a credit-bearing program. Confirm the current definitions and documentation requirements in the federal performance guidance and your state's MSG directive before relying on any type.

Two rules to remember:

- **One per program year for the indicator.** Recording several gains in a year is fine, but the indicator counts whether the participant achieved at least one.
- **Dated and documented.** A gain without a date and source document will not survive a data validation sample. CCC's catalog cites TEGL 05-25 as setting a 10 percent sample goal; whatever sample your files land in, expect the evidence to be checked.

## Recording the credential

When the apprentice completes, record the credential in CalJOBS with:

- the credential type that corresponds to a Registered Apprenticeship completion certificate (check the current CalJOBS credential type values),
- the date the certificate was issued, and
- the source document, such as the certificate from DAS or DOL, or a sponsor completion letter that your state accepts.

Interim certificates matter too. Some programs issue an industry certification partway through. Whether a given interim certificate is a recognized credential depends on the federal definition; check it before recording it as one.

## Worked example: Darnell's first two years

Darnell entered his electrical apprenticeship in the fall. Here is what his case manager recorded:

| Date | Event | Recorded as | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| End of year 1 | JATC progress report: year 1 OJL hours and RTI complete, moved to second-year wage step | MSG, training milestone | JATC progress letter on file |
| Mid year 2 | Passed the program's required code exam | MSG, skills progression | Exam result from JATC |
| End of year 2 | Moved to third-year wage step | MSG, training milestone | JATC progress letter |

Note that the exam and the year-2 wage step fall in the same program year. Both are recorded, but the indicator counts Darnell once for that year. The year-1 milestone counts for the prior program year.

Sample case note:

```text
[date] Received JATC progress letter: Darnell R. completed year 1 OJL and RTI,
advanced to 2nd-year wage step effective [date]. Recorded MSG - training milestone,
date [date]. Letter uploaded to documents.
```

## Building the habit

The most common reason apprentices have no MSG on record is that nobody asked the sponsor for the paperwork. Practical steps:

- At enrollment, ask the sponsor how and when they issue progress reports and wage-step notices.
- Put a recurring reminder in your calendar aligned to the sponsor's schedule.
- Agree with the sponsor on a simple progress letter that names the participant, milestone, and date.
