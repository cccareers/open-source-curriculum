## Description

This course puts you inside a one-week Agile sprint, on a small team, working against a legacy codebase you didn't write — and you're doing it as the QA engineer, not as a developer. Every ceremony you'll practice here is taught from that seat: what a QA engineer contributes in refinement, in planning, in standup, through the sprint, and in review.

You will learn Agile's values and a Scrum-flavoured ceremony cycle, then spend the bulk of the course applying them: reading an unfamiliar codebase and its documentation, turning acceptance criteria into testable checks, building a sprint test plan against a real delivery date, escalating a blocking defect clearly, keeping a defect record honest from discovery to verified closure, and reporting what you did and did not verify in a review.

Most of this course's 50 hours are supervised team sprint work, not reading. The lessons give you the frameworks, templates, and worked examples; the sprint itself — the final project — is where you use them.

## Objectives

- Explain Agile values and the QA engineer's role in each ceremony
- Orient yourself in an unfamiliar legacy codebase and its documentation
- Turn a user story's acceptance criteria into testable checks
- Plan a sprint's testing work against scope and delivery dates
- Collaborate daily with a team and escalate issues clearly
- Track defects from discovery through verified closure
- Demonstrate completed work and give useful retrospective input

## Prerequisite Knowledge

- ops100 Git / Chrome Devtools, or equivalent fluency with Git, pull requests, and browser debugging
- Enough programming background to read an unfamiliar JavaScript codebase (web101, node100, alg100 or equivalent)
