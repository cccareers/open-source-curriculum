## Goal

You are an eligibility technician at the Valley Regional Workforce Board's AJCC. Your supervisor is preparing for a state monitoring visit and has pulled three intake cases that staff flagged as "not sure." Your job is to make the eligibility determination for each one, list the documentation that supports it, and write the eligibility case note a monitor would accept without follow-up questions.

The cases are fictional.

**Case A: Marcus, 26, separating service member.** Marcus leaves active Army service next month with an honorable discharge expected. He wants training as an HVAC technician. He has his separation orders but not yet his final discharge paperwork. He mentions his wife, Tasha, is also job hunting since the family relocated for his last assignment.

**Case B: Rosa, 47, laid-off apprentice.** Rosa was in her second year of a carpentry apprenticeship when her employer, a residential builder, closed its local division. She is receiving unemployment insurance. She wants to finish her apprenticeship with a different sponsor and needs help with tools. She has no layoff letter. The employer sent a group text to the crew.

**Case C: Kevin, 19, school status unclear.** Kevin was enrolled at a continuation high school but hasn't attended since January. The school says he's still on the roster. He lives with his mother, who receives CalFresh. He has no pay stubs and is unsure of his mother's income.

## Requirements

For **each** case, produce:

1. **Determination.** Program (Adult, Dislocated Worker, or Youth), category or definition relied on, and eligible / not eligible / pending. If pending, state exactly what's missing.
2. **Element-by-element evidence table.** Each required element, yes/no, and the document from the current Acceptable Documentation List (WSD24-04) that supports it.
3. **Self-attestation decision.** For any element where you'd consider a signed applicant statement, state whether the current list permits it for that element and what you would record.
4. **Priority of service.** Where the Adult program applies, the tier under WSD24-06 / WSD24-15 and its basis. Where it doesn't apply, say so.
5. **Eligibility case note.** Using the structure from Lesson 6.

Then add a one-paragraph **cover memo** to your supervisor naming the single biggest monitoring risk across the three cases and what the office should change.

## Constraints

- Cite only the current versions of WSD24-04, WSD24-06, WSD24-15, WSD18-03, and your board's local policy. Don't cite rescinded guidance.
- Where you rely on local policy, name the policy. Where your board's policy is unknown to you, write "[local policy: confirm]" rather than guessing.
- Don't invent document types. If you aren't sure a document is on the list, say so and say what you'd check.
- Keep each case note under 250 words.
- Timebox: about 20 minutes for the three cases, then 10 minutes of peer review.

## Definition of Done

- [ ] Three determinations, each naming program and category
- [ ] Every element in every table is tied to a named document or marked as a gap
- [ ] Each self-attestation decision states whether it is permitted and why
- [ ] Priority status recorded or marked N/A, with the reason
- [ ] Three case notes that a peer, reading as a monitor, can follow with no questions
- [ ] A cover memo with one specific, actionable recommendation

## Hints

- **Marcus:** decide whether he can be served now or must wait for discharge paperwork. Check what WSD24-04 accepts for separating service members. Tasha is a second potential applicant, so think about which category her situation might fit, but don't determine her eligibility in Marcus's file.
- **Rosa:** the group text isn't a layoff letter. Think about what other evidence of the closure or layoff would be on the list, and whether "unlikely to return" applies to someone continuing in the same trade. Read that element carefully.
- **Kevin:** "on the roster" and "attending" aren't the same. Decide school status from the definition in WSD24-04, not from the school's enrollment system. Then consider which low-income evidence (such as the household's CalFresh receipt) the list accepts before you reach for self-attestation.
- Your peer reviewer should write down every question they would ask. Zero questions is the target.
