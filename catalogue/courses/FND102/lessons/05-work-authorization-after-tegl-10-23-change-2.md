## What changed

Federal guidance on work authorization has changed. The federal TEGL 10-23 Change 2 has been rescinded, and California staff now look to the state directive **WSD18-03** for when work authorization has to be verified. Your job in this lesson isn't to memorize either document. It's to know which one governs, when the verification question comes up, and how to show in the file that you answered it.

Read the current WSD18-03 and any state notice your board has circulated about the rescission before you change your practice. If your board has issued local guidance, that is your third reference. Where these documents differ from habits staff picked up under the old federal guidance, the current documents win.

## Why this is an eligibility question

WIOA Title I programs require that participants be legally able to work in the United States. That is why it sits on the eligibility checklist alongside age and Selective Service. A gap here doesn't just make the file weaker. The participant may have been enrolled without a required element.

## The decision logic

At each eligibility determination, work through these questions in order:

1. **Which program and service is this?** The verification requirement attaches to eligibility for specific programs. Confirm in WSD18-03 which programs and situations require verification.
2. **Does WSD18-03 require verification here?** Answer this from the current directive, not from what the team did under TEGL 10-23 Change 2.
3. **If yes, what evidence does it accept?** Match it to the current Acceptable Documentation List in WSD24-04. Note that some documents prove citizenship and others prove authorization to work, and they aren't always the same thing.
4. **Does the document have an expiration date?** If the authorization is time-limited, record the expiration date and check WSD18-03 and your local policy for what you must do if it expires during participation.
5. **Record the answer** on the eligibility record and in the case note: required or not, the document, and the directive relied on.

## Worked example: Ana

Ana, 31, applies for Adult services after a seasonal packing job ended. She is not a U.S. citizen and presents an employment authorization document with an expiration date eleven months away.

- Program: Adult. Verification question applies (confirm in WSD18-03).
- Evidence: employment authorization document. Check that it's an accepted type on the current list.
- Expiration date recorded, plus a calendar reminder set ahead of expiration, per local policy.
- Case note: "Work authorization verified per WSD18-03 (current version). Employment authorization document reviewed 4/2, expires [date]; copy in file. Reminder set per local policy to review before expiration."

Notice what the note does *not* say: it doesn't cite TEGL 10-23 Change 2. Files that cite rescinded guidance are a flag to any monitor reviewing post-rescission enrollments.

## Clean-up checklist for your team

- [ ] Intake scripts and checklists no longer cite TEGL 10-23 Change 2
- [ ] Case note templates name WSD18-03
- [ ] Staff know where to find the current version of WSD18-03
- [ ] A process exists for authorizations that expire during participation

## Practice

1. Open your office's intake checklist or case note template. Find every reference to work authorization and to TEGL 10-23 Change 2. Mark what needs updating, and draft the replacement wording that cites WSD18-03.
2. For each fictional applicant, write whether you would check WSD18-03 for a verification requirement, and what you would record:
   - **Tomas**, U.S.-born citizen, applying to the Dislocated Worker program, presents a California driver's license and a birth certificate.
   - **Mei**, lawful permanent resident applying to the Adult program.
   - **Sam**, 17, applying to the Youth program.
3. Discussion (5 minutes): what will you tell a colleague who says "we always did it this way under the federal TEGL"?
