## Why the hard cases matter

Easy cases decide themselves. Monitoring findings come from the cases in the middle: the worker whose layoff letter is vague, the apprentice who is employed but underpaid, the 19-year-old who "sort of" left school. This lesson gives you a repeatable way to work those cases and connect every eligibility element to evidence.

The method has three steps:

1. **Pick the program and the category first.** Adult, Dislocated Worker, or Youth, and within that program, which category or definition you are relying on.
2. **List the elements that category requires.** Each element is a yes/no question.
3. **Match each element to an item on the Acceptable Documentation List** in WSD24-04. Use the current version of the list. If you can't name the document, the element isn't documented.

## The three programs at a glance

| Program | Core questions you answer | Where judgment usually comes in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adult | Age, eligibility to work, Selective Service (where it applies), and, for priority, low-income or other priority status | Low-income calculation, Selective Service for men past registration age |
| Dislocated Worker | Which DW category applies and the elements of that category | "Unlikely to return," general announcements, self-employed, separating service members |
| Youth | Age, school status at enrollment, low income where required, and at least one barrier | In-school vs. out-of-school, which barrier is documented |

For exact elements and definitions, use WSD24-04 and your board's local eligibility policy. This lesson teaches the decision logic. It doesn't replace those documents.

## Dislocated Worker: name the category

A Dislocated Worker determination is only as strong as the category it names. The common categories are a laid-off or terminated worker, a worker affected by a plant closure or substantial layoff, a formerly self-employed person, a displaced homemaker, and certain military spouses. Separating service members are also commonly served as Dislocated Workers. Check WSD24-04 for the exact definitions and the documentation for each one.

**"Unlikely to return"** is the element that causes the most findings. It means the person is unlikely to return to their previous industry or occupation. It is a judgment, so your file has to show the reasoning. Good evidence includes labor market information showing decline in the occupation, the employer's own statements, and the person's documented search history. Your board's local policy may define what counts. Find that policy and follow it.

## Youth: school status is set at enrollment

Whether a young person is in-school or out-of-school is set at the time of enrollment, and it determines which age range, income, and barrier rules apply. Don't assume. A student on summer break, a student in a GED program, and a young person who stopped attending but is still on a roster can each come out differently. Look up how WSD24-04 treats each situation before you decide, and record the source you used for school status.

## Worked example: Maria

Maria, 44, was a medical billing clerk at Foothill Family Clinic for eleven years. The clinic moved billing to an out-of-state vendor and laid off its billing team. She has a separation letter and is receiving unemployment insurance.

| Element | Answer | Evidence (check against current list) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Category | Laid off / terminated | Employer separation letter |
| Eligible for or exhausted UI | Yes | UI claim record |
| Unlikely to return | Yes | Regional LMI shows declining in-house billing roles; letter says function permanently moved |
| Selective Service | Not applicable (female) | Noted in file |
| Eligible to work | Yes | Verified per WSD18-03 (Lesson 5) |

The judgment call is "unlikely to return," and it is backed by two independent sources, not just Maria's word.

## Worked example: Devon, an apprentice

Devon, 23, is a first-year electrical apprentice at Central Valley Electrical JATC and is employed. He asks about WIOA help with tools and supportive services. Being employed doesn't rule out the Adult program, but it changes which questions matter: income, priority status, and whether your board serves employed workers. Before you decide, check the apprenticeship guidance in WSD24-04 and your board's local policy. Write down which provisions you relied on. Apprenticeship files are where documentation gaps have been found. CCC's catalog cites a DOL OIG audit finding that many sampled apprenticeship grant files lacked eligibility documentation.

## Quick checklist before you sign

- [ ] Program and category named in writing
- [ ] Every element answered yes/no
- [ ] Each "yes" tied to a named document from the current Acceptable Documentation List
- [ ] Judgment elements ("unlikely to return," school status) have a written rationale
- [ ] Local-policy decisions cite the local policy by name

## Practice

Work in pairs or breakout rooms (about 10 minutes).

1. **Ray, 52**, ran his own small landscaping business for nine years and closed it after two seasons of drought cut his contracts. Choose the program and category, list the elements, and name the document you would ask for on each one. Use your copy of the Acceptable Documentation List.
2. **Aaliyah, 18**, stopped attending high school in March and enrolled in an adult-school GED class in May. She applies in June. Decide in-school or out-of-school, cite the WSD24-04 provision you relied on, and list what you would need for her barrier.
3. Debrief: what was the hardest element to document in each case, and what would a monitor ask for first?
