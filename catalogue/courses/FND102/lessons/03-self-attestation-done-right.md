## What self-attestation is, and isn't

Self-attestation means the applicant signs a statement that a fact is true, and that signed statement is the evidence. It's a legitimate tool. Without it, some people who qualify could never be served: someone fleeing a housing situation with no paperwork, or a young person with no access to family income records.

It is not a shortcut for when collecting documents is inconvenient. Monitors are trained to spot files where self-attestation shows up on element after element with no explanation. That pattern reads as "staff didn't try."

## The two questions you ask every time

Before you accept a self-attestation, answer both questions:

1. **Is self-attestation allowed for this element?** The Acceptable Documentation List in WSD24-04 shows which documentation types each eligibility element accepts. Some elements allow applicant statements and some don't. Check the current list for the specific element. Don't go from memory, and don't assume that because it worked for one element it works for another.
2. **Did you try the better sources first, and can you show it?** Your board's local policy may require staff to try third-party or documentary evidence before accepting a statement, and to record what was tried. Even where it isn't strictly required, documenting the attempt is what makes the attestation defensible.

If the answer to question 1 is no, self-attestation isn't an option. Keep working the case or determine the person ineligible for that category.

## What a defensible self-attestation contains

Use your board's approved form if one exists. Whatever the form, a monitor will look for:

| Part | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| The specific element | Exactly which fact is attested (e.g. "household income for the period your income determination uses"), not "all eligibility" |
| The applicant's statement | In plain words the applicant understands |
| Signature and date | Applicant's, dated at or before the eligibility determination |
| Staff signature | The staff member who took the statement |
| Reason documents weren't available | The barrier, in one or two sentences |
| What staff tried | Sources requested or checked, and the result |

Read the statement aloud or have the applicant read it, and confirm they understand they are attesting that it's true. Note in your case note that you did so.

## Worked example: Jordan

Jordan, 20, is staying with a friend after leaving their parents' home. Jordan applies to the Youth program and is relying on a low-income determination. Jordan has no pay stubs because they haven't worked, and no access to family records.

**Decision path:**

1. Element: low income. Check the current Acceptable Documentation List. Is applicant self-attestation an accepted type for this element for this situation? (Confirm in WSD24-04. Don't assume.)
2. Better sources tried: checked CalFresh enrollment through the county (Jordan isn't enrolled), asked about school records (not applicable).
3. If permitted, take a signed statement that names the element, the reason (no access to family records, no personal income), and what was tried.

**Weak note:** "Youth self-attested low income."

**Defensible note:** "Low income documented by applicant self-attestation (form signed 3/14) as permitted for this element under the current Acceptable Documentation List. Applicant left family household 2/2026 and has no access to family income records; no personal earnings. Staff checked county CalFresh status 3/14 (not enrolled). Statement read to applicant, who confirmed understanding."

The second note answers the monitor's three questions before anyone asks them: was it allowed, why was it needed, and what did you try?

## Red flags in your own files

- Self-attestation used for three or more elements on one participant with no stated barriers
- Attestation dated after the eligibility determination
- Generic forms that don't name the element
- The same reason text copied across many participants

## Practice

1. Pull the current Acceptable Documentation List. For each of these elements, write down whether applicant self-attestation is an accepted documentation type: age, Selective Service status, Dislocated Worker "unlikely to return," Youth school status, low income. Note the page or row for each.
2. Rewrite this note so a monitor would accept it: *"Client self-certified income. No docs."* Invent reasonable fictional facts for the barrier and the sources tried.
3. Discussion (5 minutes): when does a run of self-attestations in a file signal a real access barrier, and when does it signal a staff shortcut? How would a supervisor tell the difference?
