## Description

In CHW101 you learned what a community health worker is, where the role came from, and why health systems have come to depend on people who share the language, culture, and lived experience of the communities they serve. This course names the work itself. The national C3 framework — the Community Health Worker Core Consensus Project — identifies the set of roles that together define the scope of CHW practice, and every one of them is a real job task you will be asked to do.

This is an orientation course, not a skills lab. Your goal here is to be able to look at a situation in the field and say which role it calls for: this is cultural mediation, this is health education, this is a referral that needs closing, this is advocacy, this is outreach. You will learn what each role is, when it applies, and what it looks like when someone does it well. CHW103 is where you drill the skills themselves through practice and feedback.

Two commitments run through every lesson. The first is equity: these roles exist because access to health is not distributed fairly, and each role is a specific way of narrowing that gap for real people. The second is scope. A CHW does not diagnose, does not prescribe, and does not give medical advice. Knowing exactly where your role ends and a licensed provider's begins is not a limitation on your practice — it is part of what makes you trustworthy to both the community and the care team.

## Objectives

- Describe the C3 framework roles that define the scope of CHW practice
- Explain cultural mediation and humility, including the CLAS standards
- Describe how education, coordination, coaching, advocacy, outreach, and evaluation fit together in practice
