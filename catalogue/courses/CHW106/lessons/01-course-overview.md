## Description

You are already in the field. By the time you reach this course you have a placement, a field mentor, and a caseload of real people whose situations do not pause while you learn. This capstone is where everything the program has taught you stops being coursework and starts being practice that someone depends on.

The work here runs on two tracks at once. The first is demonstrating that you can do the job: showing the Community Health Worker roles in real encounters, keeping an evidence log that documents what you did without ever exposing who you did it with, and working under active supervision — bringing concerns to your mentor early rather than carrying them alone. The second track is launch. You will turn months of internship work into a resume, a cover letter, and a professional bio that a hiring manager can verify, and you will sharpen the workplace habits that decide whether an employer keeps you: reliability, accurate and timely documentation, teamwork, and the ability to take feedback without defending against it.

One rule holds across every part of this course, without exception. Client details never appear in your portfolio, your resume, your class discussion, or your conversations outside the placement unless they have been de-identified. Your accomplishments belong to you; your clients' stories belong to them. Learning to describe your work powerfully while protecting the people in it is one of the most important professional skills you will take out of this program.

## Objectives

- Demonstrate CHW roles in practice with real clients under supervision
- Refine a resume, cover letter, and professional bio for CHW roles
- Demonstrate professional workplace skills: reliability, documentation, boundaries, and teamwork
