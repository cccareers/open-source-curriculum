## Description

Selling business software is not a personality contest and it is not a script you memorize. It is a repeatable conversation: you find out what is actually going wrong inside someone's business, you say plainly whether your product addresses it, you deal honestly with the reasons they hesitate, and you agree together on what happens next. This course teaches you that conversation from the ground up, assuming you have never sold anything before.

The work here is people-and-language work rather than tooling. You will not be asked to open a CRM, build an outbound sequence, or model pricing — those belong to later courses in this pathway. What you will practice is the part no tool does for you: asking a question that produces a real answer, hearing the difference between a complaint and a requirement, saying something useful when a buyer pushes back, and ending a call with a commitment instead of a vague promise.

Expect to talk. Every lesson closes with a hands-on exercise built on a realistic buyer scenario, and most of them are worth doing out loud with a partner willing to play an unhelpful customer. Selling is closer to a motor skill than to a body of knowledge, and reading about it will only get you part of the way there.

## Objectives

- Describe the stages of a business software sales cycle and the roles involved at each stage
- Use open questions and active listening to surface what a customer actually needs
- Translate a customer's stated problem into a matching software capability
- Respond to a routine objection without discounting or arguing
- Ask for a specific, dated next step at the end of a sales conversation
