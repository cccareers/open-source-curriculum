## Goal

Produce a content plan grounded in real audience evidence, then execute one flagship piece of it end to end: written, published in a CMS, and distributed across at least four channels.

The point of the project is the **chain**, not any single artefact. A reviewer should be able to start at any sentence in your published piece and trace it backwards — to a brief, to a pillar, to a problem in your inventory, to a quote from a real person, to a business goal. Any link that breaks is where the work needs another pass.

Three competencies are demonstrated together here, because in practice they are never separate: deciding and distributing content across platforms, publishing and managing it in a CMS, and writing the copy itself.

Work with one organization throughout — your employer, a client, a volunteer project, or a fictional brand you define in a paragraph. If you have been using the same brand since lesson 02, continue with it and reuse what you built. Reusing your own earlier work is expected, not penalised; refine it rather than starting again.

## Requirements

### Part A — The plan

1. **Goal chain.** Business goal → marketing objective → content goal → content KPI → pillars. One line per rung. The content goal names a specific audience and a specific window in their life.
2. **Audience evidence.** At least **25 verbatim quotes** drawn from at least three sources, with the source named for each. Real conversations, support or sales records, reviews (yours and competitors'), community threads, search queries, or internal site search. Paraphrases do not count.
3. **Problem inventory.** Every quote in a table: verbatim, problem, moment, segment. Sorted and counted by problem.
4. **Pillars.** Three to five, in the six-column format from lesson 02: pillar, audience problem in their words, business outcome, journey stage, primary formats, lead channel. Each pillar traceable to specific rows of your inventory.
5. **Exclusions.** At least four things you are deliberately not making, each with a reason. At least one reason is a capacity constraint rather than a relevance judgment.
6. **Capacity arithmetic.** Your honest weekly production capacity, converted to a slot count for a quarter, showing the working.
7. **Editorial calendar.** Twelve weeks, with pillar, working title, format, owner, and status per row. At least two rows must be updates or merges of existing content, and at least one must be a buffer week. Sequencing reflects your audience's seasonality or buying cycle.
8. **Content brief** for the flagship, using every field from lesson 02 — including the stated answer, the must-answer list, the accessibility section, the out-of-scope section, and the single CTA.

### Part B — The flagship piece

9. **The written piece**, 900–1,500 words, delivering the brief's one question in the first forty words. Include the one-sentence promise at the top of your working document.
10. **A documented six-pass edit**: promise, structure, sentence, evidence, accessibility, CTA and voice. Record the word count after each pass and one sentence on what that pass changed. The sentence pass must remove at least 15% of the words.
11. **One multimedia asset** produced to a creative brief — a short video, an audio clip, or a static graphic that carries information the words cannot. It ships with captions and a transcript if it has audio, or with alt text and the underlying data as text if it is a graphic. Include the creative brief, the script or layout plan, and the shot list if you filmed anything.
12. **Three before-and-after copy rewrites** taken from the organization's existing live copy — one long-form passage, one short-form or social caption, and one set of microcopy (a button, a link, a form label, or an error message). Each with a diagnosis naming the specific defects, and before and after word counts.

### Part C — Publish and structure

13. **Published in a real CMS** at a live URL. The piece uses real heading blocks in sequential order, real tables with header cells where there are tables, descriptive filenames and alt text on every image (or a deliberate empty alt, noted as deliberate), and link text that stands alone.
14. **A content model** for the site: at least three content types with purpose, fields, taxonomies, and lifecycle, in the YAML format from lesson 05. At least three fields are structured data rather than free text, each with a stated place it will be reused.
15. **Taxonomy design and governance**: the taxonomies, their terms, which types they apply to, one-or-many per record, and a named owner. Plus the rule for what happens when someone wants a new term. If the site has existing freeform tags, count them, count the single-use ones, and give the consolidation plan.
16. **Internal metadata recorded** for the flagship: pillar, owner, source or SME, review-by date, and assets used. A custom field, or a spreadsheet keyed by URL — either is acceptable.
17. **A maintenance plan**: the review cadence, the owner, and the first three specific actions, including at least one merge naming the surviving URL and the URLs that redirect to it.

### Part D — Distribute

18. **Repurposing map** with at least **six** derivatives across at least **four** distinct channels, one of which is your own site or email. Every row names the part of the research it comes from, its job, an effort estimate, an owner, and a date. Include one refresh row for the original. Sum the effort and check it against your capacity from requirement 6.
19. **Three derivatives produced for real** — the finished copy or exported file, on three different channels. Each is useful standing alone, reuses no sentences from the original, and adds something the original does not have.
20. **A six-week dated distribution schedule** with owners, including a community or comment engagement pass, one re-share of the best-performing derivative, and a scheduled evergreen loop with the months named.
21. **A tagged link table** covering every derivative, using one documented convention, with derivatives that repeat on a channel distinguished from each other. State which links must not be tagged and why.
22. **Accessibility documentation per derivative**: captions and how they are delivered, the actual alt text written in each platform's field, whether any meaning is carried by colour alone, and the link text used.

### Part E — The measurement declaration

23. **One page** naming, for the flagship and for each derivative, the job of the piece, its single primary KPI, the target, and the date you will review it. This is a forward declaration written before the data exists, not an analysis. It is what makes the plan falsifiable.

## Constraints

- **One organization, one brand voice, throughout.** A plan for one company and a piece for another does not demonstrate the chain.
- **Real evidence only.** Invented quotes and invented personas fail the project. If you cannot reach customers, use reviews, community threads, support records, and search data — all are real and all are obtainable.
- **A real CMS and a live URL.** A document that looks like a web page is not a published piece. A free personal site created for this project is perfectly acceptable.
- **Equipment you already own** for the multimedia asset. No budget is expected or rewarded.
- **Licensed music and images only**, with the source of each recorded.
- **Accessibility is a requirement, not a bonus.** A piece that ships without alt text, without captions on audio content, or with faked headings is incomplete regardless of how good the writing is.
- **No new competencies invented.** Everything here is practised in lessons 02 through 07; nothing requires a tool or skill this course did not teach.
- **Scope discipline.** If the flagship exceeds 1,500 words, it is probably two pieces. Split it and put the second in the calendar.

## Definition of done

The project is complete when every line below is true.

**The chain holds**

- Every pillar traces to at least three rows of the problem inventory.
- Every inventory row traces to a named source.
- The flagship's brief traces to a pillar, and the pillar traces to the business goal.
- The published piece answers the exact question its brief stated, in the first forty words.

**The writing works**

- The one-sentence promise is stated and the piece delivers it and nothing else.
- The six-pass edit log is present, with word counts, and pass 3 removed 15% or more.
- There is exactly one primary call to action.
- The three rewrites each carry a diagnosis and before/after word counts.
- Read aloud, no sentence is one you would not say to a customer's face.

**The CMS is structured**

- The live URL renders with correct content type, all required fields completed, and the fields you filled actually visible on the published page — verified by loading the live URL, not the preview.
- Headings are real and sequential; no bolded pseudo-headings; no heading levels skipped.
- Every table is a real table with header cells. No screenshots of tables.
- Every image has a descriptive filename and alt text, or a deliberate empty alt that is documented as deliberate.
- Taxonomy terms come from the governed list; no new terms were invented while publishing.
- Internal metadata is recorded and the review-by date is in a queue somewhere real.
- The page is not an orphan: at least three existing pages link to it.

**The distribution is real**

- Six or more derivatives mapped across four or more channels, with owners and dates.
- Three derivatives actually produced and published or scheduled.
- Effort total checked against capacity, with any cut rows named.
- The six-week schedule has no two items on the same day and no gaps longer than a week in weeks 1 to 4.
- Every external link is tagged to one convention; internal links are not tagged.

**Accessibility passes**

- Captions on every piece with audio, corrected by a human, with a published transcript.
- Alt text written in each platform's own field for every image derivative.
- No meaning carried by colour alone.
- Link text stands alone out of context everywhere, including in the email or newsletter derivative.
- Any spoken number in a video also appears as on-screen text.

**It is measurable**

- Every asset has one primary KPI, a target, and a review date, declared before publication.

## Hints

**Do part A properly and the rest gets easier.** Almost every project that stalls stalls because the pillars were invented rather than derived, and every later decision then has to be argued from taste. Spend the first third of your time on evidence.

**Get the quotes before you plan the calendar.** Reading twenty three-star reviews takes forty minutes and will change what you write. Doing it after the calendar is set means you will ignore what you find.

**Pick a flagship the audience is already asking for.** The best project topic is the question your inventory shows most often, not the most impressive one. A well-executed answer to a common question beats an ambitious answer to a rare one.

**Write the answer in the brief.** If you cannot state the answer before drafting, you are researching, not briefing — finish the research first. This one habit saves more rewriting than anything else in the course.

**Produce the multimedia asset before you finish the article.** Shooting changes what you know: you will discover the shot that explains the thing better than your paragraph did, and then the paragraph can go.

**Publish a throwaway test record first.** Ten minutes finding out what your CMS actually renders will save you an hour of confusion on the real one. Delete it afterwards.

**Fill alt text at upload.** Eight seconds each now; a miserable afternoon later.

**Write the derivatives from the research, not from the article.** Open your notes, not your published page. Derivatives written from the article always read like summaries of the article, which is the failure mode this course spends a lesson on.

**Do not fire everything on day one.** Spread it. A queue is what protects you the week something goes wrong, and it will.

**State what you are not doing, everywhere.** In the strategy, in the brief, on the repurposing map, in the measurement declaration. A reviewer reads exclusions as evidence of judgment, and they are the sentences that make the rest defensible.

**Budget the last hour for the definition of done.** Work the checklist line by line with the live URL open. Most of what it catches takes two minutes to fix and would have been visible to everyone forever.

## Submitting

Submit one document with clearly labelled sections A through E, plus:

- the **live URL** of the published flagship,
- the **exported multimedia asset**, its caption file, and its transcript,
- the **live or scheduled links** to the three produced derivatives,
- and a short **decisions log**: five sentences naming the five hardest choices you made — a pillar you cut, a topic you refused, a paragraph you deleted that you liked, a channel you declined, and a CTA you removed — with one line of reasoning each.

The decisions log is read first. It is the fastest way to see whether a plan was made or merely assembled.
