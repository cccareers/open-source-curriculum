## Description

Almost every incident you will ever help investigate ends up being a question about identity. Who was that? Should they have been able to do that? Why did that account still work? This course is about answering those three questions before someone else has to ask them in an incident review.

You will start with the vocabulary, because the single most common mistake in this area is treating authentication and authorization as one idea. Once those are separated, you will look at how a second factor is chosen and rolled out to real people who have real constraints, how permissions are grouped into roles that grant only what a job needs, and how an account's privileges quietly grow over the years an employee stays with an organization.

The work here is administrative and defensive. Attacks on credentials appear only as the reason a control exists — you are learning to build and check the controls, not to defeat them. Everything is taught vendor-neutrally: a directory is a directory whether it is on-premises or in a cloud tenant, and the concepts transfer to whichever product your employer happens to run.

## Objectives

- Distinguish authentication, authorization, and accounting, and name the factor type any given credential belongs to
- Select and roll out an MFA method appropriate to a stated user population and risk level
- Model roles and permissions that grant least privilege for a described organization
- Trace an identity through joiner, mover, and leaver events and identify where privilege accumulates
