## Scenario

A hiring manager at a CCC employer partner asks your instructor, "What do your pre-apprentices actually know about us before they reach out?" Your instructor wants to answer with a real example. Your job: produce a two-page dossier on one priority employer-partner account that shows how you chose the people, what you said to them, what you tested, and what you learned when one of them talked to you.

That same dossier becomes a Week 7 portfolio artifact. It is the most direct proof you can offer that your outreach is researched, multi-threaded, and measured — not a template blast.

## What you will produce

1. **A two-page account dossier** for one priority employer-partner organization (shared document).
2. **An experiment log** with at least one completed A/B test (Test 01) and its honest read.
3. **Two new rows in your evidence ledger** (from the Week 1 project): the Week 2 numbers row and a caption card for the dossier, plus your dated Week 2 reflection.

## Before you start (prerequisites, starter files or data)

- ICP v1 written and at least 30 contacts tiered A/B/C (Lesson 2, Practice 1-2).
- At least one priority account mapped with 2-3 contacts (Lesson 2, Practice 4).
- Test 01 sent or in progress (Lesson 3, Practice 4).
- Your evidence ledger from the Week 1 project. If you skipped it, create it now with the Week 1 project's column headers and fill the Week 1 row from your CRM history.

Dossier template:

```text
ACCOUNT DOSSIER — [Employer], prepared by [you], [date]

1. Why this account (3-4 sentences, in ICP terms)
2. Account map
   Lane            | Name / title        | Hook (sourced)            | Approach day | Status
   Practitioner    |                     |                           |              |
   Decision-maker  |                     |                           |              |
   Process owner   |                     |                           |              |
3. Messages sent (paste each, mark the variant, mark the audience-ladder version)
4. What happened (replies, booked conversations, referrals — with dates)
5. Debrief highlights (if an interview happened): their language, hiring reality,
   names given, signal on the account
6. Next actions (dated)
```

## Milestones

1. **Choose the account and justify it** (20 min). One paragraph in ICP language: role, org, and relevance criteria. If the honest justification is weak, choose a different account.
2. **Complete the map** (40 min). Fill all three lanes, or two lanes plus a sentence on why the third was not findable. Every hook must name its source (careers page, post date, talk title). Do not invent a name to fill a slot.
3. **Paste the messages** (30 min). Include the actual text you sent to each mapped contact. Annotate how you adapted tone, length, and ask size for each lane (recruiter vs. manager vs. practitioner).
4. **Write up Test 01** (30 min). In your experiment log, record the variable changed, counts per variant, reply rates, and a one-sentence read in the format `direction + numbers + "small sample" + next action`. If Test 02 is done, add it.
5. **Record outcomes** (20 min). Replies, booked conversations (invite accepted, not verbal yes), and any referral, with dates.
6. **Add the debrief** (30 min, if an interview with this account happened). Summarize using the Lesson 4 debrief categories. If your first interview was with a different account, include it in a short appendix instead and note that this account has not yet converted — that is honest and acceptable.
7. **Update the ledger** (30 min). Add the Week 2 numbers row (same columns as Week 1, plus three new ones: `a_tier_count`, `accounts_mapped`, `conversations_booked`). Add a caption card for the dossier. Write your dated Week 2 reflection: hard, boring, surprising.
8. **Peer red-team** (20 min). A partner reads the dossier and tries to find one hook that is unsourced or one claim the numbers do not support. Fix it.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The account is a priority employer partner and the justification uses ICP criteria.
- [ ] At least two lanes are mapped with named, real contacts; each hook names its source.
- [ ] Every message is pasted verbatim, with variant and audience annotations.
- [ ] Test 01 is written up with counts, rates, and a read that does not overclaim.
- [ ] Booked conversations are counted only when a calendar invite was accepted.
- [ ] The ledger has a Week 2 row, a dossier caption card, and a dated reflection.
- [ ] No private or non-professional information appears anywhere (Lesson 2's "sources" rule).

## Evidence checklist

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Targeting | Account chosen by preference; justification generic | Justification in ICP terms; 2-3 lanes mapped with sourced hooks | Also explains which lane to approach first and why, and the order was followed |
| Message adaptation | Same message to every lane | Visible changes in tone, length, and ask size per lane, annotated | Annotations predict a reaction and the outcome is compared to the prediction |
| Test honesty | Declares a winner from one small test | Directional read with numbers and "small sample" | Two tests in the same direction before changing the control |
| Conversion | Positive replies left as open threads | Replies converted with concrete windows and an invite | Soft yeses logged with a dated follow-up that was then sent |
| Ledger | Missing or estimated numbers | Week 2 row, caption card, and reflection complete | Ledger notes what changed from Week 1 and why |

## Stretch goals

- Build a second dossier for a non-partner Tier 2 account and compare reply behavior between the two.
- Draft the warm-referral message (Lesson 3, Practice 8) and attach it to the dossier, ready for when a contact names a colleague.

## Reflection prompts

- Which lane replied first? Does that match what Lesson 2 predicted?
- What did you learn about this employer that you could not have learned from its careers page?
- If you were the hiring manager reading this dossier, what would make you more willing to take a call?

## Instructor notes (common pitfalls, how to adapt for time)

- **Pitfall: picking the "dream" employer with no hook.** Push learners toward the account where they have the strongest sourced hooks, not the biggest brand.
- **Pitfall: verbal yes counted as booked.** Enforce the invite-accepted rule; it carries into the Week 3 funnel analysis.
- **Privacy:** the dossier names real people. Keep it in a document shared only with the instructor; for the Week 7 portfolio, learners should redact contact names to roles ("engineering manager, Employer X") unless they have permission.
- **Short on time:** Milestones 1, 2, 4, and 7 are the core (about 2 hours).
- **Arc:** second link in the chain described in `enhancements/PREAPP-ARC.md`. The Week 3 project uses this account's contacts as the "buyer" for the scored pipeline.
