## Purpose

After watching, a learner can turn a friendly but open-ended reply into a confirmed 20-minute meeting by replying fast, offering two concrete windows, and sending the invite themselves — and can handle the soft yes and the yes-then-silence.

## Audience and prerequisites

Week 2 participants who have started receiving replies. Assumes they know what an informational interview is (Lesson 4, "What an informational interview actually is").

- Dates and times appear as text (not only in a calendar grid), and time zones are written out.
- Transcript includes the full scheduling message as copyable text.

## Check for understanding

1. Why is "Whenever works for you!" a weak reply to a yes? **Answer:** It hands a busy person an open-ended scheduling task. Concrete windows turn the decision into a pick.
2. A contact replies "Sounds good!" to your proposed times but hasn't accepted the invite. Is the meeting booked? **Answer:** No. It counts as booked only when the invite exists and they have accepted it.
3. A yes went silent five business days ago. What do you send? **Answer:** One short follow-up that re-proposes two new times without asking whether they are still interested. Then let it rest and work the other mapped contacts at that account.
