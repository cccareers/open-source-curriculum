## Summary

Week 2 is the strongest-written week in the sequence. The A/B testing lesson teaches honest small-sample reading better than most professional material. Main issues: one model message has a credibility line that a Week 2 learner could not truthfully write (and names a language the program does not teach), and two tiering systems (Week 1 priority tier vs. Week 2 fit tier) are introduced without saying how they relate.

## Depth and coverage gaps

- **No worked ICP-to-tier walk-through** for "Define ideal contact profiles and qualify prospects, mapping multiple contacts per priority organization." The Marcus ICP is good; a three-row example (one A, one B, one C, each with the sentence that justifies it) would model Practice 3.
- **The audience ladder has no worked example** for "Adapt tone, length, and the size of the ask to the recipient and relationship stage." Practice 7 asks for three versions of one message; showing Dana's message rewritten for a director, a peer engineer, and a recruiter would help weaker writers.
- **No sample debrief** for "Conduct a structured 20-minute informational interview and debrief it into actionable intelligence." The seven debrief categories are clear, but a filled-in example (half a page) would set the quality bar.
- **Privacy guidance for real names in shared documents** is implied but not stated; the supplementary project adds a redaction note for portfolio use.

## Proposed additional projects

- **Drafted:** `projects/01-priority-account-dossier.md` — a two-page dossier on one priority employer-partner account (map, messages, A/B test, outcome, debrief), plus the Week 2 evidence-ledger row. Becomes a Week 7 portfolio artifact.

## Assessment ideas

- **Over-reading drill:** give five experiment-log lines; learners mark which reads overclaim and rewrite them in the `direction + numbers + "small sample" + next action` format.
- **Question audit:** a list of 12 interview questions; learners strike job-asks-in-costume, careers-page questions, and yes/no questions.
- **Scheduling message rubric:** same-day, 20 minutes named, two windows, link as fallback, invite sent by learner.
