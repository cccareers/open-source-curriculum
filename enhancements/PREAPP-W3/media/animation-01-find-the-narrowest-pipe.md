## Concept and misconception it fixes

Misconception: "My numbers are low, so I'll send more messages." The animation shows the funnel as connected pipes of different widths. Pouring more in at the top barely changes what comes out when the bottleneck is downstream, and it scales the failure. Fixing the narrowest pipe changes the output.

## Visual language (shapes, colors with color-blind-safe palette, labels)

- Five horizontal pipe sections, left to right, labeled with the lesson's stages: Researched → Messaged → Replied → Conversation held → Next step booked.
- Pipe width is proportional to the stage-to-stage conversion rate; each joint carries a text label with the percentage.
- Dots (contacts) flow through. Dots that do not pass a joint drop into a labeled tray beneath it.
- Colors (Okabe-Ito): dots in blue #0072B2; the narrowest joint is outlined with a thick dashed vermillion #D55E00 border *and* a "bottleneck" text tag. Trays in gray #999999.

## Interaction variant (optional)

A slider per joint (0-100%) and an input slider. Learners enter their own five counts; the pipes resize to their real rates and the narrowest joint is highlighted automatically. A "what if" mode lets them raise one joint by 10 points or double the input and compare the end result.

## Production notes

- Numbers in scenes 1-2 must match the lesson's example table exactly (120/95/19/7/2).
- Scene 4 numbers are illustrative: 95 × 40% = 38; 38 × 37% ≈ 14; 14 × 29% ≈ 4. Later joints keep the lesson's rates; round to the nearest whole contact (14 × 29% = 4.06 → 4), not down at each step. These are illustrative expected outcomes, not measured results.
- Use "reached at least this stage" counting, consistent with the clarification added to W3-04 in this pass.
