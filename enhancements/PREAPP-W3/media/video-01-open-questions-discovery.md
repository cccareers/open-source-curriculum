## Purpose

After watching, a learner can tell open from closed discovery questions, use the follow-up and confirm-back techniques, and score a conversation 0-2 on need, authority, and timing.

## Audience and prerequisites

Week 3 participants preparing for the recorded mock call. They have read "The framework: need, authority, timing."

## Script

Two actors: **Rep** (a new SDR at a fictional onboarding-software company) and **Buyer** (Priya, IT operations manager at a fictional 300-person credit union). Same buyer, same facts, two calls. Buyer's hidden facts (shown to the viewer only at the end): new hires wait ~2 weeks for system access; it got worse after a reorg; the CIO signs anything over $10k; their access-management contract renews in March.

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Two-actor video call recording (or animated avatars) with a live talk-time meter.
- Question labels (Open / Closed / Leading / Follow-up / Confirm-back) as lower-thirds.
- Need/Authority/Timing scorecard graphic, 0-2 each.
- Final "hidden facts" card for the viewer.

## Accessibility

- Captions with speaker labels (Rep / Buyer / Instructor).
- Question-type labels are text, not color-coded alone.
- The silence counter is shown as numerals and announced in the audio description track ("four seconds of silence").
- Transcript provided, including both scorecards as tables.

## Check for understanding

1. Rewrite "Do you have budget for this?" as an open question. **Answer:** e.g. "How does your team usually fund something like this?"
2. Why did Call 2's confirm-back matter? **Answer:** It proved listening and corrected a wrong number (60 to 50 hires) before it reached the notes or a proposal.
3. A hiring contact says, "We might open a junior role sometime next year; I'd have to check with my director." Score authority and timing. **Answer:** Authority 1 (can influence, does not decide; you now know the director decides). Timing 1 (vague window).
