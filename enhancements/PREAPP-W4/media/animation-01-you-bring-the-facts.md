## Concept and misconception it fixes

Misconception: "The AI knows about this company, so I can ask it." The animation shows the difference between asking a model to *recall* facts (it generates plausible sentences with nothing behind them) and handing it *sources* (every sentence can be traced back). It then shows the critique pass sorting a draft's claims into sourced, overstated, unsourced, and filler.

## Visual language (shapes, colors with color-blind-safe palette, labels)

- Sources are labeled document cards: SOURCE 1 (careers page), SOURCE 2 (about page), SOURCE 3 (LinkedIn post).
- Sentences in a draft are horizontal bars. A sourced sentence has a visible thread connecting it to a source card, plus a small "S1/S2/S3" tag.
- Unsourced sentences have no thread and a dashed outline plus a "?" icon.
- Palette (Okabe-Ito): sky blue #56B4E9 for sourced, orange #E69F00 dashed for unsourced, vermillion #D55E00 with a strikethrough for removed, reddish purple #CC79A7 with a wavy underline for overstated. Shapes/icons always accompany color.

## Interaction variant (optional)

A drag-and-drop H5P activity: learners get a 6-sentence AI draft and three short source texts, and drag each sentence into Keep / Rewrite (overstated) / Verify-or-cut / Cut (filler). Feedback reveals which source supports each kept sentence.
