## Scenario

Your company's standard "Warehouse Execution Platform" deck has 26 slides: company history, a logo wall, a six-bullet capability overview (the one in Lesson 5 Exercise 4), an architecture diagram, three case-study slides full of adjectives, a 48-bar grouped chart (Lesson 4 Exercise 4), and "Next Steps: Let's partner!" Rina Okafor at **Fairmount Distribution** has told you she will forward whatever you send to her COO, who will not attend the meeting and has never heard of your company.

You will tear the standard deck down and rebuild a 10–13 slide Fairmount deck that passes the headline test and the read-without-you test with a stranger.

## What you will produce

1. A teardown log: every one of the 26 standard slides marked keep / rewrite / appendix / delete, with a one-line reason tied to your Lesson 2 brief.
2. A paper storyboard (headline + evidence + beat for each slide).
3. The rebuilt deck as a PDF, with a front summary slide, assertion headlines, three data visuals from the Fairmount data, source lines, and an indexed appendix.
4. The headlines-only list.
5. Results of two read-without-you tests with people who don't know the scenario: their verbatim answers to the four questions, plus "which slide would you delete?"
6. A change log after each test.

## Before you start

- Have your Lesson 2 brief, Lesson 3 spine and Lesson 4 chart specs for Fairmount ready.
- Build a stand-in "standard deck" from the description above (rough slides are fine; the teardown is the point), or use your employer's real standard deck with customer data removed.
- Recruit two testers. Neither should be a salesperson if you can avoid it.

## Milestones

1. **Teardown (45 min).** Classify all 26 slides. Most should go to the appendix or be deleted. Write your kill list for anything someone at your company will push back on.
2. **Storyboard (30 min).** No presentation tool yet. 10–13 cards, each with a full-sentence assertion headline.
3. **Grey boxes, then evidence (90 min).** Build three charts: the DC accuracy trend (Brant highlighted against a 99.5% reference line), the location-stability vs. tenure comparison, and the credits trend shown as a big number with its comparison.
4. **Forward-proofing (20 min).** Summary slide, source lines, PDF export, a check that speaker notes are clean, a document-style filename, and an accessibility check (contrast, no meaning carried by color alone, readable on a phone).
5. **Test, revise, retest (60 min).** Tester 1 gets four minutes, then answers the four questions. Revise. Tester 2 does the same.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every one of the 26 standard slides has a decision and a reason.
- [ ] The rebuilt deck has no more than 13 main slides plus an indexed appendix.
- [ ] Every main-slide headline is a full-sentence assertion about Fairmount; the headlines-only list reads as a complete argument.
- [ ] The three charts each have one accent element, direct labels, a source line, and a bar axis that starts at zero.
- [ ] The summary slide gives situation, finding, cost, proposal, and ask in six lines or fewer, with every number traceable.
- [ ] Tester 2 answers all four questions correctly.
- [ ] The PDF filename reads like a document, and the speaker notes contain nothing a customer shouldn't read.

## Evidence checklist

- [ ] Teardown log
- [ ] Storyboard
- [ ] Deck PDF
- [ ] Headlines-only list

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Teardown judgement | Keeps most slides "just in case" | Clear keep/rewrite/appendix/delete with reasons | Kill list anticipates internal pushback and names what each cut slide would have displaced |
| Headlines | Topic labels ("Our Solution") | Assertions about Fairmount throughout | Headlines alone persuade a stranger |
| Data visuals | Busy, legend-dependent, truncated bar axes | One claim per chart, readable in two seconds | Refutes the discipline/tenure belief without blaming the Brant manager |
| Forward-survival | Fails the read-without-you test | Tester 2 passes all four questions | Both testers pass; the "delete" answer is acted on with a reason |
| Craft | Inconsistent placement and sizes | Consistent layouts, two type sizes, generous margins | Accessible on a phone; alt text on images where the tool supports it |

## Stretch goals

- Write the two-page prose memo version for the COO (Lesson 5's two-artifact option) and compare how long each takes a tester to read.
- Build the five-minute collapse version from the same deck: three slides.

## Reflection prompts

- Which slide was hardest to cut, and whose reaction were you protecting?
- What did the testers misunderstand that you thought was obvious?

## Instructor notes

- Learners resist deleting the logo wall and the company-history slides. Ask what decision each one moves.
- Watch for the 95.5–98.5 axis in learners' accuracy charts. On a line chart a non-zero baseline is allowed if it is labeled. On bars it is not.
- For a shorter session, skip the build and grade the teardown log, storyboard, and headlines list.
