## Scenario

You are presenting the **Fairmount Distribution** readout built in the Lessons 2–7 practice: four DCs, 97.2% order accuracy against a 99.5% customer requirement, mispicks 3.1× more likely on recently relocated items, and a room that includes the Brant DC manager who championed the discipline initiative. Ted Alvarez (CFO) leaves at minute fifteen.

This is a rehearsal project before the Lesson 8 capstone. You deliver the **five-minute** collapse version first, then the **twenty-minute** substance version, both under live interruption from a panel playing the Fairmount attendees from persona cards.

## What you will produce

1. A one-page brief recap (fields 1, 5, 7 from Lesson 2) and a three-length collapse plan.
2. Two recordings: five-minute version (one interruption) and twenty-minute version (five interruptions).
3. A transcription of every return sentence you used.
4. Panel scorecards.
5. A one-page self-review with six behavioural changes.

## Before you start

Complete the Lesson 3 spine, Lesson 4 chart specs, and Lesson 7 reframe for Fairmount. Recruit two or three panelists. Panelists receive these cards; the presenter does not see them.

**Panel cards**

| Who | When | Line | What a good handle earns |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ted Alvarez, CFO | Within the first two slides (both versions) | "Roughly what does this cost? I have ten minutes left." | If you give a real range, anchor it to the pilot, and return: Ted stays engaged and asks one question about the $3.1M customer. |
| Analyst | During the evidence sequence | "Your location-change number includes our October seasonal reset — 1,800 moves by design." | If you neither defend nor fold, and ask whether excluding the reset changes the finding: say "Brant still moves about 2,940 items a month outside October." |
| Brant DC manager | During the reframe | "So you're saying the discipline program was a waste of time." | If you put tension on the system, not the person, and make the old diagnosis part of the new one: nod and say "Fair enough — go on." |
| Sam Beaudry, IT | During the proposal | "Whatever we buy has to work when the wireless drops in the freezer aisle." | If you answer about offline capture directly and briefly: "Okay, that's the one I needed to hear." |
| Rina Okafor | After the ask | "Can we just talk again after peak season?" | If you answer with trajectory and ask "what would have to be true for this to be a yes?": name a concrete obstacle (October capital timing). |

## Milestones

1. **Collapse plan (30 min).** Full, half, and five-minute versions as slide lists. Decide what Ted must hear before minute fifteen.
2. **Return sentences (20 min).** Write one return sentence for each of the four interruption types (clarifying, objection, tangent, hijack).
3. **Five-minute version (recorded).** Finding, cost, ask. Ted's price interruption only.
4. **Twenty-minute version (recorded, one take).** All five interruptions.
5. **Self-review (45 min).** Watch once silent, once audio-only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Five-minute version states the finding, the cost, and a specific ask (person, scope, start date, measure).
- [ ] Price interruption answered with a range anchored to the pilot, then an explicit return — no full pricing discussion.
- [ ] Every interruption in the twenty-minute version handled with an audible acknowledge → handle → return, each under 90 seconds.
- [ ] The tangent (freezer-aisle Wi-Fi if raised as a tangent, or any off-thread question) is parked visibly with an owner and date.
- [ ] The Brant manager is never made the cause of the problem.
- [ ] The ask is on a slide and followed by at least five seconds of silence.
- [ ] No "I'll skip ahead," no reading slides aloud.

## Evidence checklist

- [ ] Brief recap + collapse plan
- [ ] Two recordings
- [ ] Return-sentence transcription
- [ ] Panel scorecards (one per panelist)
- [ ] Self-review

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Collapse discipline | Speeds up or announces cuts | Five-minute version is complete and calm | Five-minute version alone would earn the pilot; Ted's needs met before minute 15 in both versions |
| Interruption handling | Answers at length; thread lost | Acknowledge–handle–return on each; returns in one sentence | Uses interruptions to advance the argument (e.g., analyst's challenge strengthens the finding) |
| Reframe under pressure | Retreats ("every business is different") or insists | Holds the finding, invites a test | Brant manager adopts the new frame in the room |
| Presence | Fillers, rushed pace, eyes on slides | Pauses after key claims; addresses each person | Silence used deliberately after the reframe and after the ask |
| Close | "So, any questions?" | Written, specific ask then silence | Gets a yes, a smaller commitment, or a named obstacle with a date |

## Stretch goals

- Repeat the twenty-minute version with the panel choosing interruption timing freely.
- Run the read-without-you test on your deck with someone outside the course and report their four answers.

## Reflection prompts

- Which return sentence felt most natural, and which did you have to rescue?
- What did the Brant manager's line make you want to say, and what did you say instead?

## Instructor notes

- Learners often spend three minutes on Ted's price question. Stop the recording at 90 seconds and restart that segment.
- The analyst's seasonal-reset challenge mirrors Lesson 7 Exercise 7; reward "I'd like to understand that — does excluding it change the Brant number?" over any defense.
- For a shorter session, run only the five-minute version plus two interruptions.
