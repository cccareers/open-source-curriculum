## Purpose

After watching, the learner can open a sales presentation on the buyer's world rather than the vendor's, reach the stakes inside ninety seconds, and absorb an early interruption with acknowledge–handle–return.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read Lessons 3 and 6. Uses the Bay Ridge Elevator Services scenario exactly as set up in Lesson 2.

## Script

Setting: conference room, projector. Presenter at the front. Attendees: Dana Whitfield (VP Service Ops), Marcus Iyer (CFO, leaves at minute 20), Priya Raman (Director of IT), and the nineteen-year dispatch supervisor, Walt.

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Host to camera. | "Same room, same deck, two openings. Watch Marcus's face. He leaves at minute twenty, and he decides in the first ninety seconds whether to stay mentally present." |
| 0:15 | Title: "Take 1." Slide on screen: company logo, "About Us." | |
| 0:18 | Rep (Jamie) at front, looking at the screen. | **Jamie:** "Thanks so much, everyone, for your time today — really excited to be here. Quick bit about us first: we were founded in 2011, we serve over four hundred field service organizations, and we were named a leader in the category last year. Our platform has four core modules—" |
| 0:36 | Marcus glances at his phone. Caption: "[Marcus checks his phone]". | |
| 0:38 | Jamie continues; slide changes to a four-module diagram. | **Jamie:** "—dispatch, mobile, truck stock, and contracts. Today I'll focus on dispatch and mobile, since Dana mentioned those, and Priya, I've got architecture material toward the back for you." |
| 0:52 | Marcus interrupts. | **Marcus:** "Sorry — what does this cost, roughly?" |
| 0:55 | Jamie, flustered. | **Jamie:** "Oh — great question. So pricing depends on a lot of factors — seat count, modules, term length, we have a few different packages... let me actually jump to the pricing slide, it's near the end, I'll skip ahead..." |
| 1:10 | Jamie clicks through eight slides rapidly. Walt folds arms. | |
| 1:15 | Freeze. Callouts with icons and text. | Host VO: "Ninety seconds in. The protagonist is the vendor. Beats one through four — Bay Ridge's world, the change, the stakes, the struggle — are all missing. Priya was just told the next twenty minutes aren't for her. And the price question pulled Jamie off the thread entirely; 'I'll skip ahead' told the room that what he skipped was optional." |
| 1:55 | Title: "Take 2." Slide: a single sentence — "Before I show you anything, I want to test one thing with you." | |
| 1:58 | Rep (Alex) faces the room, not the screen. | **Alex:** "Before I show you anything, I want to test one thing with you — because if I'm wrong about it, the rest of this meeting isn't worth your forty-five minutes." |
| 2:08 | Pause, two seconds. Caption "[2-second pause]". | |
| 2:10 | Slide: "Bay Ridge maintains 11,400 units with 240 technicians on a route model that has worked for forty years." | **Alex:** "Bay Ridge maintains eleven thousand four hundred units with two hundred forty technicians, on a route model that's worked for four decades — a technician, a truck, and a set of buildings they know cold. Two things have moved underneath that model. Your unit count is up fourteen percent, mostly newer buildings. And a third of your technicians have under two years in the trade." |
| 2:32 | Slide: "Renewal rate: 91% → 88% → 84%." Large type. Source line visible. | **Alex:** "The visible symptom is a nineteen percent callback rate. The expensive one is this: your renewal rate has gone ninety-one, eighty-eight, eighty-four. Forty-two contracts last year — about six hundred thirteen thousand dollars of recurring revenue." |
| 2:48 | Alex looks at Marcus; pause. | *(pause)* |
| 2:50 | Alex turns to Dana. | **Alex:** "Dana, you've told me you read that as a tenure problem, and everything you've done about it — the mentor pairing, the second dispatch supervisor, the weekly callback review — is exactly the right response to that diagnosis. In eighteen months it has moved the callback rate one point." |
| 3:08 | Walt shifts in his chair. | **Alex:** "I think the diagnosis is wrong, and I think your own data says so." |
| 3:12 | Marcus interrupts. Step chip: "Acknowledge." | **Marcus:** "Before you go further — roughly what does this cost?" |
| 3:15 | Alex. | **Alex:** "So the question is the price tag before you invest the next twenty minutes. Fair." |
| 3:18 | Step chip: "Handle." | **Alex:** "Full deployment for an operation your size lands in the low-to-mid six figures a year. What I'm going to ask for today is a sixty-day pilot in one district, which is a much smaller number, and I'd rather earn it before I quote it." |
| 3:30 | Step chip: "Return." | **Alex:** "Which brings me back to the renewal number — because that's what the pilot is designed to move. Here's what your callback data looks like sliced a different way." |
| 3:38 | Freeze. Callouts with check icons. | Host VO: "Same ninety seconds. Bay Ridge is the protagonist. The change, the stakes, and the struggle all land before the product appears — and the struggle is told with respect for Dana's work. Marcus's price question got a real range in two sentences and an explicit return that tied it back to the number he cares about." |
| 4:15 | Host. | "Notice what Alex didn't say: no company history, no thank-you for your time, no module list. And he looked at people, not the screen." |
| 4:30 | Host. | "One check on honesty: 'low-to-mid six figures' is only acceptable if your company permits ranges and it's true. If you can't give a range, say so and offer when you will." |
| 4:45 | Practice prompt. | "Pause. Walt, the nineteen-year supervisor, says: 'We tried a mobile app three years ago. The techs wouldn't touch it.' Write your acknowledge, one question, and your return." |
| 5:00 | Sample answer. | Host VO: "One good version: 'That's worth taking seriously — what was it about the app they rejected? ... Right, so if it added data entry rather than removing it, that's the exact thing I want to show you in two slides. Coming back to the callback data...'" |
| 5:25 | Recap card. | Host: "Customer as protagonist. Stakes before product. Acknowledge, handle, return. And never announce a cut." |
| 5:45 | End card. | |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Take 1 slides: logo/About Us, four-module diagram, pricing slide (brief flash).
- Take 2 slides: opening sentence slide, Bay Ridge world slide, renewal trajectory big-number slide with source line ("Bay Ridge contract records, shared by Dana Whitfield").
- Step chips for acknowledge / handle / return.

## Accessibility

- Captions with speaker labels; non-verbal cues ("Marcus checks his phone," "Walt folds arms") captioned and spoken by the host in freeze-frames.
- Slides in Take 2 use dark text on light backgrounds at ≥ 18 pt equivalent; trajectory shown as numbers, not color.
- Transcript provided.

## Check for understanding

1. Which of the six beats appear in Alex's first ninety seconds? *(Answer: 1 world, 2 change, 3 stakes, 4 struggle — the product has not appeared.)*
2. Why does Alex praise the fixes Dana already tried? *(Answer: beat 4 must respect earlier attempts; it keeps Dana an ally and sets up the turn — correct responses to the wrong diagnosis moved the number one point.)*
3. What made Alex's price handle a "return" rather than a detour? *(Answer: an explicit sentence connecting the pilot back to the renewal number and resuming the next slide.)*
