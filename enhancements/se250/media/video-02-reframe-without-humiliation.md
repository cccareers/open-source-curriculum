## Purpose

After watching, the learner can deliver a commercial insight through the six-step reframe (warmer, reframe, rational drowning, emotional impact, new way, solution). The learner keeps the tension on the situation rather than the person, and lets the owner of the old diagnosis stay credible.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read Lesson 7. Bay Ridge Elevator Services readout: Dana Whitfield (VP Service Ops) believes callbacks are a tenure problem; Walt, the nineteen-year dispatch supervisor, is in the room; Marcus Iyer (CFO) leaves at minute twenty.

Warmer." | **Alex:** "Before your numbers, here's what we usually see in operations shaped like yours: three markets, eleven thousand units, a route model that's worked for decades. | 1:50 | Dana and Walt nod. Dana: "That's us." | |
| 1:53 | Step chip "2. Reframe." | **Alex:** "Here's the part that surprises people. In operations like this, the callback problem is almost never a skill problem. I know that's the opposite of what you've been working on." |
| 2:03 | Two-second pause, captioned. | |
| 2:05 | Step chip "3. Rational drowning." Slide: tenure bars 21% vs 18%. | **Alex:** "Three things from your own dispatch export. First, callback rate by tenure: under two years, twenty-one percent; two years and over, eighteen. Three points." |
| 2:15 | Slide: parts bars 4% vs 71%. | **Alex:** "Second, the same callbacks sliced by whether the part was on the truck: four percent with it, seventy-one without." |
| 2:24 | Slide: renewal losses linked to callbacks. Source line visible. | **Alex:** "Third, of the forty-two contracts you lost last year, thirty-one were buildings with three or more callbacks. Buildings with zero or one renewed at ninety-four percent." |
| 2:37 | Walt. | **Walt:** "Whose numbers are those?" |
| 2:39 | Alex points at the source line. | **Alex:** "Yours, from the dispatch export Dana's analyst sent on the fourteenth of May. If I've sliced it wrong I want to know. Does anything there look off to you?" |
| 2:47 | Walt. | **Walt:** "...No. The parts thing is true. I've said that for years." |
| 2:50 | Step chip "Give the room a way to be right." | **Alex:** "And Dana, your tenure signal is real. It was just the downstream end of this one." |
| 3:05 | Dana, arms relaxed. | **Dana:** "That actually fits what I've seen." |
| 3:08 | Step chip "4. Emotional impact." | **Alex:** "So most of those six thousand return visits weren't technicians who couldn't fix it. They knew what was wrong, wrote down a part number and drove away, and a building manager watched a second van turn up two days later." |
| 3:22 | Step chip "5. New way." | **Alex:** "So the requirement isn't more training. The truck has to be loaded from what the unit has already told you. That history has to be in the technician's hand in a machine room with no signal, and the parts decision has to happen before the route starts." |
| 3:38 | Step chip "6. Solution." | **Alex:** "That's what we do. Three slides, then I want to talk about proving it in sixty days in Queens." |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Take 1 slide with an accusatory headline.
- Take 2 slides: three evidence slides from Lesson 4 (tenure bars, parts bars, renewal-loss slide), each with a source line.
- Step chips 1–6 and the "way to be right" chip.

## Accessibility

- Captions with speaker labels; body-language cues captioned and named in the freeze-frame VO.
- Charts use direct labels and an accent shown by bold outline as well as color.
- Transcript provided.

## Check for understanding

1. Name the six steps of the reframe in order. *(Answer: warmer, reframe, rational drowning, emotional impact, new way, your solution.)*
2. How did Alex answer "Whose numbers are those?" *(Answer: he pointed to the source on the slide, Bay Ridge's own export, and invited Walt to check it instead of insisting.)*
3. What sentence let Dana keep her credibility? *(Answer: that her tenure signal is real, because newer technicians are hurt most by the information gap. Her observation became part of the new diagnosis.)*
