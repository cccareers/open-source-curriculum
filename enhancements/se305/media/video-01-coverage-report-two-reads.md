## Purpose

After watching, the learner can sanity-check a coverage figure for stale close dates, recompute coverage and expected value at their real win rate, and turn the reading into a sentence with a decision in it.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read Lesson 3. All figures come from the Meridian Freight Software dataset in Lessons 2–3 (Q4 quota $200,000; $46,000 closed; 21 deals dated in Q4 worth $612,000; 7 of them with past dates worth $196,000; 25% win rate).

## Script

Setting: a pipeline review between a rep and manager **Luis**. The CRM report is on a shared screen.

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Host to camera. | "Thirty September. Four weeks left in the quarter. Same rep data, two reps reading it to their manager. One of them is about to have a very bad October." |
| 0:15 | Title: "Take 1 — reading the number." Screen: report "Open opportunities closing this quarter: 21, $612,000." | |
| 0:20 | Rep (Kim). | **Kim:** "I'm in good shape. Six-twelve dated for the quarter against a one-fifty-four gap — that's almost four-x coverage." |
| 0:30 | Luis. | **Luis:** "What's your win rate?" |
| 0:32 | Kim. | **Kim:** "Twenty-five percent, so... break-even is four. I'm basically right there." |
| 0:38 | Luis. | **Luis:** "So, on track?" |
| 0:40 | Kim. | **Kim:** "On track. I'll keep working the deals I have." |
| 0:44 | Freeze. Callouts with icons. | Host VO: "Kim did the arithmetic correctly on numbers she never checked. She read the report — she didn't interrogate it. And 'on track' at exactly break-even is a coin flip with no margin. Her plan for October — work existing deals — follows from a number that isn't true." |
| 1:15 | Title: "Take 2 — interrogating the number." Same report. | |
| 1:20 | Rep (Alex) adds a column to the report: "Close date." Sorts ascending. Seven rows highlight with a "past date" icon. | **Alex:** "Before I quote coverage, one check. The filter is 'on or before quarter end.' Seven of these twenty-one have close dates that have already passed — some back in July. They're one-ninety-six thousand." |
| 1:40 | On-screen arithmetic builds line by line. | **Alex:** "Take them out and the clean Q4 pipeline is four-sixteen across fourteen deals. Against the one-fifty-four gap, that's two-point-seven-x — against a break-even of four." |
| 1:55 | Next lines appear. | **Alex:** "At my twenty-five percent win rate, four-sixteen expects about one-oh-four. The gap is one-fifty-four. So I'm about fifty thousand short, with four weeks left." |
| 2:08 | Luis. | **Luis:** "Couldn't some of those seven still close?" |
| 2:11 | Alex. | **Alex:** "Possibly — but a date in the past is a data error, not a forecast. I'll fix every one this week: re-date the ones with a real next step, close out the ones without. Until then I'm not counting them." |
| 2:22 | Luis. | **Luis:** "So what do you need?" |
| 2:24 | Alex; the "implication sentence" appears in a highlighted box. | **Alex:** "At my real win rate, I'd need about two hundred thousand more Q4-dated pipeline in the next two weeks, or a materially better win rate on the fourteen I have. With a sixty-one-day median cycle, new pipeline won't close this quarter — so realistically, my October is about the fourteen, and starting Q1's pipeline now. I'd like your help on the two largest of the fourteen." |
| 2:45 | Freeze. Check-icon callouts. | Host VO: "Same report. Alex checked the filter, removed stale dates, recomputed coverage and expected value at his real win rate, and said what it implies — including the uncomfortable part: new pipeline can't close in four weeks at his cycle length. Then he asked for something specific." |
| 3:25 | Host with the side-by-side. | Host: "Kim's number flattered her because hygiene failures almost always do. Nobody forgets to update a deal that got better." |
| 3:45 | Host. | "Three questions to ask of any coverage figure: What's the filter on close date? What's break-even at my real win rate? And how much of this is one deal?" |
| 4:05 | Practice prompt. | "Pause. Suppose one of Alex's fourteen clean deals is ninety-six thousand dollars. Recompute coverage without it, and say what that tells you." |
| 4:20 | Sample answer. | Host VO: "Four-sixteen minus ninety-six is three-twenty; divided by one-fifty-four is about two-point-one-x. If a single deal moves coverage from two-point-seven to two-point-one, your quarter is substantially one yes-or-no event — say so." |
| 4:45 | Recap card: check the filter; clean dates; recompute coverage and expected value at real win rate; check concentration; state the implication with a decision. | |
| 5:05 | End card. | |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- CRM report mock-up with sortable close-date column; past-date icon on stale rows.
- Line-by-line arithmetic overlay.
- Highlighted "implication sentence" box.

## Accessibility

- Captions with speaker labels.
- Stale rows flagged with an icon and the word "past," not color alone.
- All arithmetic is spoken as well as shown; transcript includes the full calculation.

## Check for understanding

1. Why did the report show 21 deals "closing this quarter" when only 14 could? *(Answer: the filter was "on or before quarter end," which includes open deals whose close dates already passed.)*
2. What is the clean coverage and expected shortfall? *(Answer: $416,000 / $154,000 = 2.70x; expected $104,000 against a $154,000 gap, about $50,000 short.)*
3. Why can't Alex simply prospect his way out of the gap this quarter? *(Answer: his median won cycle is 61 days; pipeline created now will not close within four weeks.)*
