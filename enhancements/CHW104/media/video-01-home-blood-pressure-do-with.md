## Purpose

After watching, the learner can teach a client to take and log an accurate home blood pressure reading using the seven-step do-with-not-for sequence, and can respond to "Is that number bad?" without interpreting it.

## Audience and prerequisites

CHW104 learners after Lesson 3. Actors: CHW (Lina, fictional) and client (Mr. Raymond Ellis, fictional, from project x01). Equipment: a validated upper-arm automatic cuff in two sizes, a kitchen chair, a table, a paper log.

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Overlay checklist for technique, built step by step.
- Close-ups of feet flat, arm at heart level, cuff on bare arm, cuff size range.
- Reading display is intentionally blurred, with explanatory text.

## Accessibility

- Captions; audio description narrates each body-position correction since it is visual.
- Step tags are text with numbers, not color-coded only.
- Downloadable one-page technique checklist in large print, English and Spanish.

## Check for understanding

1. Name four technique errors that can make a home reading inaccurate. *Answer: any four of — not resting five minutes, crossed legs, unsupported back, arm not at heart level, wrong cuff size, cuff over a sleeve, talking, caffeine or tobacco in the prior 30 minutes.*
2. A client asks, "Is 160 too high?" What do you say? *Answer: that is a question for the provider or nurse line; offer to call together now; if they have emergency symptoms, follow the emergency plan.*
3. Why does the client take the reading rather than the CHW? *Answer: do with, not for — the client leaves with a skill they can repeat without the CHW.*
