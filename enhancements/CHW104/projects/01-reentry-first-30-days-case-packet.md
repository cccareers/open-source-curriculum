## Scenario

This project extends Lesson 2's reentry intake into a full 30-day support packet. The client and every agency are fictional.

**Client: Mr. Raymond Ellis (fictional), 52.** Released from state prison eleven days ago after six years. Living in a sober-living residence with a shared kitchen (one microwave, one shared refrigerator shelf). Reports to parole on Tuesday mornings. Has high blood pressure and prediabetes; was on a blood pressure medication inside, received a short supply at release that has run out. Medi-Cal status unknown — he thinks an application was started before release. Answers in single words at first contact, will not give a phone number, and asks twice who reads the form. Mentions he "doesn't do clinics" because inside, "they'd make you wait weeks and then treat you like you were faking." Says his daughter, 24, lives across town and "might help if I don't mess this up." Strengths he names when asked: "I can cook — I ran the kitchen inside for three years," and "I walk everywhere."

## What you will produce

1. **Trust plan and first-visit script** — the first three things you say (with what each is meant to prove), your explanation of who sees what, and a list of what you will not ask and why.
2. **Sequenced barrier plan** — a 30-day table ordering insurance status check, medication continuity, a primary care appointment that avoids Tuesday mornings, phone access, and food — with owners, dates, and the "do with, not for" stage for each task.
3. **Medication support plan, within scope** — how you will help him get the lapsed prescription addressed by a prescriber or pharmacist (not by you), the adherence barrier questions you will ask (cost, side effects, feeling fine, complexity, access, beliefs and history), and the practical routine supports you can offer.
4. **Self-monitoring teaching plan** — a home blood pressure teaching sequence using the seven steps in Lesson 3, adapted to a shared living space, with a log format he chooses and a clear "what to do if a reading looks alarming" plan written by his clinic (you leave a placeholder for the clinic's instructions; you do not set thresholds yourself).
5. **Weight-inclusive nutrition sheet** — a one-page, plain-language, microwave-and-shared-fridge food guide that builds on his cooking skill, uses additive goals, avoids moral food language, and lists food resource referrals in order.
6. **Trauma-informed review** — a table marking where each of the five principles (safety, trustworthiness and transparency, choice, collaboration, empowerment) shows up in your packet, plus the cue you would watch for at the clinic visit and your response script.

## Before you start

- CHW104 Lessons 2–5 in full.
- Use person-first language throughout ("person who was incarcerated," not "ex-offender").

## Milestones

1. Trust plan and script (60 min). Read aloud to a partner playing Mr. Ellis in "freeze" mode; revise.
2. Sequenced barrier plan (75 min).
3. Medication support plan (60 min). Partner highlights any sentence that edges toward advice.
4. BP teaching plan (60 min). Practice the sequence with a partner and a cuff if available.
5. Nutrition sheet (60 min). Score it with Lesson 4's five material criteria.
6. Trauma-informed review (45 min).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] First-visit script explains the reason for every question and states who sees the notes and the limits of confidentiality before any personal question.
- [ ] Plan never asks about the offense or incarceration details; only what is needed for the service.
- [ ] Insurance status and medication continuity are sequenced first; scheduling avoids the parole reporting window.
- [ ] No sentence tells Mr. Ellis to take, skip, split, or change any medication, or interprets a reading. The lapsed prescription is routed to a prescriber or pharmacist the same week, with escalation guidance for symptoms listed in Lesson 3.
- [ ] BP technique steps match Lesson 3 (five quiet minutes, back supported, feet flat, arm at heart level, correct cuff on bare arm, no talking, no caffeine, tobacco, or exercise for 30 minutes, bladder emptied, two readings a minute apart).
- [ ] Nutrition sheet uses no weight goals, no "good/bad" foods, and fits a microwave and one fridge shelf; it builds on his cooking skill.
- [ ] Each of the five trauma-informed principles is evidenced at least twice across the packet.
- [ ] All names and agencies are fictional.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trust-building | Generic reassurance | Specific, kept micro-commitments; transparency about information | Anticipates testing behavior and plans the follow-up |
| Sequencing | Tasks unordered | Health-first sequence with parole constraint | Shows how each step unlocks the next |
| Scope on medications | Advises or reassures about numbers | Routes all clinical questions | Coaches him to ask the prescriber himself |
| Self-monitoring | CHW takes readings | Client does it with teach-back | Log design reflects his stated preference |
| Weight-inclusive nutrition | Restrictive or moralizing | Additive, practical, culturally and literacy appropriate | Built around a dish he already makes |
| Trauma-informed practice | Principles named only | Principles visible in concrete actions | Includes environment changes for the clinic visit |

## Stretch goals

- Plan a supported conversation, with Mr. Ellis's consent, about whether he wants his daughter involved — and how you would honor a "no."
- Draft the referral note to a reentry-focused clinic using only information he agreed to share.

## Reflection prompts

- What did you want to ask him that you decided not to? Why?
- Where did your own assumptions about people coming home show up in your first draft?

## Instructor notes

- Medi-Cal processes for people leaving incarceration (including pre-release enrollment and services) have been changing in California; do not let learners assert specific program rules. "Check status with the county or plan; verify current pre-release and reentry benefits" is the expected step.
- Learners often over-collect history. Push them to cut every question that does not change the service.
- If time is short, assign parts 1, 2, and 6 (about 3 hours).
