## Scenario

You are a CHW trainee shadowing a hospital-to-home team in a fictional Central Valley city, "Riverbend." The team hands you a de-identified case summary for a client they are worried about. Everything below is fictional; any resemblance to a real person is coincidental.

**Case summary — Client H.R. (fictional)**

- 58 years old, lives alone in a second-floor apartment with no elevator. Uses a walker since a fall last winter.
- Congestive heart failure. Readmitted to the hospital twice in four months.
- Income: about $1,100 a month. Worked in packing houses for 30 years; stopped after the fall.
- Has not filled his diuretic ("water pill") in six weeks. The discharge nurse wrote "noncompliant with meds."
- Prefers Spanish. Discharge instructions were printed in English.
- Coverage: Medi-Cal managed care. He told the nurse he "lost the card" and "doesn't know who his doctor is."
- Nearest pharmacy is 1.1 miles away. The bus stop is at the bottom of a hill.
- Mentions he goes to Mass on Sundays "when my nephew can drive me," and that a neighbor downstairs, Doña Elvia, "checks on everyone in the building."

Your supervisor's ask: "Before we see him Thursday, show me you can see the whole picture — not just the pill bottle."

## What you will build / produce

A four-part case packet (6–8 pages total):

1. **SDOH sort** — every social determinant you can identify, grouped under the five domains from Lesson 3, each tagged downstream / midstream / upstream, and each tagged "a CHW could realistically affect this" or not.
2. **Reframed chart note** — the phrase "noncompliant with meds" rewritten as a factual, non-judgmental note, plus the three questions you would need to ask H.R. to write the honest version.
3. **Navigation path** — a numbered map of the steps between "lost card, doesn't know his doctor" and "diuretic filled and taken," marking each place the system could fail him and what a CHW does at each step (with the client, not for him).
4. **Asset-paired gap map** — at least 10 assets (individual, association, institution, physical, economic, cultural) around H.R. and five genuine gaps, with every gap paired to at least one asset.

## Before you start

- Re-read Lesson 3, "Identifying social determinants in a real encounter" and "Community assets, not just community deficits."
- Re-read Lesson 4, "Coverage basics you will be asked about" and "What navigation actually looks like."
- You may invent plausible Riverbend resources (a parish, a senior center, a Medi-Cal plan member services line). Label invented ones "(fictional)". Do not use real people's names.

## Milestones

1. **Sort (60 min).** Build the SDOH table. Aim for at least 12 determinants. Example row: "Second-floor unit, no elevator, uses walker — Neighborhood and built environment — midstream — CHW could affect: partly (refer for housing transfer request; cannot change the building)."
2. **Reframe (30 min).** Write the factual note. It must contain only things the summary actually tells you, plus clearly marked unknowns.
3. **Navigation path (75 min).** Number every step: finding the plan and primary care assignment, replacing the card, confirming the diuretic prescription is still active, checking pharmacy access (delivery? closer pharmacy?), arranging interpretation for the next visit, preparing three questions for the provider. For each step write who owns it (client, CHW, plan, pharmacy, provider) and the teach-one-step-forward move.
4. **Asset map and pairing (60 min).** List assets with names (fictional is fine), then pair them to gaps.
5. **Scope check (15 min).** Highlight every sentence in your packet that touches medication. Confirm each one routes the clinical question to the pharmacist or provider.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At least 12 determinants, correctly placed in the five domains, each with a stream tag.
- [ ] The reframed note contains no judgment words (noncompliant, unmotivated, refuses) and names at least one unknown.
- [ ] Navigation path has at least 8 steps, names an owner per step, and includes a "follow up to confirm the connection" step.
- [ ] No step advises H.R. on whether, when, or how to take his medication; dosing and side-effect questions go to the pharmacist or provider.
- [ ] Asset map has at least 10 assets across at least five categories; all five gaps are paired.
- [ ] The packet uses person-first language throughout and contains no real names.

## Evidence checklist

- SDOH table (one page)
- Reframed note with three questions
- Navigation path diagram or numbered list
- Asset-paired gap map
- A short "scope check" paragraph listing what you deliberately did not do

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| SDOH identification | Lists categories generically ("housing") | 12+ specific determinants tied to case facts, correctly placed | Also notes which determinants interact (e.g., walker + hill + pharmacy distance) |
| Non-judgmental documentation | Softens wording but keeps a judgment | Purely factual; unknowns marked | Shows how the reframed note changes the team's plan |
| Navigation | Does tasks for the client | Steps are done with the client and include follow-up | Includes a gradual-release plan for the next refill |
| Asset orientation | Assets listed as decoration | Every gap paired to an asset | Explains why each first-contact asset has standing with H.R. |
| Scope | Gives medication advice anywhere | All clinical questions routed | Writes the exact referral sentence for the pharmacist call |

## Stretch goals

- Write the 60-second social-needs screening introduction you would use with H.R. at the first visit (Lesson 3, Practice 5), in plain language, and note how you would offer interpretation.
- Add a "pattern watch" note: if three other clients in the same building had the same pharmacy problem, how would you log it (Lesson 5)?

## Reflection prompts

- Which determinant did you almost miss, and what made it invisible at first?
- Where did you feel the pull to "just fix it" for H.R.? What would that have cost him next month?
- Which asset surprised you most once you started looking?

## Instructor notes

- Common pitfall: learners treat "lost the card" as the problem. The deeper issue is often that he never knew who his assigned primary care provider is — push them to find it.
- Common pitfall: listing Doña Elvia as a caregiver. She is an asset with standing, not a staff member; ask learners how they would approach her with H.R.'s permission.
- Shorten to 2.5 hours by assigning only parts 1 and 4 in class and part 3 as homework.
- Coverage specifics (Medi-Cal managed care card replacement, pharmacy delivery benefits) vary by plan and change; accept any plausible step that says "verify with the plan."
