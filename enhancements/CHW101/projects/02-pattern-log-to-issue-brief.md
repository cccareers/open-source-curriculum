## Scenario

You work at a fictional community clinic, "Eastside Family Health," with three other CHWs. Your supervisor, Ms. Park (fictional), asks the team to look at the last eight weeks of the barrier log before the quarterly meeting with the clinic's Medi-Cal managed care plan. She wants two things: "Tell me if there's a pattern worth raising, and tell me what our program can honestly claim it did this quarter."

All data below is fictional and de-identified (client IDs only).

**Barrier log extract (fictional)**

| Date | Client ID | Barrier | Organization | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3/3 | E-104 | Car broke down, missed visit | — | Rescheduled, bus pass given |
| 3/5 | E-117 | Lost Medi-Cal at renewal; says she never got the packet | County eligibility office | Re-applied; 6 weeks without coverage |
| 3/6 | E-122 | Cannot read appointment letter (English only) | Clinic | CHW explained; letter request logged |
| 3/11 | E-131 | Lost Medi-Cal at renewal; packet went to old address | County eligibility office | Re-applied; still pending |
| 3/12 | E-104 | Specialist referral denied | Health plan | Appeal started |
| 3/18 | E-140 | Lost Medi-Cal at renewal; packet arrived in English, she reads Vietnamese | County eligibility office | Re-applied; 4 weeks without coverage |
| 3/20 | E-142 | Pharmacy says prior auth needed for inhaler | Health plan / pharmacy | Filled after 9 days |
| 3/25 | E-117 | Missed cardiology visit — no coverage | Specialist | Rescheduled after reinstatement |
| 4/1 | E-155 | Lost Medi-Cal at renewal; did not understand the form asked for pay stubs | County eligibility office | Re-applied |
| 4/2 | E-160 | Interpreter not booked for visit | Clinic | Visit rescheduled |
| 4/8 | E-163 | Lost Medi-Cal at renewal; packet went to old address | County eligibility office | Pending |
| 4/9 | E-142 | Same inhaler prior auth again at refill | Health plan / pharmacy | Filled after 7 days |
| 4/15 | E-170 | Cannot afford insulin copay at outside pharmacy | Pharmacy | Referred to clinic pharmacy program |
| 4/22 | E-171 | Lost Medi-Cal at renewal; packet in English, reads Spanish | County eligibility office | Re-applied |

**Quarter activity report draft (fictional)** — written by a colleague:

- 212 client encounters completed
- 96 referrals made
- 74 clients enrolled in coverage
- 18 health education classes held
- 62 referrals confirmed attended
- Clients with controlled blood pressure among those enrolled 6+ months: 41% at start of year, 52% now
- "Our program reduced ER visits by 30%"

## What you will build / produce

1. **Pattern analysis** (one page): which entries are one-off problems, which form a pattern, and which are not yet determinable — using the four questions in Lesson 5, "Distinguishing the two."
2. **Issue brief** (one page, no client names): observation, scope and time period, who is affected, likely cause, recommendation, and the specific ask — addressed to Ms. Park, with a routing recommendation (your organization, partner, county, or legislative).
3. **Honest impact paragraph** (under 150 words): classify every line in the activity report as output, short-term, intermediate, long-term outcome, or impact; identify that the log supplies no ER-use data to substantiate the 30% claim and remove it unless supporting data are supplied; and write a 90-second "lead with an outcome, support with outputs, name one limit" statement.

## Before you start

- Lesson 5: "From one client's problem to a pattern."
- Lesson 6: "Outputs and outcomes" and "Talking about impact honestly."

## Milestones

1. Tally the log by barrier type and organization. Count distinct clients, not rows (E-104, E-117, and E-142 each appear twice; E-142's two prior-authorization entries are one client with a repeated barrier, not two clients).
2. Identify the strongest pattern (hint: it is not the car). Note what is shared: same office, same step (renewal), and three documented sub-causes among five clients (address, language, form content). E-117 reports that the packet never arrived, but the cause is unknown; do not assign this client to a documented cause.
3. Draft the brief. Lead with the count and period, add one de-identified representative story, end with a specific ask and a "by when."
4. Classify the activity report and rewrite the ER line.
5. Record yourself delivering the 90-second statement; a partner asks "how do you know?" after each sentence.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Correctly identifies the renewal-loss pattern: 6 distinct clients in about 7 weeks, all at the county renewal step, with three documented sub-causes among five clients (address, language, form content), and one unknown cause (E-117).
- [ ] Separately notes the repeated inhaler prior authorization (E-142) as a possible plan-level issue that is not yet a multi-client pattern.
- [ ] Brief is one page, uses client IDs or counts only, and names the routing level and the specific ask.
- [ ] Does not claim the cause is certain; names what would confirm it.
- [ ] Every activity-report line is classified correctly (outputs: encounters, referrals made, enrollments, classes; intermediate outcome: referrals confirmed attended; long-term outcome: BP control).
- [ ] ER claim is marked unsupported and removed: no ER-use data or comparison design are supplied. An association requires supporting data too; causal attribution also requires an appropriate study design.

- Tally table

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern recognition | Lists every barrier as equal | Identifies renewal pattern with correct count and period | Separates sub-causes and proposes which the clinic can fix itself (e.g., address updates at every visit) |
| Routing | Escalates to "the state" or the press | Routes to supervisor with correct level named | Proposes both an internal fix and an external channel |
| Confidentiality | Includes identifying story details | Counts and IDs only | Representative story is clearly composite and labeled |
| Outputs vs outcomes | Mixes categories | All lines classified correctly | Explains why "74 enrolled" is an output but "previously uninsured clients now covered" could be an outcome |
| Honest claims | Repeats the 30% claim | Removes unsupported ER claim and explains what evidence is missing | Names one population the program did not reach |

## Stretch goals

- Propose a one-line addition to the intake workflow that would catch the address problem earlier.
- Draft a logic model row connecting "renewal assistance calls" (activity) to "continuous coverage at 12 months" (outcome).

## Reflection prompts

- Which piece of evidence made you confident it was a pattern rather than bad luck?
- What would you lose if you reported this as "the county keeps dropping people"?

## Instructor notes

- Medi-Cal renewal rules, notice formats, and threshold-language requirements change; do not let learners assert specific current rules in the brief. "We observed" plus "we recommend verifying" is the correct register.
- If time is short, skip the recording and have learners deliver the statement live in pairs.
