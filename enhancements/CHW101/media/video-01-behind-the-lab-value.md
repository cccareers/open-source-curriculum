## Purpose

After watching, the learner can look at a single clinical fact (a high A1C) and name the social determinants and community assets that may sit behind it, without turning the client into a list of deficits.

## Audience and prerequisites

New CHW trainees in CHW101. Has read Lesson 3 at least once. No clinical background assumed.

## Script

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Whiteboard, center: a single sticky note reading "A1C: 9.6". Narrator's hand taps it. | "This is what the chart says about Mrs. Ana Morales. She's fictional, but you'll meet her a hundred times. Her diabetes is not controlled. If this sticky note is all you see, the next sentence in the chart usually says 'noncompliant.'" |
| 0:20 | The word "noncompliant" is written under the sticky, then crossed out with a single line. | "Let's not write that yet. Let's ask a better question: what would have to be true for managing diabetes to be easy for her — and what's missing?" |
| 0:40 | Hand draws five boxes around the sticky, labeled: Economic stability; Education access; Health care access; Neighborhood and built environment; Social and community context. | "The Healthy People 2030 framework sorts social determinants into five areas. We'll fill these in from one ordinary week of her life." |
| 1:00 | In "Economic stability": "Night shift, 2 part-time jobs." "Insulin copay — rationing?" | "She works nights at a warehouse and weekends cleaning offices. Meal timing changes every day. And last month her copay went up. When people tell you they're 'stretching' a prescription, that's rationing — and it's a cost problem, not a character problem." |
| 1:35 | In "Neighborhood": "No fresh produce within 2 bus transfers." "Fridge broken." | "Her refrigerator stopped working in the spring. Insulin needs to be stored properly. The nearest store with fresh vegetables is two bus transfers away." |
| 2:00 | In "Health care access": "Diagnosis explained in English only." "Clinic hours 8–5." | "She was diagnosed in a visit conducted in English. Spanish is the language she thinks in. The clinic closes at five, which is when she's asleep." |
| 2:25 | In "Social and community context": "Caregiver for her mother." "Fear: will asking for help affect immigration status for family?" | "She cares for her mother on weekday afternoons. And a cousin told her that using benefits could cause problems for family immigration paperwork. Whether or not that's accurate for her situation, the fear is real, and it changes what she asks for. That question goes to an immigration legal service — not to you to answer." |
| 2:55 | In "Education access": "Health literacy = her ability to find, understand, and use health information + how well organizations make that possible." | "Health literacy isn't intelligence. Healthy People 2030 describes two sides: her ability to find, understand, and use health information and services — and how well organizations make that possible for her. On the second side, so far, the system hasn't done well." |
| 3:15 | Camera pulls back: the five boxes are crowded; the A1C sticky looks tiny in the middle. | "None of this is visible in a lab value. Almost all of it becomes visible in a conversation with someone she trusts. That's you." |
| 3:30 | New color marker. Hand draws a second ring outside the boxes labeled "Assets." | "Now the half most programs skip. What does Mrs. Morales already have?" |
| 3:40 | Assets added: "Her sister — cooks together Sundays." "Parish health ministry." "Warehouse co-worker who also has diabetes." "Taquería owner who posts flyers." "Promotora network at the swap meet." | "She and her sister cook together every Sunday. Her parish has a health ministry. A co-worker on her shift also has diabetes, and they talk at break. The taquería on her corner posts community flyers. Assets aren't decoration on a needs list. They're what your plan runs on." |
| 4:15 | Hand draws arrows from three determinants to three assets: broken fridge → parish emergency fund (fictional); meal timing → co-worker break-time check-in; English-only diagnosis → promotora-led Spanish class. | "Watch what happens when we pair them. These pairings give us options to explore: the broken fridge with the fictional parish emergency fund, meal timing with a co-worker break-time check-in, and the English-only diagnosis with a promotora-led Spanish class. The plan is no longer 'try harder.' It's specific, and most of it is built on things she already trusts." |
| 4:45 | Text card: "Ask, don't infer. Ask permission. Don't screen for what you can't act on." | "Three habits keep this honest. Ask — don't infer from her neighborhood or her accent. Ask permission and explain why you're asking. And don't screen for needs you can't act on; know your referrals first." |
| 5:10 | The crossed-out "noncompliant" is replaced with: "Client reports night-shift work and a broken refrigerator; insulin storage and meal timing are current barriers. Referred to [resource]; follow-up 5/14." | "Here's the chart note a CHW can defend in a team meeting: 'Client reports night-shift work and a broken refrigerator; insulin storage and meal timing are current barriers. Referred to resource — a placeholder to fill with the actual resource — follow-up May fourteenth.' Factual. Specific. It points to a next step." |
| 5:35 | Scope reminder card: "CHWs educate, support, navigate, advocate. Questions about insulin dose or storage go to the pharmacist or provider." | "One line holds through all of it: you don't tell her how to adjust her insulin. You make sure the pharmacist hears about the fridge today." |
| 5:50 | End card: "Try it: Lesson 3, Practice 1." | "Now try it yourself with the case in Practice 1. Find the determinants. Then find the assets. Then pair them." |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Whiteboard (physical or digital, e.g., Excalidraw) with two marker colors plus a third for assets; color is never the only cue — assets are also in a dashed ring and labeled "Assets."
- Optional B-roll: hands at a kitchen table with a pill organizer; a bus stop; a church hall (stock or staged, no real clients).

## Accessibility

- Open captions in English; Spanish caption track recommended.
- Provide an audio description track that reads every displayed text label and case-note field, including the resource placeholder and follow-up date, and describes all three pairing arrows. The narration explains the pairings and reads the case note; the description track supplies any text not spoken verbatim.
- High-contrast markers on white; text at least 32 px equivalent at 1080p.
- Provide a transcript and a static image of the final whiteboard with alt text describing all five domains, the asset ring, and the pairing arrows.

## Check for understanding

1. A client's chart says "noncompliant with diet." Name two social determinant questions you'd ask before accepting that label. *Answer: any two of — where do you shop and how do you get there; what kitchen equipment works; what is your work schedule around meals; how does the food budget hold up at month's end.*
2. Why does the video add an asset ring after the five domains? *Answer: a plan built only on deficits misses the client's existing supports and erodes trust; assets are what interventions run on.*
3. Mrs. Morales asks whether she can keep insulin in a cooler. What do you do? *Answer: do not answer the storage question yourself; connect her to the pharmacist or provider today and address the broken-fridge barrier through referral.*
