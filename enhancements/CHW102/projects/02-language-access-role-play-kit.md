## Scenario

Your fictional program, "Valle Community Health Partners," serves families who speak Spanish, Mixtec, Vietnamese, and Hmong. A new CHW cohort keeps making the same interpreter mistakes in shadowing visits: talking to the interpreter instead of the client, accepting a relative's interpretation without documenting the offer, and sight-translating consent forms. Your supervisor asks your group to build and run a role-play kit the next cohort can use.

All characters are fictional.

## What you will build / produce

A kit with three role-play scenarios, each containing:

1. **Character cards** — CHW, client, interpreter, observer. The interpreter card includes one or two deliberate errors to introduce.
2. **CHW pre-brief** — the exact words used to brief the interpreter on purpose and sensitive content.
3. **Observer tally sheet** — counts of: speaking to the interpreter in third person; idioms used; segments too long to interpret; times the CHW confirmed understanding through the interpreter.
4. **Documentation template** — what gets recorded (interpreter type and ID/number, offer made, client's choice, any concerns).
5. **Debrief questions** and a model "repair" line for each planted error.

Plus a one-page **CLAS crosswalk** linking each scenario to the CLAS theme it exercises (governance/workforce; communication and language assistance; engagement/accountability).

### The three scenarios (fill in and extend)

- **A. Telephone interpreter, referral explanation.** Ms. Ofelia Cruz (fictional), Mixtec-speaking with some Spanish, needs a dental referral explained (see Lesson 4, Practice 3). Planted error: the interpreter summarizes instead of interpreting a two-part question.
- **B. "My daughter always interprets."** A mother arrives with her 11-year-old for a visit that includes a depression screening (Lesson 2, Practice 5). Planted pressure: the mother insists and says she distrusts phones.
- **C. Video remote interpretation, Deaf client.** Mr. Dao (fictional), Deaf, uses ASL; his adult son offers to sign. Planted error: the CHW starts passing written notes because "it's faster."

## Before you start

- Lesson 2, "The CLAS standards" and "Language access in practice."
- Your program's language access procedure (or a fictional one your instructor provides).

## Milestones

1. Draft character cards and pre-briefs for all three scenarios (60 min).
2. Build the observer tally sheet and documentation template (30 min).
3. Run each scenario once in groups of four, rotating roles (90 min).
4. Revise the kit from what went wrong and write the CLAS crosswalk (45 min).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every scenario uses a qualified interpreter (in-person, telephone, or video) and none relies on a child.
- [ ] Scenario B's CHW script declines child interpretation warmly, explains why, arranges an alternative, and documents the offer and the client's choice.
- [ ] Scenario C uses qualified sign language interpretation (VRI or in-person); written notes are not treated as equivalent.
- [ ] Pre-briefs mention purpose, sensitive content, and first-person interpretation.
- [ ] Observer sheet captures the four tallies listed above.
- [ ] CLAS crosswalk correctly names a theme for each scenario and one organizational gap the kit could surface.
- [ ] No scenario uses real client data.

## Evidence checklist

- Completed kit (three scenarios with all five components)
- Filled observer sheets from your run-through
- Revision notes
- CLAS crosswalk

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interpreter etiquette | CHW speaks to interpreter in third person | Direct, first-person, short segments | Pre-brief anticipates concepts without equivalents |
| Child interpretation | Allowed "just this once" | Declined with alternative | Script preserves the parent's dignity and authority |
| Documentation | Not recorded | Offer, choice, and interpreter ID recorded | Notes a reportable gap (e.g., missing translated form) |
| CLAS | Mentioned generically | Each scenario mapped to a theme | Proposes a supervisor-ready finding statement |

## Stretch goals

- Add a fourth scenario: a consent form exists only in English. Write what you say instead of sight-translating it, and the gap report.
- Record a model run of Scenario B (actors only) for future cohorts.

## Reflection prompts

- Which planted error did you not notice until the observer flagged it?
- Where did shared background with a client make you assume something?

## Instructor notes

- Learners frequently "fix" Scenario B by having the CHW interpret themselves. Unless the CHW is a qualified interpreter under program policy, redirect.
- Language access obligations and qualification standards vary by funder and setting; the kit should say "follow your program's procedure" rather than citing specific statutes.
