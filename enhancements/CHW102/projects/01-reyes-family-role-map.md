## Scenario

Lesson 6 closes with a one-page story about Ms. Reyes, whose son's asthma keeps him home from school. This project slows that story down into a case file you work through as the CHW, decision by decision. Ms. Reyes, her son Mateo (age 9), her sister, the building, and every agency below are fictional.

**Case file — what you know at first contact (Saturday food distribution, St. Brendan's parish hall, fictional)**

- Ms. Reyes, 34, speaks Spanish at home; reads some English but "not the medical letters."
- Mateo has asthma. Missed 7 school days last month. His rescue inhaler has not been refilled in about two months.
- Apartment has visible mold on the bathroom ceiling and under the kitchen sink. Landlord "doesn't answer."
- She has worked at the same bakery for six years, 5 a.m.–1 p.m., Monday through Saturday. No car. Sister lives two blocks away.
- Coverage: Mateo has Medi-Cal; Ms. Reyes is unsure of her own coverage.
- The clinic mailed asthma paperwork in English.
- Three other families in the building have mentioned mold to you at the food distribution.

## What you will produce

1. **Role timeline** — a table with at least 12 dated CHW actions over a fictional eight-week period. For each: the action in verbs, the primary C3 role, any secondary role, and one sentence on why that role (not another) fits.
2. **Cultural mediation and language access memo** (one page) — how you will handle the English-only paperwork and the clinic visit: qualified interpreter arrangement, what you tell the care team about the family's work schedule and transportation, and the CLAS theme each action reflects. Must explicitly decline using Mateo as interpreter and say what you would say if Ms. Reyes suggests it.
3. **Closed-loop referral log** — at least two referrals (e.g., pediatric asthma program; housing code enforcement or tenant legal aid) moved through the four states: made, scheduled, attended, resolved, with the evidence you would record at each state.
4. **Advocate-or-coach decision note** — for the landlord problem, document your decision using the three questions in Lesson 5 (time, client preference, stake) and the gradual release stage you start at.
5. **Outputs and outcomes report** — a half-page funder-style summary that separates outputs from outcomes for this family and for the building-level pattern.

## Before you start

- Lessons 02–06, especially "Language access in practice," "Closing the loop," "Choose between advocating and coaching," and "How the roles fit together."
- Use only the C3 role names as listed in Lesson 3.

## Milestones

1. **Week-by-week draft (90 min).** Lay out the eight weeks. Do not copy Lesson 6's story verbatim — add at least four actions it does not describe (e.g., the follow-up call when the first appointment is missed, verifying the asthma program's Saturday hours, obtaining a signed release before contacting the school nurse, documenting the building-level pattern).
2. **Language access memo (60 min).** Include the exact words you use to brief the interpreter and the exact words you use with Ms. Reyes about why her son should not interpret.
3. **Referral log (60 min).** Include one referral that fails the first time (choose a realistic reason from Lesson 4's list) and show the troubleshooting.
4. **Advocacy decision (45 min).** Write the rung-2 "ask, with the specifics" sentence you or Ms. Reyes would use with the code office.
5. **Report (45 min).** Count outputs; describe outcomes; name one thing you cannot yet claim.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At least 12 actions, each with a correctly named C3 role; at least 8 of the 10 roles appear somewhere in the timeline.
- [ ] No action crosses scope: nothing comments on Mateo's medication doses or interprets symptoms; clinical questions are routed to the clinic.
- [ ] Interpreter memo uses a qualified interpreter, declines child interpretation, and documents the offer.
- [ ] A signed release is obtained before any information is shared with the school nurse, and the client is told what will be shared.
- [ ] Each referral reaches a clearly evidenced state; one shows a failure and recovery.
- [ ] The building pattern is reported to a supervisor in de-identified form (counts, not names).
- [ ] Report correctly separates outputs (contacts, classes, referrals made) from outcomes (school attendance, a shared asthma action plan, a cited mold violation).

## Evidence checklist

- Role timeline table
- Language access memo
- Referral log with four-state evidence
- Advocate-or-coach decision note with rung-2 sentence
- Outputs/outcomes half-page

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role identification | Roles named but often misassigned | Correct primary roles with reasons | Explains secondary roles and how one role enables the next |
| Cultural mediation | Treats mediation as translation only | Mediates in both directions (client↔system) | Also mediates community-to-itself (building tenants) |
| Closing the loop | Marks referrals done when made | Four states evidenced | Failure diagnosed and fixed; directory corrected |
| Advocacy/coaching | Takes over the landlord call by default | Decision justified with three questions | Gradual release plan through all four stages |
| Evaluation | Outputs presented as outcomes | Correct separation | Names what data would be needed to claim an outcome credibly |

## Stretch goals

- Write a one-page asset map for the building and block (five categories) and mark which entries you would need a resident to confirm.
- Draft the talking points for a tenants' meeting about the mold pattern, in plain language, with your role limits stated.

## Reflection prompts

- Which role did you find hardest to tell apart from another? What distinguished them in the end?
- When did you most want to do something for Ms. Reyes instead of with her?

## Instructor notes

- Learners often label the inhaler conversation "health education" and then drift into dose advice. Stop that in the draft review.
- The school-nurse coordination is the most common confidentiality miss; check for the release.
- Housing code enforcement and tenant legal aid processes vary by city; accept "contact the city code enforcement office (verify process)" without requiring real procedures.
- For a shorter version (3 hours), assign only the role timeline and the referral log.
