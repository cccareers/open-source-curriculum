## Purpose

After watching, the learner can run an interpreted encounter correctly — briefing, first person, short segments, no idioms, confirming understanding — and can decline child interpretation without damaging the relationship.

## Audience and prerequisites

CHW102 learners after reading Lesson 2. Actors: a CHW, a client (Ms. Ofelia Cruz, fictional), a telephone interpreter (voice only), a narrator.

| 1:35 | "Move 2: Speak directly to the client." CHW turns body and eyes to client. | CHW (to client): "Ms. Cruz, how would you get to the dental clinic on Tuesday?" |

| 2:10 | "Move 4: Plain words, no idioms." | NARRATOR: "Swap 'swing by' for 'go to.' Swap 'no biggie' for 'this is free.'" |
| 2:25 | "Move 5: Confirm understanding through the interpreter — with teach-back." | CHW: "I want to be sure I explained this clearly. When you get to the clinic, what will you tell the front desk?" / CLIENT (interpreted): "That I have a referral and I need the morning appointment." |

| 3:15 | Narrator. Text: "Never ask the interpreter to 'just summarize.'" | NARRATOR: "And never ask an interpreter to step out and summarize. Everything said in the room gets interpreted." |
| 3:30 | New scene: a mother and her 11-year-old daughter at the door. Lower third: "When a family member offers." | MOTHER: "My daughter always helps me. It's fine." |
| 3:40 | CHW kneels slightly to mother's eye level, speaks to her. | CHW: "Thank you — she's clearly a big help to you. Today some of the questions are personal, about how you've been feeling, and those are adult questions. Our program’s language-access procedure covers this visit. We can arrange a qualified interpreter at no cost, and that keeps your daughter from having to carry them. Can we try the interpreter today? Your daughter's welcome to wait in the family room with a book." |

- Burned-in captions for all dialogue, including interpreted lines (shown as "[interpreted into Mixtec]" when not voiced).

## Check for understanding

1. Rewrite: "Ask him how long he's had the cough." *Answer: "How long have you had the cough?" said directly to the client.*
2. A client insists her 14-year-old son interpret for a domestic-violence screening. What do you do? *Answer: decline warmly, explain the questions are for adults, arrange a qualified interpreter (phone, video, or reschedule for in-person), and document the offer and the client's choice.*
3. Why can’t a CHW simply read an English consent form aloud in Spanish to fix a missing translation? *Answer: that is sight translation, not written translation. Consent forms can be vital documents. Follow the program’s procedure for obtaining an approved translation and qualified language assistance; report the gap. Whether qualified oral interpretation is sufficient in a particular situation must be determined through that procedure, not by the CHW.*
