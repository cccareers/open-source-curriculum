## Scenario

You've just closed **Bayfront Marine Services** (the Lesson 2 practice account): a 12-month term starting 1 March, 140 seats, $118,000 ARR. Work orders are handwritten on carbon-copy pads at four marinas, and roughly 15% are never invoiced, though nobody knows the real figure. The champion, **Priya Raman** (Operations Manager), has been in her role seven weeks. **Tom Okafor** (Managing Director) signed. **Elena Brandt**, Yard Supervisor at the largest marina, told you "the last system they tried lasted a month." Finance runs a 14-year-old package, and a file export needs about two days of a finance person's time. A fifth-marina acquisition is being considered, with timing unconfirmed.

You'll write the handoff record, then run the kickoff working session with a panel playing Priya, Elena, and your delivery lead, and send the recap the same day.

## What you will produce

1. A one-page handoff record covering all six Lesson 2 elements, with the two riskiest facts marked.
2. A kickoff agenda: replay the why; agree measures and baselines; agree the first value milestone; agree roles, meeting rhythm, and the day-30/60/90 review dates.
3. A recorded 30-minute kickoff (panel of two or three).
4. The same-day recap: measures with baselines and owners, the milestone with a date, roles, and review dates.
5. A 90-day plan table (days / who / purpose / artifact / exit criteria), revised after the kickoff.

## Before you start

**Persona cards (panel only)**

*Priya Raman, Operations Manager*
- Keen, but new and wary of overpromising to Tom.
- If asked how she'll know it worked: "Fewer jobs we never bill."
- If pressed for a baseline: she can pull invoice counts from finance for the last 3 months, and the marinas can count carbon-pad books used. Offer this only if asked where the numbers live.

*Elena Brandt, Yard Supervisor (largest marina)*
- Sceptical. Opens with: "We've done this before. It lasted a month."
- If asked what went wrong last time: "It needed a signal on the pontoons and there isn't one. And it took longer than the pad."
- Warms up only if the rep proposes something her crew can see working in a few weeks without depending on finance, and asks her to help choose the first team.

*Delivery lead (your colleague)*
- Will say the finance export needs "two days from someone in their finance team, and we don't have a name yet."
- Will ask: "What did sales promise about offline?"

## Milestones

1. **Handoff record (45 min).** Why they bought, in their words; what was promised (including anything said about offline use); success measures and known baselines; who is who (flag Priya's seven weeks in role and Elena's history); what is known to be hard (no pontoon signal, the finance export, the acquisition); what was deliberately not sold.
2. **Choose the first value milestone (15 min).** It should be small and visible, achievable by one team without finance, and measurable with a number they already have. Write down the tempting milestone you rejected (e.g., "all four marinas digital by day 30") and why.
3. **Kickoff (30 min, recorded).** You, not the delivery lead, replay the why. Agree two to four measures, each with a baseline plan and an owner. Put the review dates in calendars during the meeting.
4. **Recap (20 min).** Sent the same day.
5. **90-day plan (40 min).** Include baseline capture before go-live, an executive touchpoint with Tom around day 45 with no ask, an explicit plan for Elena, the finance dependency with an owner and date, and all three reviews.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Handoff record covers all six elements, and the delivery lead confirms they've read it before the kickoff.
- [ ] First milestone is dated, needs no finance export, and is measurable with an existing number.
- [ ] A baseline method for the un-invoiced rate is agreed (e.g., finance invoice counts against job counts from pad books over three months), with an owner and a date before go-live.
- [ ] Elena has a defined role, such as choosing the pilot crew or signing off the offline workflow, rather than being avoided.
- [ ] The finance dependency has a named owner (or a dated request to name one) and a date.
- [ ] Day-30, day-60, and day-90 reviews are booked during the kickoff and appear in the recap.
- [ ] The recap is sent the same day and is no longer than one page.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Handoff | Product-category language; risks omitted | All six elements in the customer's words | Flags the offline promise as a delivery-critical commitment and gets the delivery lead's confirmation |
| Milestone | Large, dependent on finance | Small, visible, independent, measurable | Elena's crew chooses or owns it |
| Baselines | "We'll measure later" | Method, source, owner, date before go-live | Explains why the customer will accept the figure as fair |
| Kickoff facilitation | Vendor presentation | Working session; dates agreed in the room | Turns Elena's scepticism into a design input with a named action |
| 90-day plan | Generic phases | All required rows with exit criteria | Sequences the hard dependency behind the first milestone and plans for the acquisition without selling into it |

## Instructor notes

- The common mistake is letting the delivery lead run the meeting from slides. Lesson 2 says the kickoff is a working session that produces four artifacts.
- Watch for learners proposing the fifth marina as an expansion at kickoff. Lesson 5's expand-from-proof gate applies.
- For a shorter session, skip the recording and grade the handoff, recap, and plan.
