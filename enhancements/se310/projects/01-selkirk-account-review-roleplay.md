## Scenario

This extends the Lesson 4 practice account. **Selkirk Environmental**: 220 seats at $27.50 per seat per month, term ends 30 April, 60-day notice (deadline 1 March), auto-renews. Today is 12 November. Active seats have fallen from 214 to 149; the Northern depot (62 seats) is at 9% since its manager was replaced; the missed-collections baseline (3.1%) has no current figure; your champion **Kwame Aboagye** has rescheduled twice; you have never met the Finance Director, **Helen Ruskin**; accounts payable queried the invoice by depot; and an analyst mentioned the group "is standardising on one platform for logistics next year."

You finally have 30 minutes with Kwame. A partner plays him from a persona card you have not read. Your job is to surface what Kwame has not decided to tell you, play it back, score the account before and after, and write the note that changes the forecast.

## What you will produce

1. A pre-call churn score (all twelve factors, weighted, with evidence or the default rule).
2. A one-page review plan: agenda, first four questions verbatim (none naming the product or asking for a verdict), the hedges you will return to, and marked silences.
3. A recorded 30-minute review.
4. Two-column call notes (his words | your interpretation).
5. A Heard / Concluded / Committed account note.
6. A post-call churn score, with every changed factor explained.
7. The corrected renewal record (close date, expected amount with reasoning, stage and backing event, forecast category).

## Before you start

**Persona card — Kwame Aboagye (partner only)**

- You like the product. You are stretched: since the summer reorganization you also cover two depots outside your old remit.
- If asked "how's it going?": "Fine — the drivers in the south are used to it now."
- You will mention the Northern depot only if asked about the exception or the weakest area: "Northern's gone backwards since Sandra left. The new manager, Phil, has them back on the paper run sheets."
- If the rep repeats a hedge ("backwards?", "for now?"), explain: Phil thinks the app "adds ten minutes to every round" because the route view doesn't match how Northern sequences collections.
- You will mention the group platform only if the conversation turns to next year, budgets, or your own role: "The group's bringing in a logistics platform for all the subsidiaries. Helen's on the steering group. I don't know if it covers what you do." The steering group's first decision meeting is 20 January.
- If asked about the missed-collections number: "Jaz built that report. She's gone. I honestly don't know where we are."
- If asked, near the end, "Is there anything we should be worried about that we haven't covered?" and the rep then stays silent for five seconds: "Helen asked me last month why we pay for 220 when the report says 150. I didn't have an answer."
- If the rep pitches features or promises a fix on the spot, become shorter and look at the clock.
- At the close, if asked for a specific next step involving Helen, agree to introduce the rep "before Christmas."

## Milestones

1. **Pre-call score (30 min).** Expect red or critical. Show weighted totals (maximum 60).
2. **Plan the review (30 min).** Use Lesson 3 techniques: ask about the world, ask about the exception, the contrast question, labels, following hedges, listening for absence.
3. **Run the review (30 min, recorded).** Deliver a playback of no more than four sentences in the last three minutes, naming the risk rather than the symptom.
4. **Write the note (20 min).** Heard / Concluded / Committed, dated, with owners and dates.
5. **Re-score and fix the record (30 min).** Compute the true-down exposure (220 − 149 = 71 seats × $27.50 × 12 = $23,430/yr) and the full-loss exposure ($72,600/yr). Set the renewal close date to the decision point — and argue whether the binding date is the 1 March notice deadline or the 20 January steering-group meeting.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] First four questions verbatim, none naming the product or asking for a verdict.
- [ ] At least two hedges followed back with a repeated word and silence.
- [ ] The Northern mechanism (route view mismatch, per Phil) surfaced as a specific mechanism, not "low adoption."
- [ ] The group-platform decision and Helen's utilization question both surfaced and recorded in the note.
- [ ] Playback names the risk (a group platform decision in January, with a utilization number that currently argues against you) and invites correction.
- [ ] No product defense, discount, or fix promised on the call.
- [ ] Note uses Heard / Concluded / Committed with dates and owners.
- [ ] Renewal record corrected, with the expected amount's reasoning written beside it.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Questioning | Verdict questions ("how's it going?") and agenda recitation | World, exception, and contrast questions; hedges followed | Labels a state Kwame then expands on ("It sounds like Northern has quietly opted out") |
| Silence | Fills pauses within two seconds | Holds five seconds after the "worried" question | Uses silence after the Helen mention to get the steering-group date |
| Separating heard from concluded | Notes mix his words and your opinions | Two clean columns; note has three distinct parts | Escalates the specific mechanism, not the symptom |
| Risk scoring | Scores from mood | Every factor cited or defaulted | Explains which factor drives the score and why the binding date moved |
| Record honesty | Leaves full amount and Commit | Amount reflects true-down risk with reasoning; category matches evidence | States what the manager's forecast believed before and after in two sentences |

## Stretch goals

- Write the opening ninety seconds of your first meeting with Helen Ruskin, leading with a miss.
- Draft the one-page value evidence plan to rebuild the missed-collections figure from Selkirk's own job records in under three weeks.

## Reflection prompts

- At what moment did the group platform become reachable? What did you do then?
- Which of Kwame's lines was a polite way of letting you off the hook?

## Instructor notes

- Learners commonly ask "any issues?" and get "nothing major." Stop and replay with "where does it work least well?"
- The binding-date argument is the analytical heart of the project: the contract says 1 March, but the decision is likely made on 20 January. Reward learners who move the renewal close date and touchpoints accordingly.
- For a shorter session, skip re-scoring and grade the note and the record only.
