## Summary

A strong, narrative-driven course: Tallgrass Facilities Group runs from onboarding (L2) through a listening-led review (L3), churn scoring (L4), and gated expansion (L5), with fresh practice accounts (Bayfront, Selkirk, Nadia Osei) and an Ardmore capstone. The arithmetic is almost entirely consistent; I fixed one scorecard error and found a data impossibility in the capstone. The biggest opportunity is hearing the listening techniques performed — the hedge, the repeat, the five-second silence — and rehearsing a review before the capstone transcript analysis.

## Depth and coverage gaps

- **Hearing the techniques.** L3's annotated transcript is excellent on paper; drafted se310-v01 to perform it alongside the weak version, for "Use active listening in account reviews to surface risk the customer has not stated".
- **Review rehearsal before the capstone.** Learners critique the Ardmore transcript but never run a review against a persona who hides a risk until earned. Drafted se310-x01 (Selkirk) for "Use active listening in account reviews to surface risk the customer has not stated" and "Detect churn risk early and run a renewal conversation from evidence".
- **Scorecard intuition.** The Harbourline-vs-Tallgrass comparison is the key insight of L4; drafted se310-a01.
- **Renewal conversation modeling.** L4 gives the four hard moments ("look at the price," "evaluating alternatives," "reduce seats," "three-month extension") as bullets only; a modeled renewal meeting would strengthen "Detect churn risk early and run a renewal conversation from evidence".
- **Onboarding plan for a product with integration risk.** Bayfront's finance-export dependency is a good practice case; a worked example of sequencing a dependency behind the first milestone would help "Plan an account's first 90 days so the customer reaches value before the first renewal conversation".
