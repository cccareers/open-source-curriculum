## Summary
CHW103 is the strongest skills course in the pathway: every lesson pairs a modeled dialogue with drills an observer can score, and scope is held consistently. The main opportunities are (1) naming standard MI vocabulary (OARS, sustain talk, elicit–provide–elicit) so learners can connect to MI training they will meet on the job, (2) a full start-to-finish documentation case (assessment → triage → note → reporting decision), and (3) California-specific mandated reporting content that the owner must supply from a verified source.

## Depth and coverage gaps
- **No full documentation case.** Lesson 6 gives a note structure but no complete worked note. Project x02 adds a SOAP-style adaptation with a fictional home visit. Objective: "Conduct needs assessments, document findings confidentially, and triage immediate versus long-term needs."
- **MI practice lacks a coding tool.** Drills ask observers to tally, but there is no sheet. Project x01 supplies one. Objective: "Use motivational interviewing and relationship-building skills while maintaining boundaries."
- **Participatory education** (Lesson 5) would benefit from one complete 10-minute session plan as a model (objective, opener, pair activity, return demonstration, exit card). Objective: "Facilitate participatory education sessions and adjust delivery in real time."
- **Outreach log** has guidance but no sample entry format; add a sample row. Objective: "Run field outreach with accurate contact tracking."
- **CBPR** is at awareness level, appropriately; a short worked example of a CHW's role on a community advisory board would help. Objective: "Collect program data accurately and participate in evaluation, including CBPR/PAR methods."
- **Recurring characters** exist informally (Rosa, Ms. Alvarez, Mr. Barnes, Valley Health). The projects and media in this pass reuse them to strengthen continuity.
