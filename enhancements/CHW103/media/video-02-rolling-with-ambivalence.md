## Purpose

After watching, the learner can recognize OARS moves (open questions, affirmations, reflections, summaries), notice change talk, and avoid the righting reflex in a real-time conversation.

## Audience and prerequisites

CHW103 learners after reading Lesson 3. Actors: CHW (Marcus, fictional) and client (Mr. Darnell Ortiz, fictional, 47, misses his night blood pressure dose on double-shift days).

## Script

|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Narrator. | NARRATOR: "Ambivalence isn't resistance. It's the normal state of someone considering change. Here's the same visit twice — first with the righting reflex, then with motivational interviewing." |
| 0:15 | Take 1. Clinic side room. | MARCUS: "You really have to take both doses. High blood pressure is silent — it can cause a stroke." / MR. ORTIZ: "I know. But on double days I'm not home." / MARCUS: "You could carry the pill with you." / MR. ORTIZ: "Then I'd have to remember at work. It's not that easy." / MARCUS: "Lots of people set an alarm." / MR. ORTIZ: "Yeah... I'll think about it." |
| 0:50 | Freeze. Label: "Righting reflex ×3." | NARRATOR: "Marcus argued for change three times. Each time, Mr. Ortiz argued against it — and talked himself further out of it. People are persuaded most by what they hear themselves say." |
| 1:10 | Take 2. Label "Open question." | MARCUS: "Tell me how the blood pressure pills have been going." |
| 1:18 | | MR. ORTIZ: "Mornings, fine. Nights — Tuesdays and Wednesdays I work doubles and I'm not home." |
| 1:28 | Label "Simple reflection." | MARCUS: "Mornings are handled. It's the night dose on double days." |
| 1:35 | Label "Affirmation." | MARCUS: "You've kept the morning one going every day with a schedule that changes all week. That takes a system." |
| 1:45 | MR. ORTIZ: (small laugh) "My wife puts it by the coffee." | |
| 1:52 | Label "Exploring both sides — the good things about now." | MARCUS: "What's good about the way things are now with the night dose?" / MR. ORTIZ: "Honestly? One less thing to think about at work. I already have a lot going on." |
| 2:10 | Label "Complex reflection." | MARCUS: "Work takes everything you've got on those days, and the pill feels like one more demand." |
| 2:20 | MR. ORTIZ: "Yeah. But my dad had a stroke at 55." Label "Change talk — reasons." | |
| 2:28 | Marcus pauses; does not jump. Label "Ask for more." | MARCUS: "Say more about your dad." / MR. ORTIZ: "He couldn't work after that. I don't want that for my kids." Label "Change talk — desire." |
| 2:50 | Label "Ask permission before information." | MARCUS: "Would it be all right if I told you what the clinic mentioned about the night dose?" / MR. ORTIZ: "Sure." / MARCUS: "They said it matters to keep it steady, and that if the timing doesn't work with your job, the prescriber may be able to look at options. I can't change that myself — that's their call. What do you make of that?" |
| 3:20 | Label "Scope: routed, not advised." | NARRATOR (VO): "Notice: Marcus didn't suggest a different time or skipping a dose. He gave the clinic's message and pointed the timing question back to the prescriber." |
| 3:30 | MR. ORTIZ: "I didn't know I could ask that. I could probably ask them." Label "Change talk — ability." | |
| 3:40 | Label "Summary — collecting change talk at the end." | MARCUS: "So — the morning dose is working because your wife puts it by the coffee. Double days are packed, and the night pill feels like one more thing. And your dad's stroke is on your mind; you don't want that for your kids. And you think you could ask the clinic about the timing. Where does that leave you?" |
| 4:10 | MR. ORTIZ: "I'll call Monday and ask about changing the time." Label "Change talk — commitment." | |
| 4:20 | Label "Emphasize autonomy." | MARCUS: "That's your call, and it sounds like a good one. Want to practice what you'll say? I can sit with you when you call, or you can do it on your own." |
| 4:35 | Narrator. Tally on screen: Take 1 — 0 reflections, 3 arguments. Take 2 — 4 reflections, 1 affirmation, 2 open questions, 1 summary. | NARRATOR: "Count the moves. More reflections than questions. No arguing. The reasons for change came from him." |
| 5:00 | Second scenario, short. Label "Emotional cue." MR. ORTIZ goes quiet, looks at the door, when Marcus mentions the cardiology referral. | MARCUS: "I might be reading this wrong — did I push too hard just then?" / MR. ORTIZ: "That place... they made me wait four hours last time." / MARCUS: "Four hours. No wonder it's not on your list. We can leave that for today." |

## Check for understanding

1. Label this client statement: "I could probably ask them." *Answer: change talk — ability.*
2. Turn this question into a reflection: "Is it hard to remember on work days?" *Answer: e.g., "Work days make it hard to remember."*
3. What is the righting reflex, and why does it backfire? *Answer: the urge to argue for change; when one person argues for change, the other argues against it, and people are persuaded by what they hear themselves say.*
