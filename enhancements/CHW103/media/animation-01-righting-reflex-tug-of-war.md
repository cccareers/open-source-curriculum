## Concept and misconception it fixes

Misconception: "If I give the client enough good reasons, they will change." The animation visualizes ambivalence as a balance scale inside the client, and shows that when the CHW pulls on the "change" side, the client pulls back on the "stay the same" side. When the CHW lets go and reflects both sides, the client's own reasons start loading the change side.

## Visual language

- A two-pan balance scale inside a simple client silhouette. Left pan labeled "Reasons to stay the same"; right pan labeled "Reasons to change."
- Reason tokens are labeled rounded blocks with text (e.g., "Busy work days," "Dad's stroke").
- CHW appears as a second silhouette with a rope. Arguments are rope pulls labeled with the CHW's words.
- Palette (Okabe-Ito): sky blue #56B4E9 for change-side tokens, orange #E69F00 for stay-side tokens, black outlines; every token carries its label, so color is not required to read it.

## Interaction variant

In Rive, make the CHW's lines selectable: the learner picks between an argument ("You should...") and a reflection. Arguments trigger a counter-token on the stay side; reflections and open questions let the client add change tokens. The scene ends when the learner reaches a commitment token without any argument lines in the last three choices.
