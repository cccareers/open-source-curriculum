## Scenario

Your fictional program, "Valley Health Community Partners," is preparing CHW interns for home visits. Supervisors have noticed that new CHWs ask question after question, argue for change when a client is ambivalent, and miss the moment a client shuts down. You will build, run, and code three recorded role-plays (actors only — never real clients) and produce a short self-assessment that a supervisor can sign off.

The three fictional clients reuse people from CHW103 lessons so the course stays consistent:

- **Mr. Darnell Ortiz (fictional), 47** — the client from Lesson 2 who takes his morning blood pressure pill but misses the night dose on the two days he works doubles. Ambivalent about "adding another thing."
- **Ms. Keisha Brown (fictional), 31** — the parent from Lesson 3 who smokes outside because her six-year-old asked why. "I know I should quit. Everyone tells me."
- **Mr. Walter Barnes (fictional), 74** — the isolated client from Lesson 7 who has started calling the CHW's work phone most evenings. The visit includes a boundary reset.

## What you will build / produce

1. **Three role-play briefs** (half page each): client backstory, what the client is ambivalent about (both sides), one planted emotional cue (e.g., goes flat when the CHW mentions the clinic), and one planted boundary test (Mr. Barnes).
2. **Three recordings**, 8–10 minutes each, with classmates as actors. Delete the recordings after coding if your program requires it.
3. **OARS coding sheet** for each recording: tallies of open questions, closed questions, affirmations, simple reflections, complex reflections, summaries; every righting-reflex moment (time stamp + what was said); every change-talk statement labeled desire, ability, reasons, need, commitment, activation, or taking steps; the moment the emotional cue appeared and the CHW's response.
4. **Ratio summary**: reflections-to-questions ratio per recording and the change from first to third recording.
5. **Boundary reset note**: the two-line case note documenting the Mr. Barnes reset and the supervision opener you would use.
6. **Self-assessment** (one page) with one specific goal for your next role-play.

## Before you start

- Lesson 3 in full; Lesson 2 "Active listening you can be observed doing" and "Empathy and non-judgmental language on sensitive topics."
- Agree with your actors that the scenarios are fictional and no one shares personal health information.

## Milestones

1. Write the three briefs (60 min).
2. Record Mr. Ortiz first (lowest difficulty), code it immediately (60 min).
3. Set a target from the coding (e.g., "two reflections per question") and record Ms. Brown (60 min including coding).
4. Record Mr. Barnes, including the boundary reset (60 min including coding).
5. Write the ratio summary, case note, and self-assessment (45 min).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Each recording contains at least one complex reflection. The Ortiz and Brown recordings each end with a summary that collects change talk; the Barnes recording ends with a summary of his needs and the agreed alternative.
- [ ] By the third recording, reflections equal or outnumber questions.
- [ ] No righting-reflex moment goes uncoded; each has a rewritten alternative line.
- [ ] In at least one recording, the CHW closes any information given with a teach-back check framed as a check on the CHW ("I want to make sure I explained that clearly — what will you tell the clinic when you call?"), and the coding sheet records it.
- [ ] The CHW asks permission before giving any information ("Would it be all right if I told you what the clinic said about that?").
- [ ] Nothing the CHW says advises on medication timing or dose; Mr. Ortiz's night-dose question is routed to the clinic or pharmacist with a self-advocacy plan.
- [ ] Mr. Barnes's reset is warm, specific, and offers a real alternative (e.g., a friendly-caller program), and the case note is factual.
- [ ] No real client information appears anywhere.

## Evidence checklist

- Three briefs
- Three coding sheets
- Ratio summary table
- Case note and supervision opener
- Self-assessment
- Peer or instructor signature on one coding sheet confirming accuracy

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reflective listening | Mostly questions | Ratio ≥ 1:1 by recording 3 | Ratio ≥ 2:1 with frequent complex reflections |
| Ambivalence | Argues for change | Explores both sides, emphasizes autonomy | Elicits client's own reasons and a commitment statement |
| Emotional cues | Misses the planted cue | Notices and adjusts within a minute | Checks rather than concludes ("did I push too hard just then?") |
| Boundaries | Resets coldly or not at all | Warm reset with alternative | Names the underlying need and refers to meet it |
| Scope | Gives medication advice | All clinical questions routed | Coaches the client to ask the question himself |

## Stretch goals

- Re-run Ms. Brown with an interpreter (CHW102 language access moves) and code whether reflections survive interpretation.
- Code a classmate's recording blind and compare your tallies.

## Reflection prompts

- When did you feel the righting reflex most strongly? What were you afraid would happen if you did not argue?
- Which reflection got the most useful response from the client?

## Instructor notes

- First recordings almost always show zero or one reflection and many questions. Frame the tally as the lesson, not a grade.
- Coding change talk is subjective; disagreements between coders are good discussion material.
- This project teaches MI at an introductory level. Formal MI proficiency coding (e.g., validated MI integrity coding systems) requires specific training and is out of scope; do not describe the sheet as a validated instrument.
