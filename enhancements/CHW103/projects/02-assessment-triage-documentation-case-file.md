## Scenario

You are a CHW intern at the fictional "Valley Health" clinic. Your field mentor has handed you a transcript excerpt and observation notes from a home visit another CHW conducted (fictional, written for training). Your job is to do what that CHW must now do: triage, document, plan, and decide whether anything is reportable.

**Client: Ms. Lourdes Fermín (fictional), 39.** Lives with her two children (ages 7 and 12) and her mother (age 81) in a two-bedroom apartment.

**Transcript excerpt (fictional)**

> CHW: [standard setup, including the limits of confidentiality, delivered before questions]
> Ms. Fermín: The electric bill — they sent a paper. It says the 12th.
> CHW: The 12th of this month?
> Ms. Fermín: Yes. And my daughter's Medi-Cal renewal is due the 30th. I have the envelope, I didn't open it.
> CHW: What else is weighing on you?
> Ms. Fermín: Honestly? This apartment. The landlord wants us out by summer. That's what keeps me up.
> CHW: Who helps you out when things get tight?
> Ms. Fermín: My comadre. She watches the kids when I work. And my church has a pantry on Wednesdays.
> Ms. Fermín: My mom — she's been confused lately. Last week she gave her whole check to a man who called saying he was from the bank. I don't know what to do about that.
> Ms. Fermín: Food is okay until the end of the month. Then it's rice and eggs.

**Observation notes (fictional):** Mother appeared well cared for and engaged in conversation. Space heater in use in the children's room. Ms. Fermín keeps her pill bottles on the counter; she mentioned she "stopped the cholesterol one because it was too expensive."

## What you will build / produce

1. **Needs and strengths list** — every need and every strength in the excerpt, in factual language.
2. **Triage table** — immediate / short-term / long-term, each with an action, an owner, and a date.
3. **Say-it-back script** — the under-one-minute plan you would say back to Ms. Fermín, honoring her priority (housing) while keeping the deadline item first.
4. **Documentation** — a complete encounter note using either the lesson's structure or a SOAP-style adaptation for non-clinical CHW notes. If you use SOAP, keep the lesson's header fields above it — date/time, contact type, duration, and purpose — and write "not provided" for any detail the scenario does not give rather than inventing it:
   - **S (Subjective):** what the client reported, with quotes where exact words matter.
   - **O (Objective):** what the CHW observed (no diagnoses or clinical interpretation).
   - **A (Assessment — CHW needs assessment, not clinical):** needs identified and triage level.
   - **P (Plan):** actions taken, client-agreed next steps with dates and owners, referrals with consent status, follow-up date.
5. **Reporting decision memo** — whether the possible financial exploitation of Ms. Fermín's 81-year-old mother is potentially reportable, what you would do in the next hour, who you would tell, what you would document, and what you would say to Ms. Fermín. Use your program's (or a fictional) mandated reporting policy; do not invent statute numbers.
6. **Confidentiality check** — list three ways this note could be exposed in an ordinary day and the habit that prevents each.

## Before you start

- CHW103 Lessons 6 and 7 in full; Lesson 4 "Referral forms and paperwork."
- Your instructor will provide a fictional "Valley Health mandated reporting policy" or you may use your placement's policy with all identifying details removed.

## Milestones

1. Needs/strengths list and triage (60 min).
2. Say-it-back script; practice aloud with a partner who corrects your order (30 min).
3. SOAP-style note (60 min). Have a partner circle every judgment word.
4. Reporting decision memo (60 min). Discuss in a group of three; note where you disagreed.
5. Confidentiality check and final revision (30 min).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The electric-bill paper dated the 12th is treated as a possible shutoff notice: the CHW verifies during the visit what the date means (reads the paper with Ms. Fermín or calls the utility with her), and if it is a disconnection date, triages it immediate with same-visit action. The note does not record a shutoff date as fact until it is verified.
- [ ] Renewal due the 30th is short-term with a date and owner; housing is long-term but gets a concrete first step today.
- [ ] Food insecurity at month's end and the space heater are noticed and placed (the heater as an observation and a safety question for the appropriate resource, not a CHW judgment).
- [ ] Strengths (comadre, church pantry, medications kept in one visible place on the counter, engaged mother) are documented alongside needs.
- [ ] Note contains no judgment language and no clinical interpretation. The stopped cholesterol medication is recorded as the client's report with cost as the stated reason, and routed to the provider/pharmacist and to cost-assistance navigation — not addressed with advice.
- [ ] Reporting memo treats the elder financial exploitation disclosure as a potentially reportable concern, states that suspicion (not proof) is the threshold, does not investigate, notifies the supervisor the same shift, and follows the policy's personal-duty rule.
- [ ] Memo includes honest, non-cold language for telling Ms. Fermín, consistent with what was said at the start of the visit.
- [ ] No real names or identifiers.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Triage | Everything urgent, or deadline missed | Correct buckets with owners and dates | Explains how the plan honors the client's priority |
| Documentation | Conclusions and labels | Facts, quotes, observations, plan | Client-readable; every impression marked as such |
| Scope | Advises on medication | Routes clinical items correctly | Pairs routing with a self-advocacy plan for the client |
| Mandated reporting | Investigates or promises secrecy | Recognizes, escalates, documents | Anticipates relationship impact and plans next visit |
| Confidentiality | Generic list | Three specific exposures with habits | Includes household/shared-phone considerations |

## Instructor notes

- California-specific elder and dependent adult abuse reporting categories, timeframes, and which job roles are mandated reporters must come from the program's current policy and a qualified reviewer; this project deliberately avoids citing statutes.
- Some learners will want to call the bank or the police themselves. Redirect: the CHW does not investigate.
- SOAP is a clinical format; emphasize that "A" here is a CHW needs assessment and must never include diagnosis. If the program uses a different format, substitute it.
