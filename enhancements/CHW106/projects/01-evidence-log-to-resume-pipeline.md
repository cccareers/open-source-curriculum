## Scenario

Before you mine your own log, practice on someone else's. Below is a de-identified, entirely fictional 12-week evidence log from an intern, "Sam Okoye," placed with the fictional "Harbor Community Clinic." Sam's mentor, "Ms. Delgado," has agreed to act as reference. Sam's first-draft resume bullet reads: "Assisted clients with resources and referrals."

**Sam's evidence log extract (fictional, de-identified)**

| Week | Role(s) logged | What Sam did | Outcome / follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Assessment | Completed 6 social needs screens under supervision | 4 positive for food insecurity; referrals placed |
| 2 | Care coordination | Placed 9 referrals (food, transportation, dental) | Follow-up set for week 3 |
| 3 | Care coordination | Followed up on 9 referrals | 6 confirmed connected; 2 transportation barriers; 1 wrong number corrected in directory |
| 4 | Capacity building | Coached a client to call a pharmacy herself after one joint call | Client called alone in week 6 |
| 5 | Health education | Co-facilitated a 45-min hypertension class (11 attendees) | Exit cards: 9 of 11 named one action |
| 6 | Advocacy | With mentor's OK, asked benefits office to review a denial citing the wrong rule | Denial reversed in 8 days |
| 7 | Outreach | Tabled at a laundromat, 3 hours | 22 conversations, 7 consented to follow-up |
| 8 | Care coordination | Placed 14 referrals | — |
| 9 | Care coordination | Followed up on week 8 referrals | 11 confirmed connected; 3 pending |
| 10 | Coaching | Supported 4 clients in setting own goals with simple tracking | 3 of 4 reported progress at week 12 |
| 11 | Evaluation | Flagged 17 records with blank language field to data lead | Form fixed by week 12 |
| 12 | Care coordination (documentation) | Same-day notes on all contacts (mentor spot-checked 10) | 10 of 10 complete and on time |

## What you will produce

1. **Role map** — tally Sam's evidence by C3 role, name the two weakest roles, and propose one realistic experience for each.
2. **Ten bullets, keep six** — ten action–scale–outcome bullets from Sam's log; cut to the best six. Every number must be traceable to a log row.
3. **Verification table** — beside each kept bullet: who could confirm it and how (e.g., "Ms. Delgado — spot-check record").
4. **Your own version** — repeat steps 1–3 on your real log (de-identified), producing six verified bullets.
5. **Cover letter paragraph** — the "strongest evidence" paragraph for a real CHW posting, keeping clients invisible.
6. **Short bio** (40–60 words), third person.
7. **Confidentiality audit** — read all of it as your field mentor would; record anything traceable to a person and how you removed it.

## Before you start

- CHW106 Lessons 2 and 4; Lesson 5 "Documentation: accurate and on time."
- Your own evidence log, de-identified as you go.

## Milestones

1. Role map for Sam (30 min).
2. Ten bullets, keep six, verification table for Sam (60 min).
3. Same three steps on your own log (90 min).
4. Cover letter paragraph and short bio (45 min).
5. Confidentiality audit and mentor review request (15 min).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Sam's referral numbers are computed correctly: 23 referrals placed (9 + 14); 17 confirmed connected (6 + 11); about 74% — and no bullet claims more than the log supports (3 pending are not counted as connected).
- [ ] At least one bullet evidences each of: closed-loop referral, client-owned goals, and advocacy.
- [ ] No bullet uses "assisted" or "helped" without a scale and outcome.
- [ ] Every kept bullet has a named confirmer.
- [ ] Your own bullets contain no client identifiers or identifying combinations.
- [ ] Bio is third person, 40–60 words, and contains no client story.

## Evidence checklist

- Sam's role map, bullets, and verification table
- Your own role map, bullets, and verification table
- Cover letter paragraph and short bio
- Confidentiality audit notes
- Record of the mentor's answer to "Is there anything here you would not confirm?"

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role mapping | Roles named loosely | Accurate tally with gaps identified | Proposed gap experiences are realistic for the site |
| Quantification | Vague or inflated | Accurate counts tied to the log | Uses outcomes (connection, reversal) not just activity |
| Verification | No confirmers | Confirmer for every bullet | Mentor review completed and revisions made |
| Confidentiality | Borderline details left unexamined | Fully de-identified | Explains choices for borderline details |
| Writing | Job-description language | Field vocabulary used truthfully | Cover letter paragraph tells a story the bullets could not |

**Hard gate (Lesson 3):** a submission containing any client-identifying detail is not scored on this rubric. It is returned unmarked and treated as a confidentiality incident.

## Stretch goals

- Write the 120–180 word long bio.
- Turn one log row into a 90-second interview answer (situation, action, result, what you'd do differently).

## Reflection prompts

- Which of your activities did you undervalue until you saw it as evidence?
- Where were you tempted to round up?

## Instructor notes

- Learners often claim 23 of 23 connected. Use this to teach that "pending" is not "connected."
- Some sites do not allow interns to cite internal metrics; have learners check with the mentor before using percentages.
- State CHW certification status in California is in flux; tell learners to describe their credential exactly as it stands.
