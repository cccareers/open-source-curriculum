## Purpose

After watching, the learner can turn a de-identified evidence log entry into a quantified, verifiable resume bullet and run the verification test on it.

## Audience and prerequisites

CHW106 interns with at least four weeks of evidence log entries.

## Script

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Screen: a document titled "Evidence log — de-identified" with the four-line entry from Lesson 2 (date 2026-03-14, care coordination/advocacy, specialty referral that lapsed, appointment scheduled, confirmed 3/21). | "This is one entry from an evidence log. No client name, no identifiers — just what I did and what changed. Let's turn a stack of these into resume lines a supervisor would sign off on." |
| 0:25 | Cursor highlights the verbs: "Placed," "Called," "asked the referral coordinator to review." | "Pass one: pull out the verbs. Not what the program does. What you did." |
| 0:45 | New doc, "Raw facts." Cursor types: "Placed referrals: 61 (count from log, wks 1–12). Confirmed connection: 49. Barriers resolved: transportation 7, documents 4." | "Pass two: count. Go through every log entry and tally. If you didn't count it, don't invent it. 'Roughly sixty' is fine if it's true and you say 'roughly.'" |
| 1:20 | Cursor types the weak bullet: "Assisted clients with referrals and follow-up." | "Here's the bullet most interns write. It describes the job description, not you." |
| 1:35 | Types the better bullet: "Coordinated referrals to community health and social service agencies and followed up to confirm connection." | "Better: specific verb, and it says you followed up." |
| 1:50 | Types the strong bullet: "Coordinated 61 referrals to clinical and social service partners over a 12-week internship; confirmed connection in 49 (80%), resolving transportation and documentation barriers on 11." Each part briefly underlined with a text label: "action," "scale," "outcome," "barrier." | "Pass three: action, scale, outcome — and the barrier. The number. The outcome is connection confirmed, not referral placed. And naming the barrier shows you know the referral isn't the finish line." |
| 2:35 | Split screen: bullet on left, "Who confirms? How?" column on right. Cursor types "Field mentor — referral tracker in site system." | "Now the verification test. Who would confirm this, and how? If your mentor, reading it cold, would hesitate, rewrite it until they wouldn't." |
| 3:00 | Cursor highlights "(80%)"; a note appears: "Site approval needed to cite internal rates — in any format." Then the cursor deletes "(80%)". | "Some sites don't want internal rates published. Ask before you use one, in any form — 'about four in five' is the same figure. If the answer is no, leave the rate out; the counts still carry the bullet." |
| 3:20 | Confidentiality audit: cursor highlights a draft bullet "Helped a pregnant teen at the Thursday drop-in get prenatal care" in a warning box with text "Identifying combination." Rewrite: "Connected clients to prenatal and other health services and confirmed each connection." | "One more pass before anything goes out: the confidentiality audit. 'A pregnant teen at the Thursday drop-in' has no name and still identifies someone. Write the skill, not the person." |
| 3:55 | Final resume section on screen, three bullets visible. | "Ten bullets, keep six. Cutting is where the quality comes from." |
| 4:15 | Email draft to mentor: "Is there anything on this resume you would not confirm?" | "Last step: send it to your field mentor with one question. Is there anything here you would not confirm? Revise on the answer." |
| 4:35 | End card: "Lesson 4 Practice 1–3." | "Now do it with your own log." |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Fictional, de-identified log entries and resume text only; numbers in the demo are fictional.
- Zoom to 150% for all typed text; slow cursor movements.

## Accessibility

- Captions; every typed line is also read aloud.
- Warning box uses an icon plus the text "Identifying combination," not color alone.
- Keyboard-visible editing (no mouse-only menus).

## Check for understanding

1. What three elements make a strong bullet? *Answer: action, scale, outcome (plus barrier where relevant), all verifiable.*
2. Your log shows 23 referrals, 17 confirmed, 3 pending. Can you write "connected 20 clients"? *Answer: no; pending is not connected — write 17.*
3. Why is "a teenager at the Thursday drop-in" a problem in a resume bullet? *Answer: it is an identifying combination even without a name.*
