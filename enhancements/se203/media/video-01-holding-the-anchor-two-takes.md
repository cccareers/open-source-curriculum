## Purpose

After watching, the learner can anchor with a rationale, sit through a flinch, refuse to counter an extreme anchor, and make each concession conditional on a named payment with shrinking decrements.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read Lesson 6 through "A worked pricing exchange." Same deal: 400 seats, opening $200,000, target ~$180,000, floor $168,000, walk-away $160,000.

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Prep page graphic (four numbers + ladder).
- Running-total and decrement chart overlays.
- Ladder rungs that light with the payment text.

## Accessibility

- Captions with speaker labels; pauses captioned with duration.
- Ladder states use numbered rungs and text labels, not color alone; warnings use an icon plus outline.
- Downloadable transcript and the prep page as an accessible table.

## Check for understanding

1. Name three mistakes in Take 1. *(Any three: signaled flexibility after the price; conceded to a flinch; countered an unexamined anchor; split the difference; went below walk-away; received nothing; "let me see what I can do.")*
2. Why did Alex's offer go back up when Ellen asked for quarterly billing? *(Answer: the concession was conditional — it was partly paid for by annual cash; withdraw the payment and the concession is revoked.)*
3. What does Alex offer below the floor, and why? *(Answer: reduced scope — fewer seats, no analytics — so price integrity survives and the buyer chooses between value and number.)*
