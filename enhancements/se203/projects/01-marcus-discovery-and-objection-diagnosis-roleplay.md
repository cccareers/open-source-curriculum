## Scenario

You sell the subscription-billing and renewals platform from Lesson 3. Your buyer is **Marcus**, VP Revenue Operations at a 400-person software company. Lesson 3 showed one version of the first call. This project asks you to run **two** calls with a partner playing Marcus from a persona card you have not read:

- **Call A (25 min) — first discovery.** Up-front contract, then the pain funnel, with at least three SPIN implication questions on the problem Marcus cares most about.
- **Call B (15 min) — two weeks later.** Marcus raises three objections. You may spend at most one or two diagnostic questions on each before responding.

An observer codes both calls live.

## What you will produce

1. A one-page call plan for Call A: up-front contract script, SPIN ladder (problem, three implications in three directions, two need-payoffs), and the eight funnel questions.
2. Recordings of Call A and Call B.
3. The observer's coding sheet (S / P / I / N tallies; funnel questions reached; objection diagnoses).
4. Marcus's explicit needs, verbatim.
5. An objection log (four columns: verbatim, diagnosis, what you said, what happened).
6. A 300-word self-review.

## Before you start

**Persona card — Marcus (partner only; do not show the seller)**

*Call A*
- You took the call because quarter-end renewals are chaotic. Volunteer only that.
- If asked for a specific example: a $90k account auto-renewed at last year's seat count after adding 40 people.
- If asked what you have tried: a reconciliation script (broke when the CRM schema changed) and an analyst who now does it by hand.
- If asked about cost: finance estimates $300k of under-billing last year.
- If asked how you feel about it — and only if the seller asked permission or has earned some rapport — say: "It's embarrassing. It's my number to explain."
- Growth: if asked about the future, the company plans to move 30% more customers onto hybrid pricing next year.
- If the seller pitches the product before you have said what a fix would be worth, become noticeably shorter in your answers.
- At the end, if the up-front contract named clear outcomes, choose "worth a technical session" and name your CRM admin, **Jess**.

*Call B (two weeks later)*
1. "Honestly, your price is higher than we expected." (Secret meaning: no budget line exists yet; budget conversations happen in September.)
2. "Jess thinks the integration will be fragile." (Secret meaning: proxy — Jess has not seen the product, only heard about the last script failure.)
3. "Do you support multi-entity consolidation?" (Secret meaning: genuine drawback — the product doesn't; weight it low if asked to compare it with the $300k.)

## Milestones

1. **Plan (60 min).** Write the up-front contract (all five elements, a clean-no outcome, under 90 seconds aloud). Build the SPIN ladder. List the situation facts you would research instead of ask.
2. **Call A (25 min, recorded).** Observer tallies S/P/I/N and marks which funnel question produced surface → business → personal pain.
3. **Harvest (20 min).** Write Marcus's explicit needs verbatim. Write one two-sentence capability statement in his words.
4. **Call B (15 min, recorded).** For each objection, say your diagnosis aloud to the observer *after* the call (gap type, timing, whose objection, objection/stall/condition).
5. **Log and review (45 min).**

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Up-front contract delivered with all five elements, including a named "not a fit" outcome.
- [ ] No product capability described before Marcus states an explicit need.
- [ ] At least three implication questions in at least two directions (cost, spread, frequency, risk, trajectory).
- [ ] Funnel questions 4 and 5 ("What have you tried?" / "Did that work?") asked.
- [ ] Question 7 ("How do you feel about that?") asked, followed by silence.
- [ ] Call A ends with a dated next step that includes Jess.
- [ ] In Call B, each objection is diagnosed correctly: (1) no budget line / timing, (2) proxy, (3) drawback.
- [ ] The drawback is named plainly with no roadmap promise, then weighed against the $300k.
- [ ] No discount offered or implied.

## Evidence checklist

- [ ] Call plan
- [ ] Two recordings
- [ ] Observer coding sheet
- [ ] Explicit needs list + capability statement
- [ ] Objection log

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up-front contract | Missing elements or no clean-no outcome | All five elements in under 90 s | Marcus visibly relaxes and takes the "not a fit" exit seriously, which the seller handles without overselling |
| SPIN sequence | S-heavy (e.g. 8/4/1/0); pitches on implied needs | ≥3 implications, ≥1 need-payoff, no product before explicit need | Marcus does the arithmetic aloud; trajectory question surfaces the hybrid-pricing growth |
| Pain funnel | Stops at cost | Reaches question 7 and holds silence | Surface → business → personal transition is clean and documented by the observer |
| Objection diagnosis | Answers surface content (e.g. discounts on price) | Correct diagnosis on 2 of 3, one or two diagnostic questions each | Correct on all 3; uses split, isolate, origin, or weigh appropriately and offers to meet Jess |
| On-merit responses | Over-promises roadmap, argues, or discounts | Uses Marcus's own $300k; names the drawback plainly | Converts the price objection into a dated September business-case plan without moving price |

## Reflection prompts

- Where in Call A did you most want to describe the product, and what did Marcus say next because you didn't?
- Which objection were you least sure how to diagnose, and what single question would have settled it faster?

## Instructor notes

- The persona deliberately rewards permission and silence. If learners never reach personal pain, check whether they asked permission before question 7.
- Learners often treat objection 2 as owned and argue integration architecture with Marcus. The fix is to ask whether Jess has seen it, and offer to meet Jess.
- Keep the SPIN and Sandler pieces visibly separate in the plan (contract and funnel as frame, SPIN implications on the main problem), consistent with Lesson 3's note on not blending them while learning.
