## Scenario

This is the dress rehearsal before the Lesson 9 live negotiation. You are the account executive at **Meridian Support Cloud** selling 180 agent seats to **Calder Logistics** ($62 per seat per month list, $133,920 a year, 76% gross margin, $28,000 implementation). Before you meet Dominic Vasquez from procurement, your sales manager, **Helen Marsh**, runs a 25-minute deal review. She will not approve your going into the room until your prep sheet, trade inventory, and draft mutual action plan survive her questions.

Use only the facts in the Lesson 9 brief. Priya Raman (VP Customer Operations) is your champion; budget approvals close 30 November; Priya wants to be live before peak season on 1 March.

## What you will produce

1. A one-page negotiation prep sheet: opening, target, walk-away, BATNA (each with a one-sentence justification), margin cost of 5%, 10%, and 18% across a 24- and 36-month term, a ladder of at least three shrinking rungs with the payment for each, and the scope-reduction option below your floor.
2. A trade inventory of at least twelve terms sorted into accept / trade / refuse / route, with a reason for each.
3. A draft mutual action plan, worked backwards from a 1 March go-live, with at least eight steps, named owners on both sides, dates, dependencies, and the critical path stated in one sentence.
4. A recorded 25-minute deal review with a partner playing Helen.
5. A revision log: every change you made because of the review.

## Before you start

- Complete Lessons 6–8.
- Do the arithmetic first. At 76% margin, every discount point comes straight out of margin: 10% off $133,920 removes $13,392 a year, which is about 13% of the deal's $101,779 annual margin.

**Persona card: Helen Marsh, sales manager (partner only)**

- Open with: "Walk me through your four numbers. Why is your walk-away your walk-away?" Reject any walk-away that is not genuinely worse than no deal.
- Probe the ladder: "Your second rung is bigger than your first. Why?" (Raise it only if it's true; otherwise ask, "What does rung three buy that rung two didn't?")
- Ask: "Dominic will ask for most-favored-nation pricing. What do you say?" Accept only a polite, clear refusal with a reason.
- Ask: "If he says $45 a seat from a competitor, what's your first sentence?" Accept a question about what is in the competing number.
- On the action plan, ask: "Who on Calder's side owns IT review, by name?" If the rep has a department instead of a name, say: "Then that's your first action."
- Ask: "What's the date that actually decides 1 March?" Accept the approval deadline (30 November) or the IT review start date, with a reason.
- Close with: "What do you need from me?" Reward a specific ask, such as VP approval pre-cleared for a defined rung.

## Milestones

1. **Prep sheet (60 min).** Four numbers, ladder, scope option, cheap-to-give list with what each item should buy (Premier support upgrade, training days, extra sandbox).
2. **Trade inventory (30 min).** It must classify the two legal requests (custom SLA with uncapped credits, liability amendment) as route, MFN as refuse, and seat reduction on 30 days' notice as refuse. Ramp, term, and annual prepay are trades.
3. **Action plan (45 min).** Plan backwards from 1 March. Include the approval deadline, an IT or security review with a stated duration, legal on both sides, signature, kickoff, a pilot team, and go-live. Mark every owner you cannot yet name as a gap with an introduction request.
4. **Deal review (25 min, recorded).**
5. **Revise (30 min).** Update the three artifacts and log every change.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Walk-away sits at or above the 18% authority limit, or the sheet says plainly that scope reduces below it.
- [ ] Rung decrements shrink, and every rung names its payment.
- [ ] At least one ramp option is priced (e.g., fewer seats in year one, rising with Calder's 25% headcount growth) with the per-seat rate held.
- [ ] Concessions lean on one-time items (implementation fee, training) before recurring price, and the sheet says why.
- [ ] Legal items are routed, MFN refused, and seat reduction refused with a substitute offered.
- [ ] Mutual action plan has 8+ steps, a named Calder owner (or a dated introduction request) for each, and a one-sentence critical path.
- [ ] Revision log shows at least three changes driven by the review.

## Evidence checklist

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Four numbers | Missing or unjustified; walk-away is a hope | All four justified; arithmetic correct | BATNA is honest about weakness and the sheet adjusts the opening accordingly |
| Ladder and trades | Flat or growing rungs; concessions unpaid | Shrinking rungs, each paid; scope below floor | Ramp priced with total contract value compared against a flat discount |
| Term inventory | Legal items negotiated or landmines accepted | All landmines and legal items correctly bucketed | Cheap items mapped to the procurement "win" Dominic needs to report |
| Mutual action plan | Forwards from today; departments as owners | Backwards from 1 March; named owners; dependencies | Critical path identified and a dated action to start it this week |
| Handling the review | Defends with confidence | Answers each challenge with a number or name | Concedes a weak point and revises the artifact on the spot |

## Stretch goals

- Price the 24-month alternative package that becomes available if Calder refuses 36 months, and show which concessions disappear.
- Write the same-day confirmation email for the package you expect to land, under 250 words.

## Reflection prompts

- Which of Helen's questions exposed a number you hadn't really decided?
- Which owner on the action plan are you least likely to reach, and what is your plan B?

## Instructor notes

- The usual weak spot is the action plan built forwards from today. Ask, "What has to be true on 28 February?" and work back from there.
- Learners often count the Premier upgrade at list value ($34k) as a cost. Make them use cost to serve.
- For a shorter session, skip the recording and mark the three artifacts against the rubric.
