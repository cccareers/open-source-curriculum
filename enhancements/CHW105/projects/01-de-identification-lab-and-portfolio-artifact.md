## Scenario

Your classmate Jordan (fictional) wants to put an encounter write-up from a CHW103 role-play into their portfolio. The write-up was based on a "realistic composite," but Jordan filled it with details from a relative's real experience and from a volunteer shift. Jordan asks you to help make it safe and strong before sending it to a prospective field mentor.

Everything below is fictional, but it is written to contain planted identifiers.

**Jordan's draft (fictional)**

> **Encounter summary — 03/14/2026, 2:15 p.m.**
> Client: M.G., 91-year-old woman, DOB 6/2/1934, lives at the Rosewood Senior Apartments, Unit 4B, on Calle Ortega in Lindale (pop. ~600). Only Tongan speaker in the building. Retired as the town's only midwife.
> Medi-Cal ID 9XXXXXXX2B. Referred by Dr. Patel at Lindale Family Clinic for her rare kidney condition.
> I met her at the Thursday food giveaway at St. Mark's. Her grandson Tevita (age 12) interpreted.
> Need: transportation to dialysis three times a week. I called the county ride program (ref #44812) and booked rides.
> Follow-up: Called 3/21; she made two of three rides. Her phone is (555) 014-2290.
> File name: MG_Rosewood_encounter_final.docx — Document author: "J. Rivera"; Comments: "ask grandma if this is right"

## What you will produce

1. **Identifier hunt table** — every identifier and identifying combination in the draft, why it identifies, and how you removed or generalized it.
2. **Practice-quality fix list** — anything in the draft that is a practice problem, not just a privacy problem (hint: who interpreted).
3. **Rebuilt artifact** — a clearly labeled fictional/composite version that still demonstrates documentation, navigation, and follow-up skill, with the five-line framing header from Lesson 3.
4. **File hygiene checklist** — file name, document properties, comments, tracked changes, and screenshots checked and cleaned.
5. **Portfolio placement note** — two sentences on which skill this artifact proves and where it goes in your portfolio and LinkedIn Featured section.

## Before you start

- Lesson 3, "The rule that governs everything: no client-identifying information" and "Framing each artifact."

## Milestones

1. Identifier hunt (45 min). Work alone first, then compare with a partner; merge lists.
2. Practice-quality review (15 min).
3. Rebuild the artifact (60 min). Change details enough that no one could map it back; label it as a composite/fictional scenario.
4. File hygiene and neighbor test (20 min). Ask a partner to try to identify anyone from your rebuilt version. Treat this as a limited check, not proof: anything the partner can guess must change, but a partner who cannot identify anyone does not establish that the artifact is de-identified.
5. Placement note (10 min).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At least 15 identifiers or identifying combinations found, including: name initials, exact age over 89, date of birth, building name and unit, street, small town with population, unique language-in-building, unique former occupation, Medi-Cal ID, provider and clinic names, rare condition, event location and day, child's name and age, referral number, phone number, exact dates, file name, document author, and the embedded comment.
- [ ] Notes that initials are not de-identification, and that rare combinations (only speaker of a language in a small building; town's only midwife) identify as surely as names.
- [ ] Flags child interpretation as a practice problem (CHW102 Lesson 2) and shows how the rebuilt artifact models a qualified interpreter instead.
- [ ] Rebuilt artifact is labeled "Composite scenario — no client information," keeps the skill evidence (need, action, referral, follow-up with outcome), and uses generalized geography.
- [ ] File properties, comments, and file name are cleaned; file name follows a readable convention (e.g., `Rivera_Jordan_Transportation-Navigation-Sample.pdf`).
- [ ] Partner neighbor test passed and recorded.

## Evidence checklist

- Identifier hunt table
- Practice-quality note
- Rebuilt artifact with framing header
- File hygiene checklist with initials of the partner who checked
- Placement note

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identifier detection | Finds direct identifiers only | Finds combinations and file metadata | Explains re-identification risk for each combination |
| Rebuilt artifact | Redacted version with black boxes | Clean composite that still shows skill | Shows a stronger skill story than the original |
| Practice judgment | Misses child interpreter | Flags and fixes it | Connects to language access documentation |
| Professional presentation | Unlabeled, no header | Framing header with confidentiality note | Ready to attach to LinkedIn Featured |

## Stretch goals

- Write a 100-word note to Jordan explaining the changes kindly and specifically.

## Reflection prompts

## Instructor notes

- HIPAA's Safe Harbor method lists 18 identifier types (including ages over 89 and all elements of dates except year); learners do not need to memorize the list, but instructors may use it as an answer-key reference. Confirm the program's preferred standard.
- Students who are not yet working with clients still produce this kind of risk through relatives' stories; address that directly.
