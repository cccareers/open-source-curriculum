## Scenario

Your campaign card and two-week calendar are built. Most plans like that die on day four, and most people who run one can only say afterward that it "went well." This project turns your plan into a tracked experiment that runs from Week 6 through Demo Day: a live tracker, one mid-flight change, and a results card that compares what you predicted with what happened.

The results card is your **Digital Marketing rotation artifact** for the Week 7 portfolio (as a "campaign in progress" version), and the final version — with actual Demo Day attendance — is evidence you can cite on stage in Week 8.

## What you will produce

1. **Campaign tracker** (spreadsheet): one row per calendar slot and one row per named invitation, with status, the outcome, and the diagnostic you planned to check.
2. **Attribution log**: every inbound reply or yes, with trigger post and prior touches (format from Lesson 3).
3. **Mid-flight memo** (half a page): what the diagnostics said at the end of campaign week one, the one change you made, and why.
4. **Results card** (one page), finished after Demo Day: prediction vs. outcome, by segment.
5. **Ledger updates**: Week 6 row, caption card, dated reflection.

## Before you start (prerequisites, starter files or data)

- Positioning statement (Lesson 2, Practice 1).
- Best-performing post analysis and post table with rates (Lesson 3, Practice 1-4).
- Campaign card, named list, two-week calendar, and pre-registered prediction (Lesson 4, Practice 1-5).
- Your evidence ledger.

Tracker columns:

```text
date | type (post / DM / email / comment / reminder) | segment | person (DMs only)
| hook or message | CTA | status (planned / done / skipped) | diagnostic planned
| result (impr, comments, profile views, reply y/n, yes/no/maybe) | notes
```

## Milestones

1. **Build the tracker** (40 min). Load every calendar row and every named invitation from your list. Each personal invitation is its own row.
2. **Run days 1-5** (spread across the week; about 20 min/day for tracking). Mark each row done or skipped as it happens, not at the end of the week. Log results 48 hours after each post.
3. **Keep the attribution log** (ongoing). For every reply or yes, record trigger and prior touches. Where unclear, ask the person what made them respond.
4. **Mid-flight check** (40 min, end of campaign week one). Compute invitation reply rate per segment and engagement rate per post. Identify the weakest diagnostic and match it to its cause using Lesson 4's "Checking mid-flight." Write the memo naming one change. Make only that change.
5. **Positioning audit** (20 min). Read every post and invitation you have sent against your positioning statement. Mark any that drifted.
6. **Ledger updates** (20 min). Week 6 row with new columns `invitations_sent`, `invitation_reply_rate`, `demo_day_yes_count`. Caption card for the campaign (in-progress version). Dated reflection: hard, boring, surprising — and whether you would enjoy running campaigns like this full time.
7. **Results card** (40 min, after Demo Day). Prediction vs. actual for the primary metric (attendees from the named list), by segment. Which hook types and channels produced yeses? What would you change? Update the caption card with the final result.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every calendar slot and every invitation is a tracker row with a status; skipped rows are marked, not deleted.
- [ ] Post results are logged at 48 hours from platform analytics, not memory.
- [ ] The attribution log records trigger and prior touches for every inbound response.
- [ ] The mid-flight memo names exactly one change, tied to a diagnostic and a cause.
- [ ] The results card compares the pre-registered prediction with the actual count of attendees from the named list.
- [ ] The primary metric is a conversation-tier number, not impressions.
- [ ] Ledger updated.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Execution | Calendar abandoned or tracked at the end | Rows tracked as they happen; at least 80% of invitations sent | All personal invitations sent on their dated day |
| Diagnosis | Change made by feel, or several changes at once | One change, tied to a diagnostic and a cause | Memo predicts the effect of the change and checks it in campaign week two |
| Attribution | Credits the most recent post | Trigger and prior touches recorded; asks when unclear | Identifies a case where the post was *not* the cause, and says so |
| Honesty of results | Reports reach as success | Prediction vs. actual stated plainly, misses included | Explains the gap by segment and names what the data cannot show |
| Positioning | No audit | Drifted items identified | Drifted items rewritten or cut before posting |

## Stretch goals

- Run a small A/B test inside the invitations (Week 2 method) — for example, invitation referencing their post vs. referencing your project — and report it with honest small-sample language.
- After Demo Day, send a short "thanks for coming / sorry we missed you" follow-up to every named person and log it in your CRM.

## Reflection prompts

- Which segment did you overestimate, and why?
- Did any public post produce a yes on its own, without a personal invitation? What does that tell you about public vs. private channels?

## Instructor notes (common pitfalls, how to adapt for time)

- **Pitfall: impressions as the headline.** Push the results card to lead with attendees from the named list.
- **Pitfall: changing three things mid-flight.** Enforce one change.
- **Privacy:** the tracker contains named individuals. Portfolio versions should show segments and counts, not names.
- **Timing:** this project spans Weeks 6-8. Milestones 1-6 belong in Week 6; milestone 7 happens after Demo Day. Instructors can collect the results card with the Week 8 transition plan.
- **Short on time:** Milestones 1, 2, 4 (tracker, execution, mid-flight memo).
- **Arc:** sixth link (`enhancements/PREAPP-ARC.md`). The final results card is a ready-made evidence slide for Demo Day.
