## Concept and misconception it fixes

Misconception: "The post that reached the most people did the most for me," and its campaign twin, "A Demo Day post seen by 2,000 people will fill the room." The animation follows two posts' audiences down through the three tiers and shows the wide post thinning to almost nothing while the narrower post keeps people. It then shows why a named-list campaign needs personal invitations.

## Visual language (shapes, colors with color-blind-safe palette, labels)

- People are small figures (dots with heads). Three horizontal floors, labeled: Reach (top), Engagement (middle), Conversation (bottom).
- Post A ("I'm changing careers" announcement) on the left; Post B ("What I got wrong in my first mock interview") on the right. Each column labeled with its title.
- Palette (Okabe-Ito): Post A figures blue #0072B2 with a square marker; Post B figures orange #E69F00 with a triangle marker; personal invitations shown as envelope icons in bluish green #009E73. Shapes carry the meaning alongside color.

## Interaction variant (optional)

Learners enter their own post numbers (impressions, reactions, comments, profile views, messages, meetings) for two posts; the floors render their real crowds and the scoreboard flips automatically based on tier-3 counts.

## Production notes

- Scenes 1-3 use the lesson's P1 and P6 numbers exactly (lesson "A worked read"). Each rendered figure = 10 people on the top floor and 1 person on lower floors; state the scale in an on-screen key.
- Scene 5 invitation numbers are illustrative; do not cite specific conversion rates for invitations vs. posts, since the lesson gives none.
- Keep the floor labels identical to the lesson's tier names (reach, engagement, conversation).
