## Scenario

Two weeks into an outbound campaign for the field-service scheduling platform (Lesson 2), you have six accounts that replied. Your manager, **Teresa Lund**, runs a Friday pipeline review. The team has noticed that reps score the same deal very differently, so this week everyone scores the same six call-note files on their own first, then compares scores before presenting.

## What you will produce

1. A MEDDIC scorecard for all six accounts, scored independently, with the source and date beside every 2 or 3 (Lesson 6 rule one).
2. A calibration table comparing your scores with two peers' and with the instructor key, with every disagreement of two or more points on a field explained.
3. A sorted "work next" list: next action, owner, and date for each account, plus at least one disqualification with a nurture date and the sentence you would say to the prospect.
4. A recorded 2-minutes-per-account pipeline review with a partner playing Teresa.

## Before you start

**The six call-note files** (abridged; use only what's written)

1. **Northwind Mechanical.** Dana Ruiz, VP Ops, 21 Jul: "Utilization is 61%, we've told the board 70%." Must not require new tablets. On 23 Jul she forwarded our summary to two branch managers unprompted. COO Marcus Hale named in a vendor case study; never contacted. No information on the buying process.
2. **Corbett HVAC.** Office manager, 22 Jul: "Sounds interesting, send me something." No pain described. Owner "makes all decisions," not reached.
3. **Pine Ridge Facilities.** Director of Field Ops, 24 Jul: "We lose about two days a month reconciling branch schedules." Said the CFO approves anything over $25k and "we'd go through IT security review, usually six weeks." Has not introduced anyone.
4. **Halvorsen Electric.** VP Service, 20 Jul: just signed a three-year contract with a direct competitor in May. "Ask me in 2028."
5. **Brightwater Plumbing.** Dispatch Manager, 25 Jul: "Our techs are double-booked three times a week." Mentioned that the new COO is "looking at everything." Introduced you by email to the COO, who has not replied.
6. **Kestrel Building Systems.** COO, 23 Jul (call booked by the SDR): stated budget is approved for "a scheduling fix this fiscal year," decision by end of Q3. Evaluation criteria: works with their ERP; under $60k. Wants a demo with two branch heads. No metric stated.

**Persona card: Teresa Lund (partner only)**
- For every 3 a rep claims, ask: "Who said that, and when?" If there's no quote, have them lower it on the spot.
- Ask: "What's the weakest field, and what's the exact sentence you'll say to raise it?"
- Ask at least once: "Why is this still open?"
- End with: "So what do you work Monday morning?"

## Milestones

1. **Score independently (45 min).** Apply rule one strictly: no score above 1 without a source.
2. **Calibrate (30 min).** Compare with two peers and the key. For every field where scores differ by 2 or more, write which reading of the notes is correct and why.
3. **Decide (30 min).** Next action for each account from its weakest field. Disqualify at least one (Halvorsen is the obvious candidate; Corbett is debatable). Sort by expected value as Lesson 6 describes.
4. **Review (15 min, recorded).**

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All six accounts scored on six fields, with sources beside every 2 or 3.
- [ ] Northwind Economic buyer scored 1 (named from research, never contacted), and Decision process 0.
- [ ] Kestrel Metrics scored no higher than 1, since no metric was stated.
- [ ] Halvorsen handled as a condition: disqualified with a dated nurture (early 2028) and a polite sentence.
- [ ] Every remaining account has a next action tied to its weakest field, with the exact question written out.
- [ ] Calibration table explains every disagreement of 2 or more points.
- [ ] Monday's work list is sorted, with the reasoning stated.

## Evidence checklist

- [ ] Independent scorecard (timestamped)
- [ ] Calibration table
- [ ] Work-next list with disqualifications
- [ ] Recording
- [ ] One paragraph: which of your scores moved after calibration, and why

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source discipline | Scores from impressions | Every 2/3 sourced; unsourced scores dropped to 1 | Flags where a note is ambiguous and proposes the question that would settle it |
| Calibration | Disagreements unexplained | Each disagreement explained against the definitions | Proposes a wording change to the scorecard definitions that would prevent the disagreement |
| Next actions | "Follow up" | Weakest-field action with a written question and date | Uses "walk me through the last time you bought…" style questions for process gaps |
| Disqualification | Keeps everything open | Disqualifies with evidence and a nurture date | Says the disqualifying sentence aloud in the review without apologizing |
| Prioritization | Works the most recent reply | Sorted by score, date, and cost of the next action | Explains why Kestrel (high authority, no metric) and Pine Ridge (clear process, no champion action) need different plays |

## Stretch goals

- Re-score all six after an imagined week in which Brightwater's COO replies with a meeting date. Which fields move?
- Write a one-line definition change for "Champion = 3" that would remove your team's biggest disagreement.

## Reflection prompts

- Which score did you most want to keep high, and what did you have to admit to lower it?
- What does the gap between activity (six replies) and qualified pipeline tell you about the next two weeks?
