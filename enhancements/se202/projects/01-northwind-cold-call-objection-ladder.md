## Scenario

You sell the field-service workforce scheduling platform from Lesson 2 (as Alex Chen of Northstar, or under your own name). Your tier-A account is **Northwind Mechanical** (~90 field techs, 4 branches). Lesson 3 gave you the committee map: Dana Ruiz (VP Operations, 3 weeks in), Marcus Hale (COO), Priya Nandal (Director of IT), Ken Ostrowski (Dispatch Manager, built the spreadsheet process — likely blocker).

This project is a graded drill ladder: six calls, each harder than the last, played against persona cards by a partner. You climb a rung only when you hit the rung's pass bar.

## What you will produce

1. A one-page call plan for Northwind (reason, two questions, specific ask) — Lesson 4 format.
2. Six recorded role-play calls (rungs 1–6).
3. An observer scorecard for each call.
4. A one-page objection card in your own words, revised after the ladder.
5. A 300-word debrief naming your weakest move and the drill you will repeat.

## Before you start

- Complete Lessons 3 and 4.
- Partner prints the persona cards below. **You do not read them.**

## The ladder (persona cards for the partner)

| Rung | Who answers | Partner instructions | Pass bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dana Ruiz | Friendly but busy. Answers honestly. Accepts a meeting if asked for a specific time. | Opener under 20 s; trigger → problem → question; specific two-option ask |
| 2 | Dana Ruiz | Opens with "I'm not interested" before the rep finishes the opener. If the rep acknowledges and asks one honest question, answer it: "Honestly, utilization reporting is a mess, but I've got bigger things." | Acknowledge → reframe → re-ask with no "but"; lands either a meeting or a dated callback |
| 3 | Dana Ruiz | "We already use ServiceTitan." (Stays neutral; agrees cross-branch reporting is weak only if the rep names that specific limitation without disparaging the tool.) | No disparagement; names one specific, plausible limitation and asks; ends with a dated next step |
| 4 | Front desk at Northwind | "Who's calling, and what's it regarding?" Help the rep if they are straightforward; stonewall if they are evasive or claim a prior relationship. | Honest identification; asks for routing help; leaves with a name or an extension |
| 5 | Ken Ostrowski | Defensive: "Our boards work fine. I built them." Softens only if the rep is curious about how his process works and does not imply it is broken. | Two or more open questions about Ken's process; no claim that his system is inadequate; asks whether Ken would be part of a conversation with Dana |
| 6 | Dana Ruiz | Rep must recover: partner interrupts after 40 seconds of rep talk with silence, then "Sorry — what is this about again?" | Rep names the problem plainly ("I'm doing all the talking…"), hands it back with a question, and either lands a meeting or asks "check back in a quarter, or leave you alone?" |

## Milestones

1. **Research check (30 min).** Confirm the trigger (Dana's start date), and write the source for every fact you plan to use. If you are using a real company of your own choosing instead of Northwind, build its committee map first.
2. **Call plan and objection card (45 min).**
3. **Climb the ladder (90 min).** Record each call. Partner scores it immediately. Repeat a rung until you pass it, maximum three attempts per rung.
4. **Listen back (45 min).** Measure talk-to-listen ratio for rungs 1 and 6.
5. **Revise and debrief (30 min).**

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Call plan names the trigger with its source and date.
- [ ] All six rungs attempted and recorded; at least five passed.
- [ ] No call contains "How are you today?", a false claim of a prior conversation, or disparagement of a competitor.
- [ ] Every passed call ends with a specific date/time or an explicit, honest close-out.
- [ ] Talk ratio under 40% on at least one recorded call, measured and reported.
- [ ] Revised objection card covers all six Lesson 4 objections.

## Evidence checklist

- [ ] Call plan
- [ ] Six recordings (or more, if rungs were repeated)
- [ ] Six observer scorecards
- [ ] Talk-ratio measurements
- [ ] Revised objection card
- [ ] Debrief

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opener | Generic, over 20 s, or keeps talking after asking permission | Under 20 s, asks permission or pattern-interrupts, then pauses | Trigger lands in the first sentence and the prospect engages with it |
| Reason and discovery | Pitches product before learning anything | Trigger → problem → question; two open questions; verbatim notes | Follows the prospect's thread and uses their exact words in the bridge |
| Objection handling | Argues, says "but," or folds immediately | Acknowledge → reframe → re-ask on each objection | Converts a reflex objection into a qualifying answer and a dated step |
| Gatekeeper and blocker | Evasive, tries to slip past, or dismisses Ken | Honest routing request; curious with Ken | Turns Ken into a named participant in the next meeting |
| Ask and recovery | Vague ask ("sometime next week") | Two-option specific ask, then silence; clean recovery on rung 6 | Confirms attendees and duration and states when the invite will arrive |
| Integrity | Any deception or failure to honor a "don't call" | Honest throughout | Volunteers an opt-out when the prospect sounds annoyed |

## Stretch goals

- Run rung 5 again with Ken as the *only* reachable contact; aim for a meeting that includes him and Dana.
- Record a 20-second voicemail for Dana that references touch 1 of your email sequence, and have a partner rate whether they would call back.

## Reflection prompts

- On which rung did you most want to keep talking, and what did the silence produce when you did not?
- What did Ken's defensiveness teach you about how to run the rest of this deal?

## Instructor notes

- Rung 4 (gatekeeper) is where learners most often slide into small deceptions ("Dana's expecting my call"). Stop the role-play immediately and restart.
- Rung 5 is deliberately about the blocker identified in the Lesson 3 committee map; reward curiosity, not persuasion.
- For a shorter session, run rungs 1, 2, and 6 only.
