## Purpose

After watching, the learner can hear the five moves of a cold call (opener, reason, discovery, bridge, ask), recognize the weak-take habits that waste them, and handle a reflex "not interested" without arguing.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read Lesson 4 through "Handling objections." Uses the Northwind Mechanical account and Dana Ruiz from Lessons 3–5.

## Script

Audio-forward: cold calls are phone calls. Visuals are a split screen of two phones with a live transcript and a running timer.

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Host to camera. | "Dana Ruiz started as VP of Operations at Northwind Mechanical three weeks ago. Two reps call her the same morning. Listen for how long each one talks before Dana gets a turn." |
| 0:15 | Title: "Take 1." Timer starts. | |
| 0:17 | Phone rings; Dana picks up. | **Dana:** "Dana Ruiz." |
| 0:19 | Rep (Sam). Talk-time bar grows. | **Sam:** "Hi Dana, how are you today? Great. My name is Sam and I'm with Northstar — we're a leading provider of cloud-based field service workforce optimization solutions, and we help companies like yours leverage real-time scheduling to drive efficiency and synergies across their operations. We work with over five hundred contractors, we integrate with all the major platforms, and we just launched an AI routing engine that—" |
| 0:42 | Dana cuts in. | **Dana:** "Sorry, I'm not interested." |
| 0:44 | Sam. | **Sam:** "But if you just give me two minutes, I think you'll see it's actually really different from what you're using—" |
| 0:49 | Dana. | **Dana:** "Send me some information." *(click)* |
| 0:51 | Freeze. Talk-time bar: Sam 27 s, Dana 4 s. Callouts with icons + text. | Host VO: "Twenty-seven seconds of Sam, four of Dana. 'How are you today' announced a telemarketer. The reason for the call was about Northstar, not about Dana. There was no trigger, no question, no ask. And when she objected, 'but' turned it into an argument." |
| 1:25 | Title: "Take 2." Timer resets. | |
| 1:27 | Dana picks up. | **Dana:** "Dana Ruiz." |
| 1:29 | Step chip: "1. Opener." | **Alex:** "Dana, this is Alex Chen with Northstar. I know I'm an interruption — can I take thirty seconds to tell you why I called, and you can tell me if it's worth going further?" |
| 1:37 | Silence, two seconds; caption "[2-second pause]". | *(pause)* |
| 1:39 | Dana. | **Dana:** "...I'm honestly not interested in buying anything right now." |
| 1:42 | Step chip: "Objection: acknowledge → reframe → re-ask." | **Alex:** "Totally fair — you don't know me and I haven't earned it. One question and then I'll leave you alone: I saw you came into the VP seat about three weeks ago from the contractor side. Is cross-branch utilization something on your list yet, or is your first quarter pointed somewhere else entirely?" |
| 1:58 | Dana pauses. | **Dana:** "Huh. Actually — yeah. Every branch runs its own board and I can't compare anything." |
| 2:04 | Step chip: "3. Discovery." | **Alex:** "Everybody's got their own board. When utilization looks off at one branch, how long before you'd know?" |
| 2:10 | Dana. | **Dana:** "Month end, if I'm lucky. Phoenix says they're at seventy percent and I don't believe it." |
| 2:16 | Alex. | **Alex:** "Who makes the call when a job gets reassigned mid-day there?" |
| 2:19 | Dana. | **Dana:** "Ken, our dispatch manager, or whoever's on the board that day." |
| 2:23 | Step chip: "4. Bridge." | **Alex:** "That's exactly the pattern I called about. We work with multi-branch contractors who have the same per-branch board problem — the useful part is seeing utilization across branches on one view without ripping out what each branch already uses. I'd rather show you than describe it." |
| 2:37 | Step chip: "5. Ask." | **Alex:** "Twenty minutes Thursday at 2:00, or is Friday morning easier?" |
| 2:41 | Silence, three seconds. Caption "[3-second pause]". | *(silence)* |
| 2:44 | Dana. | **Dana:** "Friday. Early. Eight-thirty." |
| 2:47 | Alex. | **Alex:** "Friday at 8:30, twenty minutes. Should Ken join, since he's running the boards? I'll send the invite while we're on the phone." |
| 2:54 | Dana. | **Dana:** "Yeah, add Ken." |
| 2:56 | Freeze. Talk-time bar: Alex 38%, Dana 62%. | Host VO: "Under ninety seconds of real conversation. Alex spoke less than forty percent of it." |
| 3:05 | Host to camera. Transcript highlights with underline. | "Look at what did the work. The permission opener and the pause handed Dana control. When she objected, Alex agreed, didn't argue, and asked one honest question built on the trigger he'd researched. Her words — 'every branch runs its own board' — went straight back into his next question. The bridge was two sentences. The ask was two specific times. And he multi-threaded on the spot by inviting Ken, the person most likely to block this deal." |
| 3:50 | Host. | "One honesty note. Alex never said 'we work with five hundred contractors' or named a customer he couldn't back up. If you have an approved case study, use it. If not, a segment-level observation is enough." |
| 4:10 | Practice prompt. | "Pause. Dana says, 'We already use ServiceTitan.' Write Alex's response — no disparaging the tool." |
| 4:25 | Sample answer. | Host VO: "One good answer: 'Good — then you've solved the hard part of getting branches onto something. Most folks running it are happy with dispatch and stuck on cross-branch reporting. Is that your experience, or has that piece been fine?'" |
| 4:50 | Recap card: the five moves and the objection pattern. | Host: "Opener, reason, discovery, bridge, ask. Acknowledge, reframe, re-ask. Drill them on the Northwind ladder project." |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Split-phone frame, live transcript, talk-time bar, step chips 1–5.
- Optional: muted B-roll of a rep at a desk with a call list and timer.

## Accessibility

- Full captions with speaker labels; pauses captioned with duration.
- Talk-time bars labeled with numbers, not just length.
- Step chips are text, numbered, and announced in VO.

## Check for understanding

1. List three habits in Take 1 that lost the call. *(Any three: "How are you today?"; reason about the vendor not the buyer; no trigger; no question; no ask; "but" in response to the objection.)*
2. Why did Alex invite Ken? *(Answer: to multi-thread, and because Ken owns the boards and is the likely blocker; involving him early is better than surprising him later.)*
3. What is the structure of Alex's response to "I'm not interested"? *(Answer: acknowledge ("totally fair"), reframe with one honest trigger-based question, re-ask implicitly by inviting an answer.)*
