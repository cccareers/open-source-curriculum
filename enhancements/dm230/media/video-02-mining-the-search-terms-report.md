## Purpose

After watching, the learner can run the weekly search terms routine on a spreadsheet export: sort by cost, tag each row Promote, Negative, Leave, or Investigate, add negatives as categories at the right level and match type, and compute the wasted spend.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read lesson 04 through "The search terms report routine." The video uses the 20-row Northgate AC Repair export from the lesson, loaded into a spreadsheet; no ad account is needed.

Header: "Northgate AC Repair, last 30 days, $3,200 spend." | "This is what Northgate actually paid for last month: not the keywords they bought, the searches that triggered them. | 0:55 | Add a "Verdict" column with a dropdown: Promote / Negative / Leave / Investigate. | "Step three: give every row one of four verdicts." |
| 1:10 | Row 1 "ac repair columbus", exact, 41 clicks, $349.60, 7 conv → Leave. | "Row one: our own exact keyword, seven conversions. Leave it." |
| 1:25 | Row 2 "air conditioner repair near me", phrase, 36 clicks, 6 conv. | 3:25 | Row 12 "ac repair jobs columbus" → Negative. Side panel shows both "job" and "jobs". | "Row twelve: jobs. Add 'job' and 'jobs' as separate entries. Negatives don't match plurals or close variants. If you only add 'job', 'hvac jobs columbus' still gets through." |

## Check for understanding

1. A query "hvac school columbus" has one click and no conversions. Can you add a negative now? **Answer:** yes; Northgate does not sell training, so it is a relevance judgment and one click is enough. Add the category ("school", "training") as phrase on the shared list.
2. A query has 2 conversions on 7 clicks. Promote it? **Answer:** not yet; it does not meet three conversions or twenty clicks. Tag Investigate unless it fits a broader pattern you can defend.
3. Why add both "job" and "jobs"? **Answer:** negative keywords do not match close variants, so each form must be listed.
