## Scenario

You have built the Alder Creek Supply business case from the Lesson 5 practice (22 inside sales reps, 3,100 quote requests a month, 22% product gross margin, $214,000 of mispriced-order credits). Two meetings are now on the calendar on the same day:

1. **10:00 — Alder Creek's Controller, Dana Whitfield**, has asked for 20 minutes to "pressure-test the numbers" before she forwards anything to the CFO.
2. **15:00 — your company's deal desk.** Alder Creek's VP of Inside Sales has asked for 20% off. Deal desk will approve or reject based on your economics, not on the customer's enthusiasm.

This project is two role-plays and the two artifacts that support them. It is deliberately the *same deal seen from both sides* — the core idea of se201.

## What you will produce

1. A one-page assumptions sheet for Alder Creek: every baseline and benefit line with its figure, arithmetic, lever, haircut, and named source.
2. A recorded 15-minute role-play with a partner playing the Controller (persona card below).
3. A one-page deal desk memo on the 20% discount request.
4. A recorded 8-minute deal desk review with a partner playing the deal desk manager.
5. A short reflection (300 words max).

## Before you start

- Complete Lesson 5 Exercises 1–4 (you need your Alder Creek baseline, future state, and downside case).
- For your own economics, use the Lesson 3 Riverbend mid-market segment as a stand-in: segment CAC $8,000 ($288,000 / 36), 72% gross margin, 1.5% monthly logo churn. State on the memo that you are using this as a proxy.

**Persona card — Dana Whitfield, Controller (for the partner only)**

- You are not hostile; you are careful. Your CFO once approved a vendor case that missed by 70%, and you were the one who had to explain it.
- Challenge 1 (ask early): "Where did the 26 minutes per quote come from? Did anyone time it?" Accept it only if the rep attributes it to the VP of Inside Sales and labels it as an estimate.
- Challenge 2: "You've counted the late-quote win-rate improvement as revenue. What's that worth to *us*?" Accept it only if the rep has converted it to gross profit at 22%.
- Challenge 3: "We're not cutting reps. So what does saving 19 minutes a quote actually buy me?" Accept cost avoidance tied to the stated 15% volume growth with no added headcount; reject anything framed as headcount reduction.
- Challenge 4 (only if the rep has handled 1–3 well): "What if you're wrong?" Accept a stated downside case at half benefits. If the rep has one ready, say: "Okay. Send me the assumptions page, not just the total."

**Persona card — deal desk manager (for the partner only)**

- Open with: "Why 20%?" If the rep says "because they asked," respond: "That's not a reason. What does it do to payback?"
- You approve only if the rep (a) states payback and LTV:CAC at list and at the discounted price, (b) names something received in exchange (term, prepay, case study, a reference), and (c) shows the buyer's own model clears comfortably at full price.

## Milestones

1. **Assumptions sheet (90 min).** Every line attributed. Revenue-side lines converted to gross profit. Labor framed as cost avoidance. Downside case at half benefits.
2. **Controller rehearsal (30 min).** For each of the four challenges, write the one-sentence answer and the source you will point to.
3. **Controller role-play (15 min, recorded).**
4. **Deal desk memo (60 min).** Compute your monthly gross profit on Alder Creek's subscription (from Lesson 5 Exercise 1) at list and at 20% off; CAC payback; simple LTV; LTV:CAC at both prices. Name the trade you would ask for.
5. **Deal desk role-play (8 min, recorded).**
6. **Reflection.**

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Every number on the assumptions sheet traces to a named Alder Creek source or is labeled as an assumption.
- [ ] Late-quote revenue gain is converted to gross profit at 22% before it enters the total.
- [ ] No headcount reduction is claimed.
- [ ] Downside case (half benefits) computed with net, ROI, and payback.
- [ ] Deal desk memo shows payback and LTV:CAC at list and at 20% off, with arithmetic.
- [ ] Memo names a specific trade (for example, 24-month term with annual prepay) rather than an unconditional discount.
- [ ] Both role-plays recorded.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source discipline | Some figures unattributed or invented | Every figure attributed or labeled as an assumption | Flags where two Alder Creek figures need reconciling and shows the reconciliation |
| Lever honesty | Revenue counted at full value, or headcount reduction claimed | Revenue converted to margin; labor as avoidance | Explicitly ranks lines by defensibility and explains why the largest line is the weakest |
| Handling the Controller | Defends numbers by restating them louder | Answers each challenge with a source and the arithmetic | Invites correction and updates the model live, turning challenges into co-ownership |
| Deal desk economics | Discount requested without payback impact | Payback and LTV:CAC at both prices, correct arithmetic | Uses the buyer's full-price ROI to argue for holding price, and proposes a trade worth more than the concession |
| Communication | Leads with features or totals without arithmetic | Leads with net and payback for the Controller; leads with payback for deal desk | Each audience gets a different first sentence matched to what they care about |

## Stretch goals

- Re-run the deal desk review with the request changed to "15% off for a three-year prepay." Use the cash-payback logic from Lesson 3 to argue whether that is a better trade.
- Build a two-column "same deal, two sides" slide: buyer's payback on the left, your CAC payback on the right.

## Reflection prompts

- Which of the Controller's challenges did you find hardest, and which line of your model was it attacking?
- Did the discount conversation change how you would present the buyer's case? Why?

## Instructor notes

- The common miss is Challenge 2: learners add late-quote revenue at full value. The Lesson 5 text is explicit about converting to margin; point back to it.
- Learners often mix up the buyer's payback (months to recover their investment) with your CAC payback (months for your company to recover acquisition cost). Make them label both.
- For a shorter session, run only the Controller role-play and the memo.
