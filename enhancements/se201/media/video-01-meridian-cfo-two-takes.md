## Purpose

After watching, the learner can open a CFO conversation with net benefit and payback in the buyer's own numbers, answer an assumptions challenge by pointing to the source, and close with a downside case.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read Lesson 5 through "Framing it for the person in front of you." All figures come from the Lesson 5 Meridian Logistics model.

## Script

Setting: a video call. **Rep** (Alex) and **Meridian's CFO** (Helen Park). The champion, the AP Manager (Tom), is also on the call but silent.

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Host to camera. | "You have five minutes with a CFO. You have a model that says Meridian saves over three hundred thousand dollars a year. Watch two reps spend those five minutes." |
| 0:15 | Title: "Take 1 — advocacy." | |
| 0:18 | Alex shares a slide: logo, "Up to 60% lower AP costs!" | **Alex:** "Thanks for the time, Helen. So our platform typically reduces AP processing costs by up to sixty percent, and customers love the dashboards. For Meridian we're projecting over three hundred thousand in annual savings, which is a huge ROI." |
| 0:35 | Helen, neutral. | **Helen:** "How did you get three hundred thousand?" |
| 0:38 | Alex scrolls through slides looking. | **Alex:** "It's a combination of things — labor, late fees, discounts. Our customers see this kind of result all the time." |
| 0:46 | Helen. | **Helen:** "Labor. Are you assuming I cut clerks?" |
| 0:49 | Alex. | **Alex:** "Well, you'd free up around four FTEs, so there's a lot of efficiency there." |
| 0:54 | Helen, flat. | **Helen:** "I've said I'm not cutting AP. Send Tom the deck and we'll take a look." |
| 0:59 | Freeze. Callouts with icons and text. | Host VO: "Three failures. The opening was a claim about the product — 'up to sixty percent' — that Helen can ignore. The total had no visible arithmetic, so the first question exposed it. And the labor line contradicted something the CFO had already said, which contaminated every honest line next to it. 'Send Tom the deck' is a polite exit." |
| 1:30 | Title: "Take 2 — analysis." | |
| 1:33 | Alex shares a single page: four lines, a source column, and a downside row. | **Alex:** "Thanks, Helen. I'll give you three numbers and then the assumptions behind them. Year one: a ninety-three thousand four hundred dollar investment, including implementation. Conservative annual benefit of about three hundred fourteen thousand. Payback in about three and a half months." |
| 1:52 | Highlight on "Source" column. | **Alex:** "Every line comes from your team. Late-payment penalties are from your GL account 6140 — ninety-one thousand two hundred last year. Volume is from the systems export Tom's team pulled." |
| 2:05 | Helen. | **Helen:** "And labor? I've said I'm not cutting AP." |
| 2:08 | Line highlights: "Avoided hiring, 2.5 clerks, $145,000 — cost avoidance." | **Alex:** "Right, and the model doesn't. The system would free about four FTEs of touch time, but your volume is growing about twenty percent a year. We've claimed only the two and a half clerks you won't need to hire over the next two years. That's avoidance, not reduction." |
| 2:28 | Helen nods. | **Helen:** "What did you leave out?" |
| 2:31 | Line: "Faster month-end close — not quantified." | **Alex:** "Month-end close. Tom cares about it and it's real, but nobody gave us a defensible dollar figure, so it's in the narrative, not the total. We also counted duplicate prevention at half, because last year's sixty-two thousand came from a single audit sample." |
| 2:48 | Helen. | **Helen:** "And if you're wrong?" |
| 2:50 | Downside row highlights. | **Alex:** "If every line lands at half, year one is still about sixty-four thousand net, and payback is a little over seven months. Even if we're wrong by half, you're ahead inside eight months." |
| 3:05 | Silence, 3 seconds, timer on screen. | *(silence)* |
| 3:08 | Helen. | **Helen:** "Send me the assumptions page, not the deck. If Tom and I agree on the inputs, I'll put it on the agenda for the 20th." |
| 3:15 | Alex. | **Alex:** "I'll send it today. Would it help if Tom and I walked your analyst through it on Thursday, before the 20th?" |
| 3:22 | Freeze. Check-icon callouts. | Host VO: "Same deal, same numbers. Take two led with net and payback, pointed at sources Helen's own team owns, matched the labor line to what she'd already said, named what was left out, and had the downside ready before she asked. That's the difference between advocacy and analysis." |
| 4:00 | Host with the Lesson 5 arithmetic on screen. | Host: "Here's the arithmetic Alex was carrying. Investment: sixty-five thousand four hundred plus twenty-eight thousand equals ninety-three thousand four hundred. Benefit: one forty-five plus sixty-three eight-forty plus eighty-one plus twenty-four eight hundred equals three hundred fourteen thousand six hundred forty. Monthly benefit twenty-six thousand two-twenty; payback three point six months. Half case: one fifty-seven three-twenty, payback seven point one months." |
| 4:45 | Host. | "Notice Alex never said 'ROI of 237 percent' first. Percentages invite argument. Months of payback against a number she owns invite agreement." |
| 5:05 | Practice prompt. | "Pause. Helen asks: 'Your discount capture assumes we go from 20 to 65 percent. Why not the 78 percent your straight-through rate implies?' Write Alex's answer." |
| 5:20 | Sample answer. | Host VO: "One good answer: 'Because not every straight-through invoice will clear inside the ten-day window in month one, and I'd rather under-claim. At 65 percent it's eighty-one thousand; if you get to 78, that's upside you'll see in the scorecard.'" |
| 5:50 | Recap card: lead with net and payback; source every line; match levers to what the buyer said; name what you left out; downside before they ask. | Host: "Five habits. Practice them on the Alder Creek case next." |
| 6:15 | End card. | |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Weak-take slide ("Up to 60%!", logo, stock photo).
- Strong-take one-page model with columns: Line, Lever, Claimed, Source; downside row.
- Arithmetic overlay for 4:00 segment.

## Accessibility

- Captions with speaker labels; "[3 seconds of silence]" captioned.
- Highlighted rows use a border and a pointer icon in addition to color.
- The one-page model is provided as an accessible table download.

## Check for understanding

1. Why did Helen's first question in Take 1 end the conversation? *(Answer: the total had no visible arithmetic or sources, so it could not survive a single "how?")*
2. Why is the labor line framed as cost avoidance? *(Answer: the CFO said she will not cut AP headcount; volume growth means hires avoided is the honest lever.)*
3. What does the downside case do that the main case cannot? *(Answer: it answers "what if you're wrong?" with numbers, showing payback still under eight months at half benefits.)*
