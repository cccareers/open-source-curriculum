## Purpose

After watching, the learner can price one customer under subscription, usage, and hybrid models and explain each one's floor, volatility, and growth. The learner can also say why the lowest expected-revenue model is often the best deal to sell.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read Lesson 2 through the Meridian worked example. All figures come from that example: average 12,000 documents/month, slow month 8,300, December peak 17,600, 149,000 last year, 150 users.

## Script

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Whiteboard: two reps, both writing "$72,000" on a board. | "Two reps close deals on the same day and both write seventy-two thousand on the whiteboard. One has locked in six thousand a month for three years. The other has sold a meter. Same headline, different deals. Let's price one customer three ways." |
| 0:20 | Draws a monthly volume line for Meridian: trough 8,300, average 12,000, December spike 17,600. | "Meridian Logistics processes about twelve thousand documents a month. The slow month is eighty-three hundred and December peaks at seventeen-six. Last year's total was a hundred forty-nine thousand." |
| 0:40 | Column A: "Subscription — 150 seats × $40." Flat horizontal line at $6,000/month. | "Model A, subscription. A hundred fifty seats at forty dollars: six thousand a month, seventy-two thousand ARR. The line is flat. Seventy-two thousand arrives whether they process eight thousand documents or eighteen thousand, and it doesn't move if volume doubles." |
| 1:05 | Column B: "Usage — $0.50/doc." A line that tracks the volume curve: $4,150 trough, $6,000 average, $8,800 peak. | "Model B, usage at fifty cents a document. Average month six thousand. Slow month forty-one-fifty. Peak eighty-eight hundred. Last year's actual volume gives seventy-four-five hundred, slightly more than A. But annualize a single month and you get anything from forty-nine-eight to a hundred-five-six." |
| 1:35 | Red-outlined warning icon: "Quote December as ARR → miss in February." | "If you quote the peak month as ARR, you'll miss your number in February." |
| 1:45 | Column C: "Hybrid — $3,000/mo incl. 5,000 docs + $0.35 over." A floor line at $3,000 plus a band above it. Values: $4,155 / $5,450 / $7,410. | "Model C, hybrid. A three-thousand platform fee that includes five thousand documents, then thirty-five cents each. Average month fifty-four-fifty. Slow month forty-one-fifty-five. Peak seventy-four-ten. Annualized at average, sixty-five-four. The contractual floor is thirty-six thousand." |
| 2:20 | Ranks the three by expected revenue: B $74,500 > A $72,000 > C $65,400. Circles C. | "Hybrid produces the lowest expected revenue of the three, and it's very often the best one to sell. Here's why." |
| 2:35 | Growth scenario: volume doubles to 24,000/month. A stays $72,000. B rises to $144,000. C: $3,000 + 19,000 × $0.35 = $9,650/month → $115,800. | "Suppose Meridian wins a big shipper and doubles to twenty-four thousand documents a month. Subscription stays at seventy-two until someone opens a seat conversation. Usage doubles to a hundred forty-four, but all that volatility was yours the whole time. Hybrid goes to ninety-six-fifty a month, a hundred fifteen-eight ARR, with no renegotiation, and it kept a thirty-six thousand floor in a bad year." |
| 3:20 | Table: Floor / Volatility / Growth without a sale / Buyer's budget risk, ticks and crosses for A/B/C. | "So compare them on four things: floor, volatility, growth without a new sale, and the buyer's budget risk. Subscription is all floor and no growth. Usage is all growth and no floor. Hybrid trades a bit of expected revenue for both." |
| 3:50 | Buyer's-side panel: "Fixed appropriation? Approval threshold? Certainty about the product?" | "The buyer is doing this math too. Some can't approve a variable line at all. A thirty-six thousand committed floor might sit under an approval threshold that seventy-two thousand crosses. And a buyer who isn't sure the product will work wants the smallest first commitment." |
| 4:15 | Mistake card: "Commit sized at December's 17,600." Eleven months of shaded unused capacity. | "The classic hybrid mistake is sizing the commit at the peak. Then eleven months a year they pay for capacity they don't use, and the renewal starts with a demand for forty percent off. Size the commit near the trough and let the meter catch the peaks." |
| 4:40 | Vocabulary strip: MRR, ARR (snapshot), ACV, TCV (includes one-time fees), booking vs. revenue. | "And keep the vocabulary straight. ARR is a snapshot of the run rate. TCV includes the implementation fee. A booking isn't revenue." |
| 5:00 | Practice prompt. | "Pause. Northgate Interiors from the practice ran sixty-four hundred rendered files last year. Under a hybrid of twenty-two hundred a month including three hundred files, then eighty-five cents, what's the contractual floor?" |
| 5:15 | Sample answer. | Host VO: "Twenty-two hundred times twelve: twenty-six thousand four hundred. That's the number that survives a bad year." |
| 5:30 | Recap card. | "What's the deal worth? There's no answer until you say which model, at what volume, with what floor." |
| 5:45 | End card. | |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Whiteboard drawings of three revenue lines over a twelve-month volume curve.
- Comparison table with ticks and crosses (icons plus text).

## Accessibility

- Captions; every number on the board is spoken.
- Lines distinguished by pattern (solid, dashed, solid floor with dotted band) and labels, not color alone.
- Downloadable worked arithmetic sheet.

## Check for understanding

1. What is Meridian's contractual floor under the hybrid model, and why does it matter? *(Answer: $36,000 a year, revenue that survives a bad quarter at the customer and that finance can treat as recurring.)*
2. If volume doubles, which model grows without a renegotiation while keeping a floor? *(Answer: hybrid, to $115,800 ARR.)*
3. Why size a hybrid commit near the trough? *(Answer: a commit sized at the peak means paying for unused capacity most of the year, which leads to a hostile renewal.)*
