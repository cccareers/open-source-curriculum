## Purpose

After watching, a learner can fill a fit matrix with numbers and quotes (not adjectives), read the disagreements between sources, and write the "strongest case against" section of a declaration memo.

## Audience and prerequisites

Week 7 participants on Monday, before writing the declaration memo. They have rotation data, reflections, interview notes, and a coach meeting scheduled.

## Script

A fictional participant, **Keisha**, is used throughout. Her data is the lesson's sample matrix.

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Instructor at whiteboard. Writes "Which pathway?" and "3 seconds." | "Ask a room which pathway they want, and most people answer in three seconds. That speed should worry you. Three seconds is a feeling — and feelings are shaped by the best guest speaker, who you sat next to, and which week was most recent." |

| 1:35 | Fills the other three rows from the lesson's sample. Writes "no evidence" in one empty cell in red marker with a box around it. | "And if she has nothing for a cell, she writes 'no evidence.' An honest gap is information — maybe it's the thing to go collect before Wednesday." |

'You lost momentum whenever a task had no clear next step' is." |
| 3:45 | Big heading: "Declaration memo" and five numbered lines: Declaration · Evidence by source · Strongest case against · What would change my mind · First 30 days. | "Then the memo: one page, five sections. The one that separates a real justification from a generic one is section three." |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Whiteboard (physical or digital) with the matrix drawn large; Keisha's entries written in a distinct marker color **and** in quotation marks so they read as quotes without color.
- Prepared cards for memo sections and failure modes.

## Accessibility

- Captions; every word written on the board is read aloud as it is written.
- Contrast: dark marker on white; no red-on-green distinctions. "No evidence" is boxed and labeled, not only red.
- Provide the completed matrix and memo excerpt as text in the transcript.

## Check for understanding

1. A matrix cell reads "really good at it." What's wrong, and what should replace it? **Answer:** It's an adjective with no evidence. Replace it with a number or a dated quote (e.g. "41 outreach, 6 meetings").
2. You performed well in a rotation but your reflection says you dreaded it. What question should you ask yourself? **Answer:** Did I dislike the work itself, or dislike being a beginner at it? Check the interview intelligence for what the daily job really involves.
3. Why does the memo need a "strongest case against" section? **Answer:** It shows you looked for disconfirming evidence. A memo with only supporting evidence tells the reader you didn't.
