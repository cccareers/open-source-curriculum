## Scenario

You've shown a maintenance-scheduling product to **Karen Osei**, facilities manager at a 350-person manufacturer, as in the Lesson 5 practice. In discovery she told you that unplanned downtime on two packaging lines costs "about a shift a month," and that her technicians track preventive work on a shared spreadsheet "nobody trusts." Over five timed rounds she raises the five common objections from Lesson 5. In every round your partner secretly decides whether the line is a brush-off, an objection, or a blocker.

## What you will produce

1. Five recorded rounds of 4 minutes each.
2. An observer scorecard for each round.
3. Your category-call accuracy (how often you read brush-off / objection / blocker correctly).
4. A rewritten four-sentence response for your worst round.
5. A personal drill list: the two objections you find hardest to hear, and why.

## Before you start

Print the cards for your partner. Each card has the line and a hidden intent. Your partner answers your clarifying question honestly, in a sentence or two.

| Round | Karen's line | Hidden intent (partner only) | Honest answer to a good clarifying question |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Value/price | "That's more than we expected." | Objection: no budget line; downtime has never been priced | "There's a software line, but it's sized for the spreadsheet add-on. Nobody's priced the downtime." |
| 2 Timing | "Let's revisit next quarter." | Brush-off: nothing feels urgent | "Honestly, nothing's on fire this week." |
| 3 Authority | "I'd have to run it past my plant director." | Objection: you haven't met him | "Raj signs anything over $10k. He hasn't seen any of this." |
| 4 Status quo | "The spreadsheet works fine for us." | Objection: the cost is hidden and falls on someone else | "Well, the night shift complains they can't see what's overdue." |
| 5 Trust/risk | "How do we know this will actually work here?" | Blocker, swapped in on the day: corporate is mandating a group-wide CMMS next year | "Actually, corporate just told us they're picking one system for every plant next year." |

Hard constraints for the rep, in every round: don't mention price flexibility, and don't use the words "actually" or "but."

## Milestones

1. **Warm up (15 min).** Write a one-line acknowledgement and one clarifying question for each objection type. Say each aloud five times.
2. **Run rounds 1–5 (25 min).** Use the five-step pattern each time: pause, acknowledge, clarify, evidence, confirm and move. After each round, say which category you think it was before your partner reveals the card.
3. **Score (15 min).** The observer marks each step present or absent, notes any banned word or price mention, and records whether the round ended with a confirmed next move.
4. **Rewrite (15 min).** For your worst round, write a four-sentence response: acknowledgement, clarifying question, evidence, confirmation.
5. **Blocker re-run (5 min).** Re-run round 5. Your job is to stop selling within 30 seconds and leave with a specific date to come back.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] All five rounds recorded.
- [ ] No price flexibility offered in any round.
- [ ] A clarifying question asked in at least four of the five rounds, before any evidence.
- [ ] The evidence in rounds 1 and 4 uses Karen's own words from discovery ("about a shift a month," "nobody trusts").
- [ ] In the round 5 re-run you stop selling within 30 seconds and leave with a date.
- [ ] Category-call accuracy reported out of 5.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Composure | Answers instantly, defensive tone | Visible pause, then a neutral acknowledgement | Acknowledgement sounds genuinely curious, and Karen keeps talking |
| Diagnosis | Responds to the surface words | Clarifying question in 4 of 5 rounds | Category read correctly in 4+ of 5 rounds |
| Evidence | Product claims or enthusiasm | Karen's own numbers or a specific, true proof point | Offers to test on her data rather than asserting results |
| Close of each round | Moves on without checking | Asks "Does that address it?" and waits | Lands a specific next step (e.g., meeting Raj) |
| Blocker handling | Keeps selling | Stops and dates a return | Asks what made them choose and when to reconnect, and records both |

## Stretch goals

- Swap roles and play Karen. Write down what it felt like when the rep did or didn't pause.
- Run the drill again a week later and compare accuracy.

## Reflection prompts

- Which objection made you most want to discount or argue? What did you do instead?
- Which clarifying question changed your read of the category?

## Instructor notes

- Round 2 is a brush-off. The right move is to test gently for interest ("Is that because something else is more urgent, or because this isn't a problem worth solving?") and to accept a no.
- Learners often treat round 5 as a trust objection and start quoting case studies. Reward the stop.
- This works as a 60-minute workshop with pairs rotating every round.
