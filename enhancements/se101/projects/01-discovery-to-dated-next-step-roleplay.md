## Scenario

You represent the small field-service platform from Lesson 4 (mobile job capture, automatic evidence matching, scheduling with skills and location, customer status notifications, exportable job history). Your buyer is **Rosa Delgado**, the scheduler at a home-services company with 30 field technicians — the same company you met in the Lesson 3 practice card. Jobs are booked by phone, written onto a whiteboard, and texted to technicians each morning. The owner, **Mark**, has told Rosa to "sort it out" before summer.

You will run one complete first conversation with Rosa: discovery, a capability mapping, a short recap, and a dated ask. A partner plays Rosa from the persona card below; an observer scores you.

## What you will produce

1. A recorded (audio or video) 15-minute role-play.
2. Your two-column call notes (Rosa's exact words | your interpretation).
3. A four-column mapping table (mechanism | impact | capability | evidence it will), including at least one "no fit" row.
4. The same-day recap email (five lines maximum).
5. A one-paragraph self-assessment against the rubric.

## Before you start

- Complete Lessons 3, 4, and 6.
- Print the persona card for your partner. **Do not read it yourself.**

**Persona card — Rosa Delgado (for the partner only)**

- Role: scheduler, 6 years at the company. Reports to Mark, the owner. Mark approves any spend over $500 a month.
- What you will volunteer: "Scheduling is a mess. We use a whiteboard."
- What you will reveal only if asked an open question about what goes wrong: roughly twice a week a job is double-booked or missed. Each one means a wasted truck roll and an apology call.
- What you will reveal only if the rep pauses after an answer: "Honestly the bigger thing is the calls — customers ringing to ask where the tech is. I take maybe fifteen of those a day and I have no idea where anyone is."
- What you will reveal only if asked about consequences of doing nothing: summer volume roughly doubles, and last summer you lost two commercial contracts over no-shows.
- Hidden constraint: two older technicians refuse to use smartphone apps. Mention it only if the rep asks how technicians would receive changes.
- At the close: if the rep asks for a dated meeting that includes Mark, agree to one of the two dates offered. If the rep asks vaguely ("can I follow up?"), say "Sure, send me something."

## Milestones

1. **Prepare (30 min).** Write six open questions using the forms from Lesson 3 (process, frequency, failure, impact, history, consequence of doing nothing). Write one closed question you will use to pin down a number.
2. **Run discovery (10 min of the role-play).** No product talk. Pause two beats after every answer. Deliver one mid-call playback ending in "have I got that right, or did I miss something?"
3. **Map (during a 3-minute "let me get that down" pause, then refine after).** Separate the double-booking mechanism from the "where is my tech" mechanism. Note the two technicians without smartphones as a gap.
4. **Recap and ask (final 2 minutes).** Problem in Rosa's words, one capability, a proposed test, a dated two-option ask that names Mark, then silence.
5. **Write up (45 min).** Clean the mapping table, send the recap email, score yourself.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] At least six open questions asked; no leading or stacked questions.
- [ ] At least one playback, and Rosa's correction (if any) recorded verbatim.
- [ ] The mapping table separates the two problems Rosa bundled together.
- [ ] At least one honest "no fit" or partial-fit row (for example, the technicians without smartphones).
- [ ] The ask names an activity, Mark, two date options, and something you will do beforehand.
- [ ] The recap email is five lines or fewer and states both sides' commitments and the date.

## Evidence checklist

- [ ] Recording file (or timestamped transcript)
- [ ] Notes in two columns
- [ ] Mapping table
- [ ] Recap email
- [ ] Observer's completed rubric
- [ ] Self-assessment paragraph

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Questioning | Mostly closed questions, or any product talk during discovery | Six or more open questions; closed questions only to pin facts | Follow-up questions built from Rosa's own words surface the "where is my tech" calls and the summer consequence |
| Listening and playback | No playback, or playback in rep's language | One playback in Rosa's words, ends with an invitation to correct | Playbacks at a mid-point and at the end; correction incorporated visibly |
| Problem-to-capability mapping | Feature list; problems not separated | Two mechanisms separated and each mapped to one capability with evidence | Volunteers the smartphone gap and proposes how to handle it honestly |
| Close | Ends in a continuation ("I'll follow up") | Dated, named, two-option ask; rep stays silent after it | Advance includes Mark and a proposed test with Rosa's own data; recap email lets a stranger say what happens next |

## Stretch goals

- Re-run the role-play with a different partner who answers "let me think about it" at the close. Land a smaller dated step in four sentences or fewer.
- Write the handoff note you would send a sales engineer before the demo with Mark (three bullets, customer's words only).

## Reflection prompts

- Which single question produced the most useful answer, and why did it work?
- Where did you feel the urge to pitch, and what did you do instead?
- If Mark joined the next meeting cold, what would he need to hear in the first two minutes?

## Instructor notes

- The most common failure is mapping "scheduling is a mess" straight to the scheduling capability and never surfacing the status-call problem. If no learner pauses long enough to hear it, replay a recording and stop at each answer to ask "what might she have added here?"
- The second most common failure is a close that skips Mark. Rosa cannot approve the spend; any advance that does not create a path to him is a continuation in disguise.
- For a 90-minute session, cut milestone 5 to just the mapping table and the ask.
