## Purpose

After watching, the learner can end a conversation with an advance rather than a continuation, using a three-part recap, a specific, dated, two-sided ask with two options, and silence afterwards. The learner can also handle "let me think about it" without dropping to a vague follow-up.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read Lesson 6. Uses the Lesson 3 and Lesson 6 scenario: Priya manages a support team, breached the four-hour first-response commitment eleven times last quarter, and her director sees that number every quarter.

| 0:10 | Title: "Take 1: the continuation." Video-call split screen. | |
| 0:13 | Rep (Jordan). | **Jordan:** "This has been really helpful, Priya, thanks so much. I'll send over some materials on our platform and follow up in a week or so?" |
| 0:20 | Priya, pleasant. | **Priya:** "Sounds great, thanks." |

| 0:50 | Title: "Take 2: the advance." | |
| 0:53 | Chip: "Their problem, their words." | **Alex:** "Before we run out of time, let me sum up where I think we are. First response gets breached about eleven times a quarter, when tickets land during a handover and sit in the shared inbox. That number goes in front of your director every quarter." |
| 1:05 | Priya. | **Priya:** "That's right." |
| 1:07 | Chip: "What changes, and the proof." | **Alex:** "The part of ours that speaks to that is automatic assignment on arrival, so nothing sits unowned during a handover. | 1:20 | Priya. | **Priya:** "That would be interesting, actually." |
| 1:23 | Chip: "Specific, named, two-sided, dated, two options." | **Alex:** "Then here's what I'd suggest. Forty-five minutes with you and whoever can pull that ticket history. Is that Sam? I'll prepare the walkthrough beforehand. Would Tuesday the 12th in the morning work, or is Thursday better?" |
| 1:36 | Silence, 4 seconds; on-screen counter; caption "[4-second pause]". | *(silence)* |
| 1:40 | Priya. | **Priya:** "Thursday. Sam's off Tuesdays." |
| 1:43 | Alex. | **Alex:** "Thursday it is. I'll send the invite this afternoon and a note confirming what we'll need from Sam." |
| 1:48 | Freeze. Six-property checklist ticks: specific, dated, named, two-sided, proportional, then silent. | Host VO: "Under two minutes, and nothing clever. A recap in Priya's words, one capability, a proposed test, a dated ask with two options, and then silence. Priya agreed to spend something real: time, and Sam." |
| 2:20 | Title: "Variation: 'Let me think about it.'" | |
| 2:23 | Priya, at the ask. | **Priya:** "Let me think about it." |
| 2:25 | Alex. Chip: "Acknowledge, then clarify." | **Alex:** "Of course. So I send the right thing over, is it the fit you're weighing, or the timing?" |
| 2:31 | Priya. | **Priya:** "Timing. We've got an audit in two weeks." |
| 2:34 | Alex. Chip: "Smaller dated step." | **Alex:** "That makes sense. Then let's not do the full walkthrough now. Could I send you a one-page summary of the eleven breaches by Friday, and we put twenty minutes in for the week after your audit, say Wednesday the 27th?" |
| 2:46 | Priya. | **Priya:** "Yes, that works." |
| 2:48 | Freeze. | Host VO: "'Let me think about it' almost always hides an unresolved concern. One clarifying question found it, and Alex asked for a smaller step that still has a date." |
| 3:10 | Recap email on screen, five lines: problem / what I'll do / what you'll do / date / confirm. | Host VO: "Same day, write it down: the problem as you understood it, what you agreed to do, what they agreed to do, and the date. Five lines, no attachments unless you promised one." |

| 3:50 | Practice prompt. | "Pause. Write the advance you'd ask for at the end of a first call with someone who doesn't own the process. One sentence: activity, people, two date options." |

## Check for understanding

1. Why is Take 1 a continuation? *(Answer: no date, no named people, and nothing owed by the customer. Only warmth.)*
2. Name the six properties of a good ask. *(Answer: specific, dated, named, two-sided, proportional, and then silent.)*
3. What did Alex do with "let me think about it"? *(Answer: acknowledged it, asked whether it was fit or timing, then proposed a smaller step with a date.)*
