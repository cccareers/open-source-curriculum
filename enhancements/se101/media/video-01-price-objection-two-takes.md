## Purpose

After watching, the learner can hear a price objection, ask one clarifying question to find out which objection it really is, and respond with the customer's own evidence instead of a discount or a rebuttal.

## Audience and prerequisites

Apprentices in their first weeks. Assumes Lesson 5 has been read up to "A response pattern that works."

## Script

Setting for both takes: a video call. **Rep** (Jordan) is selling a maintenance-scheduling product. **Customer** (Karen Osei) is facilities manager at a 350-person manufacturer. In discovery she said unplanned downtime on two packaging lines cost "about a shift a month," and that her maintenance techs track preventive work on a shared spreadsheet that nobody trusts.

## Accessibility

- Open captions for all dialogue, with speaker names.
- Callouts use icon + text + outline style, never color alone (red/green also distinguished by cross and check icons).
- Described visuals: "Karen folds her arms" and "Karen uncrosses her arms" are spoken by the host in the freeze-frame VO for audio-only learners; the 3-second silence is announced on the timer and in captions as "[3 seconds of silence]".
- Provide a downloadable transcript of both takes.

## Check for understanding

1. In the weak take, what question did the discount answer, and what question should Jordan have asked instead? *Answer: it answered "can you make the number smaller?", which Karen never asked. He should have asked whether the issue was the number itself or the lack of a budget line.*
2. Why does Jordan's strong take *not* claim a specific ROI? *Answer: he did not know what a shift of output was worth; asserting it would be an unsupported claim. Letting finance supply the number makes the evidence the customer's own.*
3. If Karen had answered the clarifying question with "There's no budget until next April, full stop," what category is that, and what should Jordan do? *Answer: a blocker. Stop selling, confirm the fact, and agree a specific date to reconnect before April planning.*
