## Purpose
After watching, the learner can turn raw security numbers into informative metrics, assign RAG status by stated definitions, write a bottom-line paragraph that leads with the bad news, and frame a decision request with options.

## Audience and prerequisites
cyb150-06. No tools needed.

## Script
| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Presenter. An inset shows a report page that is all green. | "Here's a security report where everything is green, every month. Executives stopped reading it a year ago. The month something finally goes wrong, nobody notices. Today: how to write the report people actually act on." |
| 0:20 | Three questions on screen: How are we doing? Compared to what? What do you need to decide? | "A posture report answers three questions, in order. How are we doing, measured. Compared to what: a target, last quarter, a commitment. And what do you need to decide. Miss any one and the report has failed, even if every number is correct." |
| 0:45 | Vanity metric card: "22,914 malicious emails blocked." It flips to: "Phishing simulation click rate: 7% (was 6%), target under 5%. Harder template this quarter." | "This is Meridian's raw Q3 material. '22,914 emails blocked' is scenery: bigger isn't better or worse. Replace it with something that has a comparison. Click rate seven percent, up from six, target under five. And we say why in the same line: we used a harder test this quarter. Report the bad movement honestly, with the reason beside it." |
| 1:35 | Formula on screen: [measure] [now] [vs target or commitment] [trend] [who owns the gap]. | "Every metric follows one pattern: measure, current value, target or commitment, trend, and who owns the gap." |
| 1:55 | RAG definitions card from the lesson. | "Now colour. Define it in the report. Green: at or better than target. Amber: off target, with an owner and a dated plan. Red: no owner or plan, or a customer or regulatory commitment currently not met." |
| 2:20 | Patch metric row: "Median days to patch critical, internet-facing: 8 / target 7 / was 11 / in 14 customer agreements". Cursor hovers between AMBER and RED. | "Here's the hard one. Patch time improved from eleven days to eight. There's an owner and a plan. Tempting to call it amber, right? But seven days is written into fourteen signed customer agreements, and we're at eight. By our own definition, that's red." |
| 3:00 | RED selected. Caption: "Red = a decision request, not a failure." | "Red isn't the security team failing. It's a request for a decision. With published definitions, red becomes usable instead of embarrassing. And if someone asks you to soften it, ask what evidence would support amber. Usually that ends the conversation." |
| 3:30 | Bottom-line draft typed, sentence by sentence. | "Now the bottom line, written last and read first. 'Meridian's posture improved this quarter, and one customer commitment is still not met. Our SOC 2 Type II report was issued with a single exception, a late access review. Patch time for critical internet-facing flaws fell from eleven days to eight, against a seven-day commitment in fourteen agreements. A laptop was stolen in August. It was encrypted and wiped remotely, and the notification question is open with counsel. Two decisions are requested.'" |
| 4:30 | Callout on the laptop sentence: "facts only — no 'not a breach'". | "Look at the laptop sentence. It states facts and who owns the open question. It does not say 'this was not a breach'. That's a legal call, and it isn't ours." |
| 4:50 | Decision request card: "D1 ISO 27001 for the European customer. Owner: CTO. Needed by: 2026-10-31. Option A: hire a consultant-led programme (~$X, 12 months). Option B: assign internal lead and defer other work. If no decision: an 18-month contractual clock runs with no owner." | "Finally, decisions. Not 'we need resources'. Give an owner, a date, two options with a rough cost, and the consequence of choosing neither. The ISO commitment has an eighteen-month clock and nobody assigned. That's an ask with teeth." |
| 5:35 | Recap. | "Comparisons on every metric. Colours by definition. Bad news in the bottom line. Facts instead of legal conclusions. Options with consequences. Now write your Q3 report for the practice." |

## On-screen assets and B-roll
- Report page mock-ups; metric flip cards; RAG definitions card; decision request card. Leave "$X" for the instructor to set, as the course provides no cost figures.

## Accessibility
- Captions throughout. Every card is read aloud.
- RAG states always appear as the words GREEN/AMBER/RED alongside the colour, and the selected state is shown with a thick outline.
- Talking-head shots well lit for lip-reading; insets at least 40% of the frame.

## Check for understanding
1. Why is "22,914 malicious emails blocked" a vanity metric? *It has no comparison and measures attempts, not how effective the defences are.*
2. Patch time improved and has a plan, but still misses a contractual commitment. What colour? *Red, by the stated definitions.*
3. What must a decision request contain? *An owner, a date, at least two options with cost or effort, and the consequence of no decision.*
