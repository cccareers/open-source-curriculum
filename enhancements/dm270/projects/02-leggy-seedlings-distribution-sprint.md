## Scenario

Lesson 07 found that "Why Seedlings Get Leggy" works (73.2% engagement, 1.60% signup rate) but got one social post and 1,940 sessions. The decision was **Scale**: give it the full six-week window from lesson 06. You are the person doing it. Priya has 10 writer-hours a week and is already committed to the calendar; you have **12 hours across six weeks** for this sprint, plus 30 minutes of Elena's time.

The rule of the sprint: **write derivatives from the research notes, not from the article.**

## What you will produce
1. A **repurposing map** (all eight columns from lesson 06) with at least six derivatives across at least four channels, including a refresh row, with the effort column summed against 12 hours.
2. **Three finished derivatives** on three different channels, each adding something the article does not have.
3. A **six-week dated schedule** with an evergreen loop.
4. A **tagged link table** and **accessibility documentation** per derivative.

## Milestones
1. **Name the research**: one paragraph on what the piece cost and what it bought, listing the parts the article does not use.
2. **Map**: six or more derivatives; each row names the research source; sum the hours; cut rows until it fits 12 hours with a little slack.
3. **Produce three** derivatives for real. Suggested set: a carousel explaining the supplied 18-inch versus 3-inch tray comparison and the hanging-kit note; a 30-second vertical video answering "windowsill?" from existing footage; a newsletter feature leading with the "your kit ships hung too high" fact.
4. **Schedule** six weeks with a comment/community pass, one re-share of the best derivative, and an evergreen loop timed to next February-March.
5. **Tag and document**: one tagged URL per derivative with distinct `utm_content`; alt text written out; captions delivery stated.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Every map row cites a specific line from the research notes.
- [ ] Effort total is shown and is at or under 12 hours; any cut rows are named.
- [ ] No derivative reuses a sentence from the article; each states what it adds.
- [ ] The video derivative has a two-column A/V script with timecodes, a word count checked against 130-150 words per minute, and a corrected caption file excerpt.
- [ ] Every spoken number in the video also appears as on-screen text.
- [ ] Each image has alt text written in full; the carousel's text also appears in the caption.
- [ ] At least one derivative answers a reader question from the notes.
- [ ] Two channels are explicitly excluded with reasons, at least one being "we cannot sustain it" or "no audience there".
- [ ] The schedule has a dated re-share and an evergreen loop with months named.

## Evidence checklist
Research paragraph, map with hours arithmetic, three finished derivatives (copy or exported files), A/V script and caption excerpt, schedule, tagged link table, accessibility notes, exclusions.

## Rubric
| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |

## Instructor notes
Common failure: learners open the published article and summarise it nine times. Insist the research notes file is the only allowed source for derivative copy. The "kit ships hung too high" and "windowsill: rotate daily" lines are the strongest unpublished material; the six-seedling chart is mentioned but not supplied: do not invent its values. Use the provided comparison for the carousel, or obtain the notebook chart before proposing that derivative. The existing footage (22 clips) means the video derivative should cost two hours, not a reshoot. Lesson 07's arithmetic predicted about 93 signups at 1.60% on 5,800 sessions; Q2 delivered 88 on 5,200 (see project dm270-x01), so this sprint is the next step, not the first.
