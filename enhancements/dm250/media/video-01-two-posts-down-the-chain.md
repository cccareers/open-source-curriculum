## Purpose
After watching, a learner can take two posts' raw numbers, push both to gross profit, and name the exact link in the chain where the weaker one lost, instead of ranking posts by reach or likes.

## Audience and prerequisites
Apprentices who have read lesson 06 up to "The worked case that makes this concrete." Comfortable with a spreadsheet; no platform account needed.

## On-screen assets and B-roll
- Spreadsheet template with chain-order row labels (provide as a downloadable `.csv`).
- Thumbnails of the two Fernwood posts (mock-ups; no real brand assets).
- Lower-third style: formula shown in monospace beneath each computed cell for 2 seconds.

## Accessibility
- Burned-in and uploaded captions; every number spoken is also typed on screen.
- The leak is marked by an orange dashed border **and** the text label "LEAK: response link", never by colour alone.
- Spreadsheet zoomed to at least 150% so cell text is legible on a phone; cursor highlight enabled.
- Audio description track or transcript describes each table state ("Post A column now shows gross profit $22.08").

## Check for understanding
1. Post C reached 20,000 people with 900 profile visits and 30 clicks. Which link is weaker, response or visit, and why? *Answer: visit; profile visits/reach is 4.5% (healthy) but clicks/profile visits is 3.3%, so people arrived at the profile and did not go further — check the bio link and offer.*
2. Why is "carousels beat Reels" the wrong conclusion from this comparison? *Answer: the difference appears at the response link and is explained by topic relevance to the audience, not by format; a Reel on the grind problem might perform like Post B.*
3. Name one caveat that belongs in the report alongside Post B's contribution. *Answer: the $63.84 is lifetime gross profit realised over about seven months, while the labour cost is incurred now (or: Post A's reach has unattributed brand value).*
