## Purpose
After watching, a learner can write an idea's spine (claim, evidence, payoff) and adapt it into platform-native containers that change the hook, audience situation, call to action and commitment level instead of cross-posting the same copy.

## Audience and prerequisites
Apprentices who have read lesson 03 up to "One Idea, Five Formats". A document editor; no design tool needed.

## On-screen assets and B-roll
- Adaptation table template (Container | Hook | Audience situation | CTA | Commitment).
- Mock platform frames (generic, not real UI screenshots) to avoid dating the video.
- Lesson 03 copy for all five containers as on-screen text.

## Accessibility
- Captions; all on-screen copy is read aloud or reproduced in the transcript.
- Table cells carry text tags, not colour-only highlighting.
- Mock frames have high-contrast text at phone size.

## Check for understanding
1. Why does the carousel ask for a save rather than a click? *Answer: Instagram does not surface clickable links in feed captions, and saves are the signal it rewards for teaching content.*
2. What changed between the carousel and the LinkedIn post besides length? *Answer: the audience situation — home brewer vs office manager handling staff complaints — so the evidence and CTA change while the claim stays.*
3. Name two signs a post was cross-posted rather than adapted. *Answer: "link in bio" on Facebook; hashtag stacks on LinkedIn; burned-in text where the platform UI sits; the same first line on every surface.*
