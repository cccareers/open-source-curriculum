## Purpose

After watching, the learner can take a funnel of raw counts, compute step-to-step rates, name the weakest journey stage, and explain why buying more awareness would not fix it.

## Audience and prerequisites

New digital marketing apprentices who have read the customer journey section of lesson 02. No tools beyond a calculator.

## Script

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Hand-drawn bucket with water pouring in the top and a jet spraying from a hole near the bottom. | "Here is the most expensive mistake in marketing: pouring more water into a bucket with a hole in it. Today you will learn to find the hole first." |
| 0:20 | Title card: "Find the leaking step." | "We will use the small online retailer from lesson two." |
| 0:30 | Whiteboard writes five rows: Sessions 40,000; Product pages 12,000; Add-to-cart 1,200; Checkout started 600; Orders 150. | "Five numbers. Sessions, product page views, add-to-cart, checkout started, orders. Before you have an opinion, compute the rate between each pair." |
| 1:00 | Presenter writes arrows between rows with rates: 30%, 10%, 50%, 25%. | "Forty thousand to twelve thousand is thirty percent. Twelve thousand to twelve hundred is ten percent. Twelve hundred to six hundred is fifty. Six hundred to one hundred fifty is twenty-five." |
| 1:40 | Circles the 10% and the 25%. | "Two numbers look low. Ten percent of product viewers add to cart, and only a quarter of people who start checkout finish. Which one is the leak? Low is not enough. You need to ask: low compared with what, and at what point in the decision?" |
| 2:10 | Draws journey labels beside rows: Awareness, Consideration, Decision. | "Product page to cart is consideration. People are still comparing, and many leave for good reasons. Checkout is decision. These people already chose you. They had their card out. Losing three out of four of them is the leak." |
| 2:45 | Bucket returns; arrow labelled "More ads" pouring in, water spraying out the checkout hole. | "So what happens if you double the ad budget? You get twice as many people, and three quarters of the ones who reach checkout still walk away. You have paid for the same leak twice." |
| 3:10 | List appears: unexpected shipping cost; forced account creation; payment method broken on mobile. | "The fix is investigation. Is the shipping cost a surprise at the last step? Are you forcing people to create an account? Does a payment button fail on phones? Each of these is a decision-stage problem, and none of them is solved by awareness spending." |
| 3:45 | New funnel for the meal-kit company from lesson 02 practice: impressions 900,000; clicks 18,000; started signup 2,700; completed 2,300; kept past first box 460. | "Your turn. This is the meal-kit company from the practice section. Pause the video and compute each rate." |
| 4:00 | Pause card with a 5-second countdown, then rates appear: 2%, 15%, 85%, 20%. | "Clicks to impressions is two percent. Fifteen percent of clickers start signup. Eighty-five percent finish it. And only twenty percent keep the subscription past the first box." |
| 4:30 | Circles 20% and writes "Retention" beside it. | "Signup is fine. The weak link is retention: four out of five new customers leave after one box. More impressions would buy more people who leave. The money belongs in the first-box experience and the onboarding emails." |
| 5:00 | Three-line checklist appears: 1. Compute every step rate. 2. Label each step with a journey stage. 3. Fix the weakest stage before you add traffic. | "Three moves. Compute every step. Label the stage. Fix the weakest stage before you buy more traffic." |
| 5:30 | Caveat card: "Check the volume behind the rate." | "One caution from lesson seven: a rate built on a handful of people is not a finding. Twenty-five percent of six hundred is a pattern. Twenty-five percent of eight is noise." |
| 5:50 | End card. | "Next time someone says marketing isn't working, ask them where it stops working." |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Whiteboard (physical or digital) with the two funnel tables pre-ruled.
- A simple bucket illustration reused in three scenes.
- Optional B-roll: anonymised phone screen recording of a checkout form demanding account creation.

## Accessibility

- Captions verbatim from narration.
- Every number written on the board is also spoken aloud.
- Circled values are additionally marked with a star symbol so the cue does not depend on pen colour.
- The pause card announces "Pause now" in narration, not only visually.

## Check for understanding

1. A funnel shows 50,000 sessions, 5,000 sign-up starts, and 4,600 completed sign-ups, but only 300 active users after 30 days. Where would you invest first? **Answer:** Retention or onboarding; sign-up completion is 92%, but only 300 of 4,600 (about 6.5%) stay active.
2. Why is "the product page to cart rate is only 10%" not automatically the leak? **Answer:** Consideration-stage drop-off is expected because people are still comparing; you judge a step against its stage and against your own history, not against an absolute idea of "low."
3. What should you check before acting on a 40% drop at one step? **Answer:** The absolute volume behind it; a large rate change on a handful of people may be noise.
