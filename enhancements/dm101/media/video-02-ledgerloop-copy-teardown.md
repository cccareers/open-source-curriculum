## Purpose

After watching, the learner can run the four-step review pass from lesson 03 (headline and CTA alone, delete adjectives, check claims, read aloud) on a weak block of copy and rewrite it so every line traces back to a positioning statement.

## Audience and prerequisites

Learners who have read lesson 03 through "Copywriting best practices." Familiar with a word processor's track-changes view.

## Script

| Time | Visual / On screen | Narration |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Document on screen with the original LedgerLoop block: "Welcome to LedgerLoop. LedgerLoop is a very powerful and innovative cloud-based solution that leverages cutting-edge technology in order to streamline your bookkeeping workflows and help take your practice to the next level. Learn More" | "This is a real kind of homepage block. Every word is grammatical and nothing is said. Let's fix it the way a working copywriter would, one pass at a time." |
| 0:25 | Split screen: left the weak block, right the LedgerLoop positioning statement from the lesson. | "We will not start from the weak copy. We start from the positioning statement, because every line we write has to trace back to it. Audience: independent bookkeepers with five to thirty clients. Problem: four to six hours a week re-keying transactions. Differentiator: one reconciliation queue. Reason to believe: eleven bank aggregators covering ninety-four percent of US small-business accounts." |
| 1:10 | Highlight only "Welcome to LedgerLoop" and "Learn More." Rest of block greyed out. | "Pass one. Read only the headline and the call to action. 'Welcome to LedgerLoop.' 'Learn More.' Would a bookkeeper scanning for one second know what this is or what happens when they click? No. The headline is about the company and the button does not say where it goes." |
| 1:45 | Track changes strikes through: very, powerful, innovative, cloud-based, cutting-edge, in order to. | "Pass two. Delete every adjective and filler phrase and see what breaks. Very. Powerful. Innovative. Cutting-edge. 'In order to' becomes 'to'. What is left: 'LedgerLoop is a solution that leverages technology to streamline your bookkeeping workflows.' Nothing broke, which tells you the adjectives carried nothing." |
| 2:30 | Remaining phrase highlighted: "streamline your bookkeeping workflows" and "take your practice to the next level." A ladder graphic appears: "optimize your workflow" / "stop re-typing transactions" / "get Friday afternoons back." | "Pass three. Check each claim for evidence. 'Streamline your workflows' cannot be checked; it sits high on the abstraction ladder. 'Take your practice to the next level' could be printed by any company on earth. Climb down the ladder until you reach something the bookkeeper recognises: stop re-typing transactions." |
| 3:20 | Typing the new headline: "Every client's books reconciled in one queue." | "Now we write. Headline pattern: outcome. The primary message from the messaging set is 'Every client's books, reconciled in one queue.' It is about the reader, it is specific, and a competitor without a single queue cannot put their name on it." |
| 3:55 | Typing body: "Direct bank feeds for 94% of US small-business accounts pull transactions in automatically, so you stop re-typing them Friday afternoon. Built for bookkeepers running 5 to 30 clients." | "Body copy. Benefit first, feature as the reason to believe. 'Direct bank feeds for ninety-four percent of accounts' is the feature. 'So you stop re-typing them Friday afternoon' is the benefit in the customer's own words, which came from the interviews. The last sentence tells the right reader this is for them, and tells the wrong reader to keep scrolling." |
| 4:40 | Typing CTA: "See the reconciliation queue" and under it "3-minute tour, no signup." | "The call to action starts with a verb and says exactly what happens next. The line beneath it removes the cost of clicking. This is a consideration-stage page, so we ask for a tour, not a purchase." |
| 5:10 | Sticky note beside "94%": "Evidence on file? Source: aggregator coverage report, dated." | "Before this ships, one more check. Ninety-four percent is a factual claim. Under the advertising rules in lesson eight, you need the evidence in hand when you publish. If the source is a vendor estimate from two years ago, either update it or soften the claim." |
| 5:40 | Pass four: presenter reads final block aloud; waveform on screen. | "Pass four. Read it out loud. 'Every client's books reconciled in one queue. Direct bank feeds pull transactions in automatically, so you stop re-typing them Friday afternoon.' That is a sentence you could say to a customer's face." |
| 6:10 | Before and after side by side with word counts: 34 words vs 43 words including the CTA line; claims column: 0 checkable vs 3 checkable. | "The new version is actually a little longer, and that is fine. Length was never the problem. What changed is that the new version makes three claims a reader can check, names its audience, and tells them what happens when they click." |
| 6:40 | End card: "Your turn: rewrite the FreshCrate block from Practice Part 3." | "Now run the same four passes on the FreshCrate block in the practice section. Start from a positioning statement, not from the bad copy." |

## On-screen assets and B-roll

- Word processor with track changes visible; font size at least 18 pt for legibility.
- Positioning statement and messaging set from lesson 03 as a pinned side panel.
- Abstraction ladder graphic (three labelled rungs).

## Accessibility

- Captions verbatim; deleted words are read aloud as they are struck through so the change is not conveyed only by colour or strikethrough.
- Highlight colour paired with a bracket marker in the margin.
- The final before-and-after comparison is also read aloud.

## Check for understanding

1. Why does the rewrite start from the positioning statement rather than from the weak copy? **Answer:** Because every claim must trace back to positioning; editing weak copy line by line tends to polish sentences that should not exist.
2. A colleague proposes "The smartest way to reconcile." What two tests does it fail? **Answer:** A competitor could put their name on it unchanged, and it is an unfalsifiable claim with no evidence.
3. Why is "See the reconciliation queue" better than "Buy now" on this page? **Answer:** The reader is at consideration; the ask should be the proportionate next step, not a decision they have no basis to make yet.
