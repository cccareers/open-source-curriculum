## Scenario

Half Step Coffee (Brief 2 in the course project) is a single-location roaster with 4,100 social followers, an email list of 900 addresses collected at the counter, and a goal of growing online subscriptions ($22 a month, 45% gross margin, average stay 7 months). The owner, who also roasts, has about four hours a week for marketing, and a part-time barista, Jo, has offered two hours a week.

Before spending a dollar on ads, the owner wants the free, warm assets working: an organic social plan that runs for 30 days, a welcome sequence for new email subscribers, and a reply playbook for the comments that will follow. She also learned that the 900 counter emails were collected on a paper sheet headed "Join our list for news & offers," and she wants to know if she can use them.

## What you will build / produce

1. **Presence plan (one page):** primary channel and why, the account's primary and secondary job, three to five content pillars, and the metrics you will judge it by.
2. **30-day content calendar:** a table with date, pillar, post idea (a real working title), format, owner (Owner or Jo), and the journey stage it serves. Capacity must fit six hours a week, including the daily reply pass.
3. **Welcome sequence:** a four- or five-email table (number, day, job, subject, preheader, CTA), email 1 written in full, and exit rules.
4. **Reply playbook:** triage buckets with a drafted reply for each of the five sample comments below, and an escalation policy.
5. **Compliance memo (half page):** whether and how the 900 counter emails may be used, plus a pre-flight checklist pass over everything above.

## Before you start (prerequisites, starter files or data)

Sample comments received on the shop's account last month:

```text
C1  "Do you ship to Canada?"
C2  "Ordered beans 9 days ago, still nothing. Second time this happened."
C3  "Your Ethiopia roast is the best thing in my kitchen."
C4  "Saw your ad - 'the healthiest coffee in town'? Prove it."
C5  "lol overpriced hipster bean water" (account created yesterday, no posts)
```

Last month's post performance export (use it to choose pillars and cadence): `avg_*` columns are already per-post averages; `profile_link_clicks` is the total across that row's posts.

```text
post_type,posts,avg_reach,avg_saves,avg_comments,profile_link_clicks
roast_day_video,4,1850,22,14,41
brew_guide_carousel,3,2400,96,9,63
shop_photo,9,610,3,4,6
staff_pick,2,1100,18,11,19
promo_discount,3,700,2,1,12
```

Assumption for the welcome sequence: new subscribers arrive through a website form offering a free "Dial In Your Grinder" one-page guide.

## Milestones

1. Read the performance export and choose pillars; justify cutting or reducing at least one post type with numbers.
2. Draft the presence plan and the calendar. Total the hours.
3. Write the welcome sequence, applying give, give, prove, ask.
4. Draft the five replies and the escalation policy.
5. Write the compliance memo and run the pre-flight checklist from lesson 08 over the calendar, sequence, and replies.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The calendar fits the stated six hours a week, and the reply pass is scheduled with an owner.
- [ ] Pillars are justified with the export (for example, brew guides earn the most saves and link clicks per post; shop photos earn the least).
- [ ] Every email has one job and one primary CTA; the subscription offer does not appear before the third email.
- [ ] Exit rules state what happens when a subscriber buys a subscription mid-sequence and when someone opens nothing.
- [ ] C2 is handled with acknowledge, own, move to private, close the loop; C5 is not argued with.
- [ ] C4 is recognised as an unsubstantiated health claim in the shop's own ad, and the memo recommends withdrawing or rewording the claim rather than defending it.
- [ ] The memo concludes the counter list may be usable for "news and offers" from Half Step itself, recommends a re-permission email for any use beyond that, and notes that any EU or California contacts change the analysis. The memo is labelled as orientation, not legal advice.

## Evidence checklist

- Presence plan and 30-day calendar (table).
- Welcome sequence table plus email 1 in full.
- Reply playbook with five drafted replies and escalation policy.
- Compliance memo with the completed pre-flight checklist.

## Rubric

| Criterion | Developing | Meets | Exceeds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Presence plan | Lists platforms | One primary channel, one job, data-backed pillars | Explicitly excludes a platform with reasons |
| Calendar realism | Exceeds capacity | Fits six hours with named owners | Includes a planned slack slot and a reuse plan for top posts |
| Welcome sequence | Asks for the sale in email 1 | Give, give, prove, ask with exit rules | Sequence measured end to end with a numeric target |
| Replies | Defensive or generic | Correct bucket and pattern for all five | Escalation thresholds written as numbers |
| Compliance | Says "all compliant" | Finds the health-claim and list-consent issues | Writes the corrected ad line and the re-permission email |

## Stretch goals

- Write the brief, specific public "close the loop" reply for C2 after it is resolved.

## Instructor notes (common pitfalls, how to adapt for time)

- Saves per post (already averaged in the export): brew guide carousels 96, roast-day videos 22, shop photos 3. Link clicks per post: 63 / 3 = 21, 41 / 4 = 10.25, and 6 / 9 = 0.67 respectively. Many learners keep shop photos because they are easy; push them to defend it with the numbers.
- Learners often write a reply to C4 that defends the claim. The point of the exercise is that the problem is the shop's own copy.
- Do not let learners assert a definitive legal ruling on the counter list. Credit memos that state assumptions and recommend checking with counsel or the platform's consent guidance.
- For a three-hour version, cut the calendar to two weeks and the sequence to three emails.
